What was wrong, and it was not the reduction

Per edge and per limit the reduction already reproduced its own integral to 1e-14. What kept the assembled flux 2–12× outside the 1e-10 gate was round-off, amplified twice on the way to a section total. Both factors were measured before anything was changed, on each section's own worst target:

The cancellation is the Stokes reduction itself and cannot be removed: the edge antiderivative is of order the squared major radius while the flux the two limits differ by is not, and the shipped boundary quadrature carries exactly the same structure. What distinguishes the two is the third column. The quadrature's per-limit values are accurate to about one ulp, which is why it lands at 2.4e-12; the closed form's were accurate to hundreds, and hundreds times the same cancellation is 1e-9. So the lever was per-limit accuracy, and the routes that address the cancellation itself were not needed.

Where the hundreds of ulp came from

Instrumenting every contraction inside one edge limit found numerators of degree six carrying monomial coefficients up to 1e+09 against contractions returning 1e+06 — the monomial basis on a unit interval is conditioned at four decades by degree six, and the answer was being formed out of terms that exceeded it by as much. The two deepest were the same term in the flux and in the radial field: the arsinh β₂ weight against the plane denominator.

The cure is the harmonic basis, whose coefficients are bounded by the numerator's own size and whose moments decay. It cannot be applied naively, because it pulls against the pole treatment that the previous rung installed: a root a squared-aspect-ratio past the range end makes that pole's own moment large, so the weight on it — the numerator's value AT that end — has to be exact in the RELATIVE sense, and in a harmonic series that value is an alternating sum. Each range function is therefore carried as

N = N(phi = 0) x  +  N(phi = pi) y  +  x y T

with both end values formed from the geometry and only the bulk as a series. Products multiply end values and sums add them, so exactness survives the algebra; and because x y/(y + p) is bounded by one, whatever rounding is left in the bulk reaches the answer unamplified however close the root comes. Splitting a denominator also stops being a root-finding problem — its two shifts follow from its own two end values and its x y coefficient, with no subtraction anywhere.

Three new moment families, and why the third needed two routes

A harmonic numerator needs harmonic moments. All three come from Delta^2 = (1 + k'^2)/2 + (k^2/2) cos 2a folding back under the integral:

  • P_n = int cos(2na)/Delta da obeys a three-term relation and is its MINIMAL solution, so it is run downward — on the RATIOS, which stay bounded where the moments themselves would overflow at a far target, and which carry k^2 = 0 exactly. Downward fails only as k^2 -> 1, where the branches become degenerate; there it is run upward on R_n = P_n - (-1)^n K, the difference from the divergence every order shares. Validated against a 40-digit reference from k'^2 = 1 to 1e-14: worst 15 ulp of K.
  • D_n = int cos(2na) Delta da is three neighbouring P, no new special function.
  • V_n, one per pole factor. A NEAR root is handled by taking its weight out exactly, as above; a FAR root is the opposite case and the exact-weight route is wrong for it — its terms grow with the shift while their sum falls, and at a shift of a hundred it has thrown away four decades. That was the one real bug this rung found, and it showed up as a 1.7e-07 disagreement on a b₁ = 0.1 edge. The far route contracts the family's own moments directly, and the family is a diagonally-dominant tridiagonal solve closed thirty-two orders out.

The corner: a logarithm removed rather than resolved

Both residual arsinh integrands turn genuinely log-singular at a range end whenever the target is level with an edge end, on an edge's extended line, or on a vertex — all of which a grid across a section produces by alignment. No panel grading resolves a logarithm; the previous _LAYER_FLOOR constant recorded that as a measured trade-off. It is now removed instead:

arsinh(N/W) = log(N + sqrt(N^2 + W^2)) - log W

with the first term bounded, so subtracting a model of log W that matches the denominator's own expansion at that end leaves a bounded integrand, and the model's integral is elementary. Two details had to be right for it to work: the branch follows the sign of the numerator's value AT the end (exactly zero there means the numerator vanishes with the denominator and no logarithm survives — the vertex case), and the numerator's sign can turn over INSIDE the half, where that branch's own sum cancels and has to be recovered through W^2 from the other one. Missing the second cost three orders and looked like the method failing.

The gain is where it was aimed: a target on a vertex went from 1.9e-07 to 3.2e-12 on the hexagon and 2.2e-07 to 3.3e-12 on the thin plate, with no change in node count. The one thing that got worse is a target a PICOMETRE off a corner, where the panel still reaches only the floor: 5e-10 to 5.5e-08, five orders inside the band either way, and recorded as such.

The evaluation is 2.2× more expensive than it was and still 2.6× cheaper than the 858 µs/pair boundary quadrature it replaces, while being one to two orders more accurate and — unlike the quadrature — accurate ON the contour, where the 16×48 rule is at 2.6e-04.

Where the 181 µs went, and it is not the harmonic algebra: the moment work got CHEAPER by 12 µs, because one harmonic family replaces two power stacks each with sixty orders of downward-recursion headroom, and the four pole families that used to sit on top of them are now skipped entirely unless some root is far enough out to need one. The whole increase is the residual quadrature. Half of it is the node count, doubled because the most slender section's near-contour targets do not converge at 64 — that limit is pre-existing and unrelated to this rung, measured identically on the previous code. The other half is what the regularised integrand costs per node.

The coefficient algebra started at 2.3× the floor and was brought back to 1.79× by four changes: the range-function product collapses its two end-value terms onto the single two-term series they span, products that carry no component weight are formed once for all three rows, the arctangent's antiderivative is built from scalars rather than once per column, and the pole family is skipped where no root needs it.

The next cost win, and it is structural

Both the first residual quadrature and the entire moment stack depend only on the target and the edge's ENDPOINT — u, the endpoint radius, and the modulus they set — not on the edge's slope. Every vertex of a closed section is the endpoint of two edges, so a vertex-organised assembly would form them once instead of twice, taking roughly 40% off the current figure. The same observation says the arsinh β₁ term TELESCOPES to zero over a closed polygon with no horizontal edge, since it too is a function of the vertex alone — it is being computed and cancelled at present.
