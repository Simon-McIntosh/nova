The primitive: one integral, both arguments complements

Bulirsch's cel is the complete elliptic integral in the general form that covers all three kinds at once, and its natural arguments are exactly the two quantities a caller can supply exactly and cannot recover once lost:

cel(k', p, a, b) = ∫₀^(π/2) dα (a cos²α + b sin²α)
                   / ((cos²α + p sin²α) √(cos²α + k'² sin²α))

K = cel(k', 1, 1, 1)     E = cel(k', 1, 1, k'²)     Π(n | m) = cel(k', 1 − n, 1, 1)

A float parameter cannot carry its own complement: once k'² is below the spacing of the numbers next to one, 1 − k'² has rounded it away, and K, which grows like −log k', comes back wrong by about eps/k'². For a ring the complement is the target's squared distance to the source point over the squared ring span, so a micron from a metre-scale ring is already 1e-12 — the loss is a working configuration, not a corner case.

Accuracy, against four references chosen so none assumes the convention

Extended-precision (80-bit) arithmetic-geometric mean for K, whose π/(2 AGM) form has no cancellation anywhere in it; the hypergeometric series in longdouble for E beyond k'² = 0.1; the small-complement expansion 1 + (k'²/2)(log 4/k' − ½) below 1e-6, whose error is O(k'⁴ log k'); and scipy's Carlson forms between, validated against the two where they overlap.

The third kind holds 1e-13 against Carlson in the arrangement that keeps both of its terms positive, and round-off against two exact identities that need no reference at all — Π(n | 0) = (π/2)/√(1 − n) and Π(m | m) = E/(1 − m). Those two are what pin it across eighteen decades of pole, where every numerical reference is itself losing digits: the ordinary R_F + (n/3) R_J arrangement is a difference of near-equal terms once the pole is far past the range and is out by 3.5e-08 at p = 1e15, which is why the elliptic module previously carried a second, reflected form as a special case. The pole argument removes the case.

The trip count is a measured claim, asserted in both directions

A traced evaluation cannot iterate to a convergence test, so the descent runs a constant number of times. Each trip takes a geometric mean, so the correct digits double once the running pair is of one size and the exponent gap halves before that — which is why ONE constant covers three hundred decades. Measured over a dense sweep of the whole double range including denormals, and eighteen decades of pole: twelve trips reach the algorithm's own round-off floor and ten leave the smallest complements wrong in the sixth decimal. The constant is fourteen; the test fails if either bound moves.

Derivatives

nova differentiates through solves, so a value alone is not enough. Against the closed-form derivatives, which need only the values: the first kind and the third kind are exact to a ulp in every argument. The second kind is not — E approaches one as the complement vanishes and its whole dependence on the complement is a variation of relative size k'² log k' on that one, so the descent recovers the value to a few ulp but forms the derivative as a difference of larger terms and loses about a digit per decade: exact at k'² = 1e-3, 1e-10 at 1e-8, worthless by 1e-20. A caller that needs it there has it exactly from the two values, dE/dk'² = (K − E)/(2 k²). Bounded by test rather than left unstated.

The closed form inside a traced tile

Three things the host driver does with Python control flow cannot be done from a traced value, and the packed driver does each with arithmetic instead:

  • a dead edge — a dropped horizontal one, or a batch pad — is multiplied by its own zero weight rather than skipped, so the loop over edges runs to a static bound;
  • a corner's geometry comes from the edge that STARTS there where that edge is live and from the edge that ENDS there where it is not. Those are the same point wherever both are live, and the second is the only one available once a dropped edge's corners have been replaced by the packed placeholder — a where, not a dictionary;
  • the arsinh β₁ term is still accumulated ONCE per corner against the signed number of live edges meeting there, so it still cancels EXACTLY around an unbroken chain rather than to round-off — which is what the corner organisation bought and what a per-limit accumulation would give back.

What that costs is the shortcuts, and it is measurable: at one array width, one section, 4096 targets, the host driver runs 171.4 µs/pair and the packed driver 230.8 µs/pair — 1.35× for always forming every corner's pole families and first residual. The arithmetic is otherwise identical: the packed driver agrees with the host driver to 5.5e-13 and the traced kernel agrees with the packed driver on numpy to 2.5e-12, ONE compilation for a whole build, and through the zarr store the traced closed form agrees with the host driver to 2.3e-12.

The compile is the finding, and it is not a footnote to the per-pair rate but half the build. 101 s on one H200 and 136 s on an L4, against 1.3 s for the quadrature kernel; on XLA:CPU it never finished at all — an hour at a 32×32 tile, and fifty-five minutes on a SIXTEEN-pair one, XLA raising its own slow-compile alarm and carrying on. The traced closed form is a DEVICE-ONLY path, leaves polygon_analytic_greens as the host route — so the premise of one code path serving both devices, which holds for the quadrature kernel, does not hold for this one.

The graph was attributed by lowering it and counting instructions, which is cheap where compiling it is not. It is exactly linear in the corner count at 27,947 HLO operations per edge — 83,889 / 111,836 / 167,730 / 195,677 for three, four, six and seven edges, an intercept of forty-eight — against the quadrature kernel's 3,272 for the same block, and compile time tracks the count at about half a millisecond per operation. But the recursions are only 31% of it (harmonic_moments 3,580 operations per corner, the pole families 454 each, the descent 402, each pole seed 290), so rolling every one of them into a scan is worth about 1.45× rather than the order of magnitude it looks like. The other 69% is the harmonic-series coefficient algebra, which is not a recursion and which shrinks quadratically with the moment stack's length — a compile prize belonging to the rung that shortens it.

What that means for choosing a route. At 5.5 µs/pair on the H200 against the host closed form's 171.4 on one core and a projected 19.5 on a 16-core pool, the compile equals the device kernel at 18.4 M pairs and the device only beats the pool above 7.2 M:

Two things would remove the caveat rather than live with it, and neither is the scan: assemble builds a fresh evaluator per call, so a repeated build pays the compile again where a warm one would not, and JAX's persistent compilation cache is not wired up, so a new process pays it again too. With the compile amortised the device is a flat 3.5× the 16-core pool at every size.

The point-filament kernels, and two defects that were not the modulus

The flux's near-field error is entirely the modulus and the complement removes all of it. The field components' is NOT, and that only came out because it was measured: two things in the printed brackets cost more than the modulus does.

  • np.maximum(d², _R_FLOOR) was an absolute floor on a SQUARED LENGTH. It engages 32 micrometres from the filament at ANY ring radius — the radius does not appear — and inside it the field stops diverging and is simply wrong.
  • a² − R² − dz² is a difference of terms of order a² whose value is of order a d, so it arrives with relative error eps a/2d. Written −d² + 2a(a − R) — the same quantity exactly — nothing cancels, and the pole is then isolated in the one term that genuinely diverges.

The cap engaged at k'² = 4.9e-13 — 1.4 µm from a unit ring, 8.7 µm from an ITER-scale one — and did not bound the error: inside it K was frozen while the true K kept growing, so the flux error climbed from 2.6e-02 at engagement to 3.7e-01. At the engagement point the cap was a bad trade in its own right: unclipped, the same target was wrong by 3.5e-06 rather than 2.6e-02.

What a coincident target returns, deliberately

The divergence. psi is inf; the field is nan, because its sign depends on which way the filament is approached and the limit does not exist. Before, both were finite, silent and wrong — the kernel's value at a phantom standoff of 1.4 µm, which is the one failure a caller cannot detect. A filament model asked for a point ON the filament has been asked a question it cannot answer, and the finite-section kernels exist for exactly that target and are smooth through the conductor. One ULP off the filament these now return the true finite values, which is the other half of the same contract: adjacent is not coincident, and only the complement can tell the two apart.

Two things measured and deliberately NOT fixed

The far-field bracket. (1 − m/2)K − E has a series starting at m² formed out of terms of order one, so it loses log₁₀(16/m²) digits wherever K and E came from, and no route fixes it. The crossings are at 11 ring radii for 1e-12, 68 for 1e-9 and 420 for 1e-6 — a probe three ring radii out measures 5.5e-15 and a psi grid whose inner edge is at R = 0.1 m on a 6.2 m ring measures 4.5e-13. Nothing in the machine is far enough out to pay for replacing the bracket with its series.

The banded scheme downstream. moment_filament takes SECOND differences of these kernels in the source position on a step of 2e-3 section radii and divides by step², which multiplies any absolute kernel error by 2.5e5/cell² — so it had to be measured rather than argued. Every route agrees to 1.00× inside four section radii and 1.3× at thirty: the corrected filament is limited by the TRUNCATION of the moment expansion, not by the kernel's round-off. Re-running benchmarks/nearfar_error.py after the change reproduces every recorded cutoff distance bit for bit.

What is next

The closed form is now reachable from a device, so the adoption rung is unblocked — the decision it listed as a prerequisite is locked below the evergreen's decisions. Two cost rungs follow from what was measured here: the residual quadrature is 79 % of the closed form's floor at 128 nodes, and the traced kernel's 136 s compile is the unrolled moment recursions, which a fixed-trip scan would collapse.
