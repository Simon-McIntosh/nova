The decode

The nine (delta_r, delta_phi, delta_z) points are a circle of radius hypot(delta_r, delta_z) sampled at 45° steps, drawn in a plane that may be TILTED relative to the element's local section plane; delta_z is the out-of-plane component, not a free offset. The polar reading alone loses the tilt — PF6 reads a 2.345 mm spread and CS1L a 4.959 mm spread on delta_r — while the hypot invariant collapses every conductor in all three files to a constant with spread below 10⁻¹⁴ mm:

4 × 8.150 = 32.600: the CS entry's stored diameter (16.30 mm) equals the documented cable-space radius. No feature of the ITER CS conductor exists at Ø16.3 mm (the central spiral is ~Ø9 mm, the cable space Ø32.6, the jacket 49 mm square), and the factor is exact and uniform across all six modules, so the parsimonious mechanism is a diameter→radius conversion applied to a value that was already a radius during the CS extraction. The PF file (different date and contact) applied the same halving to a genuine diameter and lands exactly. Nothing here is scaled to fit: the identification is by exact factor against DET-07879-A's documented conductor, with the PF entry proving the decoder. Residual uncertainty: the source spreadsheet (CS.xls, provider Vincent Bontemps, contact Guillaume Davin) was not opened; the claim should be reported upstream for confirmation and correction. For modelling, the CS conductor section is the Ø32.6 mm cable space.

Feeder criterion

An element belongs to the winding pack iff BOTH its endpoints lie inside the module's gross r-z outline inflated by one jacket width (49 mm); elements between the first and last pack element in path order stay pack (in-pack joggles and crossovers — PF1 keeps 105 such). Everything else is feeder, split at r = 2.5 m (CS) into the axial run alongside the stack and the radial run to the terminal near r ≈ 18.3–18.7 m.

The packs sit on a different outline than the shipped model

The CS3U pack's radial histogram resolves 14 discrete layers at r = 1.3458 … 2.0330 m (layer pitch 52.9 mm), centred at r = 1.689, z = 5.436. That is the OLDER CS geometry revision nova carries (itergeom: 1.687, 5.464, 0.740 × 2.093) — not the current outline the shipped model and every converged comparison use (1.722, 5.313, 0.719 × 2.075). PF1's pack resolves 16 layers × 16 double-pancake rows whose centreline spans match the model outline's dimensions almost exactly (0.898 vs 0.959 − pitch, 0.921 vs 0.984 − pitch) but sit +9 mm outward and +48 mm higher than the model outline's centre. A machine-table value computed on the as-built outline would therefore differ from one computed on the shipped outline — exactly the "machine value computed on a different outline" candidate the field-coil-gap hunt (§4) lists, now with geometry behind it. The radial offset alone is first-order worth roughly ΔL ≈ L·ΔR/R ~ +1.7·10⁻³ H on PF1's 0.7035 H — the same scale as the +4.1·10⁻³ gap — so the wound-filament solve now in flight arbitrates with no outline argument at all.
