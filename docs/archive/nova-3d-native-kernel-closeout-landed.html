The headline: the element tier carried a worse defect than the kernel that prompted the look

The brief asked whether nova/biot/constants.py carried the same conditioning defect the axisymmetric rectangle kernel had just shed. It did, and it mattered more, because solve.py dispatches segment="circle" to Circle(Constants, Matrix) and "arc" to Arc(Constants, Matrix) — so every coil, plasma and passive row of a reconstruction operator is assembled through the code that was wrong.

Four distinct defects, all the same shape

  1. The poles and the modulus by subtraction. np2[1] = 2r/(r − c − eps) with c = √(γ² + r²): as γ → 0 the denominator is a catastrophic subtraction, and an absolute epsilon was then subtracted from it to stop it reaching zero — so the fudge was proportionally largest exactly where the true value is largest. Taken from the geometry, r − c = −γ²/(r + c) exactly, so the characteristic is −2r(r + c)/γ² and its pole ((r + c)/γ)². The far characteristic was 110 % wrong a tenth of a micrometre off a metre-scale corner plane; the modulus complement 1.9e-03.
  2. The third pole carries no γ at all. 1 − n₃ = ((rs − r)/b)², so it was 1.9e-03 wrong for any 1e-6 r corner offset at any γ — a wider engagement than the corner plane alone.
  3. The second kind's weight in the vertical row. 2r − b k² cancels as the target reaches the ring; exactly 2r(γ² − b(rs − r))/a². It capped Circle.Bz at 7.1e-09 a hundredth of a micrometre off a 6.2 m filament while ψ and Br sat at 1e-16. Fixed in Constants; the circle.py and arc.py adoption is deferred.
  4. The radial row's weight. v = 1 + k²(γ² − br)/(2r·rs) is one plus a term reaching minus one, and is identically (3γ² + (rs − r)(rs + r))/a². Printed form 6.5e-05 wrong at a corner a micrometre from the plane; collapsed form 1.9e-16.

Two results that came out of measuring rather than assuming

The complement-native descent is the wrong route for the first and second kinds. The instruction was to route every integral through it. Measured over two million elements on a compute node, it costs the first and second kinds 1245 ns/element against Cephes' 290, because ellipkm1 already takes k′² directly so the sensitive kind never sees the parameter, and the second kind is bounded and takes the parameter without loss. For the third kind it wins — 544 ns against Carlson's 664 — where the printed R_F + (n/3)R_J sets two nearly-equal terms of opposite sign against each other. Accuracy is the same either way (the ring's flux holds at 5.2e-16). Only the third kind was rerouted.

The two third-kind implementations do not compete; they cover different domains. An unconditional reroute broke n = 1.3, where the pole 1 − n is negative, the root falls inside the integration range, and the integral is a principal value (−1.739) that the complement-native descent has no branch for and silently returns 0 for. The routine is now selected on the pole's domain: a caller supplying pole= and complement= gets the descent, a caller holding only the characteristic and the parameter keeps Carlson's forms, which carry the wider domain. So the "duplicate" is retained deliberately — deleting it would reintroduce a zero where a principal value belongs.

The two test assertions — one was measuring the wrong thing, one had lost coverage

The pin. The scaled bound at 1e-13 was measuring which targets were chosen, not the algebra: the corner value is a cancellation of terms reaching 206× the four-corner stack's scale on the shipped targets and four decades of it on targets they do not visit, with the third-kind sum cancelling by a further 800× inside that — so one rounding of one term's last bit arrives as a couple of thousand ulp of the corner value. The reference now returns the magnitude of the widest term each value is assembled from, and the pin is taken over that: 2.7 ulp on the shipped set and ≤3.9 across several hundred random target and section geometries, over which the cancellation factor spans three decades and the bound does not move. So the ulp pin is restored and is geometry-independent, which the original 4-ulp pin never was. Injected single-term slips — a dropped factor of three, a flipped (−1)^p, transposed characteristics, rs + c read as rs − c — sit thirteen decades clear of either bound, and the term pin catches a 1e-14 relative coefficient slip that the scaled one does not. The scaled bound is kept at 1e-13 as the cruder statement of the same thing, because tightening it toward the term bound would only make it a statement about the target set.

The spread. Not a tolerance on an error — an oracle-free convergence statement: the symmetric difference quotient across a corner plane must settle on the derivative rather than grow as J/2d. But two of the four crossing configurations had no oracle at all (a radius outside the section's radial span crossing a face level, and a level below the section crossing a corner radius), and they are now restored at 1e-10 with 1.5e-12 measured on the 4 m section and 2.2e-11 on the 2 cm one. The two statements are complementary, not substitutes, and that is now demonstrated in both directions: an injected jump of 1e-10 of the field fires the ladder and not the oracle; a wrong value on the plane alone fires the oracle and not the ladder.

And the ladder was measuring the section, not the kernel. Standoffs fixed in metres make the quotient's floor a property of the geometry: the noise is the kernel's relative round-off divided by the relative field difference a rung forms, which is of order d/L for a section of extent L. A 6.2 m ring with a 2 cm section — a production winding-pack shape — therefore spread 2.9e-02 where the 4 m section spread 4.3e-03, so the bound passed on one aspect ratio and failed on the other. Diagnosed as the quotient's floor and not a kernel defect on three counts that fail differently: the ladder is flat to a part in 1e4 through the window with the whole excursion in the single finest rung; consecutive-rung ratios are 1.00 where an uncancelled jump gives a clean 10.0 at every rung; and the analytic polygon oracle agrees to 1.6e-11 at the same centre, where a surviving branch term would be order the field. Standoffs are now fractions of the section's extent, which holds d/L fixed and the floor with it — 2.4e-04 and 5.1e-04, a factor of two apart rather than seven — and the bound tightened from 1e-2 to 2e-3. Section shape is a test input for both corner-plane tests now.

The window is a fixed fraction rather than chosen from the measured plateau, deliberately: selecting the window from the data can pick a span on which growth looks flat, which is the one thing the test exists to see.

The failing test was the environment, with a concrete cause

test_grid_solve_finite_section_winding passes in every configuration tried: alone and paired, bare and with JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu, on a login node and on a compute node, in a whole-file run and in the full lane. Ordering was ruled out mechanically rather than empirically — pytest_randomly and xdist are both absent, so collection order is deterministic and a full-file plus full-lane run exhaust the orderings that exist.

The cause is the login node's GPU: an L4 with 73 MiB free of 23034, 97 % held by another process. The test asserts segment[0] == "bow"; bow.py imports zeta, which imports jax — so the path initialises a CUDA backend, and JAX walks its preallocation down from 16.47 GiB emitting CUDA_ERROR_OUT_OF_MEMORY. Whether that degrades to CPU or fails outright depends on what the other process holds at that instant, which is exactly why an intermittent report never reproduces. Compute nodes have no GPU and pass deterministically.

Two failing tests found along the way — both wrong tests, correct code

The hollow circular section. test_box_section[skin] drove both profiles against two solid rectangles, which is right for a square annulus and wrong for a circular one — the square encloses the circle and carries 4/π more area. It passed only while the element evaluated a curved section as its bounding box, and it reported the two profiles agreeing to every digit, which two geometries that do not share a field cannot do. That bit-identity is the proof of a defect at the older revision, not evidence of health. The circular annulus now gets two statements it can make: its area as the two inscribed polygons' difference in closed form with the facet count named rather than folded into a constant, and — away from the section — the two profiles' flux excess over a filament of the same current as their mean square radius, whose ratio is exactly 4/3 for any pair of widths, so a bounding-box evaluation collapses it to 1. Measured within 0.4 % of 4/3 against the 25 % that separates 4/3 from 1. A disc-sectioned coil was ruled out as the referee: its far-field moment is 2.91× where the geometry says 0.96×.

The lean-core probe. It imported the whole subtree with the plotting stack blocked at the finder, so any module reaching for an absent extra aborted the run — and ten of twelve extras are absent in a lean environment, which is the environment the guard exists for. An absent extra is now stood in for by an empty package that answers any attribute, so every module executes its body and is inspected. A blocked root is never stood in for, and every blocked root is also an extra, so the order of those two tests carries the whole correctness — verified against controls, including regex, which is both absent and an extra.

What is deferred, and why

The arc.py / circle.py adoption is measured and scoped but not landed. It is a pure gain rather than a prerequisite: the third kind's routine is selected on the pole's domain, so Arc keeps its present behaviour until it supplies the pole, and the landed commits are strictly non-regressive. Four items, all measured:

  • Arc.ellipj is a numerical round trip with an exact answer — ellipj(ellipkinc(θ, m), m) returns sin θ and cos θ to round-off (verified 0.0 to 1.1e-16 over a sweep) because that is the identity. Its dn, though, is 2.6e-10 wrong at a target on the arc's end plane where √(cn² + k′² sin²θ) holds at 9.0e-18, and dn divides _Bphi_hat and enters Winc and _Ar_hat.
  • Arc.Ip's two subtractions are exact differences of complements: k² − np2[p] is np2_pole[p] − ck2, and 1 − np2[p] sn² is cn² + np2_pole[p] sn². No new machinery needed.
  • Arc.Pi_inc needs pole= and complement=; residual today is up to 1.0 at γ/r = 1e-10 on the end plane and 1.8e-04 on the third pole.
  • Arc.Kinc/Einc: an exact parameter alone buys only 5.9e-05 → 1.1e-05, so closing it needs incomplete_kind(θ, ck2, parameter=k2) rather than the cheap half.

Also open: the coincident / all-to-all diagonal contract. The removed eps fudges were functionally a disguised self-coupling model — they kept a filament kernel finite on its own diagonal by nudging the geometry, so the operator's diagonal was set by floating-point epsilon rather than by the conductor's cross-section. The corner-level case now has a stated contract (γ held at one where the target is level with a source corner, the bounded product put at zero there, which is the mean of its two one-sided limits), but the coincident case — a target on the filament or inside its own section, which is every diagonal entry of an all-to-all matrix — is not yet decided. The likely answer is that the diagonal must come from a finite-section kernel rather than a filament at all: the point kernel is +7.2 % median and +18 % max on cell self-flux terms where the closed form is 6.8e-12 on the contour.
