The decision, and what decided it

arc-element-shape asked whether the class extends Bow, inheriting its arc geometry and overriding the section evaluation, or extends Arc directly and treats the polygon section as a peer of the rectangular one. It is locked extend-arc, and the argument is a reading of what each base actually contains rather than a preference.

The five rows the frame consumes — _Ar_hat through _Bz_hat, rotated into the source's local cartesian frame by _Ax_hat/_Ay_hat/_Bx_hat/_By_hat and thence to global by Matrix._vector — are Arc's. Bow inherits them unchanged. What Bow adds is a __post_init__ that stacks the four corners of a rectangle onto every geometry array, a _stack helper that tiles the rest to match, a zeta quadrature over that stack, and the five row expressions that consume it. A polygon section needs none of those, so extending Bow would inherit a base whose entire body has to be disabled — and disabling __post_init__ conditionally would make which base ran depend on a flag, where the frame plumbing has to be the same either way. Extending Arc inherits the plumbing and nothing else, which is exactly what the two elements share. The test asserts the relationship rather than describing it: PolyBow is an Arc and is not a Bow.

What the cost table says about routing, which is what the decision asked for

The record carried 2.65× — the closed form against Bow on the rectangle they share — and that comparison was between a BARE kernel call and a FRAMED one. Measured on the same terms it is 3.02×: the frame costs the reduction another 16 %, and it costs Bow the same 16 % it was already paying. That does not change the conclusion so much as sharpen the question it was asked about.

The routing the cost table appeared to argue for — an in-class flag between the closed form and the quadrature, the way PolySection.closed_form chooses between two exact ring kernels — is not the routing that is live here, and it is not a cost question at all. Three measured reasons:

  • The two evaluations are already routed between, at the segment level. A rectangular-section winding reaches Bow and a polygon one would reach PolyBow; the choice is the frame's segment column, not a class flag. A second route inside the class would duplicate a decision the registry already owns.
  • Bow's alternative only exists for a rectangle. Its zeta quadrature integrates a rectangle's four corners and nothing else, so on the sections this element is for there is no cheaper evaluation to route to — only a wrong one.
  • And on those sections it IS the wrong one. A hexagonal winding routed to Bow today comes back at four thirds of its value, on every row and every target (1.3330 … 1.3335 measured): Bow integrates over the rectangle the frame's width and height bound while dividing by the frame's area, which is the hexagon's — and a regular hexagon fills three quarters of that box. So the cheaper element is 186.5 µs/pair and 33 % out; the dearer one is 926.6 and right. There is no trade to route.

The routing that IS worth having is the other one PolySection carries — standoff, exact near and a point filament far, where the gap is four orders rather than three-fold. It is deliberately not built here for the same reason PolySection.standoff defaults to None: a principled cutoff has to come from the section's own second-moment error bound and none of the geometry-derived candidates measured so far qualifies. Exact everywhere is the shipped lane for the ring and it is the shipped lane here.

The section, and the column it does not come from

The class builds its corners from the frame's own section descriptor — the name, the width and the height, about the arc's own (r, z) — through nova.geometry.polygen.PolyGen, in the same convention Bow builds its rectangle from.

Not from the frame's poly column, which is what the axisymmetric PolySection reads. For a ring, poly IS the cross-section. For a SWEPT element it is the projection of the 3-D volume onto the poloidal plane, and it disagrees with the frame's own dimensions in two ways at once: its axes come back transposed — a 0.06 × 0.04 winding projects to a 0.04 × 0.06 rectangle — and it is stored at single precision. This rung followed Bow's convention on the grounds that it is what the landed gate measures against.

Resolved 2026-07-27, and the transposition was a real defect in a third place — the sweep. Chasing it against the swept SOLID rather than against either description of it showed the vtk volume itself was turned: for a {"rect": (0, 0, 0.06, 0.04)} winding the solid the frame plots, meshes and volume-integrates spanned 0.04 in radius and 0.06 in z. A cross-section is authored in its own x-z plane — width along x, height along z, y its normal — so the axis a vector-aligned sweep pins to world vertical is the section's THIRD; Section.sweep pinned its FIRST, which lands the width along z and the height along the path's own normal. Every swept conductor came out with its two section dimensions exchanged, invisible only for a square one. Fixed on develop (23f055bc): the frame's width and height are authoritative, Bow and PolyBow were reading them correctly all along, and poly now agrees with both.

Volume could not see it — a section and its transpose sweep the same volume — which is why the existing sweep test passed throughout and why nothing in the suite moved when the rotation was corrected. A test that pins the extents instead now guards it, and requires the two alignments to agree for a path at constant height; it fails on the old code for the vector alignment and passes for axes, which was already right. What the defect was worth, had it reached the kernels: 5.9e-04 overall and 1.2e-03 on Br for the 3:2 rectangle above, growing with aspect ratio. Two reasons to read poly survive the fix and are carried by f-pbow-06 — it is the section of record, and it can express a polygon no (width, height) descriptor can — but it needs double precision first: its corners round-trip through a VTK mesh, whose points are float32, and that alone costs 7.9e-09 relative, four orders above the closed form's measured 3.5e-12.

A generated section arrives as a shapely ring, so its first corner repeats at the end — and a generator that sweeps an angle closes on a corner that is its own first to within round-off rather than exactly, which leaves the ring one corner longer still. Both are edges of zero length. They are dropped against the SECTION's own extent rather than an absolute tolerance, so a thin plate keeps corners a coordinate-absolute test would merge at a major radius of six metres.

What is refused

Five section names are admitted — disc, ellipse, hexagon, rectangle, square — and they are exactly the generators whose third and fourth arguments are the section's own two dimensions. skin and box are refused for two reasons that are each sufficient: their fourth argument is a HOLLOWNESS factor, so building one from (width, height) would silently return a nearly solid square rather than the ring the frame means, and the section they describe has an interior boundary that a single corner list cannot carry. polygon, shell and outline are refused because a swept element keeps no corner list of its own to read — the winding path consumes its cross-section into a vtk sweep and does not store it. A disc is admitted and costs what its corner count implies: the generator resolves a quadrant in sixteen segments, so sixty-four corners against a hexagon's six.

The frame's stored matrices are float64 and are what the tests read, and one assertion exists solely to record that the two elements differ at all.

Only the rectangle column supports a framed-against-bare reading: the frame's rectangle is the bare variant's rectangle. The two hexagons are the same size and the same corner count but not the same ORIENTATION — the generator puts a corner on the r axis where the benchmark's own hexagon puts one on z, which leaves four live edges rather than six — so 926.6 against 928.7 is a coincidence of two differences and not a measurement of either.

The useful figure is the 16 %. The element class is the reduction plus a source assembly, two rotations and the matrix storage, and none of that scales with the section — so the same 78 µs is 16 % of a rectangle and 8 % of a hexagon, and it falls further on anything with more corners. Whatever the arc costs, the class is not where it goes.

What the next rung inherits

The element exists and nothing routes to it. Every swept winding is sent to Bow by one line in nova/geometry/polyline.py — thicken = {"arc": "bow", "line": "beam"} — whatever its cross-section is, so reaching this class means setting the frame's segment column by hand, which is the route the tests and the benchmark take and the same one benchmarks/polygon_route_cost.py takes to reach PolySection.

Making that automatic is the next rung and it is a DECISION rather than a patch, because the shapes divide three ways and only one of them is straightforward:

  • hexagon — six corners, 4.97× the cost and 33 % of the error removed. The case that makes the change worth making, and the section a plasma cell already uses.
  • disc and ellipse — correct but sixty-four corners, so roughly ten times a hexagon's cost, against a Bow approximation measured at 4/π — 1.27305 … 1.27324 on a disc and 1.2749 … 1.2755 on an ellipse, the second a hair above because a sixty-four-gon slightly under-fills its own curve. Right and dear against wrong and cheap, which is a caller's trade and not this element's to make silently.
  • skin and box — cannot route at all: hollow, and Bow's rectangle is further from a thin ring than it is from a hexagon. They stay where they are until a section with an interior boundary has a representation.

Two smaller things are named rather than left implied. The poly column's transposition against width and height for a swept element is a genuine disagreement between two descriptions of one conductor, and something reads it. And the packed, unbranched driver the tiled path needs is still not written — nothing in the reduction inspects a value, so packed_analytic_greens's counterpart is a driver rather than a reformulation, and the tiled backend's memoisation key and the persistent compilation cache both already handle a second kernel.
