The reduction, and where the interior limit changes it

Per edge, per limit u = z' − z and per row l the reduction forms X_l(α) = ∫₀^α da w_l g_l, and arcamplitude.fold turns the arc's two amplitudes into its value. The five weights are −sin φ and cos φ on eq 9's antiderivative, cos φ and sin φ on eq 11b's bracket, and one on eq 11b's z row. Three of the five have an ODD fold and supply a quarter-turn value; those three are exactly the three the full turn forms, so the quarter comes from nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py's own evaluation and a full sweep reproduces the ring by the fold's arithmetic rather than by a second derivation.

Which family each term lands in was the structural question, and it has a one-line answer. Every transcendental's derivative carries exactly one explicit factor of sin 2a and the by-parts weight supplies the other, so every term of a cos φ row contracts against the plain harmonic moments and every term of a sin φ row against the sin 2a-weighted ones. No term mixes them. That is what let the contraction machinery become one object over a family (momentchannel.Channel) rather than two copies: a denominator is factorised ONCE, because the shifts belong to the denominator, and seeded twice, because the seeds belong to the family.

The by-parts constants are not free, and the two residuals want opposite ones

For a cos φ row the weight is an even series and splits into its mean and an oscillatory part whose antiderivative Σ w_n sin 2na/(2n) vanishes at a = 0 on its own and at a quarter turn, so only the interior upper end is new. For a sin φ row the weight is a pure sine series and the constant is a choice — and the two residual terms want different ones.

  • arsinh β1 takes the antiderivative vanishing at the LOWER limit, sin²2a/2 for a sin 4a weight. It diverges at a = 0 for a target level with the corner and only that zero makes the product finite; −cos 4a/4 puts a divergence into a term that has none. It happens to vanish at a quarter turn as well, so this row needs nothing at the other end. This is the previous rung's finding and it held.
  • arsinh β2 takes the one vanishing at the UPPER limit, W − W(α), and that is this rung's own. Its antiderivative in the range variable does not vanish at the far end, and the transcendental it would multiply there runs to a logarithm wherever the target sits on the edge's extended line — which at a quarter-turn amplitude is what a full sweep IS, so the configuration is the reduction's own closure test rather than a corner. Subtracting the value at the amplitude puts an exact zero on the weight the near pole's divergence carries, and the pole's own seed then multiplies a weight that vanishes as fast as it grows. The figure's right panel measures what the other choice would cost: the term reaches twenty times the row it belongs to by an offset of 1e-14 section radii and grows without bound, so the cancellation never quite fails and never stops growing either.

A pure sine weight has no mean, so the sin φ rows need no arsinh quadrature at all: both residual integrals stay on the cos φ rows, and the two extra rows cost nothing in quadrature.

The arctangent, and the dead band that only half survives

The cos φ rows keep the full turn's antiderivative −½ P(t), so the a = 0 dead band survives as it is — ±π/2 with the sign of u (r₁ + r) — and the upper boundary becomes the arctangent itself, taken as arctan2 of its numerator against r sin 2a D. That denominator is non-negative over the range, so the two-argument form is the principal value and runs onto either dead band of its own accord; where it vanishes the amplitude has reached a range end and the band is taken directly, the full turn's −arctan b₁ vertex limit included. The sin φ row's arctangent weight is sin²2a cos 2a, whose antiderivative −(r²/6) sin³2a vanishes at both ends — so that row forms no dead band at all.

What the residual quadratures needed

Both boundary layers still sit at a = 0 and a = π/2, because that is where the denominators vanish, but an interior amplitude can stop short of the upper one. So each panel carries its own bounds in the offset from the end it is graded from, the sinh map stays anchored at the layer whether or not the panel reaches it, and the model is integrated between the panel's own limits. The full turn is both panels running zero to a quarter turn, and its gate did not move.

This was also the rung's one real defect and it is worth recording how it presented. The second residual was left reading a module constant for its limits while the first read the corner's, so it integrated over the whole quarter range at every amplitude. What that produced was diagnostic: the two sin φ rows were correct to 1e-14 and the three cos φ rows wrong by factors of two to four — and only the cos φ rows carry a residual quadrature at all, because only an even weight has a mean. The failure pattern named the term before anything was inspected.

Why two measures rather than one

The gate records an absolute agreement normalised by the largest row AND each row's deviation against its own size, and they disagree by three decades on exactly the rows that have become small. Both are needed. The absolute one is the tight statement — a few parts in 1e12, which is the assembly's own differencing floor: each per-limit antiderivative is of order the squared major radius where the answer is of order the section's scale, so an edge's two limits are differenced against each other first and what survives is round-off on the LARGER quantity (7e-19 absolute on a rectangle, against rows reaching 1e-6). The per-row one is the only measure that says anything about a small component, and two of the five are: the azimuthal field row is four orders below the rows beside it at a plasma-cell aspect and holds 3e-11 of itself, and the radial field row falls just as far over a sweep symmetric about the target azimuths, where the absolute floor shows through it. That second case is what sets the looser table entries — it records how small a row can get before the floor is visible, not an accuracy the reduction lacks.

The ratio is remarkably flat across section shapes, which says the extra cost is per corner rather than per edge. It is also far BELOW what the moment stacks alone would predict: a corner is evaluated at both amplitudes and at a quarter turn, each partial-range family is a tridiagonal solve carried a hundred and thirty orders past the last moment wanted against the ring's downward recursion over forty, and there are two families rather than one — which multiplies out to something like seventeen, not four and two thirds. So the solve is not where the time goes; the residual quadratures and the range-function algebra are, and both are shared with the ring. That is the useful part of the measurement: the arc is not dear because the partial-range primitives are dear.

What the next rung inherits

The evaluation is a function. What is left is the element class, and its shape is the plan's one open decision (arc-element-shape): extend Bow and override the section evaluation, or extend Arc and treat the polygon section as a peer of the rectangular one. Two things the measurements above bear on it. First, Bow already carries the five rows and the frame plumbing for them, and the agreement at zero edge slope is on all five — so extending it inherits a tested consumer. Second, the closed form costs 2.65× Bow on the same section, so a class that can route between them is worth more than one that cannot: the same choice the full turn's PolySection.closed_form flag already exposes.

One side finding, not this rung's to fix. Bow's agreement is bounded from ABOVE only, because the point solver's output precision is not fixed: the same four targets come back float32 in one process and float64 in another, and the arc-to-Bow deviation moves from 2.2e-07 to 2.0e-10 with it. Both are inside what the comparison is for, but a lower bound on that agreement would be a claim about nova's storage rather than about either reduction, so there is none — and something in the frame stack is deciding a dtype from process state.

Two smaller things are named rather than left implied. The packed, unbranched driver the tiled path needs — packed_analytic_greens's counterpart — is not written; nothing in the reduction inspects a value, so it is a driver rather than a reformulation. And one corner is open and bounded rather than covered: the odd rows' arsinh β2 boundary runs to a logarithm at r₁ = −r, which needs the edge's extended line to cross the axis at the target's own height, and both the boundary and the far-pole seed return zero there rather than an infinity.
