PolyBow operator dispatch and finite-arc banding — landed

2026-07-29. The production route is geometry-aware: use packed JAX for large polygon-ring builds, use an end-aware host band for finite polygon arcs, and keep cheap geometry kernels on the host until a fixed-shape packed driver demonstrates an end-to-end crossover.

The apparent geometry-assembly bottleneck

The earlier “geometry assembly is about ten times slower than the solve” finding conflated the bare numerical kernel with the complete FrameSpace operator path. A current matched straight-prism run measures framed/bare ratios of 33.7× at 512 pairs and 25.9× at 4096 pairs. A warm 512-pair profile attributes 23 ms to Source, Target, and FrameSpace metadata, 12 ms to xarray construction, 5 ms to operator wrapping, 4 ms to Matrix construction, and about 1 ms to the bare polygon kernel. The section and path geometry are constructed outside that timing.

This is an orchestration tax exposed by a cheap kernel, not evidence that geometric meshing is intrinsically expensive. The same matched measurement on the computationally expensive finite-arc kernel is only 1.10× at 512 pairs and 1.02× at 4096 pairs. Caching inside the interactive frame model would optimize the wrong boundary; dense repeated construction should bypass per-object xarray assembly through packed operator builders.

Finite-arc band and filament placement

The band coordinate is derived from the finite sweep. Inside the sweep it is distance to the section contour. Outside either end it combines the poloidal contour gap with the angular chord to the nearest end. The assignment therefore depends only on source and target geometry and handles a target that is poloidally close but azimuthally beyond the conductor.

Area centroid + section moments4.5425e-8retain

RMS radius only1.804e-4reject

The study covers hexagonal, trapezium, and thin-plate sections on radial, vertical, and off-end routes. RMS plus moments is statistically indistinguishable from centroid plus moments while requiring a displaced filament and first-moment bookkeeping, so the area centroid remains the simpler correct equivalent.

At 4096 targets the derived seams route 2225 pairs exact and 1871 to the moment filament. Wall clock falls from 2.5936 s to 1.3775 s, a 1.8828× speedup, with 5.805e-8 worst relative error. Exact thin-plate references eventually become cancellation-limited at extreme standoff, so acceptance evidence is bounded to 80 section radii beyond every seam.

GPU compilation and sharding

The packed finite-arc driver agrees with the NumPy reduction to about 2e-12 relative error and joins the geometry registry, but the graph is not a viable production executable. A one-pair H200 graph takes 695.94 s to compile and 18.82 ms/pair warm. Removing the static closed-chain residual takes 720.74 s and 18.10 ms/pair, so that specialization does not address the graph-size problem. The host measures 404 microseconds/pair at a 4096-target width.

Multi-device execution shards pair blocks and replicates the small geometry arrays. Four cards improve steady execution by only 1.53× at the measured width, while increasing cold cost. A two-term fit projects 1.686 µs/pair on eight cards, 1.68× over the one-card baseline. That point is explicitly a projection: three H200s were occupied by existing DeepSeek and embedding services, which were left untouched.
