The step that decides everything, and the value it collapses on

The incomplete integrals descend by the arithmetic-geometric mean, and each trip has to carry the amplitude along with the modulus. Written the way the identity usually is — phi <- phi + arctan((b/a) tan phi) — it collapses at exactly the configuration this module has to reproduce. A quarter-turn amplitude has a tangent of 1.6e16 rather than infinity, so a small ratio returns a step of nothing where the true step is a quarter turn: the amplitude never doubles, and the first kind saturates on log(2/cos(pi/2)) — 38.025, a number that depends on nothing but the floating-point representation of a right angle. Against the true 346.8 at a complement of 1e-300 that is an 89 % error, and it is not a corner case: a quarter-turn amplitude is what the FULL TURN is, so the arc would have failed the one test that matters most.

Taking the step from the amplitude's sine and cosine separately removes the saturation and takes the continuous branch with it, and the whole turns the arctangent cannot see are restored arithmetically rather than by a case. With that, a quarter-turn amplitude reproduces the shipped complete routine to 2.3e-16 over the whole double range of complement, 1e-300 included — the arc closing onto the ring, to the last bit. The arrangement being repaired is run inside the test rather than described, so the 89 % is measured on every run.

The same argument runs one level up. The amplitude's own pair comes from the caller, because the arc has it exactly: with psi the target's azimuthal separation from an arc end, sin alpha = cos(psi/2) and cos alpha = -sin(psi/2), so a target ON the end gives a cosine of exactly zero where cos(pi/2) gives 6e-17.

Three cases, one formula, and no branch

Urankar folds an amplitude back into the quarter the antiderivative covers in three printed cases (eqs 25 and 26, p. 1178), each with its own theta and its own multiple of the quarter-turn value. They are one formula in the half-turn count, and it follows from what the integrands are rather than from the cases: every one is a function of twice the angle, so all are half-turn periodic, and each is even or odd about zero. The rows with an odd integrand integrate to an even function whose half turn vanishes; the rows with an even one integrate to an odd function whose half turn is twice the quarter-turn value. Writing alpha = n pi + delta,

    X_even(alpha) = X(|delta|)
    X_odd(alpha)  = 2 n X(pi/2) + sign(delta) X(|delta|)

and cases A, B and C are n = 0, 1, 2. That matters beyond tidiness: the case selection is a branch on a PER-TARGET value, which a traced tile cannot take, and the one-formula version has none — so the arc's amplitude bookkeeping is batchable from the start rather than after a rewrite. It is checked against the printed cases transcribed literally, against direct integration of two model integrands over four turns of amplitude, and against the full turn, where the two ends' folds cancel to -2 X(pi/2) — the ring the shipped reduction evaluates, recovered from the arc's own bookkeeping rather than assumed.

Of three moment families, only one knows where the range ends

This is the finding that decides how much of the shipped full-turn reduction an arc can reuse, and it came out better than the plan assumed. The root moments fold Delta^2 back under the integral and the pole moments fold their own pole factor back; both are identities of the INTEGRAND and not of the limit, so the shipped routines are correct at an interior amplitude exactly as they stand. That is now pinned against partial-range quadrature rather than argued.

The plain harmonic moments are the one family that moves, and the change is structural rather than a boundary term to bolt on. Their recursion comes from [sin 2na Delta] vanishing at both ends of the quarter range; at an interior limit it gains a source, and with it the moments stop being the minimal solution the complete family exploits — a cut range puts a jump into the periodic extension, they decay only as 1/n, and no branch of the homogeneous relation describes them. So the family is not recursed downward at all here. It is solved, as a tridiagonal system.

And the solve alone is not enough, for a reason worth stating because it is the common configuration rather than an exotic one. The system's contraction ratio is [(1 + k'^2) - sqrt((1 + k'^2)^2 - k^4)]/k^2 — 0.17 for a distant target, and reaching ONE as the modulus does, which is a target on the source ring. There the relation has (-1)^n exactly in its kernel and nothing damps the closure. The upward recursion covers precisely that end and by the same number, what grows going up being the reciprocal of that ratio. Both constants are set by a sweep of amplitude against parameter and asserted from both sides: worst 8.7e-15 over ten orders with the pair, against 1.3e-7 with either direction alone, and 1.2e-13 at forty orders less headroom.

One more piece of exactness earns its keep here. The source term 4 sin(2 n alpha) Delta(alpha) is generated by the Chebyshev recursion from 2 sin alpha cos alpha, so at a quarter turn its seed is exactly zero, every harmonic after it is exactly zero, and the system the arc solves IS the homogeneous one the complete family satisfies. Taken as sin(n pi) of a floating-point right angle the seed is 1e-16 instead, and the arc would differ from the ring at every order for no physical reason.

What is not here, and why it is a rung rather than an omission

The pole families need a seed, and at an interior amplitude that seed is an incomplete integral of the THIRD kind. It is not implemented, and the analysis that says why is the useful output of this rung — the plan's open decision offered two routes and the evidence says neither is right as posed.

  • Carlson's symmetric forms, purpose-written reproduce the cancellation the complete case rejected them for. The reduction needs the seed in two orientations: a root just past the far end of the range, where Carlson's R_F + (n/3) R_J is a sum of positives and is fine, and a root just past the NEAR end, where the characteristic is hugely negative, the two terms are of opposite sign and nearly equal, and the arrangement loses about sqrt(p) — five digits at a shift of 1e-10, which is the shift a slender section actually produces. The seed multiplies a numerator end value that is small by exactly the same amount, so it needs full RELATIVE accuracy and the loss reaches the answer undiminished.
  • Extending the complete routine with an amplitude is what the first and second kinds did here, and for them it worked. It does not carry over to the third: Bartky's rearrangement, which is what makes cel a single fixed-trip descent over eighteen decades of pole, has no amplitude in it, and the incomplete counterpart is a different algorithm rather than the same one with an argument added.

A third route came out of the analysis and is the one to try first. The seed's ill-conditioning is entirely in an ELEMENTARY factor: with the pole written as cos^2 a + p sin^2 a, the whole growth is arctan(sqrt(p) tan alpha)/sqrt(p), which is exact, and the remainder carries a factor of k^2/(p - 1) that makes it small exactly where the direct evaluation is worst. Splitting there means the remainder needs only eps sqrt(p) relative accuracy — which the Carlson arrangement already delivers. Complementing it, a root past the FAR end is the orientation where Carlson is a sum of positives, so the two orientations are covered by different means and each by the one that suits it. Sizing and pinning that is the next rung.

What is next

The seed, then the reduction. With an incomplete third kind the pole families close and the whole moment stack the arc contracts against is available; the reduction itself — the by-parts boundary terms the interior limit reinstates, the arctangent's endpoint value where the full turn had a dead-band, and the azimuthal field row that has no rectangular oracle — follows on top of it. The amplitude fold and the partial-range moments are both traceable and batched over per-target amplitudes already, so nothing landed here has to be revisited to make the arc tileable.
