What the rung was scoped on, and why that was wrong

The followup that opened this rung began “No primitive is missing”, and listed the closed stack: the interior-amplitude first, second and third kinds, the partial-range harmonic moments, the root and pole families verified unchanged at an interior limit, both pole seeds, and the amplitude fold. That inventory is correct for the three rows the full turn already forms. It is not correct for the five rows an arc has.

Two of an arc's rows carry sin φ where the other three carry cos φ: the vector potential's radial component and the field's toroidal one. Over a full turn that odd weight integrates to nothing — it is exactly the parity argument that leaves an axisymmetric ring with no toroidal field, and the reason polygonanalytic.py never forms the component at all. So the moments those two rows contract against have no counterpart anywhere in elliptic.py to be the partial-range version OF. They are a fourth family, and they had to be derived and built before either new row could be written.

The weight is the radical's own differential, and that is what makes it cheap

d(Δ²) = −k² sin 2a da, so a weight of sin 2a turns every integral in the family into an elementary one in Δ. Three consequences, and together they are why the two extra rows cost almost nothing:

  • Both pole seeds are elementary. Where the cosine family needs an incomplete integral of the third kind — the rung before this one, and the hardest primitive in the plan — this family needs one inverse hyperbolic tangent: ∓2 ∫₁^Δ dD/(D² − q²) with q² = k'² − shift·k² for the root past the far end and 1 + shift·k² for the root past the near one.
  • The radical fold and the pole recursion carry over verbatim. Both are identities of the INTEGRAND — Δ² folded back under the integral, and the pole factor multiplied back — so neither knows what weight the integrand carries. nova.biot.elliptic.harmonic_root_moments and harmonic_pole_moments are correct on the new family exactly as they stand, which the tests check rather than assume.
  • The family itself is the same tridiagonal system. Taking d/da[cos 2na Δ] where the plain family takes d/da[sin 2na Δ] gives the same three-term relation with 4[1 − cos 2nα Δ(α)] in place of 4 sin 2nα Δ(α), and T₀ = 0. The solve is now shared: the two callers differ in their right-hand side and their two seeds and in nothing else.

The first moment is where the relation at order zero lands, and it is worth writing down because the printed form of it is unusable: 2(1 − Δ)/k² is a difference of two nearly-equal numbers over a small one, and a distant target — where k² is what is small — loses every digit. 2 sin²α/(1 + Δ) is the same number with neither.

The gap has to be supplied, not subtracted

The seeds' whole accuracy sits in one quantity. Written as artanh(z)/z, the argument z reaches ONE as the root reaches the range end — which is the configuration a slender section produces at every corner — and artanh there is a logarithm of 1 − z. Forming that gap by subtracting a ratio from one caps the relative accuracy at ε over the gap: five digits gone at 1e-11, everything gone by 1e-14. Both callers have it in closed form as a ratio of positives —

  far root:   1 − z = (1 + q)(1 + Δ)(cos²α + s) / ((Δ + q)·pivot)
  near root:  1 − z = s (1 + Δ − k² sin²α/(1 + p)) / pivot

— and log1p of 2z/(1 − z) then holds at both ends: full relative accuracy as z vanishes with the pole, and no cancellation as it approaches one. The left panel of the figure is that claim measured.

One further property came out of the same algebra rather than being designed in. The far-root seed's q² = k'² − shift·k² CHANGES SIGN as the shift passes k'²/k² — the pole crossing the radical's own root — and the two branches either side of it are the same series, artanh(iz) = i·arctan z. So one even function covers both and nothing selects on a value; the test sweeps densely through the crossing, including the point where q² is exactly zero and both arguments vanish together.

The references are fixed Gauss rules with panels graded geometrically into BOTH ends, which is what lets these sweeps reach two configurations the cosine family's own tests have to leave out: a quarter-turn amplitude, where the radical's layer of width k' sits ON the range end, and a pole root within 1e-4 of the range start, where the integrand is a spike of that width. Uniform panels are not a reference at either, and the convergence test refines the rule to show it.

The range-function algebra moved, and the full-turn gate did not

The two-exact-end-values-plus-harmonic-bulk representation is the entire conditioning treatment of the polygon reduction, and the arc reduction needs the same object. It moved verbatim into nova/biot/rangefunction.py, with the account of why neither half can be dropped moved with it, and the full-turn module now imports it. The gate is unmoved: 112 passed, and the closed form against the boundary quadrature at 6.3e-14 / 1.4e-13 / 2.5e-13 on a trapezium.

What the next rung inherits, and what it should not re-derive

The assembly was started and not landed. What came out of starting it is worth more than the code was: the reduction's shape at an interior limit is now known term by term, and the next rung should build from it rather than work it out again. Four things, all verified by hand against the shipped full-turn expressions:

  • The rows and their parities. Write X_l(α) = ∫₀^α w_l g_l da; the arc's value at one edge limit is 2 Σᵢ fold(limitᵢ, X_l), and a full sweep collapses that onto −2 X_l(π/2) — minus the full-turn module's own per-limit value divided by four. The five weights are cos φ·g, −sin φ·g, cos φ·bracket, sin φ·bracket and the vertical integrand as printed. The cos φ rows have ODD antiderivatives and take the quarter-turn value through the fold; the sin φ rows are half-turn periodic and do not.
  • Which channel each term lands in. Every term of an odd row lands in the sine family and every term of an even row in the cosine one, because the derivative of each transcendental carries exactly one factor of sin 2a and the by-parts weight supplies the other.
  • The by-parts constant is not free. For a weight that is a pure sine series the antiderivative must be the one vanishing at the LOWER limit — sin²2a/2 for the sin 4a weight on arsinh β1 — because arsinh β1 diverges at a = 0 for a target level with the corner and only that zero makes the product finite. Taking −cos 4a/4 instead puts a divergence into a term that has none.
  • The one piece still missing is a basis conversion. An odd row's arsinh β2 term needs its antiderivative as a range function with exact end values, and what is available is a harmonic series. The far end value is exactly zero by the choice above; the near one is a closed-form sum; the bulk is a double Chebyshev deflation at t = ±1, which rangefunction.deflate already performs. That conversion is the next thing to build, and it belongs in rangefunction.py beside its inverse.
