Polygon-section coupling: schemes, backends, and the closed-form conditioning result

Measured 2026-07-25 on sun_debug compute nodes and one H200 (betelgeuse), fresh process, median of 3 unless stated. Companion to the biot-operator-assembly plan; the drivers are benchmarks/tiled_backend.py, benchmarks/analytic_cost_floor.py and the acceptance sweeps in tests/test_biotbandedcoupling.py.

1. What a section can be coupled with

A finite section carrying uniform azimuthal current density can be coupled to a target four ways, and they differ by four orders of magnitude in cost and by eight in accuracy. The headline is that none of them is best everywhere, and the axis along which they trade is not the one the cost table alone suggests.

Two per-component facts drive every band choice. First, flux-only bounds mislead: at 8×24 the flux error is 1.5e-04 while BZ is 2.9e-02, a factor of 170, so the bands are gated per component. Second, the point filament's error is not a convergence error at all — for a full ring the curvature that the section's second moment multiplies is set by the major radius, so the error flattens onto an (a/R₀)² floor (~2e-5 per plasma cell, verified to 3000 section radii) that no switch distance repairs.

2. The three-band scheme, and what a wall-clipped cell changed

Bands are assigned by distance to the section contour, not the centroid: 16×48 inside 2.2 contour radii, 8×24 out to the far seam, moment-corrected filament beyond. Seams are geometric, so a stored operator is reproducible and the seam is smooth in state for autograd and Newton outer loops.

The locked design put the far seam at 6.8 contour radii with a quadrupole-corrected far field. That holds for a regular hexagon and fails for a wall-clipped cell: a hexagon's third moments vanish by symmetry, making its quadrupole-corrected filament fourth-order accurate, but a cell the first wall cuts keeps a non-zero third moment and so a third-order residual — measured 3.7e-05 of local magnitude at 6.8 radii, 37× outside the gate, decaying only as d−2.5 and not reaching 1e-6 until ~29 radii. Two changes follow, both measured: the corrected filament carries the section's third moments as well as its second (a bit-exact no-op on a symmetric section, so it costs nothing there), and the far seam is derived from the section's own skew — 6.8 radii when skew ≤ 1e-3, else 16.0.

Cost: the whole 2339-target column falls to 13.1 µs/pair on a hexagonal source and 52.0 µs/pair on a clipped one, against 869 and 1008 exact-everywhere — 66× and 19×. The banded scheme evaluates 1.20% (hexagon) / 4.28% (clipped) of the exact node count; the clipped cell pays for its wider seam.

3. Assembling the operator: one traced path, two devices

The pair space is embarrassingly parallel, and the tiled build pads every tile to one fixed shape precisely so a single trace can serve both devices. Three results decide the backend. The traced path beats the process pool on the CPU — 78.8 against 112.7 µs/pair, 1.43×, in one process rather than sixteen, which also removes the per-worker memory multiplication that forced the tile budget to be divided by the core count. vmap beats scan on both devices, so the single path needs only one combinator. And the GPU buys a further 15–40× over the best CPU build for a 1.5 s compile amortised over the whole build, using 1.3% of the card. A numba CPU twin would duplicate a page of Urankar algebra to chase a rate the traced path already exceeds; the numpy path is retained as the parity oracle it now is.

Parity numpy↔traced is 2.6e-17 (CPU) / 5.8e-17 (GPU) worst absolute deviation against component peaks ~1e-5, identical tile-level and through the assembled zarr store, with one compilation per build. Tile size is where the device headroom sits: 400 pairs/tile costs 15.68 µs/pair at 131 MB device high-water, 1,600 costs 7.18 at 864 MB, 6,400 costs 2.83 at 1.43 GB of a 112.6 GB card. One caveat on reading the CPU rows: the traced backend threads across the whole allocation, so those are one process using up to 16 cores against a 16-process pool on the same allocation — a fair node-level comparison, not a per-core one.

Projected first build of a 2000-cell exact-everywhere operator (4×10⁶ pairs): 7.5 min at 16 numpy cores, 5.3 min traced on those same cores, ~30 s on one H200 at 40×40 tiles, ~11 s at 80×80. With the banded scheme instead of exact-everywhere, a hexagonal build lands in seconds. The ≤20 min budget is met with a wide margin on CPU alone, which was the question this plan opened with.

One limitation found and documented rather than papered over: TilePlan.peak_bytes models one quadrature block and therefore does not bound a batched tile, whose blocks are all live at once — measured 864 MB for a 40×40 tile, ~3.7× even the naive block-count product, and growing sub-linearly with tile size because XLA reuses buffers the model cannot see. The benchmark driver now reports the device high-water mark per run so a batched budget model can be calibrated from measurement instead of derived from a formula that would not hold.

4. The closed form: its limit is the section's aspect ratio, not the target's distance

Urankar Part V evaluates the toroidal integration analytically, leaving per edge only two smooth arsinh integrals. For the full turn the reduction is the paper's case C: every elliptic integral is complete (4×H(π/2)), and cn K = 0 annihilates the radial vector potential, reproducing axisymmetry as a structural check rather than an assumption.

What is and is not the paper's. Taken from Part V verbatim: the Stokes reduction of the cross-section integral to a contour sum over polygon edges, the closed-form edge-parameter antiderivative (eq 9/9a — the D, arsinh β₁, arsinh β₂, arctan β₃ family already in polygon.py), the angle transformation φ = π − 2α, the field taken from the paper's own field integrand (eq 11b) rather than by differentiating the reduced flux, and the case-C specialisation. The final coefficient algebra (eqs 16a–16c, 21a–21c) is independently derived rather than transcribed: it reproduces the paper's structure — the same residual integrals, four complete Π per edge, the same boundary term, the same separation into a G-pole and a B-pole family — but the printed packaging divides by b₁³ and b₁⁵, which is 0/0 for a vertical edge (a hexagon has two), and two printed forms are ill-conditioned as printed and had to be re-derived by stable routes. The dual-basis conditioning treatment below is ours; a 1990 derivation had no reason to address it. Correctness therefore rests on agreement with a converged numerical oracle of the same integral, not on the transcription being verbatim.

Cost. 134.0 µs/pair for the flux and 134.4 for the flux with both field components — the field is essentially free because all three share every reduction and differ only in polynomial weights, which is the whole case for transcribing eq 11b. Sharing the pole stacks took the assembly from 15× its floor to 1.43×, but the measured floor itself rose from 16 to 93 µs/pair because the primitive set changed: two 69-order two-direction moment stacks, four shifted-pole families, and a graded quadrature evaluating sinh/cosh per node. Two thirds of the remaining floor is the moment stacks, whose length is set by the headroom a downward recursion needs rather than by the nine orders the numerators actually reach — so the next cost win is in the primitives, not the assembly.

The accuracy behaves in a way the cost table would not predict: it is governed by the section's aspect ratio, and the worst target is a near one, not a far one. A first implementation lost four orders per decade of aspect ratio, reaching O(1) error at the 1/100 aspect a tokamak coil pack presents — which would have made the closed form useless exactly where it is wanted. That turned out to be an expansion choice, not a property of the method, in two stages.

The ε−4 trend is gone: 4.1×10⁷ improvement at aspect 1/100 and 1.2×10⁷ at 1/300. The flux is nonetheless still 3–20× short of the 1e-10 gate at every section but the rectangle, so the closed form is not registered as a production candidate and the gate was not widened; the distance from it is pinned in both directions instead, so any improvement forces the record to be updated.

Mechanism, and the exact repair. The derivative of the arctangent term is rational over the product of both edge denominators, G²B². Expanding it by partial fractions over their combined pole set divides by the difference of two characteristics that both approach unity as (section/R₀)², so the weights diverge as ε−2. No such expansion is needed. Because the arctangent's numerator N and the weight W = (r sin φ D)² satisfy the paper's identity G²B² = N² + W, the derivative's numerator obeys

2 W N' − N W'  =  2 G²B² N' − N (G²B²)'

— the N² parts cancel identically — so the quotient is 2N' − N(G²)'/G² − N(B²)'/B²: a polynomial plus one term over each denominator separately. This is precisely why the paper's own eq 14c carries only the G-pole family and eq 14d only the B-pole family. Applying it measured a 974× improvement at aspect 1/300 and 44× at 1/3 (figure above, left).

The second mechanism, and its cure. Each pole family's largest moment is multiplied by the numerator's value at the end of the integration range — an O(ε²) quantity that a monomial contraction forms as an alternating sum of O(1) terms, losing two decades per family and hence the ε−4 trend. The cure is to take each pole factor in the variable that vanishes at its own end of the range (cos²α at one, sin²α at the other), so that value is a leading coefficient rather than a reconstructed sum. This needed complete cn moments alongside the existing sn moments and a shifted-pole recursion over either basis, both with stable three-term forms, and both bases built directly from the geometry so the small offsets (r′−r, r₁−r) are exact rather than differences of major-radius-sized terms. Two conditioning traps surfaced on the way, each caught by a test rather than by inspection: the cn recursion's parasitic branch is −k′²/k², which fixes its direction switch at 1/3 rather than the 0.3 first chosen; and a shifted pole cannot be seeded through Π at a characteristic of −1/shift, because the Carlson form there is a difference of two terms of order K whose sum falls as √shift, costing five digits at a shift of 1e-10 — a mirrored evaluation with two positive terms is required.

Two further effects mattered as much as the pole treatment. The residual arsinh integrands carry boundary layers of angular width |u|/2r and |r₁−r|/2a₀r at each end; a target level with a section vertex makes that 1e-5 radians, and 48 plain Gauss nodes lost seven digits on it. Grading each half of the range from its own end is exact for the layer's leading quadratic and moves the nearest singularity from that width to π/2 off the real axis. And the u = 0 configuration is now evaluated rather than floored — with both shifts vanishing together with the numerator's leading coefficient there, flux went from 8.7e-09 to 1.7e-12 and BR from 1.9e-08 to 2.4e-14 at a hexagon's vertex levels. A field grid whose rows align with section corners hits that on every row, so it was not an exotic case.

What still limits it — no longer the reduction. Per edge and per limit the reduction now reproduces its own integral to 1e-14, node-independent from 48 through 384 nodes. The residual 3–20× is round-off amplified by two cancellations that happen after the reduction. First, the edge/limit differencing: the edge antiderivative's arsinh-β₂ weight is of order the squared major radius and is u-independent, while the flux the two limits differ by is not — localised on the thin plate's worst target, eight per-edge values of ≈174.71 sum to 2.34e-03, a 7.1×10³ cancellation, and another 10× across edges. Second, the monomial contraction: numerator coefficients reach 1e7 while the contraction returns 1e4, so individual terms exceed their sum by up to 1e5 — which is what puts each edge value at ~270 ε rather than a few ε. The shipped quadrature has the same differencing structure but its per-edge values are accurate to ~1 ε, which is why it lands at 2.4e-12. Neither is reachable by a better pole split; the two routes that address them are a cos 2nα harmonic numerator basis (worth ~30× on these numbers) and forming the limit difference before the large antiderivative is assembled.

An erratum in the printed paper

Eq 11b's vertical row prints its rational term as −b₁²D/a₀². It is −b₁D/a₀², linear in the edge slope. Applying eq 4b's own prescription for that row (cos φ ∂g/∂r − (sin φ/r) ∂g/∂φ) to the flux antiderivative g, the B² denominator cancels through r²sin²φ + Y²/a₀² = B²/a₀² and the remainder collapses on Γ = a₀²γ + b₁Y with Γ² + B² = a₀²D², leaving the slope to the first power. Checked against a numerical derivative of g at five edge slopes (b₁ = −5, 0, 0.1, 1, 4): the linear form matches to the 5e-10 finite-difference floor everywhere, the printed quadratic form only at b₁ = 0 and b₁ = 1, where b₁² = b₁.

That coincidence is why the error survives casual checking — a rectangular section has every edge at slope 0 and a 45° edge sits at slope 1, so the two sections anyone would test first are exactly the two that cannot tell the forms apart. With the printed form the assembled BZ is wrong by 286% over a trapezium, 7.6% over a thin plate and 1.1% over a hexagon. With the correction, the closed form agrees with an independent boundary quadrature to 1.3e-12 on a trapezium whose edge slopes are −5.0, 0.2, 4.375 and −0.417.

It was found by the one test in the suite that checks two different rows of the paper against each other — holding BR and BZ to a five-point stencil on the closed form's own flux. A per-edge test comparing the reduction against a transcription of the same printed row cannot find it, because both sides carry the same misreading. The same test campaign also showed that the field reduction had been right all along and its reference was wrong: the α-quadrature oracle ran one Gauss rule across the point where arctan β₃ jumps by π, leaving a kink inside the rule and an error up to 5.6e-05.

The practical consequence for nova is the reverse of the naive reading: a slender section at large major radius is the hard case for the closed form and the easy case for the boundary quadrature, whose 16×48 rule is machine-precise there. So the closed form is not a drop-in speedup for a coil pack — it becomes one when the remaining expansion lands, and that is worth doing because the same evaluation is the template for circular cross-section ELM coils.

5. Defects surfaced by this work

  • cylinder_greens is discontinuous across horizontal section faces: targets 1 µm either side of a face plane inside the radial span return ψ = −1.22e-4 / +1.65e-4 where the polygon kernel gives a smooth +2.16e-5. Root cause in the corner_fields γ-sign branch terms.
  • polygon_greens divides by 2πr without guarding the axis, so BR and BZ come back non-finite for a target at r = 0 (ψ is fine). The closed form stays finite there instead, BR vanishing by the parity of the reduction.
  • PolySection placed its far field and measured its section radius about the vertex mean. For a section whose corners pair up that is the area centroid, but for a trapezoidal or wall-clipped one it is displaced, leaving a dipole error the far field cannot absorb. Both now use the shoelace area centroid. benchmarks/nearfar_error.py still carries the same assumption — harmless there because it only measures a hexagon.
  • The printed sn_moments recursion is unusable past order ~2 below k² ≈ 0.1, and the printed eq 24a/23 forms are ill-conditioned as printed; stable re-derivations are in nova/biot/elliptic.py and are what callers should use.
