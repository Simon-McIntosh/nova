Re-reading the boundary-flux offset in one gauge

The displaced-seed record above reads the positive arm's boundary flux at -0.5289095779831239 Wb with a boundless span of 49.3895 Wb against an authored analytic zero hardcoded at 0.0, and calls that a half-weber displacement of the flux level. The two numbers are not in one gauge. The fixture poses its exterior as the authored total field minus the plasma image, so a solved state and the authored field share the gauge whose zero level is the analytic separatrix; the compensator columns that carry the centroid row are anchored elsewhere, at the magnetic axis (reference radius 6.2 m), and their additive constant therefore separates the two readings. The negative arm confirms the shared gauge where it can be checked: its reported boundary flux -0.0018776984518051078 Wb matches the authored flux at its own boundary point, +5.986607067106857e-08 Wb, to 1.9e-3 Wb.

The gauge-free substitute is the span between the magnetic axis and the boundary, which no additive constant can move, evaluated on both sides at the same two points. This is a re-read of the banked receipts: no solve ran and no field was recomputed, because the banked JSON already carries both points, the level at each, and the compensating field, and the fixture's closed form supplies the authored levels those points should carry.
