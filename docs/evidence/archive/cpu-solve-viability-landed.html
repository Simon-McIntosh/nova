CPU solve viability — cumulative execution evidence

The greater-than-three-hour CPU regression is closed as understood, not accepted as healthy and not repaired here. The exact bank route proves the slowdown is present at HEAD; paired same-HEAD controls refute callbacks and explicit thread-pool configuration as causes; compiler-event timing identifies HEAD-side traced-program growth as the remaining explanation.

Headline: HEAD took 11887 s (3.30 h) against 2200 s (37 min) pinned, with per-trip walls of 944 s, 1108 s, then about 700 s; it used 1.62 effective threads and spent only 3.4 ms in callbacks. The paired thread run stayed at 1.34 effective threads, recorded 396 compiler events taking 2945 s, and averaged 1646.6 s per trip against the 314 s pinned reference.

The frozen symptom

SLURM job 1260726 established the anomaly before any diagnosis was attempted. On a rigel node with 16 CPUs and 64 GiB, generate_topology_visuals.py --cache-only --regenerate-mast-shot 22086 reached its three-hour limit before MAST_STATES_READY, still inside the first shot-pair stage. At 1 h 35 min wall it had accumulated 2 h 28 min CPU, or about 1.5 effective cores of 16. That contradicted the producer's committed 25–50 min per-arm history and the approximately 37.5 s width-32 H200 reference, so the non-finish was frozen as a regression rather than normalized as an ordinary CPU/GPU gap.
