The three oracle gates — allocation, coupling, iteration

Gate A, allocation. On the exact input (analytic flux at the nodes) the global split spline books the analytic total to 1.00003 with zero boundary root error; the six-point ring quadratic books 0.99997 with a 0.15 mm median root error; the linear edge root falls 0.55 percent short (0.9945, 1.8 mm median root error) on weak, moderate and strong. The terminal-state shortfalls are state error (100 percent weak, 99.7 percent strong), and on the single-null 300 row every representation loses 20 percent at the terminal because the read admits no saddle and puts the boundary at the limiter, 21 percent of the span away — the §3 and null-identification census defect priced in current. Ship: the lead's locked choice of the global spline stands on its own number; the clip's linear edge root is the one representation with a measurable allocation error, so the consistent read (§4) replaces it.

Gate B, coupling. Gate B found a one to two percent smooth error when clipped first moments were imaged through the frozen linear-basis blocks; the held line rebuilt polygon-analytic blocks per trip, which changes the Biot geometry per trip — neither cacheable nor batchable nor differentiable. The lead's locked design is the frozen interaction matrix with the clipped current expanded to its moments about the atomic centroid (amplitude, gradient, curvature) to second order, imaged by matmul: a cached construction linear in cut cells, exact at the wall targets through the frozen Biot wall blocks.

Gate C, iteration. On the single-null 500 row under the fixture exterior, where the analytic flux is a fixed point to 4.9e-15, the production Newton-Krylov returned from perturbations of 1e-4 to 1e-1 of span to residual 6.2e-15 and distance 1.4e-14 from the analytic flux. Where the fixed point is the reference, the iteration contracts to it from a tenth of a span away; Gate C was never the iteration.

The reposed fixture exterior and the exact-clip memory findings

The fixture exterior is re-posed from the analytic density integrated over each cell's clipped region inside the analytic separatrix, cached with the machine. Gate at 1000 cells: exact-clip one-map floor 3.58e-5 of span on weak and 3.42e-5 on moderate (booked 0.99999) against 0.136 and 0.127 under the old whole-cell-integrated exterior; whole-cell booking now over-books 8.6 percent; single-null exact 0.109 at 500 and 0.045 at 1000 cells, apparent order 2.5 in pitch.

The exact-clip solve memory array is identified (H200 job 1270367, optimised HLO under docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/reposed-fixture/optimised-hlo). It is f64[cells, 196480, 6], 3.0 GiB per copy at 342 cells, repeated across the Newton JVP and transpose paths from fixed_point.py:3459: clipped_support_quadrature fans every cell's fixed-capacity support polygon into capacity-minus-two triangles and places a 64-point Gauss rule on each, and the exact clip's support capacity is 24 times 128 vertices, so 3070 triangles times 64 points equals 196480 quadrature points for every cell, whole cells included. In whole-cell mode the capacity is 24, the array is f64[342, 1408, 6] at 22 MiB and the program's temporaries total 0.19 GiB — 97 GiB against 0.19 GiB, so the exact-clip solve allocates 97, 131 and 279 GiB of booleans at 300, 500 and 1000 cells and fails even on an empty card. The fix is linear in cut cells: a chain capacity justified by the position accuracy the clip needs (a pitch over a thousand), the quadrature applied only to the cut cells at that capacity, and whole cells on the 24-vertex path.

Four fixed-capacity wedges in the single-null X-point cell

The analytic saddle, rather than the production null census, selects the cut cell by point-in-polygon. This distinction is decisive on the coarse meshes: the production census does not admit the saddle at 132 or 340 realised cells. At the requested 300-cell rung, the unique strict-containment candidate is realised cell 5. The saddle is interior and 6.500849 mm from its nearest edge. Four roots cross the boundary, but edge 2 carries two of them at fractions 0.2072052925 and 0.9283123862 despite same-sign endpoint samples. The endpoint-sign path therefore found only two roots and emitted an all-zero saddle row. This reproduced the recorded defect before the repair.

The repair accepts up to two ordered roots per edge, pairs the four roots cyclically by branch, and emits four polygons with a static capacity of 12 vertices. Every polygon begins with the analytic saddle; unused coordinates are bitwise zero. A saddle coincident with an edge root is represented by the same layout, leaving the repeated-root wedge with exact-zero area. Each wedge is integrated with its own profile. The independent analytic integrator also preserves disjoint vertical intervals instead of replacing them with their enclosing range. Neither repair changes an equality guard or widens the 1e-6 current tolerance.
