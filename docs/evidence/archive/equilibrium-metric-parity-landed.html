q_100's 21–35% gap is a non-coincident domain, and the profile is what proves it. The reference value sits at the stored LCFS — the separatrix, where q diverges on a diverted equilibrium — while nova evaluates at its last resolvable pre-separatrix surface ψ_N = 0.995. The discriminating evidence is that the transformed qpsi_c profile stays within the low 1e-4 across the whole common grid, which ends at ψ_N = 0.984375, and only the endpoint scalar jumps. An interior numerical disagreement could not be confined to the last point.

The ~1% scalar area and volume gaps are the same effect at a different endpoint. The reference scalars are enclosed to the stored LCFS, nova's to its outermost ψ_N = 0.995 surface, while the paired cumulative profiles are compared only on coincident stored surfaces through ψ_N = 0.984375. That non-coincident scalar boundary accounts for a ~1% gap sitting beside a ~3e-4 cumulative-profile agreement for the same quantity — the scalar and the profile are simply not integrated to the same boundary.

The declaring contract is flux-function-forward-equilibrium §5, which names V′, the inverse-square-radius and diffusion-coefficient surface averages, F and safety factor on the toroidal-flux coordinate. None of the 47 is one of them — F and q were already paired by the census, while V′ and the surface-average metrics are nova-side fields with no reference counterpart at all. So the reference offers nova no additions the transport contract needs.

What it does offer is 11 valid cross-checks of computations nova already has — not 11 additions. Fifteen further existing computations are not valid cross-checks: their conventions are incompatible or unstated, their x-point ordering is unstated, or their boundary domain is non-coincident. That last class is the same effect the comparison above measured — reference boundary scalars enclose to the stored LCFS while nova stops at ψ_N = 0.995, which is why a ~1% scalar mismatch sits beside ~3e-4 interior-profile agreement.

The 21 genuinely absent entries each carry a zero-hit definition-search receipt over all nova Python sources, so "absent" is evidenced rather than assumed.

Consequence: convention-pinning is prerequisite work, not comparison work. β_p, l_i, rotational pressure, thermal energy and x-point identity cannot be compared until their conventions are pinned, and LCFS-derived scalars cannot be compared until both codes evaluate on a coincident boundary. Nothing from these 47 should be added to FluxSurfaceGeometry for the transport contract's sake.

Read-path detail worth keeping: the stored gridr/gridz are proved uniform at stored dtype precision and then reconstructed with an endpoint-preserving float64 linspace, and a genuinely non-uniform stored axis raises rather than being silently regularised into a uniform one. Each shot uses its own +1 four-way-agreeing sign receipt from the current-sign census rather than a single assumption.

Legacy internal-inductance radius restored on the pulse-design path

Commit 88effd71 (merged aba2ede0); gate passed. The legacy Plasma.li_3 property in nova/biot/plasma.py normalised by a literal 6.2 m — ITER's major radius — with the geometric-radius expression commented out beside it, so any non-ITER machine on the pulse-design path misstated internal inductance by the ratio of 6.2 to its true geometric radius (7.48× on MAST). The property now reads self.lcfs.geometric_radius.

Both pulse-design consumers were audited read-only before the input changed and carry no baked-in compensation: nova/imas/pulsedesign.py copies li_3 unchanged into the waveform and the equilibrium global quantities, and nova/imas/pulseschedule.py reads flux_control.li_3.reference.data unchanged. The direct regression (tests/test_plasma_internal_inductance.py) pins the legacy property against the forward moment observation's internal inductance on shared three-cell state at geometric radius 0.83 m, agreeing at rtol 1e-12 (1/1 passed, 7.46 s); the deliberate non-ITER radius means reinstating the literal fails the comparison by the 0.83/6.2 ratio.

This defect was found by the moment-infrastructure survey and is explicitly excluded as the cause of the 0.602 field-energy instrument ratio (wrong magnitude; that measurement never routed through this property).
