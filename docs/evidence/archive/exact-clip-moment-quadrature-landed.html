Closed-form polygon moments of the quadratic density

The route now carries no quadrature node at all: a cut cell's moments are the polygon's exact boundary integral, and the arc's vertex spacing is the entire approximation. The density over a clipped support is a local quadratic in the cell-local coordinates, so its area, first and second moments are boundary integrals by Green's theorem. With the antiderivative A_pq = x^(p+1) y^q / (p+1), the contour of A_pq in dy gives ∫ x^p y^q dA. Every edge of the clip's polygon is a straight segment, so its piece is dy times the exact integral along it: parametrising the edge from (x0, y0) to (x1, y1) as x(t) = x0 + t dx, y(t) = y0 + t dy, the monomial (p, q) receives the edge's own dy times C(p+1,i) C(q,j) x0^(p+1−i) y0^(q−j) dx^i dy^j / ((p+1)(i + j + 1)), which is exact for every polynomial integrand. No rule order, node count or convergence argument enters the reduction.

The monomial table covers the whole triangle p + q ≤ 4, and the quadratic density is carried by the six coefficients (1, x, y, x^2, xy, y^2) whose second moments reach total degree four, so each of the route's moments is one contraction of those coefficients against the table. The retired alternatives are both quadrature: the dense triangle fan spent 196,480 points per cut cell at the declared 3,072-vertex support (245,632 at the weak row's realised 3,840), and the sampled-arc per-edge Gauss route spent 472 evaluations and then 621 once the shifted first-moment paths moved the order to four. Both are now reference arms only. Every table in the closed form is a compile-time constant, an unused vertex slot carries an exactly zero edge weight, and the traversal sense is read from the polygon's own signed area, so no shape and no branch depends on data.

The two independent checks, and the padding check

tests/test_polygon_moments.py states the closed form against two references that share none of its machinery. The first is exact and rational: each of the fifteen monomials is compared with the unit-triangle identity p! q! / (p + q + 2)! at 1e-14 relative, over the full p + q ≤ 4 triangle. The second is a dense Duffy fan, deliberately independent of Green's theorem: the polygon is fanned into triangles from its first vertex and each triangle carries a forty-node product rule, which resolves a degree-four integrand exactly. It is applied to a convex hexagon and to a concave five-vertex arrow at 1e-13 relative; both carry vertices in every quadrant, so a sign slip in the boundary orientation cannot cancel, and the vertex fan is read as a signed decomposition, since a concave polygon's fan carries mixed orientations.

The padding and winding check is relative and not bitwise, and the distinction is the point of it. A vertex buffer padded from n to n + 24 slots carries exact-zero edge weights in the added slots, so those slots contribute exactly nothing — but the summation still runs over a longer table, so the arithmetic is not the same sequence of additions and the result is not bit-identical. Demanding bit identity there would test the floating-point association order rather than the padding, and it would fail on a correct implementation. The check therefore compares relative at 1e-13, for both the padded buffer and a reversed winding, and two negative controls keep the check from passing vacuously: a mismatched density moves the dense reference by more than 2e-3, and a malformed vertex buffer is refused.
