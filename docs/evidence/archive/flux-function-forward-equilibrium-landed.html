Load-bearing findings

  • The absolute-source map has a vacuum twin. With the net-current rescale removed, the free-boundary map always has a second fixed point — the vacuum field — and a source driven only marginally above the limiter sits on a fold between branches: at marginal drive, every ≥5% seed perturbation collapsed to vacuum under Picard, Anderson, and Krylov alike; at 1.4× drive, seeds 0.7–1.3 all reach the same equilibrium. Seed policy, not the accelerator, decides the branch. Pinned by test; this is the strongest practical argument for the deferred one-amplitude target-current closure.
  • The relaxed iteration floors near 1e-7 where a boundary cell alternates in and out of the boolean core mask; the mixed ladder damps the cycle to 2.6e-14 on the same map. Pinned by test.
  • A flux-function gradient vanishing at the boundary makes the discrete fixed point a single point rather than a lattice-quantised family; untapered constant gradients gave seed-dependent currents in the third digit.
  • The topology mask is a connectivity selection carrying no gradient, so derivatives through the solve omit the boundary-motion term; central differences must span enough cell-quantised motion (5e-5 agreement at 1e-3 relative step, degrading to 9e-2 at 1e-6). Registered as GRADIENT_STEP with the mechanism stated.
  • The finite-difference GS residual of a Green-operator solve is a two-discretisation consistency measure, not a convergence measure (2.7e-2 on the solve fixture vs 4.9e-5 analytic at comparable spacing); the convention pin therefore lives with the manufactured equilibrium.

Carried forward: fp64 CPU/GPU parity; rotation, SOL-continuation, and Ambix/IMAS interface gates (later nodes). Out-of-scope debt flagged to owners: nova/biot/boundary.py (dev script) and a nova/biot/plasma.py docstring still reference the old ForwardProfile/ForwardFluxOperator signatures.

Double-precision null-fit default

Commit 092d896a (merged as f629233a, 2026-08-11, owner decision) flips Null2D's fit precision default to double; single stays an explicit throughput switch pending its deferred study. The accelerator profile, and the routes that were measured against it

The H200 profile (05c82dba/e4e3e6d8) answered "how fast can it go" with a reversal of the working assumption: one solve is 7× slower on the H200 than on a host node (1.27 s vs 0.17 s at 1587 cells), because the fixed-budget ladder is 20 sequential primal map evaluations of which 99.6% is the topology read — 145× slower on device — while the coupling product it was assumed to be bound by is 0.03% of the map and 14× faster there. Batched, one card reaches 600 solves/s at 1024 members (×764 over single-solve, unsaturated, 617 KB/member) — the headroom a parallel-in-time or waveform-coupled scheme harvests without touching the solver; crossover against one host node is at N≈8. Ranked residual gains: the coupling assembly recomputed per process (97 s–30 min, 230–300× a solve, single-threaded, with the backend="jax" tiled device route unused behind a policy default); the uncompiled public entry point (eager receipts cost 51% on device, 9× on host); the disabled persistent compile cache (to 8.8×/process). Negative results pinned: fp32 would buy ~0.2%, transfers are 0.06%, GMRES width is the wrong lever. Figure h200-throughput.png.

The follow-on study (benchmarks/forward_newton_ladder.py, figure newton-ladder.png) located the device read cost to a single nested lax.scan — 7.6 µs of launch latency per iteration for six float comparisons, ~2400× a host core's per-iteration cost, trip count equal to the ring count — and vectorized it (a cyclic sign-change count) plus three sibling scans: the read drops ×140 on device and the whole shipped solve ×25.6 (1.254 s → 0.049 s) with bit-identical labels and flux. It also answered the plain-Newton question by measurement: a lagged-topology Newton with the analytic diagonal+rank-2 Jacobian converges in 3–4 topology reads with no mask oscillation and passes every correctness gate, but against the fair baseline — the ladder driven through the vectorized read — it wins only one cell of four (H200/566) and loses the 1587-cell batch by 2.6×; the direct O(driven³) solve is what fails, not the lagging. Verdict adopted: land the read vectorization, keep Newton–Krylov. Two forecast corrections worth keeping: a traced Jacobian costs 1.3 frozen-map evaluations (not ~10), and there is no flood fill on the forward path — connectivity is a 15 µs half-plane cut. Next device target, measured and named: the wall read's unfused custom lax.reduce (278 µs, 67% of the fixed read) wants a lexicographic-key reduction.

The vectorization landed in the package (e4cd3075/1a3c4e37, merged 2026-08-11) and the payoff reproduced slightly better than the study predicted: on the H200 the read drops 57.98 ms → 0.405 ms (×143) and the whole shipped solve 1.273 s → 0.046 s (×27.6), batch peak 600 → 1440 solves/s; host read ×1.39 — confirmed by a same-allocation control restoring the traversal onto the same package objects. Bit-identity holds exactly on the host backend (labels, every flux, both null tables, the converged 1752-node solve); on the H200 everything that selects or normalises is exactly equal while the fitted null coordinates move within the backend's own measured batch-to-batch self-disagreement (≤7.1e-14 — a device-codegen property, not the change; the shipped gate now pins positions to that measured spread and everything else to exact equality).
