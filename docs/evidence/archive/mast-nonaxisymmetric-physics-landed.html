Receipt: docs/figures/mast-saddle-loops/reach.json.

Verdict: reachable for 36 of 36 loops through the level-1 XMB channel xmb/sad_out_<family><index>, once each raw key is read at the store's own width: the m family is spelled three-digit past loop 9 (sad_out_m010–m012). Coverage is thin, and it shapes the sign node. Of the 86 admitted cohort shots only 26 carry an XMB group at all. A deliberately misspelled channel is recorded as unreachable rather than dropped.

Review at the corrected head: 87/120, no blocking finding. Its two follow-ons are folded into the sign node: the benchmark re-implements the vacuum-cohort admission predicate that nova/imas/mast_vacuum_cohort.py owns, and no test guards per-family key resolution.

§4 — driven transients identify one passive direction of fifteen

The grouped driven Jacobian in nova/imas/mast_passive_decay_modes.py carries exact geometric inductance, fifteen component-family resistance groups bounded by their nominal intervals (the seven rodgr circuits excluded, because no sourced material exists for them), and all thirteen coil drive scales as nuisance columns with uncertainty intervals, because the ampere-turn semantics are unconfirmed. Receipt: docs/figures/mast-passive-identifiability/spectrum.json.

One passive direction of fifteen stays above the 386.98 µT RMS sensor floor once the drive scales are projected out: leading singular value 1.169 floor units, the next 0.147. All 400 selected shots were scored (168 admitted, 232 refused, none missing; 6,650,343 observations), and half-step checks move the spectrum norms by under 0.13 %. Driven transients do not break the degeneracy the free decays found: the passive circuit is identifiable along one effective combination, not per family.

Controls: two collinear synthetic groups report rank 1, and bypassing the drive-scale projection fails the test that a drive-aligned passive direction is removed. A drive-permutation spectrum is not a control here: a Jacobian at fixed parameters is independent of the measured response, so permuting shots leaves its spectrum unchanged. That control belongs to the held-out fit. Gate: tests/test_mast_passive_decay_modes.py, 49 passed at the landed head on the reviewer's re-run. Review 94/120, no blocking finding. Its reuse follow-ons go to the held-out fit node: driven_field duplicates zoh_mode_response in nova/circuit/propagate.py, and the measurement driver imports the registry that machine-description-retirement deletes.

§4 — the identifiable direction improves held-out residuals but saturates its sourced interval

benchmarks/mast_passive_held_out.py fits the one identifiable grouped passive direction, with every drive scale free within its interval, on 131 training shots of the vacuum cohort, and scores it on 37 shots held out before scoring (split: docs/figures/mast-passive-held-out/split.json). Receipt: docs/figures/mast-passive-held-out/receipt.json.

ArmHeld-out RMS, seed → fitSquared-residual improvement
Real drives6.4014 → 5.6355 mT22.50 %
Drives permuted across shots32.1174 → 32.0706 mT0.29 %

Adjudication. The node's own control rule required the permuted gain to be non-positive, and it read the 0.29 % as a failure. That rule is degenerate: a single bounded coordinate can almost always find a small positive gain at an interval edge, so it passes only for a no-fit outcome. Compared side by side, the real gain is about 77 times the permuted one, so the improvement belongs to the real drives. All 50 decay rates stay positive (minimum 16.0 s−1), so the system is passive.

Not promoted: the fit pins coil-case resistivity at 0.900 µΩ·m, the upper edge of its sourced interval [0.690, 0.900] (seed 0.740). The data want a value the sourced interval does not allow, so the fitted number is an interval artefact. Either the coil-case material or temperature is outside its documented range, or the identifiable direction absorbs an unmodelled path. Promoting it needs an independent source for the coil-case resistivity, or a model change, not a widened guess. driven_field now propagates through zoh_mode_response in nova/circuit/propagate.py. Gate: tests/test_mast_passive_decay_modes.py, 53 passed at the reviewed head. Review 108/120, zero added failures.

§4 — no source licenses coil-case resistivity above 0.90 µΩ·m; temperature is the live lead

Asked why the passive fit pins coil-case resistivity at the sourced 0.90 µΩ·m ceiling, a cited source check (docs/figures/mast-coil-case-resistivity/receipt.json) found stainless coil cans with electrical connections to the vessel, and reference steel at 0.811 µΩ·m at 300 K and 0.890 at 400 K. No source documents a grade, segmentation or measured can temperature that licenses more than 0.90. The interval and the fit stay unchanged, and a test refuses any receipt claim without a citation.

The physically closest route to the ceiling is temperature: reference steel crosses 0.90 µΩ·m near 415 K (142 °C), within the module's own range up to the 200 °C bake. The next §4 node is therefore a can-temperature estimate for the fitted shots (a thermal record or model), with the vessel-coupled current path as the alternative. Review 109/120, zero added failures.
