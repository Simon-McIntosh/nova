Reading

  • A single real member solves in seconds, not milliseconds. The recorded solve time is the whole trip loop: per-trip host reconciliation, any retrace below the top-level compiled boundary, and the final device synchronisation. The banked 0.784 ms per member and 1.163 ms per slice are batched-throughput figures over a replicated operator and are not comparable to this width-1 latency.
  • The strict settled exit is a modest saving. Between 1.4 % and 20.4 % per MAST member, 2.1 % and 10.7 % on the two DIII-D frames; the members that converge quickly (21985/51, 22086/43) gain the most, the stagnating members the least.
  • Heterogeneous frames recompile. Each DIII-D frame compiled for about 1470 to 1510 s with resident memory reaching 67 to 68 GiB; MAST pure arms compiled for 645 to 681 s against 133 to 144 s for the mixed arms of the same members. This is the member-data-as- trace-constant cost the batched-operator-boundary plan exists to remove.
  • Not persisted. Executed and no-op trip counts, terminal residuals, state hashes, bit-identity where the exit fired, and the per-trip quantum at HEAD are recorded as null: the driver's atomic receipt write came after the scheduler timeout and the flushed stage log did not carry them.

Exact re-evaluation of the condition ratio does not recover the trip (negative, held)

Commits: six held in worktree msc-projected-krylov-condition-from-gmres-hessenberg, tip 25d4ec74; receipt docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/trip-quantum/hessenberg-reuse/profile.json (H200 job 1262745). Five semantics-preserving evaluations of the projected-Krylov condition ratio were measured on the same fused production solve of 22086/43 pure: the original projection reused, a GMRES-layout Hessenberg with a device SVD, a Gram eigenspectrum, a host SVD callback, and a fixed-sweep Jacobi spectrum of the GMRES Gram matrix. The decision sequence is proven identical to the baseline on a fixture with a refused Krylov direction, and the 75-test gate is green.

Reading. The spectrum arithmetic is not the cost. What the live ratio's data dependency does to the compiled program is: with a constant, the Arnoldi and whatever depends on it are dead code and the trip runs in a tenth of a second. Candidates are the Arnoldi's extra Jacobian-vector products, a conditional on the ratio that serialises the trip, or a materialised Krylov basis breaking fusion. A device-trace and HLO comparison of the Jacobi variant against the constant names the mechanism before another repair is written; a cheaper proxy or deferred telemetry would change the trusted-Krylov admission meaning and is the lead's decision.

Two corrections

The constant-ratio ablation was not the same computation minus the spectrum: with the ratio constant the solver settles a trip after 4 promotions instead of 12, so its 0.75 s solve came from a more permissive path. The exact evaluation costs 0.064 s per trip more than the constant, which is the entire removable arithmetic; the earlier 98 percent attribution to the SVD is withdrawn. And the compiled trip itself costs about 0.49 s, kernels plus host gaps, while the production solve averages 5.77 s per trip: nine tenths of a production trip lies outside the compiled trip, in the Python-level driver (active-set loop, per-trip topology read and relinearisation, receipt assembly, host transfers of state and history pytrees, or repeated dispatch of many small programs). A host-side profile of one warm production solve, cProfile with device synchronisation and py-spy, is the next measurement.

The host is not the cost: one dispatch, one compiled outer loop (merged)

Receipts: docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/trip-quantum/host-profile/ (synchronised cProfile, native py-spy flame, transfer census; H200 job 1262767). The warm production solve of 22086/43 pure takes 41.13 s over seven reported trips, and the compiled runtime owns 99.99 percent of it: the Python wrapper is 0.5 ms per trip and the device-to-host transfers 0.1 ms per trip, 19 buffers and 17 kB. There is one Python dispatch for the whole solve; the active-set loop is a fori_loop over the active-set budget at fixed_point.py:3387 with a compiled conditional skipping inactive iterations. So the gap between the 0.38 s one-trip program and the 5.88 s per trip of the full solve lies inside the executable's outer loop, in what later iterations execute, which the next measurement records per iteration with ordered timestamps: executed against active iterations, promotions and linear actions per iteration, and whether the inner loops run to their static budgets after settlement.

Reading

The conditional skip of inactive iterations works, so a while-loop exit saves nothing material. The cost is that the same trip, compiled inside the outer loop, runs 9 to 32 times slower than compiled alone, and slows further as the trip index grows while its counted work stays constant. The repair does not need to explain the compiler: drive every trip as one dispatch of the common fast executable with an explicit carry, from the host, until settlement, keeping the trip executable vmap-able over a batch with per-lane active masks. Upper bound: 41 s to about 3 s per solve.

Reading

The standalone 0.38 s first trip does not reproduce under repeated dispatch with the full carry, so the fused-loop hypothesis is withdrawn alongside the host-overhead and condition-spectrum hypotheses. What every measurement agrees on is that a trip's cost grows with its index for the same static budgets, from 3.4 s to 12.2 s, and that the slowest trips are those where most promotions are rejected: the budgets hide dynamic work.
