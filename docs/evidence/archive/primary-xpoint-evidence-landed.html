Hex adjacency: a real disagreement, and an inverted conclusion

Node hex-connectivity-partition, commit 8d4f3f33, gate PASSED, 2026-08-25. The component kernel labels 4-connected components on a rectangular lattice, while the shipped plasma mesh is hexagons on a half-offset tiling whose six-neighbour rings are shared by the null search and the derivative operator. So the reachability partition is computed on a different topology from the physics mesh.

The measurement is sound and the conclusion drawn from it was inverted — by my dispatch. Zero private cells, on a fixture built to contain a private lobe, means six-connectivity bridged the two lobes through the saddle and then admitted a wall cell sitting behind it. That is the error class this record documents elsewhere, arrived at from the opposite direction. By the governing definition — reachable from the O-point without crossing a saddle — passing through the saddle point is crossing one, so the private lobe must stay unreachable. One level below the saddle the lobes are genuinely disconnected in the continuum; the neck is narrower than a cell, so the apparent connection is pure discretisation.

So neither adjacency is correct alone. 4-connectivity under-connects in the bulk while accidentally cutting the saddle — which is why it has appeared to work. 6-connectivity matches the mesh in the bulk while wrongly bridging the saddle. The repair is not a choice of adjacency but edge-based admissibility: admit a neighbour link only where the shared edge has a portion strictly on the same side of the binding level, so a level crossing a cut cell closes the neck. Excising the saddle cell as a cut vertex is the equivalent complement.

A test now encodes the wrong expectation. test_hex_neighbour_keeps_a_pinch_joined_that_four_neighbours_sever asserts the bridging as intended behaviour. That is worse than a stale comment, because a test defends what it encodes — it must be inverted rather than superseded. Production was not switched over, which is what kept this from becoming a regression.

Cost is recorded with its own caveat: the node labelled its 1,341,435-slice figure a scalar arithmetic projection rather than measured batched throughput, so it prices the kernel and not the campaign.
