§4 — the observable is a discrete identity, not a truncation signal

Node divergence-floor-attribution, commit a59932ae, gate PASSED, 2026-08-25. Artifacts docs/figures/roundoff-scale-acceptance-bounds/divergence-floor-scaling.json, divergence-floor-scaling.png and corrected-criteria.json.

The mechanism. The terminal route is ForwardProfile._receipt into conservation_ledger(self.lattice, …), where the lattice is a FluxLattice whose central radial and vertical differences commute. divergence_j is therefore a discrete identity rather than an approximation, and what remains of it is a floating-point cancellation floor. The rejected 9.042e−4 bound had transferred 7.316e−5 from a separate non-commuting StencilMesh route: the arithmetic was correct and the operator was the wrong one.

A hypothesis recorded in the plan — that the acceptance quantity is a masked extremum whose regional cancellation beats a pointwise bound — was wrong and is retained here because it was the plausible reading before measurement.

Failability is demonstrated, not argued. A plausible one-part-per-million radial-current component scale mismatch at pitch 0.05 m drives the relative observable to 7.568e−8, failing the bound at 7.18×. The bound was derived from machine precision and selected before cohort inspection, so the 69/69 rescore at frozen widths 1 and 4 is a result rather than a construction.

Stated limits. 22.63× headroom is failable but not tight — it would not catch a twentyfold degradation — which is the accepted cost of a preregistered precision-derived scale over one fitted to the cohort it scores. And the −1.7203 interval, while excluding +2 decisively, admits both −1 and −2, so cancellation is established as the mechanism without the differencing order being pinned.

There is no hidden third instance of the category error. The distribution is effectively bimodal at 1.0× with a single outlier, and that outlier is conservation.divergence_j at 22.627416998× — the envelope this plan knowingly registered and recorded as failable but not tight. Thirty-two numeric envelopes sit at exactly 1.0× because their banked calibration observations reach the limiting component, which is as tight as a bound can be. conservation.divergence_b reproduces the 1.610865580× discriminating anchor, so the census is calibrated against a bound independently known to discriminate.

The flagged bound remains demonstrably failable, by a named perturbation rather than an assertion: multiplying the radial poloidal-current component by 1 + 1e−6 while leaving the vertical component unchanged reaches 7.568358363e−8, which is 7.182846291× the 1.053671213e−8 envelope.

A stated limitation rather than a silent one. Thirty-five criteria are exact-equality, where bound-over-largest-observed is zero over zero and indeterminate. These are reported as not assessable with the reason, not given an invented ratio, and none is dropped from the table; all remain failable by any one-representable-unit mismatch. Half the registry being unassessable by this metric is worth knowing — covering only the tractable thirty-four would have read as full coverage.

No bound was changed. Repair or reclassification of the single flag is left to a separately authorised node, since this plan's whole argument is that a bound must change kind for a stated reason rather than magnitude to fit a measurement.
