What this record shows

Every figure here is a poloidal panel of a terminal forward state on the Solov'ev certificate machine, drawn as line contours on shared physical levels with the analytic field in blue, the solved field in ochre, both null sets marked and the wall drawn, and every number is read from a persisted receipt named beside it. Read top to bottom it tells one story in four steps. The production chord clip leaves a localised current-attribution error in the boundary-cut cells on the low-field side (§1). Exact cut-cell moments remove nearly all of that error from the linear flux image (§2). Put into the solve, exact placement does not converge to the reference: it either stalls far from its fixed point or converges to a different equilibrium (§3). On the single-null case the solve is already exact and it is the topology read of the X-point cell that fails (§4). The cells the RCA node is drawing close up are named in §5 and its figures land there when the node reports.

§1 — The defect as it looks in production: a low-field-side patch on every limited row

The committed certificate solves the limited Solov'ev cases with the chord clip. At 300 cells the weak-rotation reactor case reproduces the analytic flux everywhere except a handful of boundary-cut cells on the outboard side, where the error appears as small closed structures that sit on cells rather than following any flux surface. The row is unqualified: it never converges (active-set settled at a terminal residual of 5.6e-3) and its axis error grows with resolution, 13.4 mm at 110 cells, 30.2 at 300, 52.4 at 500 and 62.7 at 1000, which a converging discretisation cannot do.

The census on the 500-cell weak row (forward GS contour evidence) put the mechanism in numbers: the outboard window R 7.25 to 7.75 m, Z 0 to 0.5 m holds 1.44 percent of nodes and 6.33 percent of squared flux error, a 4.39-fold enrichment, and the same patch appears on a rectangular tiling at matched resolution (enrichment 4.32), so the site is the clip-and-moment path the two tilings share and not the kernel or the tiling (docs/figures/uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling/solver-discriminator/error-maps.svg). The polygon-analytic kernel was exonerated separately: packed-to-scalar relative RMS 3e-11 to 1.2e-10 with no correlation of kernel error with cut cells.

§2 — Where the error comes from: the boundary-cut cells' current moments

The production clip does not clip. Two census nodes reading the same code on 2026-09-10 found that the committed profile support traces its clip against an all-ones flux and qualifies the result by the profile participation label (nova/equilibrium/forward_operator.py, _profile_support), so every cell is booked whole or not at all by which side of the separatrix its centroid falls on. That has been the case since commit c9a89e58 on 2026-08-27, which replaced a chord clip against the signed flux with the all-ones trace. Every measurement in this record is downstream of it: the 16-of-43 participation below is the count of cut cells with inside centroids; the single-null dominated cells are two-crossing cells booked whole; the X-point cell is booked whole; and on the transport machine 1073 A of scrape-off-layer current (0.196 percent of the plasma current) is 844 A in sixteen separatrix-crossing cells with outside centroids plus 228 A in fifteen fully open cells carrying the edge-extrapolated profile, none of it geometric clipping (receipt docs/figures/forward-solve-api/sol-ledger-census/sol-ledger-census.json, 157 cells). The exact-support mode on the held stack is therefore the first real clip in production since August, and its amplitude of 2.2 to 5.6 against 0.93 to 1.05 for whole-cell booking (§3) is a comparison against a baseline that was never clipping, not evidence that the exact clip loses current; the unit-amplitude census settles which of the two is right against the analytic total.

Unit-amplitude census (run r-20260910T052734465931, receipt docs/figures/uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling/amplitude-census/receipt.json). §4 — The single-null case: the solve is exact and the X-point is not read

The diverted rows use the Cerfon-Freidberg single-null reference with the plasma 0.836 minor radii clear of the machine axis, replacing the degenerate polynomial lens whose separatrix passed 49 mm from its own axis. Under the chord clip the solve reaches the analytic field: flux error at the 1e-17 level at 300 cells, converged. The joint qualification rule landed this session (residual, admitted topology read, position inside one pitch) then finds that no X-point is admitted at 110 or 300 cells, so only the 500-cell row qualifies jointly (axis error 1.43 mm, X-point error 1.20 mm against a 71 mm pitch bound), 1 of 4 diverted rows and 1 of 16 overall.

Where the read loses the X-point, stage by stage. The census assertion node (run r-20260910T053033936623, receipt and report under the session reports directory, panels under docs/figures/null-identification-authority/census-assertion) replayed the production fixed-design topology read on the persisted terminal flux of the three single-null rows, with machine cache keys matching the parts exactly and the 500-cell row as the positive control. The X-point is lost at the very first detector. The sign-change census tests each rotated centroid ring for four cyclic sign changes: at 110 cells the whole-grid crossing histogram is 0 once and 2 fifty-four times, and the three rings nearest the analytic saddle, 1.6 to 2.5 pitches away, read two; at 300 cells the histogram is 0 once and 2 one hundred and ninety-seven times, with the nearest ring 0.71 pitch from the saddle reading two; at 500 cells a single ring 0.23 pitch from the saddle reads four, and the same containment, polish, dedupe and type-gate stages admit it at 1.199 mm. So the ring test fires only when a centroid happens to sit within about a quarter pitch of the saddle; between centroids the saddle reads as a regular point. The solved flux is right on all three rows and every later stage is vacuous, never wrong.

The cell containing the saddle is the case the boundary-ring saddle-gap audit measured on the earlier reference (saddle-gap-current-check): the analytic separatrix crosses it four times, production clips it with one straight chord with endpoint-to-crossing displacement up to 14.95 mm, and the mishandling was bounded at about 0.2 A because Solov'ev current carries almost nothing in the throat. That bound held for confined current. It says nothing about the read, which is what fails here, and the mechanism recorded then as a prerequisite for the SOL closure (the saddle carried as a clip vertex, branch-paired chords, per-branch supports) is now cut-cell-current-attribution §2.

§5c — Gate B: the coupling on its own, with the oracle's currents frozen

No clip decision and no iteration enter. The single-null rows carry a coupling error of 12 and 23 percent of their span at 110 and 300 cells even with the anchor corrected, and the maximum grows with resolution rather than falling; the first-order moments are inert there. That is a separate defect from the static rows' smooth dipole error and belongs with the four-crossing X-point cell and the divertor legs of §3, where the exact chain returns no polygon and the moments of the cells around the saddle are not what the oracle integrates.

Three things are read from this table. The coupling's own error with exact currents is one to two percent of the span at 110 cells and falls by a factor of 2.4 to 300 cells, second order in the pitch. The error is a smooth field: a best-fit function affine in R and Z explains 97 to 99 percent of its rms on every row, with a radial coefficient of 0.078 Wb per metre on the weak row, so it is a broad gradient across the domain and not a boundary-cell effect. And the per-order breakdown is the finding: the second-order moments change nothing to four digits, and removing the first-order moments as well halves the rms error on every row while leaving the maximum nearly unchanged. Adding the dipole term of each cell's current to an exact monopole makes the image worse, which a correct moment expansion cannot do. Either the first-order coupling carries a sign or scale error, or the point the kernel expands about is not the point the moments are taken about. This is the compatibility-term defect the lead pointed at in the curvature terms, found one order lower. The discriminator node cca-first-order-coupling-discriminator tests it on a single cell against an exact Biot integral and on the weak row's frozen currents with the sign, the expansion point and the unit scale each varied.

The first-order term, decided (run r-20260910T225638998875, receipt and two panels under docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/first-order-coupling). On one interior hexagon of the weak mesh carrying a prescribed linear current density, the production coupling's zeroth-plus-first image reproduces the exact ring-inductance flux at every other node to 2.2e-13 Wb rms, against 6.5e-4 with the zeroth moment alone and 1.3e-3 with the first moment's sign flipped, exactly double; the kernel's expansion point and the moment reference coincide at the section centroid and no unit factor is missing. The kernel is right. On the weak row's frozen image the added first-order error is entirely the cut cells: first-order moments on interior cells alone give an rms over span of 0.0061 against the zeroth baseline of 0.0057, on cut cells alone 0.0101. A cut cell's physical first moment is converted to a density coefficient with the full atomic cell's area-normalised second moments and imaged with kernel blocks integrated over the full cell, while its current occupies only the clipped plasma region. Flipping the first moments removes the whole added error (0.0106 to 0.0051) because it happens to mimic the misplaced dipole; moving the expansion point to the region centroid recovers nothing (0.0106). So the coupling defect is the consistency item stated exactly: for a cut cell, the moment conversion and the kernel blocks must be taken over the clipped polygon, not the atomic cell.

The coupling, corrected (run r-20260911T001005914789, receipt and panel under docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/cut-cell-coupling, commit dbf3434f on the held line). With every cut cell's first moments re-referenced to its clipped polygon's centroid, inverted with the clipped polygon's second moments, and imaged through polygon-analytic kernel blocks integrated over the clipped polygon, rebuilt once per trip on the frozen support, the frozen-current image error over the span falls to 0.000266 on weak 110 (zeroth-only floor 0.00603), 0.000282 on moderate (0.00566), 0.000317 on strong (0.00573) and 0.000118 on weak 300 (0.00222): twenty to fifty times below the floor on every row, so the first-order term now removes error instead of adding it. A cut cell's image of a linear density matches the exact ring-inductance integral over its clipped polygon to 2e-11 rms. Gates A and B are met on the held line: the allocation is exact under the spline read, and the coupling with exact currents is exact to its second-order residual. What remains is gate C, the iteration.

The single-null rows first reported an error larger than their span (1.87 and 1.72 of the span at 110 and 300 cells) with no dependence on moment order. That was the gate's own anchor, not the coupling: the certificate clip cut at the read's unadmitted-saddle level of 0.0010 Wb, 21 percent of the span inside the analytic separatrix, so the certificate current excluded the outer shell of the core lobe (7234 A of 37173 A) and the gate reported the shell's field as error, correlating 0.9999 with it. Pinned to the analytic separatrix level, the diverted 110 error falls to 0.12 of the span; the read level itself is the §3 work.

No row improves on its committed counterpart, and the amplitude column says where the remaining defect is. On every static row the current amplitude at the cold seed is about two: the cold disc seed's own flux places half the cells outside the clip, while the partition labels that whole-cell booking used cover the whole target region, so the clip books half the plasma current and the normalisation doubles the profile before the first trip. The frozen support has removed yesterday's runaway (terminal amplitudes 1.7 to 3.8 against 9 to 18), but a doubled profile settles unconverged in the wrong place within a few trips. The single-null rows start near unity and run away to amplitudes above twenty, the four-crossing geometry of §3 acting inside the iteration. Gates A and B pass on this line, so the iteration's defect is the seed's compatibility with the clip: at the seed the clip and the labels disagree, and by the amplitude rule a seed amplitude of two means the seed support is booking the wrong current. The remedy belongs to the seeding step, a seed flux whose separatrix level reproduces the labelled region or a first trip booked by the labels with clipping from the second, both under the per-trip freeze.

Seed compatibility repaired, iteration still short (run r-20260911T061214771592, receipts under docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/cold-seed, commit 1e79e056 on the held line). Keeping every profile-owned cell in the seed's support brings the seed amplitude from about two to 1.012, 1.024 and 1.031 on the weak, moderate and strong 110-cell rows. The re-solved rows then stop unconverged after six to seven trips with terminal amplitudes 0.953 to 0.962, residuals 0.015, 0.008 and 0.010 against the committed 0.007, 0.012 and 0.014, and axis errors 69, 21 and 6 mm against 2, 3 and 3 mm: two rows better on residual, every row worse on position, none at the reference. The one-percent seed rule has never held at a cold seed, the committed rows themselves sat at 0.90 to 0.93, because the seed's own flux distorts the normalised-flux distribution over the labelled region by about five percent; meeting it strictly is a seed-construction change. With gates A and B passed and a compatible seed, what remains is the frozen-geometry fixed-point iteration itself stalling short of the reference: trip budget, geometry damping between trips and the seed construction are the levers, and they are a lead decision.
