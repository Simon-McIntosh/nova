What this shows

A converged free-boundary Grad–Shafranov solve on DIII-D shot d3d_shot_00000c4a7b frame 102 at 2200 ms, driven entirely by prescribed inputs — 24 poloidal conductor currents, flux functions extracted from the labelled map, and a prescribed total plasma current of 1,283,617.67 A — with zero label-fitted coefficients and zero current adjustments. The plasma current is imposed by exact profile-amplitude elimination, which removes the vacuum root from the map's range rather than penalising it.

The solve reached relative residual 1.5899788903681545e-09 in four iterations with terminal topology diverted and current relative error 0.0 against a 1e-12 tolerance, reproducing the landed prototype figure exactly on a CPU rerun.

The core agrees, and that is the real positive

Through the confined region the blue and red-dashed contours track each other closely — the nested surfaces around the magnetic axis near R ≈ 1.7 m are near-coincident on a level set neither map was allowed to rescale. That is the extracted p′ and FF′, the 24-conductor coil model and the pinned current all producing the right interior structure from prescribed inputs alone.

The two panels are also nearly indistinguishable, which is itself a result: the removed offset is −0.003 Wb/rad, negligible against the flux span. The disagreement below is therefore shape, not gauge — and that had to be checked, because a per-frame additive gauge has masked real disagreement elsewhere in this campaign.

The X point is 45 cm away, and the figure shows why that matters

Nova's X point (○) sits at the upper inboard corner — outside the limiter and outside the released grid — while the labelled X point (×) sits on the upper-left divertor leg where the machine actually diverts. The solve is nominally diverted and passes a topology-class test, but the saddle carrying that classification is not the divertor X point. It is a saddle in the external field far from the plasma.

This is the finding the receipt's numbers do not convey. Current pinning selected a diverted branch — decisively, against an unpinned control that ended limited at residual 0.514 after 24 iterations. But selecting the diverted class is not the same as selecting the labelled branch. The consequent boundary is a smaller, rounder shape that does not reach the true divertor leg, and the 0.0537 m LCFS separation understates that because it is a symmetric mean over a boundary whose disagreement is concentrated near the X point.

So the honest verdict on this frame: a converged, self-consistent, diverted equilibrium from prescribed inputs — and not the labelled one. At 0.2472 gauge-matched fractional RMS against a 0.0429 representability ceiling, it sits 5.8× the best any exact solver could do against these labels.

What it implies for the design

The figure makes the case that two mechanisms are complementary rather than redundant, which had been argued from numbers alone:

  • A current constraint selects the branch class. It is what moved this solve off the vacuum root, and it is indispensable where an external field already reads diverted-class so a topology pin cannot discriminate the two roots at all.
  • A topology constraint must pin the X point's location, not merely its existence. A saddle outside the vessel satisfying a diverted classification is exactly the loophole a class-only criterion leaves open. That belongs to the plan that owns topology pinning and hysteresis selection, and this frame is a concrete fixture for it.

Nothing here is a fit. The label is used to extract flux functions and to seed the branch — both declared instruments — and the prescribed current is an admissible input of the same class as the conductor currents, available in the competition input set or from a partner transport solve. The label was never a scoring target inside the solve, and the fractional RMS above is reported as a diagnostic rather than a criterion.
