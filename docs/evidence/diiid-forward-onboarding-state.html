The one-paragraph state

Everything that reads the machine works, and works to a precision better than the data deserves: geometry validated at sub-millimetre, topology extracted to a fraction of a grid cell, flux functions recovered at the labels' own consistency limit, and the plasma removable from a labelled map to a 0.48 % local floor. What does not yet work is reproducing a labelled equilibrium with a free-boundary solve — and as of the correction at the foot of this document, that is a solver problem rather than a data one. Adding the five recovered conductor currents cuts the diverted-branch residual 3.53× and holds the branch; the exact autodiff tangent then returns non-finite values in a neighbourhood that finite differences show to be smooth and full rank. Convention has been excluded across 16 variants. The onboarding recipe — the actual deliverable — is demonstrated end to end. The competition's second kill gate is not, and the honest reading is now that it is unfinished rather than unreachable.

Read §the correction before relying on anything below about the free-boundary root. Two earlier conclusions in this campaign — that no diverted root is findable, and that the shortfall is a property of the released dataset — were drawn from fixed-budget solves on an incomplete current set and are retracted there. The vacuum-forward, tare, extraction and geometry results are unaffected.

What the machine looks like, and what is missing from it

Read the red against the dotted box — it is the most important thing in this document. ECOILB is the tall inboard stack at R ≈ 0.75–0.90 m, entirely left of the grid's inner edge. E89UP and E89DN sit at |Z| ≈ 1.9–2.0 m, far above and below the grid's ±1.55 m. Only the E567 pair comes near it. The conductors we cannot see are mostly outside the domain we are asked to reproduce.

That geometry explains a result that had been merely numerical. A distant conductor's field across the grid is smooth and low-order — it can shift and tilt a map, not repair its local structure — and five such fields are close to mutually degenerate over that footprint, and degenerate with an additive gauge. So when the recovered currents were inserted into the free-boundary solve they clamped the residual to ≈0.31 rather than reducing it, collapsing a 25.4× spread to 2.5×. Well-conditioned as a fit (held-out R² 0.974–0.998), useless as a closure. The picture makes it obvious why.

The plasma comes out cleanly

This is what the 0.4842 % interior residual looks like. Replacing the node-centred point filament with exact zeroth, radial and vertical moments over the true clipped polygon improved that residual 7.3-fold. A consistency check worth keeping: on total current the two routes barely differ (recovered/recorded Ip 0.9957 against 0.9939), which is correct because total current is an integral insensitive to intra-cell distribution — the whole gain is local, which is exactly the quantity that matters next.

What is left over, and why it is not hardware

Two features in this figure settle the question, and they are spatial rather than statistical — which is why they matter.

First, the strong exterior patches sit at the grid boundary and are unchanged by taring. Compare the two columns: the top-left corner, the right edge near |Z| ≈ 1.0–1.3 m and the left-edge column are identical before and after the plasma is removed. Those are exactly the locations where a finite-difference Δ* stencil runs out of neighbours. Now compare them against the machine frame at the top of this document: none of them coincides with an omitted conductor — ECOILB is inboard of the grid, the E89 pair is beyond it vertically. Patches that do not move when the plasma leaves, and that sit on the stencil boundary rather than on hardware, are boundary artefacts.

Second, the tared interior is concentric rings, not noise. Flux-surface-aligned residual is the signature of a profile-representation error. Physical current, once the plasma has been removed, has no reason whatever to follow ψ contours.

Both readings agree with the magnitude accounting, which was arrived at independently: the labels' own strict-GS inconsistency generates 450,094 A·turn of apparent exterior current — 99.56 % of the 452,071 A·turn the patch search found — and that figure matches the separately measured 0.9968 irreducible non-GS residual share. Factor-two decimation then moved the total −37.6 % and the patch count 391 → 288, which physical current would not do.

Can we solve for the vacuum field?

Yes — and it is worth separating that question from the one that has been failing, because the plan has been treating one blocker as if it belonged to both.

A vacuum forward from known coil currents is well supported by what is already measured. The coil model reproduces exterior flux at per-frame median R² 0.99908 on the passed differencing gate. The geometry it drives is validated at sub-millimetre against an independent description. The plasma can be removed from any labelled map to a 0.48 % local floor, so a tared map is available per frame. Nothing in the chain from conductor geometry to exterior flux is in doubt.

What fails is different in kind: finding a free-boundary root that reproduces a labelled map. That needs the boundary condition the labels cannot supply to better than their own 4.3 % inconsistency, and §9 established the shortfall is not the five omitted conductors — inserting them, even recovered accurately and predictably, clamps the residual instead of reducing it. The machine frame above shows why in one look: those conductors are largely outside the grid, so their field over it is smooth and near-degenerate.

The practical consequence: producing vacuum flux maps for every slice is well-founded and should work. Recovering what the labels cannot specify is not. Those are separate problems and only the second is blocked.

The onboarding recipe — the actual deliverable

Beneath the competition, this plan was the first true second-machine onboarding and the direct test of nova's machine-portability claim. That test passed, and it is worth stating separately from the gate that did not.

From a shipped description to receipts-bearing forwards, every step is demonstrated: read the conductor set through the machine dataclass seam and corroborate it against an independent source; measure and pin the convention once; verify the coil model against labelled exterior flux; extract topology, flux functions and current density from a given map; remove the plasma exactly; publish the result as a digest-pinned, DD-version-enforced artifact resolvable from a registry. Each of those has a receipt and a bar fixed before scoring. The recipe is reusable, and DIII-D was its first instance rather than its only one.

Correction — the no-root finding was the wrong diagnosis (2026-08-21)

The state above said the free-boundary root was blocked by a data limitation. That was wrong, and the owner's two objections were both better founded than the answer they got. Two measurements settle it. Commits a1858f15 (convention sweep) and e4fb5249/3bd78213 (full-current root find).

Convention is exhaustively excluded

A COCOS artefact was the right thing to suspect, because the two validations the pin rested on — the gauge-free differencing gate and the label-boundary fixed solve — are both blind to the degree of freedom a free-boundary root needs. So the residual was re-evaluated linearly at the labelled state, no root find, across 16 variants declared before scoring: source COCOS 1–8 and 11–18, jointly spanning both poloidal-flux signs, both toroidal-current signs, both flux-derivative signs, and total-versus-per-radian. 20 frames × 16 variants = 320 records.

The pinned COCOS 5 control reproduced the landed free 0.21565, fixed 0.04058 and vacuum share 0.84986 to 3.2e-8, 3.6e-8 and 1.1e-7. No variant reached the preregistered gate of 1.05× the fixed level; the best free residual stayed at 0.21565, which is 5.31× the fixed level. Convention is not the explanation.

And the obstacle is two solver defects, not the data

Nothing is budget-limited. The 10×-budget host Newton–Krylov route converged — relative residual 7.75e-11 in 89 accepted iterations and 457 map evaluations. It did not run out of iterations. But it crossed topology at iterations 31, 50 and 88 and terminated limited, with no finite X point.

Two defects, both solver-side. The accelerator cannot leave its diverted plateau because its exact tangent is unusable and GMRES therefore returns a zero step. The only route that converges has no topology-preserving globalisation. Neither is a property of the released data.

What this changes. The §5 exemption's reasoning — no admissible current source exists, therefore no solver work can help — is wrong on both clauses: the recovered currents are admissible and inference-predictable at held-out R² 0.974–0.998, and solver work is exactly what is needed. f-dfo-007, which this orchestrator resolved as superseded, is reopened as f-dfo-014; the measurement that justified closing it was itself run at fixed budget on incomplete currents. What remains genuinely unestablished is a state simultaneously below 1e-6 and diverted — now a solver target, not a data limitation.

The entries above this correction that concern the vacuum forward, the tare, the extraction and the geometry stand unchanged. What is retracted is the inference that the free-boundary shortfall was a property of the dataset.
