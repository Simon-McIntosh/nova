Forward GS contour evidence

The retained certificate is explicitly non-converged evidence. Every map uses unfilled poloidal-flux contours; a compared solved/reference pair receives one shared physical level array, so a mismatch cannot be hidden by independent colour scales. The wall, analytic boundary and containment-aware critical-point read stay on each frame.

How to read every panel

Blue contours are the analytic Solovev field and ochre contours are the terminal forward field, drawn at the same absolute flux levels. The red loop is the analytic separatrix and the dark vessel outline is the typed wall geometry. A solid triangle marks the magnetic axis and a filled cross an admitted saddle; containment is passed to the null painter, so a finite point outside the vessel is not allowed to impersonate an in-vessel X-point. The marker vocabulary matches the poloidal convergence atlas: solid triangle = magnetic axis; filled cross = admitted saddle; hollow circle = another qualified X-point inside the wall; hollow square = qualified X-point outside the wall.

A wrong answer is visible without a residual calculation: a red boundary that does not follow the terminal separatrix, a marker displaced from the corresponding reference critical region, a finite X marker against or beyond the vessel, or contour families with kinks, extra islands, or an implausible compression are each a topology or field-shape failure. A small scalar residual alone cannot exclude any of them.

Retained Solovev certificate

The magnetic-axis error grows under refinement: on the exact-oracle weak-rotation case it is 13.352807 mm at 110 requested cells and 30.247708 mm at 300. A convergent spatial and nonlinear solve would instead drive that position error toward zero as resolution increases; growth is stronger evidence of a wrong terminal state than either residual in isolation. The 500- and 1000-cell replays continue the trend at 52.413422 mm and 62.655666 mm.

The retained H200 receipt job 1267132 gives six terminal states. It records every one as converged = false; the maps are therefore diagnostic terminal fields, not promoted accuracy evidence. Each replayed contour uses the retained terminal residual below. All retained rungs are listed so no non-converged row is silently omitted while its individual contour render completes.

The analytic contours are closed rings on a widened grid. The analytic field is drawn on a grid spanning the closed-form boundary and the wall rather than the plasma-node extent, so its zero-flux loop is a closed curve on every static rung: the measured endpoint gap of the extracted boundary-flux contour is 0.0 m on all six. On a grid truncated to the plasma nodes the loop stops at the grid edge with gaps of 0.53–1.94 m, which reads as an open analytic separatrix — a render artifact, not physics.

Where the weak-rotation error lives

The apparent outboard-midplane feature is enriched, but it is not the whole error. On the 500-cell weak-rotation row, the window R = 7.25–7.75 m and Z = 0–0.5 m contains 1.4414% of sampled nodes and 6.3256% of squared flux error, a 4.39-fold concentration over uniform support. Yet the largest-error 5.045% of nodes carry only 14.1096% of squared error, and 78.1982% of nodes are required to accumulate 90% of it. The numerical census therefore rejects the visual hypothesis that almost all error lives in one narrow outboard patch.

Boundary-cut hexagonal supports

These figures are deliberately coupled to unmerged revision 7cc2e06f518bdd0a7b8806983470f972659df4ec. They are rendered read-only from the held nia-clipped-moment-repair worktree and written here; neither that worktree nor Nova source was modified. The refusal that rejects an unqualified topology trial, and the clipping repair that produced these measurements, remain unmerged pending the lead decision about how iteration proceeds when a trial cannot provide an axis-normalised current. The two otherwise available choices are both known-bad: consuming the unqualified axis produces the NaN source amplitude reproduced on main by job 1267281, while carrying the previous qualified axis silently corrupted three certificate rungs. That main failure is therefore not a renderer fault; it faithfully exposes the defect the held work repairs.

The held receipt establishes why the geometry matters: every boundary-cut cell agrees on both physical current and centroid—8 of 8 on the diverted 110 rung and 20 of 20 on 342—with net-current relative error exactly zero on both. The coloured cell in each panel is the straight-edged support handed to draw_plasma_cells; the complete hexagon is only a grey provenance outline. The analytic separatrix is drawn across the same cells. A disagreement would appear as the blue physical-moment centroid outside its own clipped cell or on the wrong side of the separatrix, rather than coincident with the black direct-clipped centroid.

The solid triangle in each clip panel marks the solved terminal magnetic axis — the topology read of the non-converged terminal field. The renderer now labels this marker as solved terminal axis in the panel legend; these figures are pinned to the unmerged clip repair, so they carry that label when they are re-rendered after the repair lands. The marker uses the converged-axis vocabulary but is the solver's terminal position here, not a promoted equilibrium axis; the strict terminal residual given in each caption is the companion qualification.

The calculation is intentionally mixed order. Each crossing on an atomic edge is a linear interpolation of flux on that edge, and the support is the polygon through those crossings. Area and current moments are analytic for that polygon and exact for cellwise-linear current density; observation.clipped_support_quadrature then fans the support into triangles under degree-fifteen Duffy quadrature. Thus the integration degree is fifteen while the boundary representation is first order. The plotted clip stays straight because that is what is computed, and the separately traced analytic separatrix makes the geometric discrepancy visible.

The boundary representation is quantitatively comparable and large on this coarse rung. Summed absolute straight-versus-traced area difference is 3.7246194e-2 m², or 0.305716 of the full areas of the eight cut cells. That fraction is 24.54 times the largest retained certificate residual and 259.85 times the measured 1.176525e-3 operator-composition floor. These observables are not dimensionally identical error norms, so the ratios do not assign causation; they do show that first-order boundary geometry is too large to dismiss beside the solver-scale numbers.

Affine flux-function round trip

The retained analytic-raster result recovers constant p-prime at binary64 noise. FF-prime is not exact: it has a uniform second-order radial-stencil bias while bin-to-bin scatter stays at roundoff. Its worst finest relative sup error is 8.851872538089174e-5 over two cases, three lattices and 24 bins with zero exclusions, below the measured operator-composition floor 1.1765249977300346e-3 of span at 67 by 103. The route has a structural limitation rather than a failed numerical claim: extraction requires a rectangular flux lattice, while the forward Solovev receipts are unstructured hex maps and are explicitly unavailable as non-tensor-product input. No hex-to-raster interpolation is being inferred here.
