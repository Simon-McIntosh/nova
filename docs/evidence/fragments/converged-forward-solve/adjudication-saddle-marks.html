The same point was labelled “adapter A saddle”. A hyperbolic Hessian at a point with that nonzero gradient does not make the point a saddle. Both MAST-U magnetics contacts likewise have |∇ψ| about 0.09834 and zero distance to the wall.

Rule. Evaluate ψ, ∇ψ and the symmetric Hessian on each trace's own not-a-knot bicubic spline, at the exact plotted coordinate without polishing it or moving it to a census null. Set Δψ=max(ψ grid)−min(ψ grid), the span of the panel's stored contour levels, and L=max(radial grid extent, vertical grid extent). The dimensionless gradient is G=|∇ψ|L/Δψ. A stationary saddle requires G≤10⁻⁹ and λ₋<0<λ₊. Otherwise a point within 10⁻⁹L of any actual wall segment is a wall contact; otherwise it is neither. The distance uses segment projections, not nearest vertices or an invented link between wall loops. A stationary saddle takes precedence if it lies on a wall. This is geometric classification, not certification of axis connectivity or of the selected boundary level.

The positive control is the six stationary saddles, whose G values span 6.32×10⁻¹⁶ to 3.26×10⁻¹⁴, with wall distances 0.0913–0.4011 m. The eight contacts have G from 0.00780765 to 4.78253 and wall distances at most 4.82×10⁻¹⁶ m. Thus no classification sits near either tolerance. All 14 Hessians have opposite-sign eigenvalues: the gradient check is essential.
