A two-ulp defect was found and fixed; the fidelity failure is not it. The 282-cell diverted sweep receipt at offset 0.0 showed base reporting wall_candidate = −1.0823032759226733 × 10−9, exactly equal to its saddle_candidate, against a head wall_candidate of −1.0823032759224086 × 10−9 — the wall contact winning the boundary by 2.6 × 10−22 on a test that ties at base. The source was mechanical: ceiling − leak * over computes ceiling − (ceiling − saddle_score), which in binary64 returns the saddle level only to a few ulp, where the base's jnp.minimum(ceiling, saddle_score) is exact. The attenuation is now a min against the interpolated level, and the passage through the cell owning the X-point is bounded by the located saddle level. After the fix the diverted centre reads boundary_class = 1 with wall_candidate bit-identical to base, and the limited carrier is unchanged to the last printed digit. The diverted map error did not move: 0.411603569216 at 282 cells and 0.335454581616 at 550, with support 184 and 346 against base 179 and 337. The error is carried by the five and nine extra cells the head's membership rule admits, which is the one open defect.

Earlier hypothesis, since ruled out: arm/ceiling_diff.py loads both kernels into one process and records each module's own ceiling on the identical 282-cell diverted build. At the centre offset the two ceilings agree to roundoff on 281 of 282 cells (the one difference is 5.7 × 10−22), both arms read boundary_class = 1 with no wall and no saddle candidate, and the selected set differs by one cell (179 base against 180 head), where the map row's own centre_support reads 179 against 184. So the attenuation is not the cause, and the map rows and the sweeps reach different readings at the same nominal centre; reconciling the two builds is the next step. The head must not land.
