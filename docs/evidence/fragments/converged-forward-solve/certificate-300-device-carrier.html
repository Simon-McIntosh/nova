Diverted 300-cell certificate carrier: device divergence and CPU Krylov refusal

Both arms were built in fresh processes from one git archive of revision 0f8ce37fe, dumping every construction stage to npz. The CPU arm ran in place on the fleet allocation, the H200 arm as one betelgeuse job under gpu_0003_grpA on one card (NOVA_REVISION in that job's header reads the earlier label acaffe5d3 because the launcher script hard-codes it; the tree it ran is the same archive, which is why its s0_wall.npz carries the reference arrays added at 0f8ce37fe). The same benchmark route as the trip-cap record was used: benchmarks/solovev_certificate.py::_measure("diverted-single-null", -300), replayed stage by stage by scripts/certificate_300_carrier/dump_carrier_stages.py.

The first differing stage is the analytic reference, not the carrier

The comparison names s0_wall as the first stage where the two devices disagree, and the disagreement is already present in the analytic reference those arrays are derived from. Each stage's worst per-array delta:

The mechanism, in order:

  1. Reference coefficients. The single-null reference solves a 12×12 host linear system for its basis coefficients (nova/equilibrium/analytic_single_null.py:412, np.linalg.solve(np.stack(rows), …)), and refines the magnetic axis with three Newton steps whose each iteration is another np.linalg.solve (:302). Run in two processes on two nodes these LAPACK calls return last-bit-different results: the 12 coefficients differ by at most 8.216e-15 absolute, and the magnetic axis by 3.421e-15 m. That is the only device-dependent step in the whole carrier build — every stage after it is numpy/scipy on the host.
  2. Amplification to the wall. The coefficient delta reaches the separatrix at 2.049e-13 m and the buffered, arc-length-sampled wall at 4.268e-12 m (polygon.buffer(clearance, quad_segs=64) then loop.interpolate).
  3. CoilSet ordering upstream of the hex packing is then different: the same 340 cell centres to 7.361e-13 m but a different order (slot delta up to 1.109 m).
  4. The quantised vertex merge makes the 11. MomentGeometry.from_cells receives sampling = centres[:,None,:] + offsets[None,:,:] — 340×6 = 2040 vertices — and merges them through a bin key, not an equality test: key = tuple(np.rint(vertex / atomic.tolerance)) (nova/equilibrium/stencil_mesh.py:395, the same form as separatrix_clip._point_key at nova/equilibrium/separatrix_clip.py:139). The tolerance is 128 · eps · scale (stencil_mesh.py:349), 1.0351e-13 m on both devices. A shared hex corner is reconstructed twice, as centre_i + offset_j and centre_k + offset_l, and the two sums differ by one ulp — measured 8.88e-16 m, about 1/117 of a bin. Two reconstructions merge when they fall in one bin, so the merge survives a device change only if the pair is not near a bin edge. The reference drift (7.4e-13 m in the centres, seven bins wide) resamples that phase between devices.

Measured on the two stage dumps (scripts/certificate_300_carrier/bin_census.py, report bin-census.json): the H200 arm holds 11 pairs of sample coordinates closer than a micron, and in every one of the 11 the two endpoints occupy adjacent bins — e.g. index 284 and index 291, both on the line Z bin -4495549544932, one at R bin 20820480132490 and the other at 20820480132491. Under the CPU arm's tolerance those same two endpoints land in one bin. The CPU arm itself has zero such pairs. So the 11 extra coordinates are one-ulp splits that straddle a bin edge on one device and not the other, which is why CPU merges to 1075 unique sample coordinates and H200 to 1086 (atomic mesh 923 vs 937).

The point comparison additionally reproduces the earlier 4.274e-12 m wall figure exactly, and explains the unreproducible 7.4e-13 m cell claim: the cells agree to 7.361e-13 m under nearest-neighbour matching, but they are not in the same order, so a slot-wise comparison reports 1.109 m.

The CPU Krylov action: one cell's moment derivative

Reconstructing the solver's first linear action at the CPU seed (scripts/certificate_300_carrier/trace_krylov_action.py): linear_action(v) = v - tangent(v) with tangent the directional derivative of the write-then-read map at the seed. The map value and the residual vector are fully finite (0 non-finite of 1536); the derivative is NaN in 1518 of 1536 entries, which is the qualification the solver reports as nonfinite_linear_action.

Cell 103 is an ordinary complete hexagon — centre (R, Z) = (1.52920834, -1.05661527) m, section hexagon, six authored vertices, area 8.410701056134584e-3 m² against a grid median of 8.410701056134595e-3 m² (ratio 0.9999999999999988), and it is flagged regular — so the singularity is not a degenerate or clipped cell. The census behind that statement is cell-103-census.json under the run directory, written by scripts/certificate_300_carrier/cell_census.py: section hexagon, authored_polygon_is_hexagon true, regular_flag true, area z-score 0.428, area rank 244 of 340 descending, and a full seven-entry stencil row. That rank is arbitrary inside a tie: counting the stage dump (cpu-stages/s2_polygons.npz, the source the census reads), 231 of the 340 cells sit within 1e-15 relative of the median area and 210 of those are exactly equal to it, so the rank reports a position inside a group the sort can order either way.

The spread is a second defect, not the same one: current_normalisation_amplitude (:3627) returns jnp.where(admissible, target / safe_unscaled, jnp.nan) (:3647). One non-finite cell poisons the total, the total poisons the amplitude, and scaled_current_moments (:3619) multiplies every cell by that one amplitude — so a single-cell singularity is amplified into a uniform NaN, which the Krylov qualification then reports as the whole linear action being non-finite.

Tied to the carrier divergence or independent? Independent of the 11-coordinate difference. Cell 103 exists on both devices with the same six-vertex geometry (the grid is 340 cells on both, and the 11 coordinates are sample coordinates, not grid cells), and the CPU's non-finite action is produced entirely within the CPU arm's own carrier. The two defects share only the reference solve, which is not part of the derivative path.

Proposed repair sites (named, not implemented)

  1. Identify a lattice vertex by its topology, not its coordinate. The merge at stencil_mesh.py:395 already quantises, so a coarser bin does not repair it: any bin splits a vertex that lies on its edge, and the measured split is one ulp against a bin seven times narrower than the reference drift, so the pair straddles the edge on one device and not the other. The identification has to stop depending on the float coordinate at all. Every sample vertex is generated as centres[c] + offsets[k] (scripts/analytic_oracle_fixtures/measure.py:668; the production path is PlasmaGrid._preclip_sampling_vertices in nova/biot/plasmagrid.py), so a vertex's identity is (cell, slot) reduced through the hex lattice's own adjacency — which the caller already holds, because MomentGeometry.from_cells receives mesh.stencil and that stencil is PlasmaGrid.loop_neighbour_vertices's output (defined at nova/biot/plasmagrid.py:361, invoked by PlasmaGrid.tessellate at :410). For each neighbouring pair, the shared corner is the slot whose offset best matches half the centre displacement; that choice is made among six offsets about 5.7e-2 m apart, so it has no bin edge to straddle. The repair carries that canonical id from the generator into MomentGeometry.from_cells, which merges on the id and uses the coordinate only as the representative value of a merged vertex. Pinning test: build the carrier from the reference solve on CPU and on H200, and again from a wall perturbed by 4e-12 m, and require identical node_number and identical vertex ids across all three.
  2. Reference-solve determinism. nova/equilibrium/analytic_single_null.py:412 and :302 should solve through one repository-owned deterministic routine (a fixed-order Gaussian elimination or a pinned LAPACK driver with a declared thread count), and the receipt should carry the coefficient vector's digest. Pinning test: solve the reference twice with different BLAS thread counts and require bit identity of coefficients, magnetic_axis and separatrix.
  3. Per-cell qualification before the sum. In forward_operator.py:3509 _partitioned_current_moments / :3607 cell_current_moments, qualify each cell's moment derivative and report the offending cell index in a receipt instead of letting one cell's NaN enter the total silently.
  4. Finite sentinel instead of a NaN amplitude. In current_normalisation_amplitude (:3627). Return a finite value with a separate admissibility flag (as the eager branch already does by raising), so scaled_current_moments at :3619 cannot turn one non-finite moment into a uniform NaN across all 340 cells.

Negative control

The comparator was falsified by planting a known defect into a copy of one device's stage arrays and comparing that copy against the unplanted dump of the same device — the only pairing in which a stage other than s0_wall could be the first to differ. Two defects were planted, one per run: reversing the per-cell sampling-vertex order, and appending one duplicated sampling coordinate row. In both runs the comparison reports exactly one differing stage, FIRST_DIFFERING_STAGE=s6_sampling, which is the stage the defect was planted in, and exits 0. The logs are negative-control-reorder.log and negative-control-duplicate.log under the run directory; each carries its literal command at the head and ends with EXIT=0.
