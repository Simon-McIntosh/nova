Tangent compile rows measured by child CPU seconds

The compile rows timed each fresh process by wall, which moves with unrelated load. The shared harness tests/biot/tangent_identity.py now measures each fresh compile process by the CPU seconds it spends inside lowered.compile(), taken with time.process_time() around exactly that call and reported by the child. compile_ratio asserts the 3.0 bound on the median of seven CPU medians, with no absolute floor. The wall and the whole child's CPU, from getrusage(RUSAGE_CHILDREN) deltas around the spawn, are printed beside every arm. The whole-child CPU is recorded but deliberately not the asserted measure: a fresh child spends four to six CPU seconds importing the interpreter and the module before compiling anything, a constant that would collapse the two arms toward one another and let the row measure the interpreter rather than the program.

Four passes over ten rows

Two 59-minute all_debug jobs ran the same ten cases: job A two sequential passes, job B two passes with eight CPU-bound spinner processes on the same allocation, each spinner started and stopped by its own pid. Each arm is the median of seven fresh, cache-disabled processes; eq is the expanded equation count of the primal and of the tangent; the cpu and wall columns are the first sequential pass; ratio is the tangent median CPU over the primal median CPU, the asserted quantity, given for the sequential passes (1, 2) then the loaded passes (1, 2). Each pass ran ten rows in 13 to 14 minutes: 8 passed, 2 failed, 93 deselected. The bound is 3.0 and is unchanged, as is the median of seven.

The CPU measure is stable under load and the wall measure is not. Every row moves by at most 0.2 in ratio between a sequential pass and a loaded pass, while the wall seconds of the same rows rise by about a third under the spinners: complete_pole tangent wall 0.509 s sequential against 0.712 s loaded, product 0.287 against 0.355, graded_residual 0.531 against 0.589. The whole-child CPU stays between 2.7 s and 6.4 s in every row, the import cost the asserted measure excludes.

Two rows exceed the 3.0 bound by CPU seconds in all four passes and are left red, not loosened. finite_part (test_elliptic_tangent_recurrences.py) grows from 69 to 243 expanded equations and its median compile CPU from 0.044 s to 0.202 s, 4.59 sequential and 3.14 loaded; its primal arm is the smallest compile in the set and the measure's own resolution is a large fraction of that 0.044 s. graded_residual (test_vertex_edge_tangent_recurrences.py) grows from 372 to 954 equations and from 0.321 s to 1.435 s, 4.47 sequential and 3.48 loaded. pytest reports both as failing test ids, test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals[finite_part] and test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals[graded_residual], in all four pass logs.

Until this change the bound was asserted on wall seconds, and the pass logs carry both measures for the same seven-process medians, so the two can be compared on identical runs. By wall, graded_residual already exceeded the bound: 0.140 s primal against 0.531 s tangent, a ratio of 3.79. By wall, finite_part was under it: 0.043 s against 0.099 s, a ratio of 2.30, and by CPU it is over at 4.59. finite_part is therefore the one row whose verdict this change moves, and graded_residual was red before it. Both figures are medians of seven in the unloaded first sequential pass.

A compile row spawns twenty-one fresh processes, so the repository's 300 s per-test hang guard cut the row off before it reached its assertion on a busy allocation. test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals in each of the three tangent modules now carries its own @pytest.mark.timeout(1800); every other test keeps the default timeout, and the tangent-over-primal bound stays 3.0. With that bound the four passes complete.

The declared mutation NOVA_TANGENT_COMPILE_HEAVY=1 compiles the tagged tangent program twice and sums it, so it is the only difference from a gate pass. Under the mutation the tangent program of graded_residual grows from 954 to 1910 expanded equations, its median compile CPU rises to 2.478 s against a 0.615 s primal, a ratio of 4.03, and pytest fails test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals[graded_residual] (EXIT=1, negative-control-graded-residual.log). The control bites, but that row already exceeds the bound without the mutation on this allocation, so it does not discriminate; the control is re-run on complete_pole and contract, both of which pass all four gate passes here, in negative-control.log.

The tests/biot delta against the orientation revision 72112421c runs from one driver, modules one after another, base arm then after arm, through jobC-delta.sh. The base arm's first attempt failed at collection because the harness resolves its base module with git show against the tree root and a git archive tree carries no object store; delta-base-collection-error.log records that and the corrected run points the extracted tree at the checkout's git directory. Both arms run longer than the 59-minute job wall allows, so both logs are partial: delta-base.log and delta-after.log.
