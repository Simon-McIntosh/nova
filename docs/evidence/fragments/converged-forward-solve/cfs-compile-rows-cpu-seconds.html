Tangent compile rows measured by child CPU seconds

The compile rows timed each fresh process by wall, which moves with unrelated load. The shared harness tests/biot/tangent_identity.py now measures each fresh compile process by the CPU seconds it spends inside lowered.compile(), taken with time.process_time() around exactly that call and reported by the child. compile_ratio asserts the 3.0 bound on the median of seven CPU medians, with no absolute floor. The wall and the whole child's CPU (from getrusage(RUSAGE_CHILDREN) deltas around the spawn) are printed beside every arm. The whole-child CPU is recorded but deliberately not the asserted measure: a fresh child spends about five CPU seconds importing the interpreter and the module before compiling anything, a constant that would collapse the two arms toward one another and let the row measure the interpreter rather than the program.

Two 59-minute all_debug jobs ran the same ten cases: job A two sequential passes, job B two passes with eight CPU-bound spinner processes on the same allocation, each spinner started and stopped by its own pid. Both jobs completed clean, in 28 minutes each.

Two rows fail the bound on the CPU measure in all four passes, so the failure is not a load artefact: test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals[finite_part] at 4.59 and 4.67 sequential and 3.14 and 3.22 loaded, and test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals[graded_residual] at 4.47 and 4.37 sequential and 3.48 and 3.48 loaded. Both are reproducible rather than marginal, and both are compile cost growing faster than the equation count. finite_part takes its equations from 69 to 243, a factor of 3.5, and its compile CPU from 0.044 s to 0.202 s, a factor of 4.6; its primal compiles in 44 ms, so the bound is asked of a measurement whose denominator is nearly free. graded_residual takes its equations from 372 to 954 and its CPU from 0.321 s to 1.435 s. contract is the nearest passing row, at 2.78 to 2.89.

The CPU measure is far steadier than wall when the CPU figure is substantial: the ratio for the eight passing rows stays within 0.12 of itself across the two loads, while their wall medians move by 30 to 40 percent. On complete_pole the tangent wall rises from 0.509 s to 0.712 s while its CPU rises only from 1.263 s to 1.445 s. It is not perfectly load-invariant where the CPU figure is small: finite_part's primal rises from 0.044 s to 0.070 s between the two loads, which alone moves its ratio from 4.59 to 3.14. Printing both is what makes that visible rather than hiding it behind one number.

The negative control NOVA_TANGENT_COMPILE_HEAVY=1 compiles the tangent program twice and sums it. On graded_residual the tangent grows from 954 to 1910 expanded equations and its median compile CPU rises to 2.478 s against a 0.615 s primal, a ratio of 4.03, so the row fails on the CPU measure; pytest reports test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals[graded_residual] in negative-control.log with EXIT=1. The control is what shows the assertion is live rather than satisfied by construction.

A compile row spawns twenty-one fresh processes, so the repository's 300 s per-test hang guard cut rows off before their assertion on a busy allocation. test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals in each of the three tangent modules now carries its own @pytest.mark.timeout(1800); every other test keeps the timeout it had, and the tangent-over-primal bound is untouched at 3.0. The four passes ran as two all_debug jobs, each with TMPDIR=/tmp and JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu and the shared interpreter run directly, never uv. No case came near the new bound; the longest took about five minutes.

The tests/biot delta against orientation_base_sha is the one part of the gate still open, and it is open on the harness rather than on the code. The base revision was extracted with git archive into a scratch tree under TMPDIR, and the tangent modules' _base_module() then could not resolve git show fd44f630:nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py, because an extracted tree is not a repository. Giving that tree the worktree's object store through alternates needs git init in it, and the run's write fence refuses a mutating git verb aimed outside the run's own worktree, so the delta's base arm could not be built in this attempt.
