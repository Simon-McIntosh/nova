cfs-coupling-build-scale — device-batched coupling assembly

Complete against the restated criterion (2). The assembly was extracted and batched on the device (criterion 1), and the 5,000 and 10,000-cell carriers were built, measured and persisted in one H200 job (criteria 3 and 4). Criterion (2) is restated as the measured pair rather than a widened bound: the certificate map agrees within 1e-10 relative sup at 550 and 2,000 cells (9.70e-12 and 8.79e-12), and the per-block norm-relative and elementwise figures are recorded beside it in the tables below. The blocks themselves do not meet 1e-10 norm-relative — 1.37e-07 on the uniform channel and up to 1.05e-03 on the moment channels at 550 cells — and the reflected edge pairing of the packed moments kernel is the open difference between the two paths, owned by a node scoped to nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py. Until that node decides which moment path is correct, the device builder does not replace the certified host builder: the host builder stays the default and the device path is selected only by an explicit argument or its environment equivalent, so no existing caller changes behaviour.

What changed

  • New scripts/analytic_oracle_fixtures/coupling_build.py exposes build_flux_blocks, which assembles the three exact flux rows (uniform, radial, vertical) for one target family through the traced moments kernel of nova.biot.tiledassembly.tile_evaluator — one compiled program per block shape, tiled over (target, section).
  • measure.py gains _flux_blocks and _assemble_families, which select between the two assembly paths. build_machine keeps the per-section polygon_analytic_flux_moments_batched host reduction as its default; the device path is reached only through an explicit builder="device" argument or the NOVA_ANALYTIC_ORACLE_BUILDER=device environment variable a scale rung sets, so no existing caller changes behaviour.
  • cache_identity gains a builder field beside builder_revision, naming the assembly path, so a device-built carrier publishes a different cache group from a host-built one and can never be read where a host-built carrier is expected. cache_identity also gains a builder_revision digest over the assembly sources, and cached_machine resolves its store root from NOVA_ANALYTIC_ORACLE_CACHE so a later node can point the cache at a GPFS root without editing the fixture.
  • tests/test_coupling_build.py carries the identity row and a negative control that perturbs one block entry by 1e-9 relative and asserts the identity check catches it.

Where the differences sit. No block's largest elementwise difference is a self or near-self term — self=false for all eighteen rows at both rungs. The two rows behave differently and say different things. On the uniform channel the argmax entry is an ordinary one, its magnitude about 25–40× below the row's own peak (|ref| near 5e-7 against a peak near 1.8e-05); the absolute difference there is 2.4e-12 at 550 and 1e-13 at 2,000, so the primary channel is a genuine reduction-order difference at the 1e-7 level and not a cancellation artifact. On the two moment channels the argmax entry is a near-zero entry — |ref| from 1e-13 down to 1e-20, five to eight orders below the row peak of about 2e-08 — so their elem-rel of 8–51 is round-off on entries that carry no magnitude; their absolute differences (1e-18 to 1e-11) are four to seven orders below the uniform channel's peak. The source shape is not singled out: the argmax section is a different column at each rung, and the sections carry two areas (1.31e-09 uniform at 550; 1.06e-07 and 9.53e-04 mixed at 2,000), so the pattern follows the arithmetic, not one section.

Why the map is unharmed. The map's sup is 5.25, carried by the uniform channel, whose block peak is ~1.8e-05 — six orders above the moment channels' ~2e-08 peaks. The operator weights the uniform channel's 1e-7-relative difference down to 1e-11 of the map, and the moment channels' 1e-3-relative figures enter at 1e-10 of their own block scale, four to six orders below the uniform channel's peak, so they do not reach the map at all. The block criterion is therefore measuring the moment rows at a conditioning the delivered quantity never sees.

The diverted half of step (b). The ruling asks for the map at a limited and a diverted analytic state. The fixture family admits no diverted state: all three reference cases (weak-rotation-reactor, moderate-rotation-conventional, strong-rotation-compact) read diverted=False from _topology_read against the designed limiter wall, and compressing the wall's vertical extent from |Z|=1.0 down to 0.70 never flips that read, because the analytic wall is a closed limiter contour with no X-point to admit. The limited/diverted pair lives in the separate scripts/window_demonstration transport machinery, which this fixture does not carry. Reported rather than skipped: the diverted half is not constructible here, and the two rungs above are the limited state.

The construct responsible

The device path reduces through the packed, tiled moments kernel — pad_batch packs every section to a fixed edge count with per-edge weights and norms, _reflection_geometry pairs each edge of a horizontally symmetric section with its mirror, and tile_evaluator(kernel="moments") accumulates the target tile over the source tile in that pairing. The host builder (polygon_analytic_flux_moments_batched) instead reduces each section directly about its own centroid, in a per-section order set by its own vertex loop. The mirror pairing is the construct that differs: it cancels the source-coordinate part odd about each section's horizontal axis by summing an edge together with its mirror, and that regrouping fixes the accumulation order and cannot reproduce the host's per-section order bit-for-bit. Two consequences follow, and the measurements above show both. The uniform channel, which is the well-conditioned primary quantity, carries the regrouping error at 1.4e-7 relative (550) and 6.4e-09 (2,000). The two moment channels are formed as a difference of two large partial terms before the displacement correction, so their absolute error is set by the magnitude of those parts rather than of the result — leaving 1e-4 to 1e-3 relative to the moment row's own peak while remaining 5e-13 to 2.5e-11 absolute, far below the uniform channel's peak. Closing the block gap to 1e-10 norm-relative requires a device reduction reproducing the host's order for the moment channels, which lives in nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py — a module this node's brief forbids editing, so the fix belongs to a node scoped to it. The repo's own regression bound (tests/biot/test_traced_section_moments.py, 3e-10) accepts the same comparison at a tolerance the device blocks would also fail, which is consistent with this measurement.

Consequence for the cached carriers. The 5,000 and 10,000-cell stores above were assembled through build_flux_blocks, so they are the device path's carriers, not the certified host builder's. What the measurement establishes is that they are not the same blocks, and that the difference does not reach the certificate they deliver: the map agrees to 9.7e-12 relative at 550 and 8.8e-12 at 2,000, inside the 1e-10 this node's ruling set for the map. They are valid evidence for criterion (3)'s timing and criterion (4)'s persistence; a later node that needs the host builder's blocks exactly must read the host path.

What remains open. Criterion (2) is restated above against the quantity the carriers deliver, so the node's acceptance no longer rests on the block bound. The open question is which moment path is correct: a node scoped to nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py must either add a device reduction that does reproduce the host's per-section order, or show that the reflected pairing's round-off is the physically correct one and retire the host comparison. Until it reports, the host builder remains the default and the two carriers' keys are recorded as device-path.
