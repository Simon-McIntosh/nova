Elliptic and harmonic-moment tangent recurrences

The point jet's jaxpr reaches 15 named helpers in the two elliptic modules: _descent, _accumulate, _finite_part, complete_kind, _complete_kind, complete_pole, harmonic_moments, _harmonic_rising_step, _harmonic_ratio_step, harmonic_pole_moments, _pole_forward_step, _pole_backward_step, harmonic_root_moments, cn_pole_moment and sn_pole_moment. It reaches no paired form. Each now has a private tangent. The tangent runs as its own recurrence beside the primal and uses the arrangement jax.jvp uses for each elementary rule. On 10,000 inputs per function the tangents match jax.jvp of the base revision exactly, with relative error 0. Truncating each recurrence by one term breaks that identity. The primal outputs are bit-identical to the base. The compile bound is not met. The harmonic family's tangent compiles 41.6 times slower than its primal, which is the same cost as jax.jvp of the base: a hand-written recurrence with the same arithmetic does not lower the XLA compile cost. A scanned form meets the compile bound but loses the 1e-13 identity.

Exact agreement depends on the arrangement. A first version wrote the quotient rule as (g - q g_y)/y and missed E's tangent by 4.1e-2 near a complement of 1e-16. There the tangent cancels terms of order 1/k'^2, so an ulp of difference in each term reaches the leading digits. Using jax.jvp's own forms, g/y + (-g_y x)(1/y^2) and g (0.5/sqrt x), gives bit-identical results on the whole domain. That domain includes complements down to 1e-300, the confluence at zero, poles within 1e-4 of one, and zero shifts.

Where the harmonic compile goes: with 3 orders and the full 96-order downward headroom the tangent compiles in 4.3 s against 0.5 s for the primal. With 43 orders and no headroom it takes 10.7 s against 0.8 s. With both at full size it takes 54 s, more than the parts added together. An optimisation barrier after every tangent step left it at 70.7 s, so the cost is not fusion duplicating producers across steps. That barrier was measured and then removed. Its patch had also reached the primal recurrence by mistake and was reverted, so the primal source has zero removed lines against the base.

The compile bound and exact identity cannot both be met by changing representation alone. A variant that carries the harmonic tangent's upward, ratio and product recursions as scans over per-order coefficients, with the same per-step arithmetic, compiles in 1.43 s against 0.77 s for the primal (1.86 times). It misses jax.jvp by 1.0e-10. The base primal computed in the same program also misses the primal from the jvp program by 3.7e-8. XLA's CPU arithmetic depends on the shape of the surrounding graph, and the upward recursion near a parameter of 0.99 amplifies that difference. The landed tangent is the unrolled recurrence, which holds the identity exactly. The scan variant is kept as a patch in the run directory for the node that decides between the two.
