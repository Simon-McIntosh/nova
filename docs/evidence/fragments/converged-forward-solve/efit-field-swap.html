Cross-checking nova's boundary read against imas-efit's trace records

Nova's boundary decision is measured on imas-efit's own fields, case by case, without shaping nova's rule by theirs. The adapter that presents a record's spline to nova's read is falsified against each record's own stored axis; the sweep records both codes' decision columns on every record, and marks a case the lattice cannot qualify rather than reporting nova's own axis fallback as a boundary level. Nova's decisions are also written back out in imas-efit's own record layout, so the exchange exists as files rather than as a claim.

The adapter is falsified before anything is laid at nova's door

Two observations look like a mis-read grid: a boundary failure whose exception names an empty array, and two codes placing the axis of one field 1.37 m apart. Every record already carries the axis imas-efit found, so if the rebuilt spline is imas-efit's field its gradient vanishes there to numerical precision whatever the grid ordering, units or frame. It does, on all ten readable records, with |grad psi| between 1.2 × 10−15 and 3.6 × 10−14, and the value there reproducing the record's own axis_psi to every printed digit. A transposed, mis-ordered or offset grid cannot do either, so neither symptom is an artefact of the adapter.

The same test run at both axes then settles the axis disagreement. On jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 the gradient is 1.6 × 10−14 at imas-efit's axis and 2.8 × 10−15 at nova's, so the record's spline has more than one stationary point and each code selects a different one; on AUG-17151, where the two codes agree on the axis to five decimals, both are stationary points of the same field. The axis disagreement is therefore a real difference in selection and upstream of every label difference. Where the axes agree the boundary levels still do not: on AUG-17151 A imas-efit's span is 0.18108 against nova's 0.07102, on one field with one axis.

What is committed

  • scripts/prototypes/efit_field_swap/trace_record.py — a reader and writer for the BOUNDARY_LEVEL_TRACE 2 record, laid out against read_boundary_trace_record / write_boundary_trace_record in imas-efit's src/EFIT/boundary_level_trace.f90 rather than from a guess: header, dimension line, discrete-status line, seven-reals status line, then the r grid, the z grid, the wall segments, the limiter cell indices, the decision cells, the wall polygon and the row-major psi grid. Reals are written in the Fortran ES26.17E3 spelling, so a captured record round-trips bit-exactly, and an emitted record carries 4978 lines for a 65×65 grid.
  • scripts/prototypes/efit_field_swap/spline_field.py — the adapter that presents a record's psi grid to nova's read. imas-efit carries its flux as a tensor-product not-a-knot bicubic spline (type(EZspline2), bcs=(0,0), at src/EFIT/null_detection.f90), and the adapter rebuilds that same spline, so the spline is the field and nova's Biot-Savart operator is not used and no plasma current is booked. Value, gradient and Hessian are each a tensor contraction against a B-spline basis: every derivative of a B-spline is itself a B-spline of one lower degree whose coefficients are a divided difference of the parent's, so no hand-written derivative recurrence is needed and the result stays differentiable under jax.jacfwd, which the kernel's saddle curvature terms require. All six quantities agree with scipy's bisplev to 1e-9 relative on the field and to 1e-6 absolute on the second derivatives, at 64 pseudo-random interior points.
  • scripts/prototypes/efit_field_swap/crosscheck.py — the driver that rebuilds each record's field, builds a square-lattice cell geometry inside the record's own wall polygon, runs nova's census and read on it, and records both codes' decision columns (axis level and position, boundary level, admitted saddle position and level, wall contact level, limited-or-diverted label) with the source of each column named.

The measured limitation, and its instrument

The first full run of the sweep proposed no cellwise stationary seed at all: RootCensus.candidates was exactly 0 on jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 at a 16×16 lattice (132 retained cells, pitch 0.2508 m), with RootCensus.axes 1 and RootCensus.saddles 0. The failure was attributed rather than guessed, with two read-only instruments: a diagnostic that prints the fitted quadratic's stationary point against its cell and the containment test that rejects it, and a probe that prints the sample-point stencil against each cell centre.

What they show is a defect in the lattice, not in nova. Every cell's quadratic model was well conditioned, and the stencil was not: the median distance from a cell's sampling points to its own centre was 3.27 m, and the worst cell's stencil sat at the lattice origin. The measured design condition number was 1.7 × 104. Nova's TopologyGeometry.from_cells takes absolute RZ sampling points, one set per cell, and prepends the cell centre to each set itself; the lattice was handing it per-cell offsets, so every cell's quadratic was fitted over points clustered at the origin. The fitted stationary point then landed 3.2 to 26.9 pitch lengths outside its cell, no cell contained its own seed, and the census proposed nothing.

With the lattice's sampling points placed around each cell's own centre: design condition number 19, nine cells propose seeds, four distinct saddles are admitted, and nova's read returns a boundary level of 0.31165 with class diverted — agreeing with imas-efit's verdict 2 on that record. The earlier reading of this record, in which nova said limited against imas-efit's diverted, was an artefact of the seed failure and is withdrawn.

The failure mode is worth stating because it is silent and plausible: a census that proposes nothing admits no saddle, gather then fills every saddle slot with the axis index, the merge-path margin is all -inf, slot 0 wins, and the read reports the axis level as a boundary level. That is exactly the number a reader would take for an answer. The driver therefore reports any case whose lattice admits no saddle as unqualified, with the census counts that disqualified it, and never prints the read's own fallback in its place. No axis-fallback value appears in the table.

One record class remains unresolved rather than measured: the later rows of the pairs manifest name a worker worktree that no longer exists, and the driver resolves those rows by record name in imas-efit's own test data before giving up. A row that cannot be resolved at all is recorded unread with the exception text in its note, so a gap in the table is never silently a pass.

The fourteen ITER and MAST-U records, and the two synthetic crossing records, fail for one reason that is a capacity rather than a geometry. TopologyGeometry.from_cells sizes its vertex array as len(tree_centres) + len(points) + 1, which is too small for a wall whose segments generate more contour-mesh vertices than that; build_contour_mesh then returns a mesh with zero valid vertices instead of raising, and the failure surfaces two calls later as np.flatnonzero(mesh.vertex_valid) being empty. Measured by raising the capacity alone: ITER (174 lattice cells, 56 wall points) admits 0 vertices at nova's capacity and 247 at four times it, MAST-U (156 cells, 97 wall points) admits 0 and 291, and JT-60SA (132 cells, 116 wall points) admits 156 at every capacity. Deterministic, no NaN, and reproduced under four combinations of centroid against vertex-mean and closed against stripped wall. This is a nova-core defect, out of this node's write scope, recorded as a follow-on with its reproduction.

The negative control, and why it perturbs the field

The declared control was a wall perturbation, and as declared it did not discriminate: moving one wall vertex 2 per cent toward the record's axis left the label, the boundary level and the admitted saddle byte-identical, and at 25 per cent it dropped one cell of 132 and changed none of the three. The reason is structural — the field is the record's own spline, which does not depend on the wall, and the wall reaches the decision only through the cell set. The control therefore perturbs the field, as a smooth shape change over the record grid rather than an offset or a global scale, because the read is invariant to both of those. It discriminates at every amplitude tried, on two records, while the wall control discriminates at none. Two limits are reported rather than smoothed over: the limited-or-diverted label does not flip at any amplitude up to a 20 per cent shape perturbation, so what is demonstrated is that nova's axis position and boundary level respond to the field; and on the degenerate record, whose span is 5 × 10−5, a proportionally large change is an absolutely small one, so the non-degenerate AUG row is the one to read.

The exports

Four version-2 records are written under the run directory's exports/records/, with a README giving each one's provenance. Two are certified carrier states, made by rewriting only the decision fields of a source imas-efit record, so the grid, psi and wall in them are imas-efit's and the decision is nova's. Two are tie-island states, built by the probe that owns those carriers at the cell counts it bisects and rendered by nova, so everything in them is nova's written into imas-efit's layout. On the 282-cell carrier at the bisected offset 0.16133 nova's read returns limited; on the 550-cell carrier it returns diverted. The <probe kind>-<cells>-nova-<class> name answers two different questions and the two words are not in conflict. Only the 282-cell offset is a bisected one: no offset for the 550-cell arm is recorded anywhere this node could read, so that file carries the probe's unshifted carrier and the README says so.

The decision table

Each row carries imas-efit's columns read from the record's stored status line (verdict, axis_psi, saddle_refined, wall_level, fed_back_psi) beside nova's columns computed by running the unmodified read and census on the record's spline field over the in-wall lattice. The table, its per-case instrument, and the diagnosis of the level difference live in the research document; the machine-readable rows, with the census counts behind every unqualified case, are written by the sweep to exports/disagreement.json in the run directory.

Approximations that qualify every number

  • The cell geometry is a square lattice at the record's own resolution inside the record's wall polygon, not nova's hex or Delaunay carrier mesh, so the Bell cell fit and the cell-boundary census differ in discretisation from a production nova run.
  • The record stores its wall as consecutive segments rather than one closed ring; the adapter closes it by appending the final segment's end.
  • The comparison is of two codes' decisions on one field representation, not of two physics models, because the field consumed here is imas-efit's spline rather than a nova forward solve.
  • A case whose lattice admits no saddle is reported unqualified; its missing nova column is a gap, not a zero.
