Which integrator is right on the fixture's own cells

The base-versus-head comparison below locates the movement in the exact arm's moment path but does not say which of the two rules is the accurate one. That was measured directly on the fixture's actual clipped cells: the diverted single-null configuration at 2,000 cells, whose 2,098 cells leave 1,273 non-empty polygons clipped to the analytic support (H200 job 1283985, one card, one shot, 34 s).

The reference needs no quadrature at all, and that is the point. For a uniform density a triangle's moments are its signed area and that area times its vertex mean, and a simple polygon's are the shoelace area and first moments, so each rule's uniform moment is available exactly: a rule that integrates the wrong region is caught with no quadrature error anywhere. The two published rules share the fan from each ring's first vertex and differ in one respect — the deleted rule multiplies every fan triangle's jacobian by its absolute value and by the whole ring's signed-area sign, the owner sums the signed jacobian. Because the signed fan telescopes to the shoelace formula for any simple polygon, the owner's region is exact by construction, and the deleted rule can only err where a ring's fan mixes triangle orientations — which is exactly where the fan from the first vertex stops being a decomposition of the ring.
