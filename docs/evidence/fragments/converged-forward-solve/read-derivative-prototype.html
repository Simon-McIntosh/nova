Cheaper second derivatives for the topology read

Three derivative paths were measured through the production topology read at the 550-cell certificate states. The oracle builds each nodal flux Hessian by differentiating the analytic booked-moment kernel a second time, which is the path the read uses today. The fd variant differences the analytic gradient centrally, so the kernel is traced once per point rather than differentiated twice. The interp variant contracts the cached coupling operand with the booked moments to get nodal flux, fits a quadratic over each hex cell's seven-node stencil in cell-scaled coordinates, and reads gradient and Hessian from the fit's coefficients, so no kernel autodiff runs in the read at all.

Every number below comes from one H200 job on the shared reservation, a fresh process per kind, with no compilation cache. Each variant's derivative program was compiled and called on a 64-node block, the block width at which production builds its operand; the read program was compiled once and called on each variant's assembled jet field. Instruction counts are the HLO operations JAX reports at lowering of each program. Prototype: scripts/prototypes/read_derivatives/measure.py, driver scripts/prototypes/read_derivatives/run.sh. Logs and JSON receipts are under scripts/prototypes/read_derivatives/logs/.

Two facts decide the compile question. The fit variant's derivative program compiles in 0.258603 s (limited) and 0.308345 s (diverted) with 89 instructions, against the oracle's 91.809 s and 89.596 s with 60529, so it removes essentially the whole compile cost of the doubly differentiated kernel. Central differencing does not help: it traces the kernel five times per point rather than differentiating it twice, and its 184.486 s and 179.162 s are twice the oracle's. The read that consumes the jets compiles in 11.798116 s and 11.791027 s, well under the 10 s target on its own but not under 1 ms per call.

Neither alternative reproduces the oracle. Central differencing holds the boundary flux to between 6.3e-6 and 3.8e-4 of the flux span, but its axis moves by 4.6 cm at the limited convergent state and by 1.10 m at the diverted state. The averaged stencil fit is far worse in flux, moving the boundary by 5.2e-2 of the span on the limited geometry and by 0.21 on the diverted one, and by 3.6 to 3.8 m in axis on the limited geometry. Only six of the twelve variant reads admit a valid, qualified state, and the oracle's own read refuses two of the four state instances (the limited trial and the diverted convergent state), so the axis comparisons at those instances are made against a refused reference and carry no weight on their own. No row admits an X-point, including every oracle row, so no X-point error is reported: at these states the saddle branch was not exercised by the read as this harness calls it.

Recommendation and precision budget

No measured variant is recommended for a production change, and the reason differs between them. Finite differencing is rejected on cost, not on cost alone but because it inverts the intended saving: it compiles in 179.162 and 184.486 s against the oracle's 89.596 and 91.809 s, since five kernel evaluations per point cost more than a second differentiation. The stencil fit is the only path that meets the compile requirement — 0.26 to 0.31 s and 89 instructions, two orders of magnitude below the 10 s target — and its accuracy failure is a defect of this prototype rather than of the approach: the fit is taken per cell's seven-node stencil but the resulting jets are averaged over every cell that contains the read 1743 nodes, which smeared. The next step is the plan's own-node quadratic — one fit per cell, evaluated cell-locally, with no averaging across cells — measured against this same oracle. The proposed precision budget for that attempt, and the bar none of these rows meets, is an axis position error at or below 1 cm and a boundary flux error at or below 1e-3 of the flux span, at both states and at each certificate resolution. The read that consumes the jets compiles in 11.798116 s (limited) and 11.791027 s (diverted) and runs at 141 to 144 ms per call over 1743 nodes and 550 cells, so it stays a floor above the sub-millisecond target independently of how the jets are built.

A production change would replace the second differentiation in _source_jets — printed by scripts/prototypes/read_route/jets.py:258 as jax.jacfwd of the analytic gradient — and the cached jet operand it builds, with a coupling value contraction plus a per-cell quadratic derivative. nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py would stay as the reference oracle and as the certificate kernel; no product module was changed by this prototype.
