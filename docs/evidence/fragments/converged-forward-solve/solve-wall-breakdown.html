Where a forward solve spends its time, cold and warm

On one H200, on the published diverted single-null carrier at 550 realised cells (2,105 nodes), the three programs a Newton step calls — the topology read, the booking and the map — compile cold in 34.9 s, of which 29.0 s is XLA compile, 4.0 s is tracing, 1.2 s is lowering and 0.71 s is the first execute. Warm, with every program compiled and the carrier loaded in 0.649 s and the 166.7 MB jet matrix loaded separately in 0.181 s, one Newton step costs 1,023.7 ms against the 1 ms target: the Jacobian block 735.7 ms, the map 166.2 ms, the topology read 107.8 ms, the booking 7.6 ms and the dense linear solve 6.4 ms. At batch width 16 the map falls to 21.4 ms per slice. The largest contributor is the Jacobian block, not the map.

What this run did not measure

  • The recompile experiment across cell counts.
  • The host gap of the untraced step. Profiling inflates the call wall by an order of magnitude, so the trace gives each stage's device composition and launch count (next section) but cannot be subtracted from the untraced wall to yield a host gap.
  • The route's end-to-end cold and warm walls, and the converged solve's own wall. This receipt times one convergence step stage by stage; it does not drive ForwardSolveProfile.solve to convergence, so neither the number of Newton steps the production solve takes from its seed nor the wall of that solve is measured here.
  • Recompile counting by JAX's own compile log. The counter saw no lines at the handler level, so the program count of three is the three lower().compile() calls this harness made, not an independent count.

Two facts from the trace change the picture the stage walls give. First, the map and the read are not a few large kernels: they issue 76,083 and 61,330 kernels per call. At the roughly 1.5 µs launch cost of this device that is about 114 ms of issue per map call, which is the same order as the map's whole 166.2 ms wall, so the map's cost is spread over launches as much as over arithmetic. Second, the contour-tree ranks in the map's own trace are 37.1 ms of its 139.3 ms of device time, and they belong to kernel.read — the map's internal fixed-shape read — not to the standalone topology read, whose own tree is measured in the right-hand column of the table above. The two reads are separate programs and their trees are separate costs.

Device memcpy activity is 32.2 ms per map call and 29.4 ms per read call, which is not visible in the stage walls as a separate term and is consistent with the read returning host-side data inside the measured call.

Why this carrier is far slower than the 282-cell lean-map study

The October lean-map study measured the connected read at 282 diverted cells at 6.120 ms and two field-map contractions at 0.743 ms, with a fused map at 10.176 ms. Here, at 550 cells, the same production read takes 107.8 ms and the map 166.2 ms. The cell count doubles, but the read is 17.6 times slower, so cell count alone does not explain it.

Three of the four candidate explanations are ruled out by the receipts and one is confirmed:

  • Not cell count. 282 to 550 is a factor 1.95 in cells against 17.6 in read wall.
  • Not host transfers of the operands. The operands are device-resident traced arguments: the operand store loads once in 0.649 s on the step arm and 0.690 s on the cold arm, both a store hit, with the 166.7 MB jet matrix 0.181 s of that, and the warm step reuses them, so no per-call transfer appears in the step.
  • Not synchronisation inside the stage. Each stage is timed with a single block_until_ready around one call and 50 repeats; the wall is per call, and the p95 sits within 0.6 % of the median, which a launch-jitter explanation would not produce.
  • Different route: confirmed. The study states that its exact label and dense timings start with fragments and intervals already computed, and that the connected read at 6.120 ms is the production fragment graph, not the contour tree. The production topology.read measured here builds the contour tree and null census on every call. The same study measures the isolated device contour tree at 406.1 ms at 282 cells, which alone exceeds this carrier's whole 107.8 ms read, so the route, not the mesh, is the difference.
  • The map and the read stage are different programs, and neither contains the other. The step's read stage runs nova.equilibrium.topology.read on the field; the map runs map_moments, which solves its own roots with fixed_points.solve and then calls kernel.read over those roots. Both reads build a contour tree — kernel.read through _first_tree_events on the geometry's fixed contour mesh, the step's stage through nova.equilibrium.topology.read — but they are separate programs over separate inputs. So the map's 166.2 ms contains a root solve and that other read, and none of the measured 107.8 ms, which is why the five stage walls above sum to the step without overlap. The study's 10.176 ms figure was measured, not predicted, and this receipt does not reproduce its route on either program.

The two largest contributors to the 1,023.7 ms Newton step, each measured as its own program, are:

  1. The Jacobian block, 735.7 ms (71.9 %). It materialises all 1,650 columns of the map's Jacobian through traced JVP, one direction at a time. Removing it, for instance by a matrix-free Krylov solve over the same JVP, leaves a step of about 288 ms — 288 times the target, still not close, but it removes seven tenths of the step.
  2. The map, 166.2 ms (16.2 %). Removing it leaves a step of about 857.5 ms. Its own root solve and its own fixed-shape read are what it spends the time on; the trace below shows 76,083 kernel launches per call, so the cost is spread across launches rather than concentrated in one kernel, and no single substitution inside the map removes it.

Together the two contributors are 88 % of the step, and the remaining 12 % (the read stage, the booking and the dense solve) cannot close the gap alone. The measured step is 1,024 times the 1 ms target, so the target is not reachable by shaving stages: the Jacobian route and the map's launch-heavy structure are the two changes the numbers point at.

The device trace refines the second of those without changing the order. The map issues 76,083 kernels per call and the read 61,330, so the cost is launch-bound as much as arithmetic-bound. On the most favourable reading of the trace, dropping the contour tree from the map's internal kernel.read would take the map from 166.2 ms to about 129.2 ms, since its tree ranks are 37.1 ms of the map's device time, and the step from 1,023.7 ms to about 986.7 ms — 987 times the target, and a reduction that leaves the verdict where it was. A route that still issues tens of thousands of kernels per solve cannot reach 1 ms however its stages are arranged; the study's 10.176 ms fused map is itself ten times the target. Of the two contributors, the Jacobian block is the one to remove first: it is 71.9 % of the map's parent step and its replacement by a matrix-free solve is a change of algorithm rather than of launch strategy.
