Where a forward solve spends its time, cold and warm

On one H200, on the published diverted single-null carrier at 550 realised cells (2,105 nodes), the three programs a Newton step calls — the topology read, the booking and the map — compile cold in 29.0 s, of which 21.7 s is XLA compile, 4.0 s is tracing and 1.2 s is lowering, and their first execute is 0.71 s. Warm, with every program compiled and the operand store loaded in 0.18 s, one Newton step costs 1,023.7 ms against the 1 ms target: the Jacobian block 735.7 ms, the map 166.2 ms, the topology read 107.8 ms, the booking 7.6 ms and the dense linear solve 6.4 ms. At batch width 16 the map falls to 21.4 ms per slice. The largest contributor is the Jacobian block, not the map.

Warm per-step split against the 1 ms target

With every program compiled and the operand store warm, each stage was timed over 50 calls on the same state, blocking on each result, on one H200. The Jacobian block and the dense solve were timed over 20 calls; the residual the solve acts on has 1,650 rows.

At batch width 16 on the same state the map's batched program costs 342.7 ms for sixteen states, 21.4 ms per slice, against 166.2 ms at width 1, a 7.8-fold gain and still 21 times the target.

Every program recompiles when the cell count changes, because every operand's shape derives from it; none compiles once and is reused across cell counts. Same-shape reuse is what the operand-carrying route was built for, and it is measured in the cold table above: two geometries whose shapes agree compile to one program because nothing geometric is a program literal. The map is the one program that still captures host constants (the profile, the policy and the axis sign), so a change to any of those recompiles it even at an unchanged shape; the negative-control reading of the one-program clause belongs to the plan's own gate and is not taken here.

What this run did not measure

  • The recompile experiment across cell counts.
  • A device kernel table. Two profiler traces were taken over twenty warm calls each and both failed with a CUDA error inside the profiler (a branch_index retrieval failure and a CUDA-graph kernel-node failure), so device time is not separated from host gaps here; the stage walls above are host-side walls that include every launch.
  • The production route's own end-to-end cold and warm walls. This receipt times the three programs the Newton step calls, not ForwardSolveProfile.solve; the operand build (0.65 s carrier load, 0.18 s jet load) is reported, the solve itself is not.
  • Recompile counting by JAX's own compile log. The counter saw no lines at the handler level, so the program count of three is the three lower().compile() calls this harness made, not an independent count.

Why this carrier is far slower than the 282-cell lean-map study

The October lean-map study measured the connected read at 282 diverted cells at 6.120 ms and two field-map contractions at 0.743 ms, with a fused map at 10.176 ms. Here, at 550 cells, the same production read takes 107.8 ms and the map 166.2 ms. The cell count doubles, but the read is 17.6 times slower, so cell count alone does not explain it.

Three of the four candidate explanations are ruled out by the receipts and one is confirmed:

  • Not cell count. 282 to 550 is a factor 1.95 in cells against 17.6 in read wall.
  • Not host transfers of the operands. The operands are device-resident traced arguments: the 550-cell jet matrix is a 166.7 MB store hit loaded in 0.18 s once, and the warm step reuses it, so no per-call transfer appears in the step.
  • Not synchronisation inside the stage. Each stage is timed with a single block_until_ready around one call and 50 repeats; the wall is per call, and the p95 sits within 0.6 % of the median, which a launch-jitter explanation would not produce.
  • Different route: confirmed. The study states that its exact label and dense timings start with fragments and intervals already computed, and that the connected read at 6.120 ms is the production fragment graph, not the contour tree. The production topology.read measured here builds the contour tree and null census on every call. The same study measures the isolated device contour tree at 406.1 ms at 282 cells, which alone exceeds this carrier's whole 107.8 ms read, so the route, not the mesh, is the difference.
  • The map carries the read inside it. map_moments solves the roots and invokes the selected read before contracting the moments, so the 166.2 ms map contains this read's 107.8 ms plus roughly 58 ms of roots, booking and contractions. That is why the fused map is not the 10.176 ms the study measured for a map whose read was supplied.

The two largest contributors to the 1,023.7 ms Newton step are:

  1. The Jacobian block, 735.7 ms (71.9 %). It materialises all 1,650 columns of the map's Jacobian through traced JVP, one direction at a time. Removing it, for instance by a matrix-free Krylov solve over the same JVP, leaves a step of about 288 ms — 288 times the target, still not close, but it removes seven tenths of the step.
  2. The map, 166.2 ms (16.2 %), of which the topology read is 107.8 ms. Removing the per-call contour tree by supplying the solved stationary points (the route the study measured) would take the map towards the study's 10.176 ms and the step to about 866 ms; removing the read entirely is not possible while the map depends on membership.

Together the two contributors are 88 % of the step, and the remaining 12 % (read outside the map, booking, dense solve) cannot close the gap alone. The measured step is 1,024 times the 1 ms target, so the target is not reachable by shaving stages: the Jacobian route and the tree-free read are the two changes the numbers point at.
