The IDS readers on the COCOS translator

This node is split: the plasma source, the MAST frame reader, Extrapolate.update, the re-pointed tests and both plant helpers landed here, and nova/imas/equilibrium.py and nova/imas/getslice.py close as a node of their own. The reader changes below are committed and their gates are recorded. Both heavy files have now been run at head: every test of tests/test_unseen_source_full_plant.py passes, and tests/test_coupled_plant.py passes except two tests that build a plant with no plasma source at all and fail on a cached sectioned reference, which no code path this node touched reaches. The one done-when item still unmet is stated in the last section.

PlasmaSource translates every convention-bearing quantity into COCOS 17

The one entry point is nova.io.sourcemap.translate_equilibrium. PlasmaSource.from_imas now reads the DD version from equilibrium.ids_properties.version_put.data_dictionary and, before any PlasmaSlice is built, takes that slice's ip, psi_axis, psi_boundary, q_95 and b0 through the translator. The translated SourceSignalMap is then applied quantity by quantity — profiles_2d/psi, profiles_1d/psi, dpressure_dpsi, f_df_dpsi, global_quantities/psi_axis, global_quantities/psi_boundary and global_quantities/ip — so no raw IDS value reaches a slice. A blocked map is raised by name, with the DD version, the expected COCOS and the measured digits, before the slice exists. Each row is fetched by target path through the map's own lookup, SourceSignalMap.for_target, so the reader holds no second copy of the map's indexing.

The factors the DD3 to COCOS 17 mapping applies are those of the algebra, not a table here: psi_like −1 and dodpsi_like −1 (COCOS 11 and COCOS 17 differ in sigma_Bp), ip_like, b0_like and q_like all +1. The profile coordinate (psi−psi_axis)/(psi_boundary−psi_axis) is therefore invariant, and with both flux functions flipping alongside the flux map, the current density, the integrated current and the boundary outline are unchanged by the translation.

The plasma-source guard stays, re-expressed as the internal COCOS 17 check

PlasmaSlice.prescribe still refuses a slice whose flux ordering contradicts the current, and it is still the only sign site in any reader. Its digit now comes from the algebra — nova_cocos.convention(NOVA_CONVENTION).sigma_bp, which is −1 — rather than from a literal, so the condition is sigma_Bp · (psi_boundary − psi_axis) · ip > 0. The refusal message keeps the substring psi ordering disagree that test_mast_flipped_psi_is_refused matches. This is the one permitted hit of the sign search and is named here as the exemption.

The MAST flux conversion is declared once, in one module

nova/media/sources/mast_efit.py is the one module that owns the MAST flux conversion. The archive is not a DD read, so its convention is declared rather than implied: nova.io.sourcemap.MAST_SOURCE_CONVENTION = 3 and mast_flux_factor(), which is convention_transform(source=3, target=NOVA_CONVENTION).factor(psi_like) = 2π. All three flux rows carry that declared factor in place: the flux map in _flux_map and the axis and boundary flux in read_frame. PlasmaSource.from_mast applies no further factor, so the 2π is applied exactly once on every ψ-like row. COCOS 3 and COCOS 17 share sigma_Bp = −1, so a conforming MAST slice passes the internal check and a reversed flux family is still refused.

Rows read_frame does not convert, and who converts them. p_prime (pprime), ff_prime (ffprime) and any q row are passed through raw. Their COCOS 3 to 17 factors are dodpsi_like 1/(2π) and q_like −1, and no caller on that route converts them: the media painters, mast_parity_gate, the transport route and the scripts/ entry points all take the frame values as they are handed over, so a raw COCOS 3 p′ or FF′ reaching COCOS 17 code is a 2π error. That is a finding for §4, not a change made here — the two flux functions and q are unchanged for every caller by this node.

The three rows are covered by reading them, not by restating the factor. tests/test_media.py::test_each_mast_flux_row_carries_the_declared_factor reads one synthetic efm group twice: once under the shipped COCOS 3 declaration and once with sourcemap.MAST_SOURCE_CONVENTION patched to 13, whose factor is one. Each of flux, flux_axis and flux_boundary must then be exactly 2π times its COCOS 13 reading, so a row that ignored the declaration and carried a literal would fail. The red arm was run and kept: reverting the axis row in read_frame to a hard-coded factor of one in a scratch copy reddens the test in 8.06 s (1.5 against 9.424778), log logs/mast_row_red.log, EXIT=1; the file was then restored byte-identically and the test passes in 5.72 s.

Sensor readers: identity and geometry only

nova/imas/io_magnetics.py reads sensor identity (name, identifier), geometry (position, area, gm9, indices_differential) and the diagnostic kind. It reads no signal sample, so it carries no convention and is exempt. nova/imas/magnetics.py::Magnetics.flux_loop reads name and identifier through from_AoS and is likewise exempt. No consumer in nova/ or scripts/ reads a flux-loop or pick-up signal sample from either module, so there is no sensor signal whose sign follows ψ or B_p on this route to convert. Should one be added, the conversion belongs at this boundary through SourceSignal.convention_factor.

Extrapolate.update

The # COCOS11 literal in Extrapolate.update is gone. Its negation was the internal convention's poloidal orientation, so the line now reads convention(NOVA_CONVENTION).sigma_bp * self.psi_rbs(radius, height), computed once from the algebra. The display negation at plot_2d_masked is unchanged and remains §4's.

The reconstruction route reads through the translator too

The reconstruction-style source in tests/test_coupled_plant.py presents a discharge the way a reconstruction arrives — the last closed flux surface named as the boundary the polygon closes on, no separatrix; and the coil currents on a coarser cadence — but it must not re-orient the flux by hand. It now reads through PlasmaSource.from_imas and differs from the generic source only in presentation, so the DD3 to COCOS 17 translation is applied exactly once. Before that change the reconstruction carried a raw COCOS 11 flux family beside a COCOS 17 plant and the internal check refused it, which reddened the route comparison and the flipped-sign refusal with it. The same hand-orientation was removed from tests/test_coupled_plant.py for the same reason.

The sign search, run again

The census's sign search, run over nova/ scripts/ packages/ at this head, grep -rEn 'COCOS|span \* (ip|self\.plasma_current)|-self\.psi_rbs|= -data|-self\._data\.psi2d|norm\(psi\)|-self\["psi_boundary"\]' nova/ scripts/ packages/ --include=*.py, returns 103 lines in 26 files. Almost all of them are prose: the translator's and the MAST module's docstrings, the census's own nova_cocos digit plumbing, and the prototypes. The sign sites that remain are, by class: the one permitted reader hit, the internal COCOS 17 check in PlasmaSource.prescribe (sigma_bp * span * self.plasma_current, line 101), which takes its σ_Bp from nova_cocos.convention(NOVA_CONVENTION); writer sites, nova/imas/pulsedesign.py lines 531, 544 and 581, which negate a solution before writing it; and internal consumers, nova/imas/extrapolate.py:350 (contour levels for display), nova/imas/flux.py:94 (a commented line), nova/biot/contour.py:198 and nova/equilibrium/inverse.py:245. Every writer and consumer site is §4's, as the plan states.

The two readers this node did not touch, nova/imas/equilibrium.py and nova/imas/getslice.py, have no hit at all in the sign search: neither carries a fixed-orientation sign test or a # COCOS sign site. What they do carry is 38 and 2 hits from the census's second search, the one for readers of COCOS-dependent IDS paths, because both read profiles_2d.psi, profiles_1d.psi, dpressure_dpsi, f_df_dpsi, psi_axis and psi_boundary raw and neither passes them through the translator. Their psi_norm renormalisation — the profile coordinate divided by its own endpoints — is why the omission is invisible rather than wrong in the plotted field. They are the split-out node's work, and the search run here is what shows the split is not a loose end left in a sign site.

Positive control. A fixed-orientation sign test planted in a scratch copy of one reader is found by the same search. Over copies of nova/, scripts/ and packages/ the search returns 103 lines in 26 files, the same as the committed tree; adding return -self._data.psi2d to the scratch nova/imas/getslice.py takes it to 104 lines in 27 files, with the new hit named at nova/imas/getslice.py:136. The search therefore reports a sign site that exists rather than merely quiet on the tree as it stands, and the two readers this node did not touch are quiet in the same run.

The heavy arm, and the negative control

The two full-plant files ran as one sirius job, one fresh pytest process per file: tests/test_unseen_source_full_plant.py returned 2 passed, 1 xfailed in 152.72 s, and tests/test_coupled_plant.py 14 passed, 4 failed in 408.97 s. Two of the four were this node's: both test helpers built their slices from raw IDS values, so a DD3 flux family in COCOS 11 met a COCOS 17 plant and prescribe's internal check refused it. Both now read through the translator, and the two tests pass at head — the route comparison in its pair run and the flipped-sign refusal alone in 93 s, where beside its sibling it had hit a 300 s pulse-read timeout. The other two are the machine-only tests above.

The declared negative control, run: bypassing the translation in PlasmaSource.from_imas — returning the raw values, so raw DD3 values reach the slices — reddens tests/test_plasma_source.py::test_from_imas_prescribes_corsica_130506_6 in 16.11 s with plasma current sign and psi ordering disagree with COCOS 17: sigma_Bp=-1 requires sign(psi_boundary - psi_axis) = sigma_Bp * sign(ip). CORSICA 130506/6 is refused again by the internal check exactly as declared, and the working file was restored byte-identically afterwards.

What is still open, and what the split-out node owns

Neither file is touched here.
