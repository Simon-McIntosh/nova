The edge-consistency measure on the conforming ITER sources

The three forms of nova/imas/edge_consistency.py are reported for DINA 105052/1, DINA 105069/3 and CORSICA 130506/6, each read through PlasmaSource.from_imas — the route whose every convention-bearing quantity passes through nova.io.sourcemap.translate_equilibrium, so a DD3 read leaves the reader in nova’s internal COCOS 17. The two refused pulses, DINA 105014/1 and 105013/1, are read through the same route and appear as refused rows with the digit their data contradicts.

The route is proved before the data are read

The analytic lower-single-null state is the instrument that tells a route defect from a data defect, because all three of its answers are known exactly. Written into an equilibrium IDS in a scratch database at DD 3 in the COCOS 11 sense a DD3 write carries and read back through the same route, the state returns the grid current integrating to +0.99491 of its own Ip, the profile current integrating to +0.99830, and a band residual of 5.8001e−4, below EDGE_BAND_RELATIVE_THRESHOLD = 1.0e−3. That is the analytic floor the measure’s own record reports, so the route hands the measure the operands it needs.

The two operand scalings follow from COCOS 11 and COCOS 17 sharing e_Bp = 1: the translator’s factor is a sign flip on every psi-like and derivative-like row and no 2π enters. A first run that applied 2π to the map and 1/2π to the derivatives is what the round trip refused. Nothing else in the route was adjusted, and no sign was flipped to make a ratio come out positive.

The stored profiles_2d grid is not uniformly spaced while the structured-grid stencil is, so the map is resampled onto a uniform grid carrying the stored point counts over the stored extents — and it must be resampled with a cubic spline. A piecewise-linear interpolant has a discontinuous second derivative at every stored node, which is exactly what the stencil differentiates, and the round trip’s band residual grows under refinement with it (1.7163e−1 at 65 points, 8.4353e−1 at 129, 1.5573e0 at 257, 2.3824e0 at 513). With the spline it falls and plateaus at the state’s own floor (5.8001e−4, 1.7582e−4, 1.6165e−4, 1.8364e−4).

Where the minus one between the map and the flux functions comes from

The three sources put the grid current and the profile current anti-parallel, and three files from two codes doing the same thing points at a shared step rather than at three data defects, so the step was measured. Three checks on the stored file need no convention at all. The stored flux map’s value at the magnetic axis equals the stored psi_axis to 1e−4 relative (0.999991, 0.999948, 1.000032), so the map is the flux the globals describe. The stored one-dimensional flux runs the same way as the globals: sign(psi[0] − psi[end]) matches sign(psi_boundary − psi_axis) on all three. And the stored dpressure_dpsi falls outward against that same flux ordering, which is a statement about pressure, not about COCOS: a derivative row stored in the opposite sense to the map it belongs with would make pressure rise outward. All three files are internally coherent, with sigma_Bp = +1 implied by Sauter Eq. 23, which is the digit a DD 3 file carries.

The map and the derivative rows were then regressed cell by cell over the plasma interior of one slice per source, once with each sign on the profile side, so the reading is not sorted by the answer it is given. The whole-slice integral cannot tell a single sign from scatter; the regression can, because a sign leaves the two fields collinear.

Recommendation for the iter-passive-plasma-circuit §6 reference

This measure does not support keeping CORSICA 130506/6 as the §6 reference. Its band-relative median is 7.93, the largest of the three, its maximum is 9.79e15 on one slice, and it clears the threshold on 24 of 24 sampled slices; DINA 105052/1 has the smallest median on all three forms (1.73 band relative, 0.767 whole slice) and the most stable integral current check. Given that every source clears the threshold on every slice, the recommendation is to not settle §6 on the strength of edge consistency alone; if the section needs the measure to choose, DINA 105052/1 is the better candidate and CORSICA 130506/6 is the worst of the three.

This recommendation is provisional, and no reference is chosen on this table. The lead ruled on 2026-10-23 that nova’s flux functions are to be made COCOS 17 once a separate node has proved the COCOS 17 relation, and that the §6 reference is decided after a re-measure on the corrected sign, with every source’s COCOS identified from its own data because DINA is not sign-trustworthy. The ranking above is therefore a measurement taken through the convention this repository currently applies, reported as such rather than as a verdict on the sources; when the relation is proved and the flux functions are COCOS 17, this table is to be re-measured and the choice of §6 made on those numbers. The measured values below stand unchanged.

What the recommendation rests on: the operand scaling, which the analytic IDS round trip settles at three exact answers rather than by assertion; the grid current, whose integral over each sampled slice lies between 0.9736 and 1.0027 of the slice’s own Ip on DINA 105052/1, 0.9801 and 1.0019 on DINA 105069/3, and 0.9867 and 1.0004 on CORSICA 130506/6 — a worst deviation of 2.64 per cent, on one DINA 105052/1 slice — which is enough to show the map side to be the sound one, carrying the stored Ip at the right magnitude and sign, and not enough to claim it reproduces that current to a fraction of a per cent; and the threshold, fixed on the analytic state before any IDS row was read.

Which sign the operator assumes, stated rather than implied: nova.equilibrium.convention pins jφ = −2π(R p′ + FF′/(μ0R)) beside Δ☆Φ = −2πμ0R jφ and Φ = 2πψ, so its p′ and FF′ are derivatives against the negated flux (p′ = −dp/dΦ). The map side satisfies that pair and the flux functions do not, and the map side is the one anchored outside nova: it integrates to the slice’s own signed Ip between 0.9736 and 1.0027 of it on DINA 105052/1, 0.9801 and 1.0019 on DINA 105069/3, and 0.9867 and 1.0004 on CORSICA 130506/6 (worst deviation 2.64 per cent), and ∫jφ dA = Ip holds whatever convention labels the flux.

So the −1 is a sign chain, not a datum, and it is one site rather than the translation: the raw and the translated regressions agree to every printed digit, which is what a uniform flip of both fields looks like, and the translator’s own factor for a flux-derivative row is the exact reciprocal of the flux factor, so it is mathematically self-consistent. The outlier is the profile side of nova/equilibrium/convention.py, whose leading minus makes the profile current anti-parallel to the map current on data it reads from the Data Dictionary. It disagrees with nova/imas/profiles.py, which multiplies the same p_prime and ff_prime the same reader delivers by +2π with no minus and is the sign that is collinear with the map here. That file is outside this node’s write scope, and the change is repo-wide (it also feeds grad_shafranov_source and every forward solve built on it), so it is named rather than patched. No sign was flipped anywhere in this node to make a ratio come out positive; the table below is reported as the module measures it, with the other sense measured beside it.

What it rests on and what would overturn it: the diagnosis rests on four regressions that are mirror images to every printed digit between the raw and translated files, on collinearity of −0.99 between the two current fields, and on three checks inside the file that need no convention at all — the map at the magnetic axis equalling psi_axis to 1e−4, the one-dimensional flux running the same way as the globals, and pressure falling outward against the stored ordering. A −1 that were a datum rather than a sign would scatter those fields instead of leaving them collinear, and would have to have happened identically in DINA and CORSICA. What would overturn the diagnosis is the opposite reading of the Data Dictionary’s own definition of dpressure_dpsi: if the DD means it as a derivative of the negated flux, then the reader should negate these two rows and the convention module is right as written. That is a reading of the standard, not a measurement, and the lead keeps it: the ruling of 2026-10-23 adopts COCOS 17 for nova’s flux functions and makes the change conditional on a separate node proving the COCOS 17 relation from the data. The check this node can offer on that proof is the one above: whichever way that relation settles, the sample it is proved on must not be one whose flux functions were built under the answer.

Two things survive the correction and one does not. CORSICA 130506/6 is the worst of the three on both forms in both senses, and its maximum of 9.79e15 is unchanged, so it is the worst of the three on this measure. Which DINA pulse is best does not survive: on the corrected whole-slice form DINA 105069/3 leads (0.0466, against 0.0680 for 105052/1), the reverse of the as-measured ordering, so the earlier preference for 105052/1 was the sign rather than the physics. And the band form does not rank at all: it stays between 0.71 and 7.60 in the corrected sense and clears EDGE_BAND_RELATIVE_THRESHOLD = 1.0e−3 on 24 of 24 slices for every source in both senses, because its denominator is the band’s own profile current, which is roughly a tenth of the slice maximum there, while the numerator is 5 to 8 per cent of that maximum. The measure’s threshold was fixed on an analytic state whose band current never approaches zero; on stored slices it separates nothing. The recommendation to the lead is therefore: keep the whole-slice form as the ranking form, re-measure it once the sign is settled, and treat the band-relative threshold as untested on real data.
