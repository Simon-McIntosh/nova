The sign of the flux-function current relation at COCOS 17

Two independent routes agree on the sign, and they say plus; a third is partial and adds nothing on that sign. At COCOS 17 the toroidal current density is

jφ = +2π ( R ∂p/∂ψ + F ∂F/∂ψ / (μ0 R) )

with ψ the Data Dictionary’s own signed poloidal flux and the derivatives taken against it. nova.equilibrium.convention.toroidal_current_density states the minus, together with a negated derivative (p′ = −dp/dΦ), while declaring convention(17). It therefore reproduces the historically correct current magnitude and an anti-parallel current direction when it is fed the Data Dictionary’s own derivatives. That is the disagreement followup f-ddc-006 repairs; this node changes no production module.

The two independent routes are the general digit form evaluated at the two conventions, and the DD4-native regression with its negated-map control (COCOS 17 by definition, needing no translation). imas-ambix’s convention owner is read-only and partial: it confirms the Ampère side and the 2π, and takes the bracket’s sign as an input, so it is not counted as a third agreement.

The general form, and what 11 to 17 does to it

Sauter & Medvedev (CPC 184, 2013) describe each convention by four digits (σBp, eBp, σRφZ, σρθφ), which packages/nova-cocos/src/nova_cocos/__init__.py transcribes as CONVENTION_DIGITS as Table I. Writing the poloidal flux the convention carries as ψ(c) and the flux per radian as Ψ/(2π), the Grad–Shafranov source term in those digits is

jφ = − σBp (2π)eBp [ R ∂p/∂ψ(c) + F ∂F/∂ψ(c) / (μ0 R) ]

The three pieces each do one job. eBp decides whether the factor 2π is present: it is the digit that records the move to total flux Φ = 2π R Aφ = 2πψ, and eBp = 1 supplies the 2π. The overall sign is set by σBp, which is also the sign relating ψ to R Aφ: σBp = +1 means the convention’s ψ increases the way the digit says, and σBp = −1 the other way. Since the derivative is taken against the convention’s own ψ, flipping σBp flips the derivative too, and the two signs cancel inside the written equation. σRφZ and σρθφ fix the handedness of the coordinate triples; they do not enter the source term of this equation but they fix the sign of q and of B0, which is how they are measured below.

Of Sauter & Medvedev’s numbered equations, two are cited here because the repository itself cites them and they can be checked against the code in one read: Eq. 23, sign(ψboundary − ψaxis) = σIp σBp, which nova/io/sourcemap.py:815 uses to form σBp; and Eq. 22, the consistency relation between dp/dψ and the same product, which is the cross-check at nova/io/sourcemap.py:840. The remaining equations of the paper could not be retrieved from this workstation during the run (the publisher refused the fetch), so the general expression above is stated in the digit algebra the repository implements rather than attributed to a particular numbered equation that was not read. What is measured below does not rest on those citations: it rests on the regression.

So the equation is the same written at either convention; what changes between them is which way the flux runs, and therefore the sign of the numbers the derivatives carry. A translation from COCOS 11 to COCOS 17 negates both ψ and dp/dψ, which packages/nova-cocos exposes as convention_transform(11, 17).factor(PSI_LIKE) = −1 and .factor(DODPSI_LIKE) = −1. That identity is what test_the_digit_form_gives_the_same_current_in_cocos_11_and_17 pins, and it is why the DD4 regression below and the raw DD3-source regressions must not be read as two conventions disagreeing.

The DD4-native regression, cell by cell

The measurement is scripts/flux_function_sign/measure.py. It takes the pinned DD 4.1.0 reference layer of iter_corsica_130506 named in tests/data-manifest.json (digest checked before opening; the layer is COCOS 17 by definition, so nothing is translated), opens it through imas.DBEntry at its written version, asserts homogeneous_time == 1, fits the stored non-uniform profiles_2d map with a cubic spline onto a uniform 65 × 129 grid, and forms two currents on that grid:

  • grid current: the map through nova.equilibrium.map_extraction.apply_delta_star, the repository’s one structured-grid Grad–Shafranov operator, in A m−2;
  • profile current: factor × (R · dp/dψ + FF′/(μ0R)) from the stored dpressure_dpsi and f_df_dpsi, interpolated onto the map’s normalised flux, with factor set to +2π and to −2π in two arms.

A single sign leaves the two fields collinear; a wrong one reverses the correlation. The table gives the least-squares slope of the grid current on the profile current over every cell where the stencil is complete, its Pearson correlation, and the RMS residual as a fraction of the grid current’s RMS. The last column is the integral of the grid current over the scored area divided by the recorded plasma current, which is near one exactly when the operator’s units and the total-flux reading are right.

Three of the layer’s four slices were sampled. The +2π arm is collinear at unit slope with a positive correlation on every slice; the −2π arm is collinear with the opposite slope. The integral ratio sits within half a per cent of one, which is the evidence that ψ is being read as the total flux the 2π belongs to. The residual is a quarter of the grid current’s RMS and does not distinguish the two arms — it is the sign of the slope that does, and scatter of that size is what a spline re-sampling of a coarser stored grid leaves behind.

The same layer pins eBp from magnitude alone. Integrated inside the last closed flux surface, the operator’s current is 0.9955, 0.9957 and 0.9954 times the recorded current under eBp = 1, and 6.2549, 6.2562 and 6.2543 times it under eBp = 0. The ratio near one is the eBp = 1 reading, and the near-2π ratio is what a missing 2π factor looks like.

The region matters, and it is the one place in this measurement where an earlier reading was misreported. Summing the same operator over the whole resampled box gives 1.0000, 1.0000 and 1.0001 on this layer — the same answer, because the DD4 box is only about forty per cent plasma and its vacuum carries almost no current. On the four archive files whose boxes are 6 m × 12 m around a much smaller plasma the whole-box sum overshoots badly (see the digit table below), so the whole-box figure is reported but never used for the eBp test.

The literal negative control

The declared mutation is “feed the DD4 regression the flux map with psi negated; the slope sign reverses”. Negating the map fed to the operator and leaving the flux functions where they are gives, on the same three slices, slopes of −1.01339, −1.01459 and −1.01530 with correlations −0.93769, −0.93339 and −0.93166, and integral ratios of −0.9955, −0.9957 and −0.9954. Every sign reverses and no magnitude moves, which is what a real sign measurement must do and what a measurement that is really reporting the interpolation would not.

The same mutation applied to the check itself turns the passing arm red. Running the test file with the recorded regression literals replaced by the slopes the negated-map run produced gives EXIT=1 with test_the_cocos_17_relation_carries_plus_two_pi FAILED, while the head arm passes. The log is the run’s negative-control.log.

Cross-check against imas-ambix, read-only

The convention owner is ~/Code/imas-ambix/imas_ambix/challenge/convention.py. Its _integrated_current states the Ampere half of the relation as density = −delta_star / (tau · μ0 · R) with total_flux = factor · source_flux, where the factor is 2π when the convention carries total flux. That is the same Δ*ψ = −μ0 R jφ with ψ the total flux that nova’s operator applies, and it is consistent with the eBp = 1 and 2π parts of the relation.

It does not independently state the sign multiplying the R p′ + FF′/(μ0R) bracket: the source term is an input to that function, not something it forms from stored derivatives. So the cross-check confirms the Ampere side and the 2π, and adds no evidence about the bracket’s sign. This route is recorded as partial rather than as a third agreement, and the conclusion below rests on the first two routes.

Each source’s COCOS from its own data

Five archive sources were opened at their written DD version through imas.DBEntry, with homogeneous_time checked, and read at one slice each. Three digits are measured from the stored fields:

  • σBp and σρθφ through nova.io.sourcemap.determine_digits, from the sign of ψboundary − ψaxis against Ip and from sign(q95) against Ip and B0;
  • eBp from magnitude, as above, by the ratio of the map’s integral current to Ip under each reading;
  • the sign the Grad–Shafranov relation itself implies between the map and the stored p′, FF′ — the regression’s slope sign.

σRφZ is not derivable from scalar equilibrium quantities, and determine_digits says so in its own docstring: it is supplied by the caller. All five files are written in DD 3.38–3.41, for which nova.io.sourcemap.dd_equilibrium_convention fixes it to +1, so the tuples below are completed from the declared DD version and that digit alone is not measured. It is the one digit here that could hide a disagreement.

The whole-box column is not a current comparison, and it is what an earlier draft of this fragment misreported as “within 3 to 7 per cent of one”. The measured whole-box ratio on the four DINA files is 1.6781, 1.7652, 1.7291 and 1.7290 — 68 to 77 per cent above one, not near it — while CORSICA’s own box happens to give 0.9897 because its plasma fills it.

What differs is the integration region, and nothing else. The measured source: the operator is the same, the resample is the same, and the slice’s own Ip is the same in all three columns. The DINA maps are stored on boxes spanning 6 m in R and 12 m in Z for a plasma occupying about 1250 of the 7625 scored cells, so the whole-box sum adds the current the spline’s edge terms carry in the far vacuum; restricting the sum to 0 ≤ ψN ≤ 1 gives −0.9926, −0.9903, +0.9991 and −0.9998. The interior column additionally drops the ring nearest the separatrix and lands at 0.95–0.98, which is the region an edge-sensitive measure would choose, and it is why the two columns differ.

This is now the same measurement as the edge-consistency table node (merge 90568ecf3), which integrated the grid current over the plasma and reported 0.9736 to 1.0027 of Ip on DINA 105052/1, 0.9801 to 1.0019 on DINA 105069/3 and 0.9867 onwards on CORSICA 130506/6. Like is compared with like here: the inside the last closed surface column gives −0.9926 for 105052/1 (inside their band), −0.9903 for 105069/3 (inside their band) and −0.9867 for CORSICA (their figure exactly, to four places). The two nodes read the same quantity on the same region and agree; the whole-box column was a different quantity.

The digit conclusion survives the correction. On the like-for-like region the ratio is 0.9867 to 0.9998 under eBp = 1 and 6.1995 to 6.2819 under eBp = 0, so the two readings are a factor of 2π apart and the nearer one is unambiguous on all five sources.

Three of the five are COCOS 11, which is what a DD3 file should be, and are conforming: CORSICA 130506/6, DINA 105052/1 and DINA 105069/3. Two are not conforming to the DD version they are written in. DINA 105014/1 carries σBp = −1 in a DD3 file, which reads as COCOS 17, and its flux map’s polarity is consistent with that (ψ is a minimum at the axis, and the integral ratio is +0.9991). DINA 105013/1 carries σρθφ = −1, which reads as COCOS 15, and its B0 is stored with the opposite sign from the other four.

105014/1 is the useful one for the relation rather than for the census: it is a source at σBp = −1 whose stored derivatives are still collinear with the map at +2π. So the plus sign is not a property of σBp = −1 data; it is the written equation, unchanged across the 11-to-17 mapping, exactly as the digit form predicts.

DINA 105013/1 is the one disagreement in this table and it is a finding, not a reconciliation to be chosen. Its map and its stored p′, FF′ are collinear at −2π (slope −1.00087, correlation −1.00000 to five places), which no other source does and which no rounding explains: the correlation is essentially exact. The same slice also fails determine_digits’s own dp/dψ consistency check (dp_dpsi_cross_check_pass_fraction = 0.0), so the stored pressure derivative carries the opposite sign from the one its ψ convention implies. The two facts are the same fact seen twice: this file’s flux functions are stored against the opposite flux direction from its map. Whether the archive file is wrong or the writer intended a convention the DD version does not name cannot be settled from the stored fields. DINA 105069/3 also fails that cross-check (pass_fraction = 0.0) while its regression is +2π and clean (slope +1.03385, correlation +0.99686), so the cross-check failure alone does not predict the regression sign, and the two are reported separately.

DINA sources are not sign-trustworthy by the plan’s own account, and 105013/1 is the concrete case: its digits read cleanly from its globals while the GS relation between its own map and its own flux functions disagrees with them. That is exactly the disagreement the dispatch asked to have flagged rather than resolved.

The check, and what it turns on

tests/test_flux_function_sign.py carries three tests. Two pass at the head:

  • test_the_digit_form_gives_the_same_current_in_cocos_11_and_17 — the 11-to-17 transform negates ψ and the derivative together, and the written current is unchanged;
  • test_the_cocos_17_relation_carries_plus_two_pi — it calls scripts/flux_function_sign/measure.py on the pinned DD4 layer and requires the +2π slopes to be positive and within five per cent of one, and the same call with the flux map negated to reverse every slope. The regression is run, not transcribed: a change to the measurement reddens it, and it skips with a stated reason when the layer is not cached.

The third, test_nova_owner_matches_the_cocos_17_relation, is xfail(strict=True). It drives toroidal_current_density with the DD4 state’s own dpressure_dpsi and f_df_dpsi and requires the current the flux map carries, and it separately requires the flux functions map_extraction publishes from that same state to reproduce the same current. The two assertions are not decoration: a bare flip of the owner’s leading sign satisfies the first and breaks the second, because the extractor publishes the negated derivative, and a change at the extractor alone breaks the first. Only a repair that moves the derivative convention and the sign together turns the strict xfail into an XPASS, which the suite then reports rather than absorbing.

The gate ran in one fresh process from the worktree:

UV_PROJECT_ENVIRONMENT=/home/ITER/mcintos/Code/nova/.venv PYTHONPATH=$PWD \
  uv run --no-sync pytest -p no:cacheprovider --basetemp=<scratch> \
  tests/test_flux_function_sign.py -v

The result is 2 passed, 1 xfailed in 8.55s with EXIT=0. The module served was the worktree’s own (.../flux-function-sign/nova/__init__.py), printed in the log header beside the revision and the command. The negative control makes the negated-map arm the plain map, which reddens test_the_cocos_17_relation_carries_plus_two_pi on its all(value < 0.0 for value in control) assertion with EXIT=1, while the head arm passes it. The log is the run’s negative-control.log.

The repair node must move every site listed here together, because each of them publishes or consumes the negated derivative. The paired sites, in the order the repair should read them:

  • nova/equilibrium/map_extraction.py:292 and :293 — p_prime = -coefficients[0] / TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR and ff_prime = -mu_0 * coefficients[1] / TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR. This is the publisher: it states in its docstring that it returns the negated derivative, and the measured ratio of its output to the DD’s dpressure_dpsi on the DD4 state is negative (median −1.19 to −1.25 per slice), which is what the paired change must turn positive.
  • nova/equilibrium/convention.py:84–91 — the docstring’s p′ = -dp/dΦ statement and toroidal_current_density’s leading -TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR.
  • nova/equilibrium/convention.py: flux_function_pressure and flux_function_toroidal_field — they integrate a negated gradient from the boundary inwards with a plus sign, so they invert with the same convention.
  • nova/imas/edge_consistency.py:151 — calls the owner with the profile’s p_prime, so it follows whichever sense the two sites publish and needs no edit if both move together.
  • nova/media/sources/diiid_efit.py:135 and nova/media/sources/mast_efit.py:124 — feed stored dpressure_dpsi/pprime straight into p_prime, the same mismatch this node measured on the DD4 state.
  • nova/imas/operate.py:64 — radius * p_prime + ff_prime/(mu_0 radius) builds a current density from the stored derivative names without the owner.
  • nova/imas/profiles.py — prescribe_current_density carries flux_derivative_scale = 2π with a plus sign, which is the DD’s derivative sense; it is the one site that already reads the relation this node measured, and the repair should leave it alone.

What this does not cover

The measurement reads one DD4 layer and one slice of each of five archive files. It does not measure σRφZ, which no scalar field carries; it does not settle whether 105013/1’s file or its writer is at fault; and it changes no production module. The owner repair is f-ddc-006’s work, and this node’s strict xfail is what will report the repair landing.
