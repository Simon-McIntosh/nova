Writing the fitted equivalent forward-field error into the corpus current columns, rather than into the build seam, carries the correction into the solve on all four rows: every fitted arm's installed current vector equals the measured vector less that frame's fitted correction over the nineteen shipped conductors, entry by entry, with no conductor left unchanged. On 137D the installed F1A current is −231.293 kA·turn, which is the measured −177.235 less the fitted +54.058. The earlier record's "no movement" was an instrument artefact: at the build seam the current adapter replaces the operator's current with its own resolution, so the correction never reached the field. Applied at the seam, the adapter now refuses the vector outright and names the reason; applied to the corpus columns it accepts it, and the solved state changes.

Where the fitted arm converges it moves the lower X-point further from EFIT's null, and where it does not converge it loses the plasma. 146D and 146L both converge and both move their lower X-point from within 1.8 and 4.9 cm of EFIT's null to 8.97 cm, and both land on the same terminal state. 137D and 179L both refuse a step with their support collapsed, from 339 cells to 10 and from 294 cells to 6, which is a different equilibrium rather than a smaller offset. No row closes any part of the offset, so the fitted vector is not the missing physics that the offset was attributed to: applied where the solve reads it, it is a correction that makes the agreement with EFIT worse.

How the correction reaches the solve

The forward solve builds its operator from the corpus magnetics_* columns, and its current adapter then installs its own resolution read from those same columns. A correction written into the build seam is therefore replaced before the field is built: the operator's current disagrees with the adapter's, and the adapter refuses it. Writing the correction into the columns, scaled per conductor by −error / (1000 · applied_multiplier), makes the build and the adapter read one vector, so the field and the installed current carry the same correction and the adapter's agreement check passes.

The two controls are what make that a measurement rather than a claim. Pairing each measured arm with itself reddens the solved-vector check on all nineteen conductors of all four rows, so the check can see an absent correction; and applying the same vector at the build seam alone makes the adapter refuse with build-time conductor current disagrees with the shipped current resolution for F1A: build −231293.185532 A, resolved −177235.036006 A; a conductor-current override belongs in the corpus columns this adapter reads, not the build seam.

What follows

Nothing in the prescription or the solver was changed. The correction reaches the solve and does not close the offset on any row, so the offset is not the fitted conductor-field mismatch applied at the currents, whatever the fit's representation of the external-field difference says. The earlier record's ruling-out of a per-coil calibration error stands, and the common-mode explanation it left standing still has no measured counterpart: the fitted vector is a change in the conductor response or in EFIT's implied external field, and applying it as a current correction is not the same intervention.

Measurement

The four measured and four fitted arms were solved in one allocation on the titan rung, four cards, with the fits taken from the measured arms' own solved bases and the fitted arms reading those fits. The H200 lane carried seven of its eight cards at submission, so a four-card cohort could not be placed there and the run took the next rung; this is a titan measurement and its compile and execute walls are titan's. Every row's installed current vector is the solve's own record, written by the adapter at the moment it installed it. The build-seam control ran as its own single-card allocation against the same fit receipt. 137D's fitted arm was long enough on the active set to be re-solved in a second single-card allocation, and that retry reached the same terminal linearization count and the same residual to ten significant figures (9.126644338·10-3), so its non-convergence is a property of the vector rather than of the allocation that first ran it. The solved-current check and its negative control read those records only.
