DIII-D forward convergence: what stops each gate frame

All five DIII-D gate frames fail the registered 1e-5 fixed-point criterion, at relative residuals from 0.0273 to 4.28. The banked receipts localise the failure to one mechanism on four frames and to a refusal upstream of the fixed point on the fifth. It is not an alternating-mask switch: the active set has stopped changing (mask difference zero) at the terminal promotion of every branch, and the relative residual is bit-identical across the last two promotions. All ten seeds are measured below. Nine of them stall with the plasma still confined and carrying its current; one (frame 146's diverted seed) is a plasma driven to the top of the vessel and lost; one (frame 144's limited seed) refuses at axis identification before any promotion runs. The promoted state reproduces the same residual to the bit, so the outer loop either stops on mask stability or spends its whole trip budget without moving. Step acceptance is not measured here and no accepted-step count is claimed anywhere below. The trace the probe stored is the solver's fixed-shape inner-iteration record, padded with NOT_EXECUTED decisions and nan applied factors (nova/equilibrium/fixed_point.py:235-255); in every stored arm all 24 slots are padding, so reading the trace's length or its truthiness as a count reports 24 of 24 for a solve that recorded nothing. What the record does carry is the loop-level evidence, and the terminal trip's accepted count follows from the solver's own predicate: ACTIVE_SET_SETTLED is conditioned on accepted_newton_promotions == 0 (fixed_point.py:4759-4772). On the bank that is the outer active-set loop terminating on mask stability rather than on the residual criterion.

Is the stall the mechanism, or the consequence of a plasma that left the vessel?

The frozen-mask terminal is established for four frames. Whether it is the cause of the non-convergence or the symptom of a plasma that has lost its position or its current is a separate question, and the banked record answers it only partly:

  • Frame 146 — holds, measured below. Its kept terminal places the magnetic axis at Z = +1.56 m, which is above the top of the DIII-D limiter (the limiter ring spans Z = −1.359 to +1.363 m; the ±1.664 m figure is the solve grid's bounding box, not the wall), with the closed core collapsed to 8 cells and 0.043 m² against a confined current of 0.039× the target. A column driven to the top of the vessel carrying four percent of its current is a plasma lost by vertical displacement, and the residual it stalls at is the mismatch that displacement leaves.
  • Frame 44 — does not hold, measured below. Its kept terminal does achieve the limited topology at residual 4.28, the largest of the cohort, while its seed requested diverted. The inference drawn from that in the banked record — a lost plasma — is falsified by the re-solve: the confined core carries 1.097× the target current with its axis inside the wall. A topology change alone does not establish plasma loss, because the achieved class is a property of the terminal state and the current is a separate measurement.
  • Frames 179 and 137 terminate with the topology their seed asked for and residuals of 4.18e-1 and 6.24e-2; 179's solved axis sits inside the wall but low and inboard of the label's, which is the displacement the vertical-force-balance plan already documents.

Two facts follow, and both sharpen the verdict rather than merely confirming it. First, the stall is not a slow contraction that the settled exit cuts short: with that exit removed the loop spends all 16 trips and the residual is bit-identical on every one of them, so continuing buys nothing. Second, it is not a plasma that has left the vessel on this frame: the axis sits inside the wall on both seeds, and the confined core carries 1.057× the target current on the limited seed and 0.763× on the diverted one. What stalls is the outer promotion itself: the promotion returns the same residual to the bit, so the fixed-point map is idempotent at that state. (The step-acceptance count for this arm was not measured — see the note on the withdrawn counts below — so this reading rests on the residual and the mask difference, which are recorded.)

Frame 144, diverted seed. Its limited seed refuses at axis identification, but its diverted seed solves, and it is the clearest instance of the pattern: the mask difference is 0 at the first trip and stays there, the shipped policy settles after one trip at residual 5.927, the continued arm spends sixteen trips at the same number, with a mask difference of 0 throughout. Frame 137, limited seed. Frame 146 — the one frame where the plasma really is lost. Nothing above applies to its diverted seed, and the record has to say so plainly rather than fold it into the fixed-mask finding. There the mask does not begin frozen: it moves 27 → 40 → 1 → 0 across four trips and the residual tracks it (0.128 → 0.222 → 0.065 → 0.055), so the loop is travelling. What it travels towards is a state with no plasma in the machine: the axis ends at R=1.234, Z=+1.563 m, which is 0.20 m above the limiter's top edge at Z = +1.363 m — the axis is outside the vessel, not near its top wall — and the field closes around only 8 core cells totalling 0.043 m² — a sliver against the top plate — carrying 0.039× the target current. The residual then freezes at 5.496e-02. This is vertical displacement to the point of plasma loss, and it is the only frame in the cohort whose non-convergence is explained that way. Its limited seed is a different story and belongs with 179, 44 and 137: the plasma is confined there (axis at R=1.725, Z=+0.027, 382 cells, 2.029 m², 1.134× the target current) and the residual sits at 6.350e-02 beside a mask difference of 0 from the first trip. So the two seeds of frame 146 fail for two different reasons, and only the diverted one is a plasma loss.

What the four present-plasma frames have in common. On 179, 44, 137 and 144's diverted seed the fixed-point map is stationary from the state the seed enters: the mask difference reaches zero and stays there, and the outer promotion returns the state it was given. The residual the gate reads is therefore a property of the seed, not of a path the loop failed to walk, and continuing the loop cannot lower it. Frame 146's diverted seed is the counter-example that makes the point: where the mask does move, the residual moves with it, which is what a working promotion looks like. Its failure is upstream of that — it moves to a state with no confined plasma left to converge.

The area, corrected. The probe first reported a closed plasma area of 0.0 m² on every arm beside a confined current of about 1 MA. That was a defect in the measurement, not in the plasma: the area was counted from a mask attribute the domain partition does not carry, and the handler that exists so one bad read cannot kill a frame turned the error into an all-false mask. The area is now the partition's own CORE label — the cells the field closes around the axis and connects to — and it is re-derived from the saved grids without re-solving. Re-derived: frame 179 reads 1.233 m² diverted and 1.833 m² limited against a label field of 1.679 m²; frame 44 reads 1.870 m² diverted and 1.126 m² limited against 1.248 m². Frames 137 and 146 are counted by the same code path in the probe itself (_core_mask over PlasmaDomain.CORE), which is the path the control exercises.

Which surface bounds each terminal. Two surfaces compete to bound a plasma: the separatrix through the admitted X-point, and the outermost closed surface that encloses the axis and still stays inside the wall. The inner one wins. The comparison cannot be made by taking the largest flux anywhere on the limiter ring: on a diverted equilibrium the ring beneath the X-point lies in the private-flux region, where the flux exceeds the separatrix value, so that rule reports a contact on the divertor floor while the surface it implies touches no wall at all. The class here is read by the engine's own axis-connected, saddle-aware flood fill (nova.equilibrium.connectivity_boundary.host_boundary_read) over each stored terminal flux grid, with the governed 84-point DIII-D limiter as the wall, and turned into a class by nova.equilibrium.boundary_comparison.classify_boundary_mode on its signed margin (boundary_competition.py, one read per stored arm, no solve run; the values below are the committed docs/figures/diiid-forward-convergence/boundary-competition.json). The margin is the wall's normalised flux less the X-point's, so a negative margin means the wall closes first; a margin of −∞ means the read found no X-point at all, and the wall is then the only obstruction. Recommended fix

Module: nova/equilibrium/fixed_point.py, the active-set loop in _active_set_newton_krylov and its reconcile reduction.

The stopping rule that has to change. Two predicates in the active-set loop end the solve at a stable mask without consulting the residual, and both are in nova/equilibrium/fixed_point.py:

  • stagnated (lines 4750–4758) requires selected_difference == 0 and selected_residual == previous_live_residual — the residual repeating, at any value — and reports ACTIVE_SET_STAGNATED.
  • settled (lines 4759–4772) requires selected_difference == 0, accepted_newton_promotions == 0 on the terminal trip and selected_state == state — the promotion returning the state it was given — and reports ACTIVE_SET_SETTLED.

Neither carries selected_residual <= convergence_tolerance in the unfrozen case, and that is the defect measured here: the loop is correct to conclude that its map has stopped moving, and wrong to report that as its answer. The evidence is that the gate reads ACTIVE_SET_SETTLED at residuals from 2.7e-2 (frame 179) to 4.28 (frame 44) — two to five decades above the 1e-5 criterion — while the residual is bit-identical across every trip of every measured arm and the mask difference is 0, so the terminal trip accepted no promotion because the map had become stationary rather than because the step was refused. With the exits disabled the loop spends all sixteen trips at a bit-identical residual, so the omitted clause is the only thing standing between a stationary map and a falsely converged flag. Note the asymmetry at lines 4768–4771: the frozen-topology branch of the same predicate does require selected_residual <= convergence_tolerance, so the guard exists in the file and is simply not applied when the topology is free. Two edits, in this order:

  1. Carry the inner step-acceptance receipt. Publish attempted_newton_promotions and accepted_newton_promotions per trip on FixedPointResult and through ForwardSolveReceipt. The counts are already accumulated on the solver's fixed point result (fixed_point.py:4995-4996), so this is a carry-through on the receipt rather than a new measurement; the probe now reads them there. They must not be reconstructed from globalisation_decisions: that trace is a fixed-shape record padded with NOT_EXECUTED and nan, so its length is the padding and it reads 24 of 24 on an arm that recorded nothing. A frozen mask with every step accepted is a different defect from a frozen mask with the step refused, and until the counts are carried through the receipt cannot tell them apart.
  2. Separate the three endings at a frozen mask. Once the residual is part of the predicate, the remaining states are distinguishable and should not collapse into one reason: a mask that is stable with the residual at tolerance is convergence; a stable mask with the promotion returning the same state is a stationary map (ACTIVE_SET_STATIONARY); a stable mask with promotions attempted and refused by globalisation is a different defect again (ACTIVE_SET_STEP_REFUSED). On these frames the second is what the predicate records, because ACTIVE_SET_SETTLED is conditioned on zero accepted promotions in the terminal trip; without the carried counts a later frame whose step was refused would be indistinguishable from it. Each must carry the residual it stalled at, which the gate already reads.

Frames it should converge, listed by the terminal residual each one stalls at: 179's limited seed at 2.73e-2, 146's limited seed at 5.50e-2, 137's limited seed at 5.68e-2, 137's diverted seed at 6.24e-2, 44's limited seed at 1.97e-1, 179's diverted seed at 4.18e-1, 44's diverted seed at 4.28, and 144's diverted seed at 5.93. All eight carry a confined plasma at a frozen mask, so the stopping rule is the defect on all eight, and the order above is the order in which a reader should expect them to close once the residual enters the predicate. The two smallest are a confined plasma already inside the wall; 5.93 is the largest, not among the smallest — that arm's residual grew under a mask the loop had already frozen, so its recovery is the least certain of the eight and it should be measured last rather than ranked with the easy ones. Two terminals are outside what this change can reach: 146's diverted seed is a vertically lost plasma (below), and 144's limited seed refuses at axis identification, a defect upstream of the fixed point that no change to the active-set loop can help.
