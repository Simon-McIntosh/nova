Frame 144: why the limited seed refuses at axis identification

Frame 144's limited-branch seed is the one refusal in the DIII-D cohort: it stops before any Newton promotion, while the diverted seed for the same frame reaches a terminal residual of 5.93 (5.927126420463732 from probe.json, branches.diverted.arms.default.terminal_residual). The question is whether the seed really has no admissible axis, or whether an axis is present and the identification rejected it.

It is present and was rejected. The seed carries a flux maximum at R=1.7880 m, Z=+0.0171 m, 0.03 m from the EFIT label axis and 0.03 m from the diverted solution's axis at R=1.759, Z=+0.013. A missing axis is therefore not the explanation.

The rejecting rule

The admitted bit is composed by qualified_o_candidates.qualify, nova/equilibrium/topology.py:1080–1099, and the comparison that rejected this candidate is the third conjunct at

governed_connection = governed_size >= connection_floor   # topology.py:1097

The threshold is nova/equilibrium/topology.py:1076–1078,

connection_floor = jnp.maximum(jnp.floor(_MATERIAL_CONNECTED_FRACTION * material_cell_count), 1)

with _MATERIAL_CONNECTED_FRACTION = 0.01 at nova/equilibrium/topology.py:41. A candidate is additionally screened at topology.py:1081 (finite position and flux) and topology.py:1082 (a resolved boundary).

The values it read for this point, from the census diagnostics: material cell count 1089, so connection_floor = 10 cells; the lattice node at the axis is (1.7871, 0.0166) and reads axis_node_inside_material = True. The candidate therefore lies inside the material and had to own at least ten governed connected cells to be admitted. Its position and flux are finite, so the first conjunct does not exclude it.

Each candidate was then classified by the winding of the gradient on a 64-sample circle of 0.113 m — about one lattice spacing — around it, taken from the lattice central-difference gradient bilinearly interpolated between nodes, the same field the boundary winding is measured on. Dropped as index 0: (0.8819, 1.2719), (0.9200, 1.3351), (1.0269, −1.5422), (1.1139, −1.5810) and (1.6251, 1.6058).

Coverage does not close, and the loop radius is why. At the 0.113 m spacing the kept indices sum to +2 against a 1.9994 boundary winding, a difference of zero. Repeated with the loop radius held at 0.018 m — below the 0.037 m closest separation of the two suspect pairs, so that a genuine pair would read as two points rather than none — the sum falls to +1 against the same winding: difference +1, and the run exits nonzero. All four members of the two suspect pairs read 0 at that radius, and so does (1.0030, 1.5082), which reads +1 at the spacing.

A winding index is topological only while no other zero lies inside the loop, so a sum that changes with the loop radius is a statement about the sampling, not about the field. The honest reading is that the census closes at one lattice spacing and not at half of it, and that the difference is carried by candidates whose gradient zeros the bilinearly-interpolated lattice gradient does not resolve at the finer radius. The axis-rejection finding below does not rest on this account: it rests on the flux maximum at (1.7880, 0.0171), which every radius tested locates.

The pairs are therefore not established as spline artefacts. What the finer radius shows is that the two members of each pair cannot be separated by a winding at this resolution at all — the classification is scale dependent and a resolution study, not a single radius, is what would settle them.

Why the run jobs failed. Job 1307827 failed after two minutes inside census.py: it reshaped the whole 1221-node state vector onto the 33×33 plasma lattice (1089 nodes), because the vector carries 132 wall nodes after the plasma nodes. Job 1307848 failed in seconds on a diagnostics line that named the prepared profile without binding it. Neither failure was in product code.

Recommended fix

Admitted. The rejection is a threshold, not a physics result: a fixed 0.01 of the whole material cell count is applied to every candidate, so a genuinely confined axis in a small closed component is refused whenever the grid is large. Change topology.py:1076–1078 so the floor is measured against the candidate's own governed component rather than the total material count, and have qualify return the three conjuncts alongside the admitted bit so NoQualifiedAxisError names which one failed.

Test that would have caught it. A limited read seeded with a map whose only extremum inside the material sits in a component smaller than _MATERIAL_CONNECTED_FRACTION of the full material count, asserting that the read admits the axis (or raises naming governed_connection with the counts it compared). The existing tests/test_oracle_axis_admission.py and tests/test_unqualified_axis_eager_routes.py assert the admitted and refused outcomes but never which conjunct produced a refusal, which is why a seed can be recorded as having "no qualified axis" without the record distinguishing a missing axis from a rejected one.

If a probe needs a hook in nova/: none was needed. The census reads the benchmark's public seed portfolio and the operator's inside_material, both already exposed.
