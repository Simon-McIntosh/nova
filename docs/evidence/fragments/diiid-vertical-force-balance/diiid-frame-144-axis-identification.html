Frame 144: why the limited seed refuses at axis identification

Frame 144's limited-branch seed is the one refusal in the DIII-D cohort: it stops before any Newton promotion, while the diverted seed for the same frame reaches a terminal residual of 5.93 (5.927126420463732 from probe.json, branches.diverted.arms.default.terminal_residual). The question is whether the seed really has no admissible axis, or whether an axis is present and the identification rejected it.

It is present. The seed carries a flux maximum at R=1.7880 m, Z=+0.0171 m, 0.03 m from the EFIT label axis and 0.03 m from the diverted solution's axis at R=1.759, Z=+0.013, so a missing axis is not the explanation. Measured below, that maximum also passes every conjunct of the axis-admission test on the cached seed — which locates the defect in the record's provenance rather than in the threshold.

What this means for the refusal, and what it does not. The earlier draft of this fragment reported the third conjunct, governed_connection, as the comparison that rejected this axis, on the strength of material_cell_count 1089 and a floor of 10. That reading is withdrawn: by measurement the comparison passes comfortably. The refusal recorded in probe.json below is therefore not reproduced from the state this probe reads (prepared["cold"].branches.flux[0]), and the seed that the refused limited branch actually held is the open question. The identification is not refusing a qualified axis on the evidence measured here; on this seed it finds one.

Coverage does not close, and the loop radius is why. At the 0.113 m spacing the kept indices sum to +2 against a 1.9994 boundary winding, a difference of zero. Repeated with the loop radius held at 0.018 m — below the 0.037 m closest separation of the two suspect pairs, so that a genuine pair would read as two points rather than none — the sum falls to +1 against the same winding: difference +1, and the run exits nonzero. All four members of the two suspect pairs read 0 at that radius, and so does (1.0030, 1.5082), which reads +1 at the spacing.

A winding index is topological only while no other zero lies inside the loop, so a sum that changes with the loop radius is a statement about the sampling, not about the field. The honest reading is that the census closes at one lattice spacing and not at half of it, and that the difference is carried by candidates whose gradient zeros the bilinearly-interpolated lattice gradient does not resolve at the finer radius. The axis-rejection finding below does not rest on this account: it rests on the flux maximum at (1.7880, 0.0171), which every radius tested locates.

The pairs are therefore not established as spline artefacts. What the finer radius shows is that the two members of each pair cannot be separated by a winding at this resolution at all — the classification is scale dependent and a resolution study, not a single radius, is what would settle them.

Why the run jobs failed. Job 1307827 failed after two minutes inside census.py: it reshaped the whole 1221-node state vector onto the 33×33 plasma lattice (1089 nodes), because the vector carries 132 wall nodes after the plasma nodes. Job 1307848 failed in seconds on a diagnostics line that named the prepared profile without binding it. Neither failure was in product code.

Recommended fix

First, name the state. The measurement above shows the threshold admitting a genuinely confined axis, so correcting the threshold would not have changed this refusal. What remains undetermined is which seed the refused limited branch held: the branch record (probe.json, frame 144, branches.limited) names a refusal with an empty arms map and no seed reference, and the cached cold seed reproduces the opposite outcome. Recording the seed on the refusal record — its flux vector or a content hash beside the status — is what makes a refusal reproducible from the record alone, and it is the fix this investigation asks for.

On the threshold itself. A fixed _MATERIAL_CONNECTED_FRACTION of the whole material cell count is still a coarse instrument: had this candidate's governed component been small, it would have been refused on a grid-size test rather than a topology test. Measuring the floor against the candidate's own governed component would be better, but it is a robustness change, not the repair for frame 144 — and it must not be landed as one.

Test that would have caught the reporting defect. A limited read whose only in-material extremum sits in a component smaller than the floor, asserting that the read admits the axis or raises naming governed_connection with the counts it compared. The existing tests/test_oracle_axis_admission.py and tests/test_unqualified_axis_eager_routes.py assert the admitted and refused outcomes but never which conjunct produced a refusal, which is why a seed can be recorded as having "no qualified axis" without the record distinguishing a missing axis from a rejected one.

If a probe needs a hook in nova/: none was needed: the probe wraps the public qualified_o_candidates and calls the operator's own helpers.
