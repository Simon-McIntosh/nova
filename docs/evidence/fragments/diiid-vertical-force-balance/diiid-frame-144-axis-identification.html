Frame 144: why the limited seed refuses at axis identification

The convergence record carries one refusal in the DIII-D cohort. Frame 144's limited-branch seed stops before any Newton promotion, at axis identification, while the diverted seed for the same frame solves to a terminal residual of 5.927e+00 with its axis at R=1.759 m, Z=+0.013 m carrying 1.073× the target current. The rule that rejected the seed

The admitted bit reaching that call is the reduction of qualified_o_candidates over the magnetic-axis candidates of the seed map, and the predicate itself is qualify at nova/equilibrium/topology.py:1080–1101. It admits a candidate only when three conditions hold together:

  • jnp.all(jnp.isfinite(data_o[:3])) — the candidate has a finite position and flux after polishing;
  • resolved = jnp.all(jnp.isfinite(data_b[:3])) — the boundary the candidate selects is resolved, so the trace to the wall terminates on a datum rather than a hole;
  • governed_connection — the flood component grown from the candidate's own cell intersects inside_material in at least _MATERIAL_CONNECTED_FRACTION (nova/equilibrium/topology.py:41, value 0.01) of the material cell count.

So the refusal is asserted by require_qualified_axis (nova/equilibrium/topology.py:220–228) acting on a predicate at topology.py:1080–1101. Which of the three conjuncts failed on this seed is not yet measured; the census below is the instrument that separates them, and the distinction is the whole question, because governed_connection is the only conjunct that can reject a candidate whose critical point exists and is finite.

What separates the two possible defects

The refusal message names a property of the admission test, not of the seed. Two readings fit it, and they prescribe opposite repairs:

  1. No admissible axis exists. The seed carries no critical point that is an extremum, or only extrema whose flood cannot reach the material grid. Then the seed is genuinely unusable for a limited read and the defect is upstream, in the cold-seed portfolio.
  2. A qualified axis exists and the identification rejected it. An extremum is present, finite and connected to the material, but one conjunct — most plausibly the connection fraction, which is a fixed 0.01 of the whole material cell count and therefore scales with the grid rather than with the candidate — refused it.

Reading (1) is not available without the census. A seed reported as carrying "no qualified axis" is indistinguishable, from the refusal alone, from a seed whose axis was present and refused, and the record above shows the divergent seed of the same frame does carry an axis that both the solver and the presence read agree on.

The census and its coverage check

scripts/prototypes/diiid_axis_identification/census.py is the independent read. It builds the frame's limited seed from the same bank cold portfolio the convergence probe uses, forms the gradient on the lattice, locates every zero crossing of both gradient components to sub-cell precision, classifies each by the eigenvalues of the bilinear-interpolated Hessian — extrema count +1, saddles -1 — and closes the account with the winding number of the gradient direction around the domain boundary.

The winding number is what makes the census an instrument rather than a search. On a bounded domain the boundary winding equals the sum of the indices of the enclosed zeros, so a difference between the two is a proof that a critical point was missed. The census exits non-zero on a non-zero difference precisely so that a null census cannot be read as an absence.

Recommended fix

Module: nova/equilibrium/topology.py, qualified_o_candidates, the qualify closure at lines 1080–1101.

Change: make the failure distinguishable before changing what it admits. The predicate currently returns one boolean for three conjuncts, so the host-side require_qualified_axis can only report that something failed. Have qualify return, alongside the admitted bit, the three conjuncts it composed, carry them into the topology qualification receipt, and have NoQualifiedAxisError name the failing conjunct and the candidate count. Only with that does the fix follow: if the census shows a finite, connected extremum refused by governed_connection, the fraction is the thing to change — and it should be measured against the candidate's own governed cell count rather than against the whole material grid, because a fixed fraction of a grid that changes with the expansion is not a physical criterion. If the census shows no extremum at all, the repair belongs in the cold-seed portfolio and this module should not be touched.

Test that would have caught it: a case that seeds the limited read with a map whose only extremum sits in a small closed component — larger than _MATERIAL_CONNECTED_FRACTION of its own flood but smaller than the same fraction of the full material count — and asserts that the read either admits the axis or raises naming governed_connection as the failed conjunct. The existing admission tests (tests/test_oracle_axis_admission.py, tests/test_unqualified_axis_eager_routes.py) assert the admitted and refused outcomes but never which conjunct produced the refusal, which is why a seed can be recorded as having "no qualified axis" without the record distinguishing a missing axis from a rejected one.
