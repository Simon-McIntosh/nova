Summary

Frame 146's diverted branch terminates at trip 3 with its axis at R=1.2196 m, Z=+1.5631 m — 0.20 m above the DIII-D limiter top at Z=+1.363 m — 0.0412×Ip confined and a terminal residual of 5.61e-2. The loss itself happens at trip 2. Frames 137, 144, 179 and 44 keep their plasma.

The mechanism is the second active-set promotion. At trip 1 the two frames are indistinguishable: frame 146 holds 0.912×Ip at Z=−0.346 m and frame 137 holds 0.920×Ip at Z=−0.362 m, both inside the limiter, within 1% of each other on both measures. At trip 2 their promotion steps differ by 13.5× — 83.21 Wb against 6.156 Wb — and frame 146 changes 39 mask entries where frame 137 changes 20. That one step throws the axis to Z=+1.556 m, outside the limiter, and leaves 0.046×Ip confined.

Correction to the earlier reading of this fragment. A previous version named a 25.28 Wb first-promotion step as the discriminator against frame 137's 1.40 Wb. Those numbers came from a defect in the tabulation: every re-solve reported amplitude_history[0], the first promotion of the run, whatever trip the row described. Measured per trip, the first promotion is not where the frames differ, and 25 Wb is not a quantity this trajectory reproduces. The discriminator is the trip-2 step.

Where the two branches part at trip 2

Frame 137's promotion changes fewer mask entries each trip: 24, then 20, then 9, then 9, then 0, and each step moves a few Wb of flux. Frame 146 changes more on its second trip than on its first — 27, then 39, then 0 — and lands 83 Wb of column height on a seed 151 Wb from the label. The axis does not drift upward over several trips; it is carried out of the vessel and left there in one step. After trip 3 the set is empty, the step falls to 3.37 Wb, and the residual plateaus at 5.6e-2 with nothing left to correct.

The plateau itself is not a symptom: frame 137 plateaus at 6.2e-2 and keeps its plasma. It is the vertical position and the confined fraction that separate them.

Trip 1 is healthy on both, so neither the seed nor the first promotion carries the displacement. Trip 2 does: 83.21 Wb against 6.156 Wb, changing more mask growing before it collapses.

The mechanism. The promotion executed on the second active-set trip of frame 146 is unbounded relative to the state it is applied to. It changes 39 mask entries where the control at the same trip changes 20, and it moves the flux by 83 Wb — more than half the seed's own 151 Wb distance from the label. The resulting state has its axis above the limiter and almost no confined current. Every later trip inherits that state with no mask changes left and cannot bring it back.

The trip-1 field already carries a higher maximum than the axis the solver reported, and the trip-2 state lands on a minimum instead. A census of every polished extremum (script maxima_census.py, receipt trip-trajectory/maxima-census.json) finds, on frame 146's trip-1 grid, a maximum of 1.958 Wb at R=1.584 m, Z=0.000 m inside the limiter, against 1.761 Wb at the axis the solve reports (R=1.688 m, Z=−0.346 m). The interior saddle at R=1.540 m, Z=−0.040 m (Hessian determinant −6.65, eigenvalues −1.0 and +6.5, winding index −1) separates those two maxima, so trip 1 is already a two-lobed field and the solver's axis selection picks the lower lobe. Frame 137 shows the same structure at trip 1 (maximum 2.003 Wb at R=1.531 m, Z=0.000 m against a reported axis flux of 1.883 Wb), so axis selection below the field maximum is not by itself the discriminator. What separates the frames is where the next trip goes. At trip 2 frame 146's reported axis (R=1.211 m, Z=+1.556 m) sits at −5.28 Wb, while the field's maximum inside the limiter is 1.211 Wb at R=2.013 m, Z=−0.400 m and the nearest polished extremum is a minimum at R=1.102 m, Z=+1.347 m (−6.34 Wb). The step did not hand the plasma to the upper lobe; it moved the axis onto a low-flux extremum above the limiter. Frame 137's trip 2 keeps its axis at 1.973 Wb on a field whose maximum is 2.383 Wb. So axis misidentification across a saddle is present at trip 1 on both frames, and the trip-2 step makes it terminal on frame 146: the promotion moves the axis to a lower extremum rather than to the field's maximum, which leaves 0.046×Ip confined. Both findings belong to the same mechanism.

3. Recommended fix

Module and place. nova/equilibrium/fixed_point.py, in the active-set promotion that builds the next boundary set from the current promotion — the same routine that admits or withdraws boundary cells. A step-size or trust-region bound belongs there, applied to the promotion flux update before the cells are admitted: bound the step by the reliable flux-surface set and by the flux distance between the current state and the seed, and refuse a promotion that admits more cells than the set it replaces unless that trip reduced the residual.

The test that would have caught it. A forward-solve test that runs frame 146's diverted branch to the second trip and asserts two things: that the promotion step norm does not exceed the state's distance from the seed, and that a terminal whose mask has stopped changing cannot carry an axis outside the limiter. With an 83 Wb step on a 151 Wb seed, the first assertion fails at 55% of the budget, which is the signature of a first-order method that has lost its trust region.

Relation to the stopping-rule fix (run r-20261107T141006905473, which now requires an attempted promotion before the settle exit). That fix makes the settle exit honest: a branch cannot report convergence without having attempted a promotion. This finding is the complementary half. The stopping rule governs when the loop ends; the trip-2 defect governs what a single step may do while it is still running. Frame 146 does attempt promotions, so it satisfies the new stopping rule and still loses its plasma: the step bound is what the trajectory shows is missing, and the stopping rule would not have caught it.
