Summary

Frame 146's diverted branch terminates at trip 3 with its axis at R=1.2196 m, Z=+1.5631 m — 0.20 m above the DIII-D limiter top at Z=+1.363 m — 0.0412×Ip confined and a terminal residual of 5.61e-2. The loss itself happens at trip 2. Frames 137, 144, 179 and 44 keep their plasma.

The mechanism is the second active-set promotion. At trip 1 the two frames are indistinguishable: frame 146 holds 0.912×Ip at Z=−0.346 m and frame 137 holds 0.920×Ip at Z=−0.362 m, both inside the limiter, within 1% of each other on both measures. At trip 2 their promotion steps differ by 13.5× — 83.21 Wb against 6.156 Wb — and frame 146 changes 39 mask entries where frame 137 changes 20. That one step throws the axis to Z=+1.556 m, outside the limiter, and leaves 0.046×Ip confined.

Correction to the earlier reading of this fragment. A previous version named a 25.28 Wb first-promotion step as the discriminator against frame 137's 1.40 Wb. Those numbers came from a defect in the tabulation: every re-solve reported amplitude_history[0], the first promotion of the run, whatever trip the row described. Measured per trip, the first promotion is not where the frames differ, and 25 Wb is not a quantity this trajectory reproduces. The discriminator is the trip-2 step.

Where the two branches part at trip 2

Frame 137's promotion changes fewer mask entries each trip: 24, then 20, then 9, then 9, then 0, and each step moves a few Wb of flux. Frame 146 changes more on its second trip than on its first — 27, then 39, then 0 — and lands 83 Wb of column height on a seed 151 Wb from the label. The axis does not drift upward over several trips; it is carried out of the vessel and left there in one step. After trip 3 the set is empty, the step falls to 3.37 Wb, and the residual plateaus at 5.6e-2 with nothing left to correct.

The plateau itself is not a symptom: frame 137 plateaus at 6.2e-2 and keeps its plasma. It is the vertical position and the confined fraction that separate them.

Three facts follow, each from the index rather than from a grid sample. There is a second +1 maximum inside the limiter, at R=1.585 m, Z=−0.034 m, 0.04 m from the −1 saddle: the trip-1 field is genuinely two-lobed, and the grid argmax at Z=0.000 was pointing at it. And a −1 saddle with flux above the reported axis is therefore fully explained — the reported axis is simply not the field's maximum.

At trip 2 the reported axis (R=1.211 m, Z=+1.556 m) does not converge to any stationary point either (shift 0.073 m, converged false), while the field's maximum inside the limiter is 1.211 Wb at R=2.3275 m, Z=−0.400 m and the nearest polished extremum is a minimum at R=1.102 m, Z=+1.347 m (−6.34 Wb). The step did not hand the plasma to the upper lobe; it left the axis on a non-stationary point above the limiter.

Frame 137's trip-1 argmax is R=1.531 m, not R=0.531 m: the census mask is the limiter interior, and R=1.531 m is inboard of the DIII-D limiter's outer wall but well outside its inner wall near R=1.0, so the mask is correct. Frame 137's trip 2 does carry a true +1 maximum at R=1.498 m, Z=−0.043 m (2.393 Wb), and its reported axis holds 1.973 Wb.

So the mechanism is axis identification, and it is what the trip-2 step makes terminal. On frame 146 the axis moving 0.33 m onto a non-stationary point at trip 1 precedes the promotion that leaves the reported axis above the limiter and 0.046×Ip confined. Frame 137 keeps a genuine extremum near its reported axis. Both findings belong to the same mechanism.

3. Recommended fix

Module and place. nova/equilibrium/fixed_point.py, in the active-set promotion that builds the next boundary set from the current promotion — the same routine that admits or withdraws boundary cells. A step-size or trust-region bound belongs there, applied to the promotion flux update before the cells are admitted: bound the step by the reliable flux-surface set and by the flux distance between the current state and the seed, and refuse a promotion that admits more cells than the set it replaces unless that trip reduced the residual.

The test that would have caught it. A forward-solve test that runs frame 146's diverted branch to the second trip and asserts two things: that the promotion step norm does not exceed the state's distance from the seed, and that a terminal whose mask has stopped changing cannot carry an axis outside the limiter. With an 83 Wb step on a 151 Wb seed, the first assertion fails at 55% of the budget, which is the signature of a first-order method that has lost its trust region.

Relation to the stopping-rule fix (run r-20261107T141006905473, which now requires an attempted promotion before the settle exit). That fix makes the settle exit honest: a branch cannot report convergence without having attempted a promotion. This finding is the complementary half. The stopping rule governs when the loop ends; the trip-2 defect governs what a single step may do while it is still running. Frame 146 does attempt promotions, so it satisfies the new stopping rule and still loses its plasma: the step bound is what the trajectory shows is missing, and the stopping rule would not have caught it.

Correction (2026-11-08): the flux grid was read with R and Z swapped

The claim corrected. §2 above states that "the axis the solve reports is not a stationary point": that a polish from (1.688, −0.346) travels 0.328 m onto a maximum at (1.585, −0.034); that the trip-1 field is "genuinely two-lobed" with the reported axis 0.33 m below the field's own maximum at the same flux level; that a −1 saddle with flux above the reported axis is therefore "fully explained"; and that at trip 2 the reported axis "does not converge to any stationary point either (shift 0.073 m, converged false)". None of these holds. The reported axis is a stationary maximum of the trip-1 field, the polish does not move it, no maximum sits 0.33 m away, and the trip-2 axis is also a stationary maximum. The three trip-panel captions and the two paragraphs beneath the trip-1 table in §2 report the transposed reading; this section supersedes them and they are left in place only so the record shows what readers were told.

The cause. Every reader under scripts/prototypes/diiid_vertical_loss/ bound its axes by guessing from the array shape, array if array.shape == (len(height), len(radius)) else array.T. The archive writer flattens its lattice in C order over (radius, height) — the storage order nova.equilibrium.conservation.FluxLattice declares, which trip_trajectory.py writes through lattice.shape — so a saved map is radius-major. Every reader here (fit_tensor_spline, draw_flux_contours, RegularGridInterpolator over (height, radius), and host_boundary_read's (nz, nr) field) declares the opposite order. A 33×33 lattice has the same extent on both axes, so the shape test could not decide it: it kept the array unchanged and every reader consumed a transposed field.

The reproduction. Transposing the trip-1 field before polishing reproduces the recorded displacement exactly: 0.3284652 m from the reported axis onto 1.5850, −0.0340. Bound the right way, the same seed polishes to itself, 3.4×10−9 m, at Hessian eigenvalues −7.395 and −5.095 — a maximum, not a non-stationary point. An independent replay of the archived field reached the same three facts: gradient (5×10−16, −1×10−16) at the reported axis, zero displacement, eigenvalues −7.40 and −5.10.

The binding, its check and its negative control

The shape guess is replaced by one helper, scripts/prototypes/diiid_vertical_loss/flux_orientation.py, whose analysis_field binds a saved map from the declared (radius, height) storage order and returns the reader order (height, radius). All four readers — polish_axis_check.py, maxima_census.py, core_stationary_probe.py and render_trip_panels.py — import it, and none of them consults an array shape to choose an order.

check_orientation.py is the assertion that replaces the shape test: it polishes from the axis the solve reported and requires the displacement to stay under one lattice spacing (0.0531 m, the finer of the two). On the archived native field the shift is 3.4e×10−9 m and the check passes. --transpose is its negative control: it transposes the field after it has been bound to the declared order, which is the misread this record was working from, and the check then fails at 0.328465 m.
