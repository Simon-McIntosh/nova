Removing the fitted conductor-field mismatch as a measured-current correction moves the DIII-D lower X-point by nothing, and the fitted corrections are too large to be measurement errors

Applying the fitted equivalent forward-field error as a subtraction from the measured coil currents leaves the solved equilibrium bit-identical on both frames where both arms were solved: 137D and 146D converge to the same residual and the same lower X-point to the last stored digit. The recorded inputs show the correction did reach the source seam, so this is not a correction that failed to be passed: every one of the nineteen conductors carries its fitted ampere-turns into current-inputs.json, and F1A's applied current falls from −177.235 to −231.293 kA·turn. The solved state is unmoved, and the maximum absolute difference between the two arms' terminal flux arrays is exactly zero on both landed frames. This record therefore reports the movement as unmeasured rather than zero, and reports the fit itself, which is the more informative result: the fitted corrections reach 30 to 50 percent of the current in most conductors and up to 2473 percent on ECOILA, which is not a measurement error in any plausible magnet calibration.

The fitted corrections are implausible as current-measurement errors on their own terms. Nine of the nineteen conductors take a correction between 27 and 59 percent of their own measured current, four exceed 200 percent, and only six fall under 5 percent. A magnet calibration error is a gain, so it would appear as one common fraction of each coil's current; a single coil's turns or geometry error would not move the other eighteen. Neither shape is what the fit produces.

What the fit is

The gauge-free external difference over the governed vessel is projected onto the nineteen conductor response columns by a column-scaled SVD with relative cutoff 10-8. Every fit here has rank 19 at a scaled condition number of 2984.57, and a known injected 1 kA·turn F1A error is recovered before any absence is read. The coefficients are the equivalent error of the forward field, so removing that error from the measured current carries the same sign. Each row is fitted on its own solved basis state, with the archive and frame that basis was built from.

Which of the three explanations the pattern supports

Not a calibration. A calibration error is multiplicative and per-coil, so the fitted correction would be a fixed fraction of each coil's own current and stable between frames. The fitted fractions range from 0.1 to 2473 percent within one frame.

Not a single coil's turns or geometry. Twelve of the eighteen F conductors take a correction between +18 and +98 kA·turn, which is a common-mode offset of most of the set rather than a defect in one coil, and the coefficients barely move between 137D and 146D although every measured current differs by about 3 percent.

What remains is a difference in the conductor response or in EFIT's implied external field. A near-uniform, frame-independent coefficient vector with the same sign on most coils is the shape a common-mode scaling of the nineteen response columns produces, and the response columns are the one input that is neither the measured current nor the plasma state. This record does not identify which normalization differs; it rules out the two per-coil explanations and leaves the common-mode one standing.

This recomputation does not reproduce the published fit for the same frames, and this record says so rather than reconciling by choice. The earlier attribution reported 98.02 percent for 137D and 95.16 percent for 146D, with F1A at −2.51 and −0.27 kA·turn and mixed signs across the set. The figures here are 93.11 and 90.05 percent with F1A at +54.06 and +52.14 kA·turn. Both were computed by the same function on the same archive and frame, so the difference lies in the field difference the projection is taken against rather than in the solver; the earlier run's working directories have since been retired from the run store and could not be compared directly. Every number in this record is the recomputation's, and the banked table is quoted only as a disagreement.

What this record does not do, and what follows

It does not measure how far the lower X-point moves toward EFIT under the fitted correction, because the solve was indifferent to the correction at this seam. A 30 percent change in every coil current is large enough that an unconsumed correction cannot be read as a small effect: either the solve path resolves its external field from something other than the current vector handed to it at this seam, or the operator normalizes the coil field away before the residual is formed. This record does not distinguish those two and does not change nova/ to find out.

The next experiment is stated by the finding: recompute the fit against an explicit response normalization, and drive corrected currents through a path whose external field is proven to respond by a single-coil perturbation injected the same way, before any cohort is run. Both are outside this record's fence.

The measurement ran on one titan allocation: base arms, four conductor fits and corrected arms inside one job, two rows at a time on four P100 cards, with the shared root interpreter, TMPDIR=/tmp and no uv invocation on the compute node. Every figure was rendered on a rigel job and inspected as a PNG before it was committed.
