DIII-D lower X-point error is not a rigid vertical displacement

137D and 146D falsify the whole-plasma translation explanation, and the remaining error is broader than the separatrix. The saved states reproduce lower-X separations of 7.29582 cm and 6.58904 cm. The axes move down 27.20 cm and 22.62 cm while the lower X-points move up 7.25 cm and 5.97 cm. Applying the best whole-field translation increases the lower-X errors to 9.90 cm and 7.44 cm. Their current centroids move vertically by only +0.96 cm and +0.008 cm. A shifted magnetic axis is therefore not evidence that the plasma current has undergone the same rigid vertical motion.

The binary receipt calls these rows local, meaning non-rigid under the quantitative rule below. That name must not be read as proof that the error is confined to the separatrix: the inner flux RMS errors, outside a 30 cm disk about the reference saddle, are 350 and 371 mWb, against 254 and 219 mWb inside it. The observed mechanism is a broad deformation of the flux and current distribution with an additional lower-saddle error. 146L, in contrast, is predominantly rigid and has a large upward current-centroid displacement and vertical Lorentz loading.

Current distribution and force read

Current is inferred by the public Ampere/Grad–Shafranov stencil, nova/equilibrium/map_extraction.py:145, jφ = −Δ*Φ/(2πμ₀R). The solved integral uses its saved connected CORE support; EFIT uses its labelled LCFS polygon. These are map-implied current integrals on a coarse lattice, not the native clipped source moments or the magnetics target current. No negative-current clipping, amplitude renormalisation or inferred pressure profile is applied. DIII-D grid spacing is 5.3125 cm radially and 10 cm vertically; MAST is 6.0625 cm and 12.5 cm. Digits below a cell describe the interpolant, not a resolution study.

Forces use each equilibrium's own poloidal field from the same public central derivatives: Bᵣ = −Φᶻ/(2πR), Bᶻ = Φᵣ/(2πR), and (Fᵣ,Fᶻ) = ∫2πR jφ(Bᶻ,−Bᵣ)dA. This is the toroidal-current Lorentz contribution. It excludes poloidal-current/toroidal-field stress and pressure gradients, so it is not the full force-balance residual. The radial number is the toroidally integrated radial scalar, or hoop loading; the full torus's Cartesian radial resultant cancels by symmetry. The analytic uniform-current test independently checks both the sign and magnitude of these quantities.
