DIII-D lower-X offset: EFIT's edge profiles close 47 percent of it on the one frame that converges, and break convergence on the other three

On the one frame whose edge arm converges, carrying EFIT's own p' and FF' knots to ψN = 1.0 instead of capping them at 0.95 closes 46.7 percent of the lower-X offset: 146D moves from 2.960 cm to 1.577 cm from EFIT's own null at a residual of 7.3·10-10. The base tables prescribe p' and FF' on 19 knots spanning ψN 0.05–0.95 and then cap to zero at the boundary; the edge arm carries a map extraction to ψN = 1.0. On 146D both components improve: the vertical offset falls from 2.893 cm to 1.559 cm and the radial one from 0.628 cm to 0.235 cm. On this row the cap is a material part of the remaining offset, and the edge treatment removes about half of it.

The other three frames do not converge, and that is a result in its own right: EFIT's own edge profiles break the solve on three of four frames. 137D terminates ACTIVE_SET_STEP_REFUSED at residual 4.61·10-4, 179L terminates the same way at 1.71·10-2, and 146L cycles ACTIVE_SET_CYCLE_DETECTED at 2.33·10-2 and loses its topology — support falling from 290 cells to 50 while the in-plasma maximum moves from Z = −0.025 m to Z = +0.672 m. Their terminal distances are reported as the states they are: 2.360 cm to 0.942 cm on 137D, 1.443 cm to 2.197 cm on 179L, and 42.9 cm on 146L, which is the separation of two different equilibria and not a measure of profile extent. No share of the offset is read off any of the three, here or in any total.

No product change follows from this measurement. The cap is a material contributor to the vertical offset — the measured quantity the brief asked about — but the edge treatment trades it for a radial offset on the two frames where that difference is read, and it does not converge on three frames of four. The record proposes the prescription change below and states its preconditions; nothing in the prescription or the solver was changed.

What the cap does to the prescribed profiles

The prescribed profiles are piecewise linear between their knots, and beyond the last knot the core holds value and slope through a quintic that reaches zero one edge interval out. The base tables' last knot is ψN = 0.95, so the quintic spans exactly 0.95 to 1.0 — the whole of the region this measurement is about. It prescribes p' = 0 and FF' = 0 at the boundary, where EFIT's own map prescribes p' between −2.6·104 and −3.7·104 Pa/Wb and FF' between −0.038 and +0.025 T2 m2/Wb, and it carries about half of the interval's content. On 179 the map's FF' turns positive at the edge, so the cap and the map differ in sign there and not only in magnitude.

How the two arms were built, and why they differ only at the edge

The archive holds no native profile coefficients for these shots, so both arms are extractions from the same EFIT map, computed by nova/equilibrium/map_extraction.py:extract_flux_functions. The base arm samples 19 surfaces spanning ψN 0.05–0.95. The edge arm appends ten knots at 0.955, 0.960, …, 0.995, 1.000 from a separate extraction whose surface set begins at the base arm's last surface, and it drops that extraction's first knot so the two sets share their 0.95 knot exactly.

Every one of the ten added knots is reported reliable by the extractor, from 6 to 20 retained samples per surface at a condition number between 4.0 and 7.0 (arm-tables.json, in the run record). The two arms agree to the bit below ψN = 0.95 — the largest difference in either quantity across all three frames is exactly 0.0 — because the interpolation is local and the shared knots are identical. The comparison therefore isolates the 0.95–1.0 region, and the base arm doubles as a control: its solved lower-X distances reproduce the banked cohort on every row, the largest difference being 5.8·10-9 m on 146L and below 4·10-12 m on the other three.

Where the offset goes

Decomposing the null offset into its radial and vertical parts is what makes the result readable, because the two move in opposite directions and the net distance hides it. The vertical offset falls on all three frames whose edge state remains a plasma — 2.266 cm to 0.341 cm on 137D, 2.893 cm to 1.559 cm on 146D, 0.997 cm to 0.059 cm on 179L — while the radial offset falls only on 146D and grows on the other two; on 146L the edge state is not the same plasma and neither component is comparable. So the change is not a better boundary condition so much as a different one: it buys height with radius, and only on 146D, the converged row, do both components move the same way.

The vertical change is also not a translation of the configuration. On both diverted frames the in-plasma maximum rises while the lower X-point falls, so the solved plasma is stretched vertically rather than moved: the axis-to-null separation grows by 4.1 cm on 137D and 3.2 cm on 146D, and the axis moves by less than half a centimetre radially. That holds on the converged frame as firmly as on the other, so it is a property of the state and not of the non-convergence. The edge profiles enter through the same boundary integral that sets the vertical field, so the state answers by changing its vertical extent.

Proposal for the prescription (not applied)

The measurement supports one change and does not support making it yet. Candidate: when the prescribed p' and FF' are built from a map extraction, carry the extraction's own knots to ψN = 1.0 instead of capping at the last interior knot, admitting only knots the extractor reports reliable. On this cohort that restores the 49–57 percent of edge content the cap discards, and it is the intervention that removes most of the vertical offset on all three frames whose edge state remains a plasma — 85, 46 and 94 percent of it on 137D, 146D and 179L, of which only 146D converged. Why it is not applied: the treatment is not convergent on three of four rows — ACTIVE_SET_STEP_REFUSED on 137D and 179L, and on 146L a cycling active set that leaves a 50-cell support and a displaced axis — and it moves the radial offset the other way on both frames where it is finite. Landing it would trade a measured vertical error for an unmeasured radial one and a non-convergent baseline. The precondition is a convergence repair at the edge (the active set is the failing part, not the tolerance), and the candidate should be re-measured on all four rows before any prescription changes.

Caveats

The edge arm's imaging read did not run in this cohort: the driver loads the held cohort's measure module as a library, and the block that installs that cohort's extra image read executes only when that module is the process entry point. Every number above comes from the solve receipt and the retained census, both of which did run, and none of them depends on that read; the shadow-node markers the earlier cohort's figures carry are absent here. The driver fix is committed after this cohort so a later run writes one, and these figures are drawn without those markers.

Frame 146 appears twice, diverted and limited, on the same prescribed profiles — the difference between those rows is the equilibrium's topology, not the profiles. Frame 179 contributes one row, its limited branch. Each row's distance is measured to the EFIT null the banked cohort recorded for the same frame and branch, so the two arms and the banked cohort share one reference point per row.

Three of the four edge rows terminate on an active-set condition. Whether that reflects the edge prescription itself or the active-set machinery's response to a sharper boundary gradient is not settled by this cohort, and the distinction matters for the proposal above: a tolerance change would be the wrong repair for a prescription that genuinely moves the boundary field.
