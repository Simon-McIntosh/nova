Midplane current and the spherical-tokamak double hump

The MAST double hump is already in the prescribed source, and the map and EFIT both retain it. With a peak-prominence threshold of 1% of the selected maximum, head source peaks occur at R = 0.56602 and 1.22521 m, with 0.72820 and 0.54100 MA/m². Head map peaks lie at 0.56885 and 1.21390 m (0.71993 and 0.53180 MA/m²); EFIT peaks lie at 0.56602 and 1.21672 m (0.72035 and 0.53320 MA/m²). It is not created by reconstructing current from the flux map.

The Grad–Shafranov source combines R p′ and FF′/(μ₀ R), with the shared signed flux convention and normalization used by the trace. At the inner source peak, the pressure and field contributions are 0.11976 + 0.60844 = 0.72820 MA/m²; at the outer peak they are 0.26663 + 0.27437 = 0.54100 MA/m². The field term favours the small-R side, while the pressure term grows on the larger-R shoulder. A spherical tokamak's large relative variation of R makes this competition consequential. The supplied p′ profile itself also weakens near the core and strengthens on outer shells: geometry is not the sole cause. This double hump is compatible with the equilibrium source and its EFIT reference; it does not imply multiple magnetic axes, nor is it a universal requirement for spherical tokamaks.
