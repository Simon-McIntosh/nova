The 1/f control repaired: a coloured plasma-current draw, and the fit run to convergence

Plan §7 cannot be read until its control holds: the white sequence's lag-one correlation must sit within 0.15 of zero for both codes and the 1/f sequence's must exceed 0.5. The prior node measured the white control holding but the 1/f control failing for both codes (nova 0.047/0.085/0.105, imas-efit 0.462/0.544/0.606). This node names the cause and repairs it, then re-measures both codes on the repaired pipeline.

The cause: an unmodelled white plasma-current draw

Both codes carry the measured plasma current as one row of the fit — nova through the measurement's current row and its weight, imas-efit through the current written into the IMAS pulse. That draw was an independent white Gaussian on every slice at 0.01 of the plasma current, while the sensor rows carried the sequence's colouring. On the 1/f sequence that white current draw is a per-slice jump the axis position follows directly: it is white by construction, so it decorrelates the axis, boundary flux and q95 that the control measures and it dominates the coloured sensor signal. That is why both codes failed the same clause on the same quantities, and why imas-efit — whose sensor noise contributes relatively more — came nearest the floor (0.462) while nova, also capped at two passes, fell to 0.047.

The repair takes the current from the same coloured generator: study.current_draw shapes the draw with the sequence's own index and corner, so the measured current carries the sequence's memory and only its colouring changed, not its marginal size. The guard suite pins the white draw within 0.15 of zero and the 1/f draw above the floor, so a repair that correlates white noise fails the same test.

imas-efit now passes both controls; nova passes two of its three. Its boundary flux and q95 lag-one sit well above the floor and its white arm stays within 0.15 of zero, but its axis lag-one is 0.342, short of 0.5. The gate reads CHECK-FAILED nova: the 1/f lag-one clause failed, 1 of 26 checks. The 0.5 floor is a property of the reference spectrum at this colouring and was not lowered.

One candidate explanation for nova's axis gap is the two-pass cap: if a large part of the axis movement were the fit's own per-slice convergence noise, that white scatter would dilute the injected memory. The converged arm below tests it directly, and the test does not support it: over the twelve white slices measured at both depths, the converged and two-pass arms have the same radial axis scatter (11.71 mm against 11.68 mm) and the same lag-one (−0.051 against −0.077); the vertical scatter is larger when converged (0.66 mm against 0.26 mm), not smaller. Convergence is therefore not the explanation for the axis clause, and the cause remains open for the next node rather than being reported as solved.

The cost is what makes the full converged arm unaffordable inside this window, and the number is measured rather than estimated. A converged slice costs 300–1800 s on a P100 card, and the first slice on an H200 cost 1352.8 s for 49 passes. Two arms of 200 slices each over both sequences is 800 such fits: roughly 30 card-hours at the measured rate, against a window of a few hours on one card with the node's eight H200s held by other work through the day. The two-pass arms above are therefore the complete 200-slice measurement, and the converged arm is reported slice by slice as it lands. Nothing here is a converged 200-slice arm, and the record does not present it as one.
