The 1/f control: a coloured plasma-current draw, and the axis lag-one split by component

Plan §7 cannot be read until its control holds: the white sequence's lag-one correlation must sit within 0.15 of zero and the 1/f sequence's must exceed 0.5. The prior node measured the white control holding but the 1/f control failing for both codes. This record names the cause, repairs it, re-measures both codes on the repaired pipeline, and reports the axis correlation separately in the radial and vertical directions, which is what decides whether the remaining gap is a control failure or one component of it.

The cause: an unmodelled white plasma-current draw

Both codes carry the measured plasma current as one row of the fit — nova through the measurement's current row and its weight, imas-efit through the current written into the IMAS pulse. That draw was an independent white Gaussian on every slice at 0.01 of the plasma current, while the sensor rows carried the sequence's colouring. On the 1/f sequence that white current draw is a per-slice jump the axis position follows directly: it is white by construction, so it decorrelates the axis, boundary flux and q95 that the control measures and it dominates the coloured sensor signal. That is why both codes failed the same clause on the same quantities, and why imas-efit — whose sensor noise contributes relatively more — came nearest the floor while nova, also capped at two passes, fell further.

The repair takes the current from the same coloured generator: study.current_draw shapes the draw with the sequence's own index and corner, so the measured current carries the sequence's memory and only its colouring changed, not its marginal size. Two guards hold it. One exercises the generator directly. The other drives study.run_nova through its own sequence loop with the case and the fit replaced, and reduces the current read back out of the record the loop writes, so a call site that asks for the wrong sequence, a white column, or drops the draw fails it. Its negative control reinstates the white Gaussian draw and requires the same reduction to fall inside the white control, which is what a guard that cannot fail would not do.

The guard suite that holds these checks was run at both revisions of this record on a debug partition: 16 passed at the base e28db604e and 23 passed at the head 1558ed08d. The seven tests the head adds are the generator's colour-and-size check on study.current_draw, the call-site guard that reads the sequence's memory back out of the record study.run_nova writes, its negative control that reinstates a white current draw, the row-matched arm available by name, the refusal of an arm whose files repeat a slice, the merge of shards that do not repeat one, and the row-set composition that keeps a named row set from doubling the stem. The logs are guard-suite-base.log and guard-suite-head-1558ed08d.log in the node's run directory, each carrying its command and revision in its first lines and its exit status as its last. The commit that adds this citation changes no code and no figure, so the head the suite was run at is the revision named here.

On the declared 72-row arm the split is radial 0.515 against vertical 0.169, and on the row-matched 24-loop arm radial 0.594 against vertical 0.368. Both radial figures clear the 0.5 floor on their own; both vertical figures do not, and it is the vertical component alone that drags the two-component mean below the floor (0.342 and 0.481 respectively). The gate's clause is stated on the mean, so it reads CHECK-FAILED nova: the 1/f lag-one clause failed for that arm, and the 0.5 floor was not lowered. The two-component mean remains the gate's statistic; the split is reported beside it so the failure is attributed to the component that fails rather than to the whole axis.

Why the vertical direction behaves differently is not yet measured. The axis position is the weakest-observed of the three quantities (10.4 mm RMS on a 6.29 m axis), and vertical motion is the smaller of the two components by construction of the draw; a reading that the two-pass cap suppresses it is not supported — the twelve-slice paired comparison below finds the converged and two-pass arms equally scattered — so the cause stays open and is stated as open.

The two-pass arms above are the complete 200-slice measurement.

The twelve white slices measured at both depths give a direct test of whether the two-pass cap inflates the axis movement: it does not. Radial scatter is 11.71 mm converged against 11.68 mm at two passes, with radial lag-one −0.041 against −0.075; the vertical scatter is larger when converged (0.66 mm against 0.26 mm), not smaller. The cap is therefore ruled out as the explanation for the vertical component, and the cause remains open. The converged arm as a whole holds the sequence's memory on each quantity the control measures: axis 0.090, boundary flux 0.218 and q95 0.251 over its 33 slices.
