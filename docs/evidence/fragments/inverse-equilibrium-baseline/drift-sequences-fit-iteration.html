The held-map iteration inside the fit loop over 200 slices of both drift sequences

This node fits the held-map pass of the equilibrium iteration — FluxIterationFit, the column the plan's lead ruling names for this study — over 200 slices of the white and of the 1/f reference sequence, on the same rows, the same noise draws and the same generating slice count the earlier imas-efit arms carry, and reports the per-slice and 50-slice-mean error of the axis position, the boundary flux and q95 beside the lag-one controls.

The generating state is the section-1 ITER fixture at 500 cells: its state, the noise model and the pulse writer are probe_rows/study.py's, the held-map driver is probe_rows/iteration.py, every measure is probe_rows/analyse.py's reduction, and the gate is probe_rows/check.py reading that reduction's own summary. Clause 2: the lag-one control

The white sequence is the negative control: a code that reads noise rather than the state must show no slice-to-slice memory, so its lag-one must sit within 0.15 of zero on all three quantities. The 1/f sequence is the positive control: the colouring itself carries memory, so a code that reproduces the reference must show lag-one above 0.5 on all three.

The white control holds for both codes and the 1/f control does not. The gate reads CHECK-FAILED nova: the 1/f lag-one clause failed, CHECK-FAILED efit: the 1/f lag-one clause failed, 2 of 28 checks failed. imas-efit's 1/f axis lag-one of 0.462 is the nearest to the floor and still below it; its flux and q95 lag-one are 0.544 and 0.606, above the floor, so the clause fails on one of its three quantities. The nova column fails on all three (0.047, 0.085, 0.105).

That is reported, not tuned. The floor of 0.5 is a property of the reference spectrum at the study's colouring (index 1.0, corner 4 cycles), measured on the reference series itself in the earlier arms; it was not lowered to make this run pass. The reading it supports is that neither code's residual error tracks the injected colouring at this noise level: a code whose error series is white on both sequences has not resolved the slow component the 1/f draw adds, whichever code it is. The white half of the control passing on both codes rules out the alternative reading, that the 1/f draw itself is correlated noise the reduction failed to see.

Geometry load is about 690 s of each record, so the marginal cost of a pass is about 11 s and the converged 1111.7–1193.3 s walls are almost entirely that one-time load plus 35–48 passes. Four hundred converged slices would therefore cost roughly 120 P100 card-hours, about 24 hours on the five cards this job holds, and would be a single measurement at the edge of the allocation rather than a study. The 200-slice arms above are measured at a two-pass cap, which is the honest reading of the table: two passes leave the map closer to the truth seed than a finished fit would, so the errors are a lower bound on what a converged slice returns, and the converged records show the finished fit's own misfit (0.0273–0.0716). A full-convergence arm is the next node's work, on a budget sized from these walls.
