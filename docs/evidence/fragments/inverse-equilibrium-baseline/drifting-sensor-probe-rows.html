The probe convention: imas-efit negates poloidal_angle

imas-efit's IMAS reader negates the stored probe angle (src/imas.cpp: poloidalOrientation = -probe->poloidal_angle), because the data dictionary defines the probe sensitive axis as cos(θ) R̂ − sin(θ) Ẑ, clockwise in the (R, Z) plane, while EFIT works counter-clockwise). A writer that keeps the machine's stored poloidal_angle, as the pulse must, has to project the row value along BR cos θ − BZ sin θ. The prior head projected along +BZ sin θ, which is nova's own sensor projection at angle = θ; the reader then infers a field reflected about the radial axis.

The instrument is probe_convention.py, which reads a pulse's stored probe rows and computes each candidate projection from that pulse's own reconstructed equilibrium efit-white-200-0.0100, with the field sampled from the solver's own flux map at the probe positions:

probes 48  field_rms 0.7397 T
rmse, cos θ − sin θ (IMAS convention): 0.0537 T
rmse, cos θ + sin θ (prior head):       1.2941 T

The two candidates differ by a factor of 24 on the same rows, so a pulse is written in one convention and not ambiguous. Recomputing the drift sequences with angle = −θ in nova's sensor projection — the same field values, the same positions, the same stored angles — repairs the rows; the prior convention is rebuilt from the identical state and the identical noise as the control.

Every lag-one figure in the table is lag_one in analyse.py: np.corrcoef(x[:-1], x[1:]) on the stored series, with the axis entry the mean of the two per-coordinate correlations and boundary flux divided by the generating state's fixed span (76.70379 Wb), the divisor analyse.py applies to every arm. A constant divisor leaves a correlation unchanged, so the raw boundary flux in Wb gives the same figure. On that one convention the prior-head rows carry 0.0348 / 0.1186 / 0.1131 on white and 0.3666 / 0.1866 / 0.3990 on drift, which are the values the prior arm's own summary.json records.

The drift arm carries 199 of its 200 slices. Slice 174's output equilibrium has no boundary outline — the solver wrote the time slice with an empty separatrix — so the reader refuses it rather than inventing a boundary, and the missing slice is recorded in efit-drift-200-0.0100.missing. Every recorded slice carries a finite axis and a finite q95, and the recorded q95 stays inside 2.85–2.99 across both sequences.

The prior-convention control, two white slices with the reflected rows and the identical noise, lands the axis 0.670 m from truth at (6.0135, −0.1124) m against 0.0146 m over the full corrected arm; the prior head's 200-slice arms are the 1.29–1.41 m rows in the table. The reflection, not the row count and not the solver, is what puts the reconstruction on the wrong side of the machine.

What this record does not claim

The probe rows are the row-exchange study's sensor construction: 48 poloidal-field probes at the machine description's own positions, carrying its own stored poloidal_angle, read by imas-efit's IMAS reader. The record measures the reconstruction of those rows; it does not claim they are the machine's installed probes, and it does not claim the correction is a property of imas-efit alone — a writer that projects along cos θ + sin θ and stores poloidal_angle unchanged is inconsistent with the reader by construction.

The nova column fits the same slice indices of the same noisy sequences and is reported with the slice count it reached; the slices it did not reach are named with the measured per-slice wall rather than presented as agreement or as disagreement. No cross-code difference is computed from unequal slice counts.

The slices are noise draws around one generating state, so a slice is not an independent equilibrium: the lag-one statistics measure how the reconstruction tracks the drift, not how it would track an independent series of equilibria. The 50-slice error is the RMS of the 50-slice means, which is what the loops-only reference reports, so the two are comparable.
