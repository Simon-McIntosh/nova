imas-efit on nova-written ITER magnetics rows: a qualified generating state in the solver's two resolved functions, and the section-8 comparison

This node writes noise-free ITER magnetics rows from a nova forward state that lies inside imas-efit's own resolved basis, runs the solver on them with its pressure and q0 priors off, and measures its reconstruction and the nova fit against that generating state in the section-8 bands, with a 1.10-scaled negative control. The generating state is a converged, qualified solve at the section-1 reference mesh (500 cells, P100 lane) whose residual is 1.17e-15, and imas-efit reconstructs it from the rows alone: its plasma current is 8.7e-7 of the generating value, its magnetic axis sits 0.0152 m away, and its axis and boundary fluxes land within 0.41 % and 0.09 % of the generating span. The safety factor does not match: imas-efit's |q95| is 2.907 against the generating 3.055, 0.148 low and 0.048 outside the 0.1 band, while both reconstructed flux functions keep the generating shape exactly and their amplitudes move by +5.5 % on the pressure side and −8.5 % on the diamagnetic side.

The route this record takes is the coordinator's, given on 2026-11-17: nova owns the imas-efit configuration for this case, so the auto-degree selector was switched off and both degrees named, leaving exactly one live coefficient in each of the two functions. The earlier route — inheriting the shipped auto-degree setting, which resolves pp_ndeg=0 for a large-aspect magnetics-only ITER slice — is kept below because it is the reason the configuration exists, and because the state it forbids (p' ≡ 0) does not qualify at any resolution measured here.

The basis: inherited, then chosen

imas-efit is checked out at revision fe13365d5 with binary build/sdcc/src/efit. The shipped ITER magnetics-only configuration (configuration/machines/ITER/efitOptions.xml) carries pressurePriorBetaPol="0.5", a <q0 weights="4" …/> constraint and, through the shared solver default, autoDegree="on" on both pp and ff. With both priors disabled and the selector left to choose, the solver announced this basis on a nova-written pulse (job 1318577, sun_debug):

AUTODEGREE: regime=magnetics-only/large-aspect pp_ndeg=0 ff_ndeg=2 live_pp=0 live_ff=1
  max_pp_ndeg=0 max_ff_ndeg=2 R0=6.1680 a=2.2259 kappa=2.088 epsilon=0.3609

Zero live pressure functions and one live diamagnetic function is a basis a magnetically confined ITER torus does not admit a qualified axis in. Measured: at the reference mesh on the P100 lane the basis-restricted source (p' ≡ 0, FF' = A(1 − x)) ran to completion and raised NoQualifiedAxisError (job 1321000); at 120 cells the same source either raised the same error or converged to a terminal span of 0.164 Wb against the seed's 81.26 Wb.

The run therefore carries its own configuration, efitOptions-nova2.xml: the machine template plus one numericalControls block that names both degrees and switches the selector off, with both priors at zero weight.

<numericalControls pressurePriorBetaPol="0">
  <pp ndeg="2" edge="1" func="8" autoDegree="off"/>
  <ff ndeg="2" edge="1" func="8" autoDegree="off"/>
  <ww ndeg="0" edge="1" func="0"/>
</numericalControls>
<q0 weights="0" values="1.3" sigmas="0.3" interpolationMethod="1" timeWindow="0" timeMargin="1"/>

With the degrees explicit the selector prints no AUTODEGREE line at all, and the resolved basis is published per slice instead. On the shipped ITER 135013 magnetics pulse (job 1321349, sun_debug, 11 s) and identically on both nova-written pulses below (job 1321516):

DYNAMIC_NDEG: regime= 2 kppcur= 1 kffcur= 1 kwwcur= 0 max_pp= 2 max_ff= 2 max_ww= 0
DESIGN_RANK: slice=0 rank_pp=1 n_pp=1 rank_ff=1 n_ff=1 n_meas_rows=85

These two lines are the solver's own statements and differ in kind. DYNAMIC_NDEG gives the configured and live degree per family — two configured, one live — and DESIGN_RANK gives the rank the design matrix actually reached, rank_pp=1 of n_pp=1 and rank_ff=1 of n_ff=1, so the slice is solved for exactly one coefficient in each family and the count the done-when asks for is read from the log rather than inferred.

The basis family is a solver statement too, but one the default verbosity does not print. It appears only inside the per-iteration LLS_COND record, gated on logLlsCondition in imas-efit's numericalControls; a rerun of pulse 10 with that switch on (job 1322324, sun_debug) reproduces the recorded reconstruction to the last printed digit — same ip, axis, fluxes and q95 — and prints:

LLS_COND: slice=0 iter=2 basis=bernstein path=bvls m=87 n=14 rank=14 kappa= 1.00000E+00 sigma_max= 1.00000E+00 sigma_min= 1.00000E+00 resid= 4.08395E+00

So func=8 is resolved by the solver's own label (basis=bernstein, taken from pp_base%func) rather than only by the file, and the least-squares design reached full column rank (rank=15 of n=15 at the final iteration) alongside the per-family live count above. The design carries m=87 rows for a slice whose DESIGN_RANK line counts n_meas_rows=85, and its measured groups are 24 flux loops, 48 magnetic probes and 12 constrained PF circuits, so two design rows lie outside the measured groups; the LLS_COND line names the family, the path and the rank, not those rows.

The edge constraint is never printed: no log line carries the string edge, and imas-efit's output IDS does not carry it either — equilibrium/code/parameters is the empty string in the recorded run, in the logLlsCondition rerun and in the control below, code.name being the only populated field of that group. It is nevertheless resolved from the solver's own output, in two independent statements, and the clause is met on that evidence rather than on the configuration file. The configuration read remains half of the pair: edge=1 is where the input side stands (numericalControls in efitOptions-nova2.xml), and the solver's own output is what shows the resolved family respects it — which is what the two statements below measure, and why the value is not taken on the file's word.

First, the live-count line above is edge-sensitive rather than edge-agnostic. imas-efit computes the live count of a Bernstein family as ndeg − 1 exactly when func = 8 and edge ≠ 0 (src/timeSliceNumericalControls.cpp, liveBasisFunctionCount), and emits both that count and the design rank it implies. The recorded run reports kppcur= 1 and kffcur= 1 at max_pp= 2 and max_ff= 2 with rank_pp=1 n_pp=1: a live count of one per family at a configured degree of two, which at func = 8 is attainable only through the edge drop. The control measures that dependence instead of asserting it. The same pulse, rows, weights, degrees and log switch, with the single attribute edge="0" on both fitted families (job 1322750, one all_debug job, configuration efitOptions-nova4.xml, output case/efit-out-edge0) prints:

DYNAMIC_NDEG: regime= 2 kppcur= 2 kffcur= 2 kwwcur= 0 max_pp= 2 max_ff= 2 max_ww= 0
DESIGN_RANK: slice=0 rank_pp=2 n_pp=2 rank_ff=2 n_ff=2 n_meas_rows=85

so each family's live count moves from one to two when the switch is released, at the same configured degree and the same regime= 2 in both runs, so the selector is not what the difference comes from. Second, the constraint is visible in the fitted profiles of the output IDS. At edge=1 the fitted gradients terminate at exactly zero — dpressure_dpsi −28733.6821 at the axis to 0.0 at ψb, f_df_dpsi −0.7983770 to 0.0 — and in the edge=0 control the same two arrays end at 6015.0153 and −0.2570672, which is the endpoint coefficient the edge drop removes. The retained function vanishes at the boundary in the recorded state and does not when the constraint is released, which is the constraint's own definition read out of the output rather than off the input file. The control is a control and not a second reconstruction: releasing the edge constraint moves the on-axis pressure gradient to −42152.1625 and changes the state, so it is used for this reading only.

What stays open is the announcement, not the resolution: a reader of the log alone cannot see the switch's value without the derivation above, and the output carries no field naming it. That absence belongs to the solver, which is pinned here at fe13365d5, and the carrier for changing it is imas-efit's own plan docs/plans/equilibrium-fit-provenance.html: this node names the plan and does not act on it.

The generating state

The state is a nova forward solve on the section-1 ITER machine (pulse 135011 run 7, slice 353, DD 3.39.0, 9 circuits and 14 coils, 103 passive loops) at 500 cells, with

p'  = sign · A · (3.0e5 / R0²) · (1 − x)
FF' = sign · A · 0.25          · (1 − x)      x = psi_norm, sign = −sign(Ip)

expressed as ascending-power coefficients [1, −1] with a scalar normalisation each. The amplitude A is fixed so the cell currents reproduce the pulse's plasma current through a unit-amplitude trial: unit_current = −4 295 380.4 A, A = 3.4903452801902497. The solve converged and qualified at the reference mesh (job 1321485, one P100 card, 2 cores, 24 G):

SOLVED converged=True qualified=True residual=1.165769213453697e-15
GENERATING axis=[6.289868237209081, 0.4980901175347787]
  plasma_current=-13634011.558521805  flux_axis=-73.21146725088177
  flux_boundary=3.1542034178327287   flux_span=76.3656706687145
  q95=-3.0550243513325954 (sign -1)  edge_safety_factor=-4.342231647622551

The solve carries its flux on the trimmed hexagonal mesh, which has no rectilinear axes, while the geometry reader (FluxSurfaceGeometry.from_flux_map) reads a uniform FluxLattice. The geometry is therefore read from a resampled carrier: the cell flux is resampled piecewise-linearly onto the reference map's own grid (65 × 129 over R 3–9 m, Z ±6 m) with the nearest cell value outside the hull, then smoothed with one grid sample, because the resampled carrier's piecewise-linear kinks are not quantities a cubic spline may differentiate. The axis, axis flux and boundary flux in the table are the solve's own topology values; only the shape between them passes through the carrier. The carrier's own sensitivity was measured separately: on the 120-cell seed the same read returns q95 = −3.027 at one sample of smoothing and −3.292 at two, so the q95 band below should be read with that carrier scale in mind rather than as an exact number.

Rows are evaluated by nova's own virtual diagnostic kernels at the machine's own geometry: 24 flux loops and 48 poloidal probes, each written as the total (plasma plus conductors) value a real pulse carries, with ip, pf_active (9 circuits, 14 coils), pf_passive (103 loops), the wall and a synthesised tf, at DD 3.39.0 through imas.DBEntry(…, "w", dd_version="3.39.0"). The subtraction identity holds to 6.4e-17 relative. Two pulses are written from the one solve: pulse-10 at scale 1.0 and pulse-11 with every magnetics row, its plasma current and every conductor current scaled by 1.10 — the negative control.

The two flux functions are recovered in shape exactly. Fitted against (1 − x) over the solver's own psi profile, imas-efit's p' is −28 733.7 (1 − x) and its FF' is −0.79838 (1 − x); the generating source is +27 239.9 (1 − x) and +0.87259 (1 − x). The signs differ because DD's dpressure_dpsi and f_df_dpsi are gradients in the stored flux, whose sign is opposite nova's; in magnitudes the pressure amplitude is 5.48 % high and the diamagnetic amplitude 8.50 % low. That trade is what moves q95: the split between the two functions is the only freedom the fit has. It is not freedom the rows fail to constrain — nova's own fit on the same 72 rows returns both amplitudes to machine precision, below — so what imas-efit spends here is its own weighting of the split, not a limit of the measurement.

The nova fit on the same rows

nova's inverse class is given the same two functions, the same 72 rows and the same measurement: the fitted basis has one amplitude per family on the same [1, −1] coefficients, and the fit starts from the generating source. It returns both amplitudes as one, with no coefficient left undetermined:

  coefficient_basis=[[1,0],[-1,0],[0,1],[0,-1]]  coefficients=[0.9999999999999998, 0.9999999999999998]
  pressure_normalisation=27239.947556115367  diamagnetic_normalisation=0.8725863200475624
  singular_values=[1.4129925663095804, 0.05875378757039766]  undetermined=[]
  residual_sup=8.196351776825464e-16  terminal_residual=1.5543589512715955e-15
NOVA_STATE axis=[6.289868237209092, 0.4980901175347843] plasma_current=-13634011.558521807
  flux_axis=-73.2114672508819 flux_boundary=3.154203417832682 flux_span=76.36567066871457
  q95=-3.0550243513325945 (sign -1)

Read against the generating state, every section-8 band is met at the level the arithmetic allows: plasma current and axis agree to the last printed digit, the axis and boundary fluxes to 1e-13 Wb, and q95 to 1e-15. The fit converged in zero iterations because the generating source is already the zero-residual point of the linearised row set — the information being reported is that the rows admit that split at machine precision, that the least-squares system is full rank in it (singular values 1.413 and 0.0588, condition 24), and that nothing in the rows pulls the solution away from it. This is the measurement imas-efit's 5–8 % amplitude trade is compared against.

The negative control, and what it rules out

The 1.10 pulse scales every magnetics row, the measured plasma current and every conductor current by the same factor, which is a data set no equilibrium of the stated equations produces: a solution scaled in current but not in shape is inconsistent. Both reconstructions follow the scale and neither returns the nominal state, which is what the control is for.

imas-efit returns a plasma current of −14.9974 MA against the scaled target −14.9974 MA, a fraction of 1.1e-6, with the axis moved 0.0028 m from its nominal reconstruction and the flux span scaled to 84.487 Wb from 76.748 Wb, a ratio of 1.1008 — 8e-4 above the 1.10 of the data. The nominal agreement above is therefore the solver tracking the data it was given and not a fixed-scale or fixed-geometry artefact: a change of scale in the rows produces the corresponding change of state.

The nova fit reads the same control rows and refuses them: its scaled plasma current is −15.0075 MA, 0.07 % from the scaled target, and the axis moves 0.0141 m, but the fit does not converge (converged=false, largest whitened residual 7.9e-3 against 8.2e-16 on the nominal rows), with amplitudes [0.652, 1.511] trading along the split direction to chase a state the equations do not admit, and q95 at −2.977 against the generating −3.055. imas-efit reaches the scaled current within 1.1e-6 and reports convergence (chi2 rising only from 0.073 to 0.089). Read together, the control shows the two codes disagreeing about whether a 10 % inconsistent data set is acceptable — a difference in acceptance tolerance, not in wiring.

How much of the q95 gap is definitional

The 0.148 gap compares two numbers each code computed for itself, and the two do not measure the same surface. imas-efit's q95 is its profiles_1d.q interpolated linearly at ψN = 0.95, with ψN = (ψ − ψ0)/(ψb − ψ0) taken over its own 65-node profile grid, which ends exactly at its psi_boundary. nova's q95 is its traced safety-factor curve at ρtor = 0.95, where ρtor is the toroidal-flux radius √(Φtor/π R0 B0) accumulated from the same surfaces, and its curve runs to ψN = 0.995 rather than to 1. The boundary fluxes also differ in origin: nova's is the solve's own topology value on the trimmed mesh, imas-efit's is the psi_boundary its reconstruction writes.

Both maps were therefore handed to one calculator, nova.equilibrium.flux_surface_geometry.FluxSurfaceGeometry.from_flux_map, each with its own axis, its own boundary flux and its own diamagnetic profile ((1 − x) at the amplitude the fit recovered), so that no definition of the surface or convention is carried between the two states:

ψaxis / ψboundary [Wb]−72.4809 / 3.1542−73.5282 / 3.2201
q at ψN = 0.952.98362.9189
q at ρtor = 0.953.05552.9916

On the same accessor the gap is 0.065 at ψN = 0.95 and 0.064 at ρtor = 0.95, both inside the 0.1 band, where the cross-code comparison gave 0.148. Two checks that the calculator is faithful rather than forgiving: it reproduces nova's published q95 to 5e-4 (3.0555 against 3.055) from the resampled carrier alone, reading its axis flux off the carrier's spline (−72.4809 Wb against the solve's −73.2115 Wb), so the accessor's own axis reading moves q by less than 1e-3; and it places imas-efit's own curve at 2.9189 against the 2.907 its interpolation reports, the 0.012 that separates an accessor value from an interpolation of the solver's own samples. The verdict is therefore that most of the 0.148 is definitional, and the residual 0.065 is the part the amplitude trade of the functions accounts for: the reconstruction's diamagnetic amplitude is 8.50 % low, and q rises with that amplitude.

Both numbers are kept and neither is substituted for the other: the record's band table keeps the cross-code 0.148, because that is what two independent calculations over the same rows deliver, and the single-calculator 0.065 is reported here as the definitional share rather than as a replacement. The probe is q95_compare.py in the run directory, one all_debug job (1322304) that solves nothing, and its output is case/q95-single-calculator.json.

What this establishes, and what it leaves open

Four of the five section-8 bands are met by imas-efit on rows nova wrote: plasma current within 1.5 % (measured 8.7e-7), magnetic axis within 0.02 m (measured 0.0152 m), axis flux and boundary flux within 2 % of the generating span (measured 0.41 % and 0.09 %), with each state's stored flux sign read and reported rather than compared across conventions. The q95 band is not met: |q95| is 0.148 low (2.907 against 3.055), so four of the five are met and the gate as a whole is not. That verdict has to be read in the two parts the section above establishes, because the 0.148 is not one quantity. As a gate the band stands unmet on the numbers the gate names: two independent codes, each computing q on its own surface definition, differ by 0.148. As a reconstruction error the difference is 0.065 at ψN = 0.95 where both states are placed on one calculator with matched definitions, so 0.083 of the gate number is the difference between the definitions themselves and the rest is the physical difference. The cause is visible in the recovered functions: both keep the generating shape exactly and their amplitudes trade 5–8 % against each other at fixed current and boundary flux, and since q rises with the diamagnetic amplitude the reconstruction's 8.50 % low amplitude is what moves q by the measured 0.065. It is not a shortage of information either: the same rows and the same two functions put nova's own fit on the generating amplitudes to machine precision. The physical difference between the two reconstructions is therefore the amplitude trade the solver settled on, and it costs q the 0.065 measured — not the 0.148 the gate quotes.

What the section-8 comparison may therefore claim: the exchange works end to end, imas-efit reads rows nova wrote, in a basis that holds a qualified ITER state, and it recovers the integral quantities that pin the equilibrium. What it may not claim: that the section-8 q95 band passes as the gate states it — the cross-definition comparison is 0.148 and that verdict is unchanged by the single-calculator probe. What it may claim, without substituting it for the gate: on matched definitions imas-efit recovers q to 0.065 from two live Bernstein functions on noise-free magnetics, inside the band. That residual is the amplitude trade, and it is also where a question belongs about imas-efit's constraint weighting and regularisation rather than about the basis: its chi2 is 0.073 while the amplitude error is 5–8 %, so the split is being absorbed as if it were noise. Opening more degrees in one family, or adding the pressure prior this node deliberately disabled, would mask that rather than measure it. The q95 number also carries a measured carrier uncertainty of a few hundredths from the resampled geometry read, which is of the size of the matched-definition residual and closes neither the 0.148 gate gap nor the 0.065 reconstruction gap.
