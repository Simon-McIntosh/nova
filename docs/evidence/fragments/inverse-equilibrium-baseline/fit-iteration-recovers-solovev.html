The held-map pass that recovers the Solov'ev state

The held-map pass of the inverse-equilibrium section laid down one whole Grad-Shafranov read-write cycle at the coefficients its least-squares solve chose. A whole cycle is the fixed-point iteration of the map those coefficients describe, and the Solov'ev known state of the known-answer recovery record starts 1.76 axis-to-boundary spans from its answer, so the step did not approach that map: it left the branch the rows were measured on. Measured on the predecessor's receipts and re-measured here, the map grows every pass while the coefficients walk away with it.

Two probes name the mechanism, one allocation each. Held at the true amplitudes and iterated from the record's starting field, one cycle per step moves the map by 2.80, 0.47, 0.86, 0.98 and 0.80 spans and the flux error reads 1.35, 1.47, 2.21, 3.05 and 3.60 before the shift loses its axis (job 1332134). The row model is not at fault there: at the true amplitudes the whitened rows read 1.5×10-16, so the rows are exactly satisfiable and the failure is the map step alone. The second probe holds the first pass's fitted coefficients: the rows at that map name amplitudes [-2.326, 16.909] against the generating [1, 1], because the second profile column is nearly invisible in the rows there — singular values 1.413 and 0.0531 against 1.413 and 0.2216 at the answer — and the damped cycle at those amplitudes converges to a map 3.49 spans from the true one (job 1332191). A wrong map names a profile the data never saw, and that profile names a wrong map.

The pass now lays down the fraction of its cycle that leaves the held map closest to the map its own coefficients describe, chosen from a halving ladder, and reports the update it applied rather than the undamped one. A pass also moves no amplitude by more than max_coefficient_step times the largest amplitude it holds, so a column the rows barely resolve cannot walk away from the data. The receipt carries the applied update, the fraction and the cycle trials of every pass, and tests/test_flux_iteration.py states both claims on an algebraic instrument, including an instrument whose cycle overshoots by 1.8 and recovers only when a fraction of it is laid down. Landed at 6dd018b63.

The bands are not met. The rule bounds the departure but does not contract it: from 1.76 spans the fit reaches 0.916 at pass 3 and then stalls, because the quantity it descends — the cycle's own residual |F(ψ) - ψ| at the fitted coefficients — is not monotone along the step when the fixed point of that cycle lies three spans away. A tighter coefficient trust radius does worse (1.35 spans, stalling at the first pass), which locates the fault in the step criterion rather than in the coefficient budget. Closing the bands needs a step criterion read off the operator's contraction — the quantity the forward solve already measures — not off the map's residual.

Passes and their applied updates

Each pass reports the update it applies: IterationPass.flux_update carries the largest change of the step the pass laid down divided by the axis-to-boundary span, and IterationPass.fraction the part of its cycle that step is. The gate run's three Solov'ev passes are below; the run stops at pass 3 because no fraction down to one eighth of its cycle leaves the held map nearer the map its coefficients describe than 0.435 of the span. The misfit rises at pass 1 while the cycle residual falls, which is the fault the verdict names: the criterion the ladder descends is the cycle's own residual, not the rows or the map's distance from the answer. The ladder's choice is the minimum of that residual across the rungs it tries, which test_the_pass_lays_down_the_ladder_rung_its_own_cycle_residual_favours separates from taking the first rung that merely improves.
