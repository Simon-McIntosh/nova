Multipoint link sorting: the stored-case build's quadratic, and what remains after it

The stored MAST-U fixture machine is built one element at a time, and every insertion reconstructs the multipoint frame and re-sorts its links. Two walks inside that path each rescanned the whole frame once per row, so the build was quadratic in the element count. Both now do their work in one vectorised pass: MultiPoint.sort_link resolves every label through a single first-occurrence dictionary and re-points forward links with one boolean mask, and MultiPoint.build resolves the reduction with one get_indexer pass and then a lookup over the labels the index does not carry. On one all_debug partition the link sort fell from 1,399.5 s of a 1,490.7 s build — 93.9 % of the wall — to 6.3 s in total, and the insertion phase, which the before arm had not finished after 1,500 s at 14,156 of roughly 30,000 rows, completes in 255.8 s.

The before arm's cumulative sort time grew with a fitted exponent of 3.26 against the row count — the third paragraph of a sum whose terms are each quadratic — which projects 15,918 s (4.4 h) of sort alone at 30,000 rows, consistent with the 3.8 h the diagnosis projected. Its per-call cost rose from 241 ms at 3,162 rows to 1,969 ms at 14,060 rows. The after arm's insertion phase completes at 255.8 s and 29,559 rows, with 33.1 s cumulative in MultiPoint.initialize, of which the sort is 7.0 s; its progress trace had already reached 23,588 rows at 240.3 s. After that the build enters plasmagrid.solve() and spends the rest of its wall there.

What remains, and why the full cold build is still not minutes on CPU

The residual is not frame work. Thread sampler stacks taken during the after arm (job 1338079) sit, from about 250 s onward, in biot/solve.py compose → matrix.py compute → polysection.py coupling, driven by the progress bar for batch in tqdm(self.source_batches, desc=self.name) in nova/biot/solve.py. That is the plasma-filament Biot-Savart coupling solve, 60 source batches at about 1,033 s each on a CPU partition, roughly 17 hours, and it is outside the multipoint link path. So this change removes the quadratic the node was chartered to remove — the sort's share of the wall falls by more than two orders of magnitude — and the full cold build still takes hours on CPU because of an unrelated solve. No claim is made here about that solve's cost on a GPU.

Equality, and a control that could have failed

The sort is compared against a verbatim per-element prefix-scan oracle over five frame shapes at seven row counts (1 to 60), over 120 random link layouts, and against the frame the stored MAST-U case machine produces; the reduction is compared against its own per-element oracle over the same shapes and on labels absent from the index. The focused frame population is 82 passed at base bba8365c9 (17.89 s) and 82 passed at head (19.39 s). The negative control restores the pre-change multipoint.py in a scratch tree and runs the two cost-bound tests: both fail at the stated 12,000 rows — 14974 label scans for 12000 rows against a bound of 120, and 1.40 s to reduce 12000 rows against 0.5 s — with 2 failed, 75 deselected.

Every measurement above ran on an all_debug allocation with an empty fixture-machine cache under the job's own TMPDIR, so the cold walls are comparable. Suite arms at both revisions were cut at their 2,400 s job bounds with six per-test 300 s timeouts each in unrelated modules; they are not a full-suite result and are recorded as cut short.
