Where the stored-case build spends its hours

The stored MAST-U case (45272) never reaches its first plasma-grid batch on a cold fixture machine. In the recorded stored-case run the process is silent from CASE_INPUT at 15:18:36 until the plasmagrid bar begins, and that bar then runs to 59/60 of 60 in 9 minutes before the job is cancelled at its 4-hour limit. The three hours are therefore not in the grid solve; they are in the conductor insertion that precedes it, and this fragment records the frames that hold them.

get_loc resolves a label by scanning the whole index — np.flatnonzero(self.values == label) — and sort_link calls it once per row of the frame. The frame it scans is the one the insert is rebuilding: every conductor element is inserted separately, and each insert concatenates the new subframe onto the accumulated one and re-derives its attributes, so the sort is quadratic in the number of elements already placed.

What the insert path costs, measured

Timing every sort_link call out of the process, on the plain CPU partition, gives the cost law directly. The frame grows by 24 or 25 rows per inserted element, and the largest call in each insert grows with it:

Over the 242 calls that exceed half a second the fitted exponent is 2.06 — the cost is quadratic in the accumulated row count, not in the work the insert adds. The MAST-U case inserts 1 200 active elements (24 conductors, active_element_count 1 200, no passive elements), so the frame ends near 30 000 rows and the last sort alone is about 50 s. Summing the measured quadratic over those 1 200 inserts gives about 3.8 h of sort_link for one cold build, which is the order of the 3.4 h of silence the recorded run shows before its grid solve.

No landed change moves this cost

Nothing between the two revisions touches the code on that stack. No commit in 77283793bc..HEAD changes nova/frame/multipoint.py, nova/frame/columnar.py, nova/frame/framelink.py, nova/frame/poloidalgrid.py, nova/frame/coil.py, nova/frame/dataframe.py, nova/frame/framespace.py or the fixture at tests/test_equilibrium_forward_reference.py. The only change under nova/frame/ in that range is 122 added lines in nova/frame/iron.py, which is on the iron-core path and is not reached: the MAST-U case declares "iron_core": []. So the cost is not a regression that a bisect would find, and it is not attributable to the change that landed between the two revisions.

What distinguishes a build that stalls from one that does not is the machine cache, not the revision. On a cache hit cached_machine returns from _load_cached_machine before build_machine is called at all, so no element is inserted and no frame is re-sorted; on a miss the element-by-element insertion rebuilds the frame and pays the quadratic sort. The stored-case run that was cancelled was a cold build: its three hours of silence sit before the grid solve, which the quadratic insertion accounts for, and the grid solve itself then ran 9 minutes.
