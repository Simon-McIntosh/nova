Where the stored-case build spends its hours

On a cold fixture machine the stored MAST-U case (45272) spends its pre-fit build inside conductor insertion, and the measured cost there alone is long enough to fill a multi-hour job before the plasma grid is solved. That the recorded stored-case run took this path is not established by that run's log, and this fragment keeps the two apart: what was measured, and what the recorded evidence does and does not show.

What the recorded run shows, and does not

stored-cases-titan.log carries two absolute stamps: CASE_INPUT at 15:18:36 and the cancel at 19:18:47, four hours later at the job's limit. Its only progress output is the plasmagrid bar, which reaches 59 of 60 batches with 9 min 4 s on its own clock and is killed mid-bar. tqdm's elapsed is relative to the bar, so the log cannot place the bar in wall time. It establishes that about 3.4 h of the four passed with no progress output, and it does not establish whether that silence sits before the bar or after it — no cache receipt line and no wall stamp on any bar fixes the order. The attribution of the three hours to a named phase is therefore not an observation this log supports, and the earlier reading of it here, which placed the silence before the grid solve, was an inference rather than a measurement.

One related run does place it. probe-titan.log, at 225feaed, is cancelled 13.5 minutes in having printed no plasmagrid bar at all — and tqdm draws a bar's 0 % line as soon as its loop begins, so that run spent its whole allocation before the grid solve. That is evidence that the phase preceding the bar can consume an allocation; it is not evidence about which phase consumed the recorded run's four hours.

get_loc resolves a label by scanning the whole index — np.flatnonzero(self.values == label) — and sort_link calls it once per row of the frame. The frame it scans is the one the insert is rebuilding: every conductor element is inserted separately, and each insert concatenates the new subframe onto the accumulated one and re-derives its attributes, so the sort is quadratic in the number of elements already placed.

What the insert path costs, measured

Timing every sort_link call out of the process, on the plain CPU partition, gives the cost law directly. The frame grows by 24 or 25 rows per inserted element, and the largest call in each insert grows with it:

Across the two arms 634 of those calls exceed half a second — 326 in the be5ee98f7 arm and 308 in the 77283793bc arm — and a least-squares fit of log cost against log rows over them gives an exponent of 2.085 (2.049 and 2.136 arm by arm). The cost is quadratic in the accumulated row count, not in the work the insert adds. The MAST-U case inserts 1 200 active elements (24 conductors, active_element_count 1 200, no passive elements), so the frame ends near 30 000 rows and the last sort alone is about 37 s; summing that fitted law over the 1 200 inserts gives about 4.0 h of sort_link for one cold build. That is a measured cost of this build, large enough to fill a four-hour limit before the grid solve; it is not a measurement of the recorded run's timings, which cannot be placed in wall time from its log.

No landed change moves this cost

Nothing between the two revisions touches the code on that stack. No commit in 77283793bc..HEAD changes nova/frame/multipoint.py, nova/frame/columnar.py, nova/frame/framelink.py, nova/frame/poloidalgrid.py, nova/frame/coil.py, nova/frame/dataframe.py, nova/frame/framespace.py or the fixture at tests/test_equilibrium_forward_reference.py. The only change under nova/frame/ in that range is 122 added lines in nova/frame/iron.py, which is on the iron-core path and is not reached: the MAST-U case declares "iron_core": []. So the cost is not a regression that a bisect would find, and it is not attributable to the change that landed between the two revisions.

Check

The code the stack names is covered by focused unit tests and is green at main: tests/test_multipoint.py, tests/test_framelink.py and tests/test_columnar.py at be5ee98f7 give 74 passed in 6.39s. This record changes no source, so nothing here is at stake in those tests; they are cited to show that the insert-and-link code holding the cost is exercised and passing at the revision the build was measured at.

The figures above recompute from two logs held beside this record: arms2.log holds one SORTLINK_END line per call with its row count, its own seconds and the running total, for both arms in one job, and head-sample.log holds the sixteen stack dumps. The two logs are cited by the manifest's artifacts line with their absolute paths, so a reader can refit the exponent, recount the calls above half a second and reread the dumps without re-running the build. The instrumentation that produced them, build_only.py, is cited the same way.

What would settle the recorded run

Either a stack sample of a live stored-case run at the recorded revision, or absolute wall stamps on its phases: a CACHE fixture=… status=cold|warm line and a start stamp taken before build_machine would place the four hours in one phase without inference. Neither exists in the log this record cites. On the code, a cache hit returns from _load_cached_machine before build_machine is called at all, so a warm machine skips the insertion entirely — which is why the distinction matters and why it needs a recorded line rather than a plausible story.
