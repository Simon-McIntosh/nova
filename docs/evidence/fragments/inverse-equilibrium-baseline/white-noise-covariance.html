Method

Each state carries the sensor set of the known-answer study, 24 wall flux loops and 24 poloidal field probes. Every realisation adds independent Gaussian noise to each sensor row, with standard deviation 1% of the root-mean-square signal of that row family, and to the plasma-current row at 1% of the plasma current. The fit is told exactly that scale as its row weight, the conductor currents stay noise-free, and the unknowns are the two profile amplitudes of the known-answer fit.

The variance-ratio test is a chi-square test on 255 degrees of freedom: the variance of 256 recovered coefficients along one direction, divided by the variance the receipt states along that same direction, is distributed as a reduced chi-square with 255 degrees of freedom. Two principal directions are tested on each of the three states, so six directions are tested in all. The interval is fixed by three statements: each direction keeps a two-sided coverage of 0.99**(1/6) = 0.998327 so that six independent directions jointly cover 0.99; the remaining probability is split into equal tails of (1 - 0.99**(1/6))/2 = 8.368e-4; and each tail is read off the chi-square quantile for 255 degrees of freedom and divided by 255, giving the interval 0.7447 to 1.3017. An interval that splits 0.01, rather than 0.01 parent coverage, into two tails per direction covers 0.98 jointly, not 0.99; that rule is not the one used here. That interval belongs to the linearised arm, whose 256 realisations give 255 degrees of freedom; the full arm recomputes its own interval from its realised count, which is why each state in the section above carries the interval it was judged against.

Each realisation is propagated through the fit's own least-squares operator: the response matrix is built from the fit's directional difference at the generating coefficients, on the same device as the fits, and the recovery is the same whitened solve the fit uses internally. Because the model is linear in the two amplitudes, this is the fit's estimator written once rather than re-converged per draw.

The record has two arms and they are not interchangeable, so each number below says which one produced it. The full nonlinear arm (labelled VERIFY) is the one the verdict rests on: MagneticsFit.fit re-run from a fresh seed on 32 independent noisy realisations of each state, each fit succeeding, failing to qualify, or reaching its iteration budget on its own. The linearised arm (labelled FAST) carries 256 realisations per state for the stated-noise and doubled-noise studies and 32 per state for the 0.5 and 2 sigma flux rows, each propagated through the fit's own least-squares operator; it checks that the receipt covariance is that operator's inverse, supplies the linearised flux-scaling rows, and is not evidence about the recovery itself. The doubled-noise control is run on the full nonlinear arm as well as on the linearised one, and the control section reports the full-arm result, with the linearised figures beside it.

Iterative fit against the operator

These are the handful of full nonlinear fits written by the two earlier jobs, 14, 7 and 7 attempts per state: each row is one realisation put through MagneticsFit.fit from a fresh seed, with the closed-form arm run on the same noisy rows for comparison. The gap column is the largest difference between the two recovered coefficient sets in units of the stated standard deviation; the covariance column compares two covariances and not a covariance against the receipt: it is the largest relative difference between the covariance averaged over the full fits of that arm and the covariance averaged over the closed-form solves of the same realisations. It therefore measures cross-arm agreement between two estimates, and it is only as strong as the smaller count in the arm: an arm with a single fit has one covariance on each side, and a large gap there is a single-realisation fluctuation rather than a disagreement between the methods (MAST at 0.5 sigma reads 0.94 for exactly this reason, and is reported rather than used).
