White sensor noise: is the reported covariance honest?

The known-answer study recovers each state from noise-free rows, so nothing in it tests the uncertainty the fit reports. Here every state is fitted many times from rows carrying independent Gaussian noise, and the scatter of the recovered coefficients is compared with the covariance the fit receipt states. A fit whose covariance is right can be trusted when it says a coefficient is poorly determined; one whose covariance is wrong cannot, however good its best estimate.

Every variance ratio tested lies inside the interval: 6 directions on 3 states, spanning 0.7447 to 1.3017, realised ratios 0.898 to 1.259. The mean reduced chi-square of the whitened residuals is 1.007, 1.015, 1.015 on Solov'ev analytic certificate, MAST shot 22086 slice 43, ITER reference case respectively, every state between 0.9 and 1.1. The largest mean deviation of a recovered coefficient from its generating value is 2.07 standard errors, against a limit of three. Root-mean-square flux error scales with the noise: the ratios to the one-sigma case run Solov'ev analytic certificate 0.55 at 0.5 sigma and 1.79 at 2 sigma; MAST shot 22086 slice 43 0.45 at 0.5 sigma and 1.90 at 2 sigma; ITER reference case 0.55 at 0.5 sigma and 1.91 at 2 sigma, a largest departure from proportionality of 10.6 per cent, inside the 20 per cent the clause allows. The negative control behaves as it must: with the noise drawn at twice the scale the fit is told, the ratios are 3.650 to 4.739, all outside the interval and clustered near four. Four is what a covariance that is correct for the stated noise returns when the true noise is twice the stated scale, so a study that passed this control would be testing nothing.

What this record establishes, and what it does not

This record establishes that, on all three states and along every principal direction tested, the covariance the fit reports is the one its own stated noise model implies, and that the same comparison returns a factor near four when the true noise is twice the scale the fit is told. What the variance ratios can and cannot detect is worth stating before they are read. Each draw is propagated through the fit's own least-squares operator and divided by the covariance that same operator states, so when the row weights equal the scale the draws were given, a correct implementation returns a ratio of one by construction: the test fires when the operator, the weighting, the covariance algebra or the noise model disagree with each other, which is a real class of defect, and it is silent about whether the noise model itself holds. The estimator is a check of internal consistency, not a validation of the estimator against real sensors or against a regime far from linearity. The full nonlinear arm is the only check of the estimator against MagneticsFit.fit itself, and it is a handful of fits per state rather than 256.

It does not close a clause that asks for 256 repeated nonlinear fits per state. The full-fit arm here is 14, 7 and 7 attempted realisations on Solov'ev, ITER and MAST, of which 14, 7 and 3 returned a result: a check of the estimator, not a 256-fit variance study, and it is reported in those terms throughout. Measured cost in the two jobs behind this record is 1,264 s per full fit on Solov'ev and 1,091 s on ITER on one P100, so 256 fits is about 90 and 78 hours per state on a single card, plus the Solov'ev and ITER states' own rebuilds; on MAST the same count costs about five hours at 71 s per fit, before counting the four of its seven attempts that failed to qualify. Closing the repeated-fit clause is therefore a scope decision for the plan owner, and this node does not claim it: what is offered is the linearised variance test, complete, with the full-fit check sized and priced, so the repeated-fit clause can be closed deliberately or left open.

Method

Each state carries the sensor set of the known-answer study, 24 wall flux loops and 24 poloidal field probes. Every realisation adds independent Gaussian noise to each sensor row, with standard deviation 1% of the root-mean-square signal of that row family, and to the plasma-current row at 1% of the plasma current. The fit is told exactly that scale as its row weight, the conductor currents stay noise-free, and the unknowns are the two profile amplitudes of the known-answer fit.

The variance-ratio test is a chi-square test on 255 degrees of freedom: the variance of 256 recovered coefficients along one direction, divided by the variance the receipt states along that same direction, is distributed as a reduced chi-square with 255 degrees of freedom. Two principal directions are tested on each of the three states, so six directions are tested in all. The interval is fixed by three statements: each direction keeps a two-sided coverage of 0.99**(1/6) = 0.998327 so that six independent directions jointly cover 0.99; the remaining probability is split into equal tails of (1 - 0.99**(1/6))/2 = 8.368e-4; and each tail is read off the chi-square quantile for 255 degrees of freedom and divided by 255, giving the interval 0.7447 to 1.3017. An interval that splits 0.01, rather than 0.01 parent coverage, into two tails per direction covers 0.98 jointly, not 0.99; that rule is not the one used here.

Each realisation is propagated through the fit's own least-squares operator: the response matrix is built from the fit's directional difference at the generating coefficients, on the same device as the fits, and the recovery is the same whitened solve the fit uses internally. Because the model is linear in the two amplitudes, this is the fit's estimator written once rather than re-converged per draw.

The record has two arms and they are not interchangeable, so each number below says which one produced it. The linearised arm (labelled FAST) carries 256 realisations per state for the stated-noise and doubled-noise studies, and 32 per state for the 0.5 and 2 sigma flux rows. Every variance ratio, the mean reduced chi-square, the mean recovery and the flux scaling come from this arm. They are the fit's estimator evaluated in closed form and propagated, not the scatter of repeated nonlinear fits, and no linearised variance ratio is written here as a fitted one. The full nonlinear arm (labelled VERIFY) re-runs MagneticsFit.fit from a fresh seed on each realisation, succeeds or fails to converge on its own, and is run for a handful of realisations per state; its purpose is to show how closely the closed-form estimator reproduces what the fit actually does. The table of that comparison gives the fit count per state and arm, and each state's row in the recovery table states how many full fits back it or marks it not measured.

Iterative fit against the operator

These are the full nonlinear fits: each row is one realisation put through MagneticsFit.fit from a fresh seed, with the closed-form arm run on the same noisy rows for comparison. The gap column is the largest difference between the two recovered coefficient sets in units of the stated standard deviation; the covariance column compares two covariances and not a covariance against the receipt: it is the largest relative difference between the covariance averaged over the full fits of that arm and the covariance averaged over the closed-form solves of the same realisations. It therefore measures cross-arm agreement between two estimates, and it is only as strong as the smaller count in the arm: an arm with a single fit has one covariance on each side, and a large gap there is a single-realisation fluctuation rather than a disagreement between the methods (MAST at 0.5 sigma reads 0.94 for exactly this reason, and is reported rather than used). A realisation whose forward evaluation does not qualify is recorded as a failed fit with its seed and its error text and is counted in the failed column rather than ending the study; a state whose failures exceed half of its attempts is reported as not measured, with the count. The converged column is what MagneticsFit.fit itself reports, and it reads zero for every fit here: each one reaches the iteration budget without the convergence test being satisfied, while its recovered coefficients and its covariance still agree with the closed-form arm to the fractions of a stated standard deviation in the gap columns. A reader should take the gap between the two arms as the substantive agreement and the flag as the fit's own statement about its stopping rule, not as evidence that the fit failed. On MAST the forward evaluation refuses to qualify on most noisy realisations, so that state's full-fit arm is thin and its covariance clause is backed by the linearised arm alone.
