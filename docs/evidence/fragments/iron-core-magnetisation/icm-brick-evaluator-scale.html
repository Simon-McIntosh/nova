The brick evaluator's field scale is right

§8 carried an open 1.32 factor: one uniformly magnetised limb gave 1.609e-3 T through the brick evaluator's six-rotation sum against 1.219e-3 T from an independent point-dipole sum over the same bricks, and 2.439e-3 T with the up-down mirror added. This record settles it with numbers, from one brick up.

The defect is in the image enumeration, not in the field kernel. The closed-form brick tensor reduces to the point dipole exactly as a brick shrinks against its distance, and the six-rotation composition reproduces a point-dipole sum over the same bricks to 1.1e-8 relative. The 1.32 comes from a helper that intended to yield six images and yielded eleven: in images(mirror=False), the loop variable rebinds the parameter that selects the image set, so from the second sector on the guard re-reads as true and each sector emits its mirror as well — six rotations plus five mirrors. That helper exists only on the unlanded §8 branch; the base revision's images() takes no argument and is a plain twelve-image set, so nothing in bricks.py is changed here.

Reading the table

Step 1 puts a single 0.1 m brick at 10, 30 and 100 brick edges from the field point and compares the closed-form tensor with the analytic field of the equivalent dipole. The relative difference falls as (edge/distance)4 — faster than the (edge/distance)2 the criterion asks for, because a rectangular brick has inversion symmetry and its quadrupole vanishes. The kernel and its 1/(4π) normalisation are not in question.

Step 2 takes the §8 limb as a uniform 0.1 m brick grid spanning both halves of the midplane and compares the compressed tensor with a point-dipole sum over the same bricks, at (R, z) = (8.4667, 0.6) m. With no images it is 8.29766276e-04 against 8.29766288e-04 T; with the six rotations, 1.21940670e-03 against 1.21940672e-03 T. Both agree to about 1e-8, so neither the moment map frame.T @ moment nor the rotation composition is at fault.

Step 3 is the case §8 quoted. The six-rotation sum is 1.219 mT, the analytic value. The twelve-image sum is 2.439 mT, exactly twice it, because a sector that already spans both halves has its partner under the up-down mirror and the mirror duplicates it. The pre-fix helper's eleven images give 1.609 mT: the six rotations, plus the mirror of five of them, with the k = 0 image alone unmirrored. That is 1.609/1.219 = 1.3195, the factor §8 recorded.

Where the factor first appears

At step 3, the first case in the table whose image set comes from the pre-fix helper; the factor is already 1.32 for the smallest case that uses it. The line is the inner generator of images(mirror=False) on the unlanded branch: 

for mirror in (1.0, -1.0) if mirror else (1.0,):

The parameter mirror is read to choose the iterable, and the loop then assigns to mirror. The first sector's iterable is built while the parameter is False and yields one image; every later iteration re-reads the rebound 1.0, takes the truthy branch, and yields two. The count is eleven, not six. Replacing the parameter's name with a distinct loop variable, or hoisting the condition out of the loop, removes it.

The base revision does not carry this helper: images() has no mirror parameter and yields twelve images, which is correct for the one-sided sector sector_bricks builds. The branch that introduced it (7762e7e84) is not an ancestor of the base, so there is no live defect to repair and bricks.py is untouched.

What pins it

tests/test_brick_evaluator_scale.py pins steps 1 and 2 against the analytic dipole, plus the twelve-image response() against its own point-dipole sum, and the doubling a mirror produces over a both-halves sector. Five tests pass against the base evaluator. The declared negative control restores the pre-fix image generator and reddens test_one_sector_with_six_rotations_matches_the_point_dipole (ACTUAL 0.001609 against DESIRED 0.001219, relative difference 0.3195) and test_a_mirror_over_a_both_halves_sector_doubles_the_field (ratio 2.438e-3 / 1.609e-3 = 1.516 against the required 2.0).

One correction to the §8 record

The §8 fragment quotes the 1.609e-3 T case at (16 m, 0.6 m). The measurement behind it ran at (8.4667 m, 0.6 m); at 16 m the same uniform limb gives 1.172e-4 T through six rotations, not 1.609e-3 T. The factor and its cause are unchanged, but the radius in that sentence should read 8.4667 m.
