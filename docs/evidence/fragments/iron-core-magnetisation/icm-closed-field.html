§2 — The conic sheet field, evaluated by the printed Part II closed form

Node icm-closed-field evaluates the sloped conic sheet's field by the Part II closed form (Urankar, IEEE Trans. Magn. MAG-18(3), 911–917, 1982), not only by the semi-analytic route on main. The D part of the azimuthal antiderivative — a complete first- and second-kind block — is formed in closed form and differentiated as the same expression, so sheet_field(..., closed=True) adds no quadrature there. Both components hold the section's 1e-10 floor at all twelve fixed targets and all three sloped inclinations, and tests/test_edge_sheet.py asserts it on both comparisons: against the ring reference and against the landed semi-analytic field.

Measured: worst relative field error per slope

Geometry is the section's own: segments of length 0.1 m centred at r = 1.5 m, a cell width of 0.02 m, and twelve targets at half a cell, one cell, one segment and ten segments off the middle and off each end of the segment. The reference is the ring quadrature in scripts/edge_sheet/reference.py, panel-bisected to 1e-13. The worst relative error over both components at each sloped inclination is:

The worst case is 4.2e-12, a factor of twenty-four inside the floor, and the two closest middle-station targets — the half-cell standoff that the semi-analytic route only just clears — are inside it as well.

What is closed, what is shared, and why

The bracket the antiderivative carries is S = a_1 b_1 R + A: R is the complete first- and second-kind block, and A is the arsinh remainder Part II leaves to quadrature. The split matters:

  • R is closed and differentiated as itself. With a^2 = (r + r_p)^2 + gamma^2 and k^2 = 4 r r_p / a^2, R = 2a[2(1 - k^2)K + (k^2 - 2)E]/(3k^2). Its partials are the forward-mode tangents of that expression through nova.biot.completeelliptic's own descent, wrapped in custom_jvp functions whose tangents come from the module's _complete_kind_tangent recurrence — private, and imported for this one purpose.
  • A is taken whole from the landed kernel. Part II leaves it numerical, and the landed graded route already differentiates exactly that remainder correctly to 1e-10 at every station. It is therefore evaluated once, by one function, extracted from _gradients as _arsinh_gradient and called by both routes. Sharing it means the two fields cannot disagree about a piece neither computes in closed form, and no second rule for it exists.

Evaluated that way the two routes agree end by end, to every digit printed: at the end of a failing target the triple (S, dS/dgamma, dS/dr) is (-16.90779693, 0.83563409, -9.67041253) on both, and at the start (-17.34165329, 0.68025345, -9.29365056).

The reduction that was tried and removed

Differentiating the remainder through the closed form's own by-parts reduction — the pole-reduced third-kind form the flux route uses — produced partials that were right in value and wrong in slope: the bracket value matched the landed one to nine digits while dS/dgamma came out at 0.43 times the analytic one at the segment's end and 1.83 times it at the start, dS/dr being out by 2.0 and 2.5 percent. Centred differences could not settle it and misled one attempt: the quotient and the gap — the standoff r_1 - r, a single scalar shared by every azimuthal node — agree wherever the standoff is real, and diverge only where the difference step moves the standoff by two orders of magnitude more than the standoff itself. The instrument that settled it was the landed route's own analytic kernels, and the conclusion was that the reduction's traced copy of the remainder is not worth keeping when the value it protects is already shared. That copy, its third-kind helper and the polynomial-division and Jacobi-moment helpers only it used are removed rather than left dead; the flux route's own by-parts reduction, which is what the section's flux node landed, is untouched.

Negative control

The declared mutation is the sheet current's sign on one edge. Flipping the start edge's sign inside _sloped_field_closed and running the two closed-form field tests through pytest turns both red at every sloped inclination, and the log carries the failing test ids and its EXIT= line. So a kernel that silently ignored the current's sign, or an antisymmetric pair that cancelled the two edges, fails the gate rather than passing everywhere.

State of the working tree

nova/biot/sheet.py carries SheetFlux._closed_gradient and _sloped_field_closed, with closed=True on field() and sheet_field() selecting them. tests/test_edge_sheet.py holds both components to the floor against the reference and against the landed semi-analytic field over the twelve targets at pi/8, pi/4 and 3pi/8; the existing tests are unchanged, and the whole gate is 285 passed in 100 s with EXIT=0. The gate log and the negative-control log sit in this run's directory.
