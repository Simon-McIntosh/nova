Where this stands

nova/biot/sheet.py now evaluates the sloped conic sheet's field by differentiating the Part II closed form directly, reusing the reconciled coefficient block the flux route already carries: _closed_bracket forms the azimuthal integral S in complete elliptic integrals, and _closed_gradient takes its two partials, so sheet_field(..., closed=True) costs no azimuthal quadrature beyond the one arsinh remainder the paper itself leaves there.

The value is exact: the traced closed form reproduces SheetFlux.antiderivative_closed to 3.1e-14 at every segment end tested. The derivative is not, at the start and end stations, so the field misses the section's 1e-10 floor there and this node is blocked rather than landed.

What the defect is, and what it is not

The bracket is S = a_1 b_1 R + mean - arc, with R the complete first- and second-kind block, mean the arsinh remainder the paper leaves to quadrature, and arc the pole-reduced third-kind remainder. Each was split out and differentiated separately at a failing target (r = 2.470073509, gamma = -0.382683432, where r_1 - r = 0 exactly):

The complete-integral pieces differentiate correctly, and so does the third-kind reduction on both of its branches: the reduced and unreduced splits each match a centred difference of their own value to six digits, and the unreduced derivative agrees with the reduced one where the two overlap. The defect is confined to mean, the arsinh remainder.

A difference quotient is not the instrument to settle this, and using one cost the earlier attempt its diagnosis: the quotient and the autodiff agree to four digits at every real standoff (0.5 to 2.4e-4), and diverge only where the step itself moves the standoff by two orders of magnitude more than the standoff, so near the line the quotient is the unreliable side, not the derivative.

The instrument that settles it is the landed route's own analytic kernels. _gradients forms the same bracket and its two partials term by term, and it is known good: its field reproduces the ring reference at these targets. Comparing the two triples at the same end of the same target:

The values agree to nine digits and the partials do not: dS/dgamma comes out at 0.43 times the analytic one at the end and 1.83 times it at the start, and dS/dr is out by 2.0 and 2.5 percent. That is the defect, stated against an independent derivation rather than against a quotient.

One more measurement fixes where it is not: the closed-form flux's own numerical derivative at this target reproduces the reference field (B_R = 4.551e-09, B_Z = -5.891e-09 against the reference's 4.551e-09 and -5.891e-09), so the flux route is sound at the same station and the error enters with the differentiation, not with the geometry or the bracket's value.

The narrowed question is which term of _closed_gradient supplies the wrong slope: the arsinh remainder is the only piece that is not a complete integral, and it is the piece the earlier difference quotients pointed at. Its standoff gap = r_1 - r is one scalar shared by every azimuthal node, snapped to zero inside 1e-12 r; making that snap value-only with a stop_gradient correction moved its autodiff from 1.99 to 0.15 and was reverted, so the snap alone does not account for it.

State of the working tree

_closed_bracket, _closed_gradient, _sloped_field_closed and closed= on field() and sheet_field() are in place and committed. The complete-kind and third-kind evaluations are traced through jax.custom_jvp wrappers whose tangents come from nova.biot.completeelliptic's own recurrences — the private _complete_kind_tangent and _complete_pole_tangent are imported for this and nothing else. _divide is numpy.polynomial.polynomial.polydiv, padded back to one remainder coefficient per divisor degree.

No closed-form field test was added. The twelve-target comparison the node needs would fail on six of nine sloped parameter sets, and a suite that lands red on its own new assertion is not a delivery. The existing flux and field tests are unchanged and were not re-run in this attempt; the value route they cover is untouched by the tangent tracing.

What the next attempt has

Two things were tried and are recorded so the next attempt does not repeat them. Forming the numerator as the landed route does — Gamma = a_1^2 gamma + b_1 W in place of gamma + b_1(r_p - r cos phi), algebraically the same expression — changes the triples in no digit. (r_p - r cos phi) is W + b_1 gamma, so it cannot be the difference.

The remaining question is narrow and stated: which term of _closed_gradient contributes a slope that is 0.43 times the analytic one at the end of the segment and 1.83 times it at the start. The comparison to make is per term, against _gradients' own kernels at the same (gamma, r, r_p) rather than against a quotient: R and arc already match their quotients, so the arsinh remainder is the term to hold against its analytic counterpart, and the sign of its error reverses between the two ends. If the remainder's near-singular part needs separating into a reduced expression whose derivative is conditioned like its value — as the pole remainder already is — that is the modification to make, not another quotient study.
