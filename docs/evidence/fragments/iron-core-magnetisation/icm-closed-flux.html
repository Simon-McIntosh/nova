What this node established

Node icm-closed-flux carries the sloped conic sheet's poloidal flux in closed form. The D part of the azimuthal antiderivative is a complete first- and second-kind combination. The arsinh part is reduced by parts and then by the pole structure of its own denominator: it becomes complete integrals of all three kinds plus one smooth arsinh integral, which is the remainder the paper itself leaves to quadrature. The printed eq 22a coefficient block is not used, and the derivation below shows why it cannot be: the block as printed does not reproduce the paper's own eq (13) integral.

Measured against scripts/edge_sheet/reference.py over the twelve-target set at slopes pi/8, pi/4 and 3pi/8, the closed form holds the section's 1e-10 floor with a worst relative deviation of 7.7e-12, and agrees with the landed graded route to 7.4e-12.

The D part

The landed route integrates over half the turn, R = int_0^pi cos(phi) D(phi) d phi, with D^2 = (r - r')^2 + gamma^2 + 4 r r' sin^2(phi/2). In the Jacobi variable u (d phi = 2 dn u du, D = a dn u, cos phi = 2 sn^2 u - 1), with int_0^K dn^2 u du = E and int_0^K sn^2 u dn^2 u du = [(1-k^2)K + (2k^2-1)E]/(3k^2),

R = 2a [2(1 - k^2) K + (k^2 - 2) E] / (3 k^2),   k^2 = 4 r r'/a^2

At (r, z) = (0.5, 0), end (r', z') = (1.5, 0.6), the closed form gives -0.71669812 and a dense trapezoid of the same integrand gives -0.71669812, relative agreement 3.1e-16; the other end, (0.8, -0.5), gives -0.62232451 both ways, relative 5.4e-16.

The arsinh part, reduced

Write the azimuthal integral the module needs as A = int_0^pi P(phi) arsinh(beta_1) d phi with P = r_1 cos(phi) + b_1^2 r cos^2(phi), beta_1 = Gamma_1 / B_1, Gamma_1 = a_1^2 gamma + b_1 (r_1 - r cos phi) and the exact squared denominator B_1^2 = (r_1 - r cos phi)^2 + a_1^2 r^2 sin^2(phi). Split P into its mean b_1^2 r / 2 and an oscillatory part whose antiderivative Q = r_1 sin(phi) + (b_1^2 r / 4) sin(2 phi) vanishes at both ends. Integrating the oscillatory part by parts then leaves no boundary term, and what remains is the derivative of the arsinh, which is rational:

d arsinh(beta_1)/d phi
    = a_1 r sin(phi) [b_1 r (r - r_1 cos phi) - gamma (r_1 + b_1^2 r cos phi)]
      / (B_1^2 D)

so that, with N = a_1 r (1 - c^2)(r_1 + b_1^2 r c / 2) [b_1 r (r - r_1 c) - gamma (r_1 + b_1^2 r c)] and c = cos(phi),

A = (b_1^2 r / 2) int_0^pi arsinh(beta_1) d phi
    - int_0^pi N(c) / (B_1^2 D) d phi

The first term is the paper's eq (12b) remainder and is g(pi/2)-like: smooth, logarithmically integrable at worst, and left to quadrature on the same fixed graded rule the module already uses. The identity above was checked at both segment ends of the node's reference segment: the quadrature of A and the reduced right-hand side agree to 2.4e-15 and 1.9e-15.

The second term is elementary. N is quartic in c and B_1^2 quadratic, so the division leaves a quadratic quotient, which integrates onto K and E through the Jacobi moments, and a linear remainder. That remainder sits over B_1^2, a quadratic in y = sin^2 a = sn^2 u; its two roots give the two characteristics n_p = 1/y_p, and each 1/(y - y_p) is a complete integral of the third kind. Those are the paper's own n_p^2 = 2 b_1^2 r / (d_1 -/+ a_1 c_1), reached here from the geometry rather than transcribed.

The root on the range end

A target level with the segment end puts a root of B_1^2 on phi = 0, where the bracket carries the same (1 - cos phi). Left in, the split is a difference of two quarter-turn integrals that diverge together and cancel: at a 45-degree segment with the target on the supporting line the two characteristics come out at +/-1 and the closed form lost seven digits (9.8e-07 relative where the graded route held 1e-10). Cancelling the shared factor before the split leaves a linear denominator and a single finite characteristic; the target set's deviating point then agrees with the reference to 1.1e-14. complete_pole returns zero for a pole of zero, which is correct for the callers whose numerator vanishes with it and is not our case, so the cancellation is what keeps this reduction off that branch.

Why the printed coefficient block is not used

Part II eq (13)'s phi column carries int_0^{u_i} du [S - T sn^2 u](cn^2 u - sn^2 u). Its complete limit is elementary and gives M = S(1 - 2/k^2) + b_1^2 a (4 - k^2)/(6 k^2) and N = 2S/k^2 - b_1^2 a (4 + k^2)/(6 k^2). Compared with a quadrature of that same u-integral (scipy ellipj, 400,001 points):

segment endu-integral, quadraturederived M K + N Eprinted M K + N E
(r',z') = (1.5, 0.6)-4.7406755-4.7406755-4.3464800
(r',z') = (0.8, -0.5)-4.1261708-4.1261708-3.7261758

The derived coefficients reproduce the paper's own integral to every digit carried; the printed ones do not. Reading the printed S(k) against the constraint that eq (13)'s D block reduce to b_1^2 R fails in the same way, so the misprint is not isolated to one symbol and the stamped block is not a usable source. The reduction above therefore starts from the integrand rather than from the block.

A second defect, and what the node changed

The landed module did not import. nova/biot/sheet.py took _weighted_residual from :mod: nova.biot.polygonanalytic, which no longer defines it: the weighted path moved to graded_residual(..., weight=...) in :mod: nova.biot.gradedresidual, and the old name was removed with it. Every route through the module raised ImportError at HEAD, including the graded route the closed form is checked against. The call is now the current one; the graded route's own values are unchanged by the repair, which the twelve-target agreement with the reference confirms.
