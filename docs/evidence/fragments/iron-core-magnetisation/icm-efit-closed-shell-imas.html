imas-efit closed-shell control from an IMAS pulse: S is reproduced to 1.1 % and 0.8 % once the iron outline is refined

Verdict. The section 10 control now runs end to end on a pulse with no XML machine file, and it reproduces the exact closed-shell shielding factor at both radius ratios: S = 60.42 at a/b = 0.9 (exact 61.101838, −1.12 %) and S = 193.49 at a/b = 0.5 (exact 195.055750, −0.81 %), both inside the 2 % gate. The equilibrium slices report CONVERGENCE_SUMMARY: status= 2 iterations= 1 cerror= 0.000E+000 and the iron relaxation reaches relErr = 1.1E-06; the flux scale is fully linear (IRON_BMOD Bmax = 3.0897E-07 T, four orders below any saturation). The number is only reproduced at a refined outline: the twenty-vertex half-arc a naive case produces over-shields by 20 %, and the reason is a hard 500-knot ceiling in the dependency.

The case, as IDSs

A spherical shell (inner radius a, outer b = 1 m, μr = 1000) with a one-turn coil of radius 0.03 m at 104 A inside it, and no plasma: the pulse constrains magnetics/ip to 0, so the reconstruction is the vacuum poloidal flux of the coil in the iron's presence — exactly the quantity S is defined for. make_pulse.py writes five IDSs at DD 4.1.0: pf_active (one rectangular coil, one supply, one circuit whose connections make the coil its own single-coil circuit), iron_core (the shell's poloidal half-annulus as one closed polygon plus a flat B–H table, b_field = [0, 10³], permeability_relative = [1000, 1000]), wall, magnetics (four probes, σ = 10 T) and tf. imas-efit logs Iron source: IMAS iron_core IDS (preferred) and builds the boundary itself; the solver XML carries only solver settings and the data-selection attributes. The exact factor the two numbers above are compared against is not restated here: make_pulse.py imports exact_shielding from scripts/iron_linear_magnetisation/controls.py, the repository's one closed-shell formula, now taking the radius ratio and μr as arguments whose defaults are that module's own 0.9–1 m shell.

The 20- and 40-vertex rows each appear twice in the full table, once at relativeError 1e-2 (e2, fine) and once at 1e-4 (e4, v40): the pairs differ by 0.16 % and 0.20 % at a/b = 0.9 and by 0.50 % and 0.52 % at a/b = 0.5, so the solver tolerance is not the lever and the outline resolution is.

Against §6b's 0.5 % ceiling on a final boundary refinement, the two series differ in kind: the a/b = 0.9 ladder converges (last step 0.32 %, passed = true) and the a/b = 0.5 ladder does not (last step 1.49 %, passed = false). The 1.49 % step at 0.5 is larger than the 0.80 % by which that arm lands from the exact value, so its agreement is not a converged value — it is one rung of a ladder that is still moving, and it is reported as inside the 2 % gate rather than as resolved. The probe spread at the finest outline is 0.40 % and 0.84 %. The series is not monotone: at a fixed knot count the uniform knot grid aliases against the polygon's corners, so S reads 55.15 at 80 vertices and 60.01 at 160 on the same 500 knots.

The interior probes separate the two effects. Their flux ratio is the shielded interior field, and it is flat across the whole outline series — 0.9215 to 0.9221 at a/b = 0.9 and 0.6732 to 0.6750 at a/b = 0.5, a range of 0.07 % and 0.27 % — while S ranges over 55.15 to 73.52 and 180.18 to 196.42 on the same arms. The reconstruction's interior solution is therefore stable and the exterior variance is the polygon's flux at the probe positions (the knot aliasing above), not a global field error that a larger solve would fix.

Finding for the owners: the iron outline's resolution is unreachable from a pulse

IronCoreBuilder derives nBP = max(nVertices·10, 50) and caps it at maxBreakpoints = 500 (src/ironCoreBuilder.h:20). The only lever a pulse has is the number of vertices, which changes the answer — by 20 % at a/b = 0.9 — and no pulse can ask for more than 500 knots or for a corner-aware knot distribution. The ask is a pulse-level breakpoint count (or an exposed cap), so resolution is a stated input rather than an accident of the outline's vertex count.

Findings for the owners: the options must declare the probe selection

In unified IMAS mode the probes come from magnetics, but their data selection must still exist in efitOptions: without <magneticProbes interpolationMethod=…/> (and the same for <efitCircuits> and <bVacRadiusProduct>) every slice dies at DataSelection::getValue: invalid interpolationMethod=0. The probes themselves are constrained at weight 1 with σ = 10 T, so their pull on the solution is negligible while their rows stay in the Jacobian.

Findings for the owners: a plasma-filled variant of this case is refused, and the iron is not the cause

The measurement pins ip to zero, and it had to. Two plasma-filled variants fail, in two different ways, and neither failure is iron-dependent.

The air-core member of the first pair fails on exactly the same line, so cold start seeding, not the iron, is what breaks there; the arm was re-solved on 2026-11-17 (SLURM 1323922) and reproduced the three lines. The second is a magnetics-only constraint set — four poloidal probes plus the coil circuit — whose Jacobian is rank-deficient (matrix: SVD smallest singular value is zero on 474 records of the trial series), so the iteration oscillates and the ceiling test correctly refuses it. Nothing on the shielding path needs a plasma; both are reported because a pulse carrying only a coil and a few probes is an ordinary case.

Verdict for §9, one-directionally

This is positive evidence and only positive: an independent code, reading the same geometry as a polygonal iron_core and solving for a linear μr = 1000 shell, agrees with the exact exterior shielding factor to 1.1 % at a/b = 0.9 and 0.8 % at a/b = 0.5, and thus corroborates the closed-shell representation and nova's own prototype reading (60.4 against 61.1). It does not test §9's WEST pulse-57410 probe comparison, which stays open: no WEST data was read here, and the case has no plasma. It also does not clear imas-efit's saturation path — the B–H table carries no curvature, so nothing about μr(B) was exercised.
