Derivation and production route

For counter-clockwise cell boundaries, BR = −∂ψ/∂z/r and BZ = ∂ψ/∂r/r imply ∫BR dV = 2π∮ψ dr and ∫BZ dV = 2π∮ψ dz. Nova returns total loop flux Φ = 2πψ, so the implementation uses ∮Φ(dr,dz)/V. The source sheet obeys K ds = MR dr + MZ dz. Hence VG = CLCᵀ, with L the reciprocal double edge integral of the filament mutual flux and C the signed normal incidence.

The existing complement-native elliptic filament combinations are reused. The polygon-area flux primitives are not a sheet-sheet mutual integral. Each undirected edge pair is integrated once and reused in the transpose. Coincident pairs fold the parameter square about its diagonal, with cubic grading of both Duffy coordinates; endpoint-adjacent pairs use two graded Duffy triangles. Partial collinear overlaps are split into atomic edges first. Other near pairs use order-24 Gauss quadrature and distant pairs order 8. Cell volumes use geometric first moments to avoid cancellation in thin cells at large R. This integrates the symmetric bilinear form; it does not symmetrise a collocation operator.

The two-cell collocation reproduction gives ||WG−(WG)ᵀ||/||WG|| = 0.0175572625769523. The rectangular self-block, normalised by μ₀, changes by 4.48007e-6, 7.95662e-8 and 6.12783e-9 at orders 8, 16 and 24 against order 32. Five focused controls pass, including a thin cell far from the axis and the exact weighted mean of a linear external field.

The residual now averages the external callback using the same 2πR test, by signed triangle quadrature. The saved WEST external vectors are reused as a prescribed piecewise-constant cell field; their exact weighted averages are those same vectors. This isolates the interaction change and is not a new integration of the physical coil/plasma sources. Arbitrary-point MagnetisationSolution.field uses the same sheet-field path. The mesh and constitutive fit and coefficient functions are unchanged.

Closed-form sphere consistency bound

A constant exact interior field has zero Hessian, so averaging it over a cell introduces no Taylor remainder. A nonzero O(h²) remainder of that constant field cannot justify the observed centroid variation. The discrete error instead comes from the polygon-cell consistency defect of exact uniform sphere magnetisation, amplified by the linear material resolvent. The following bound includes that actual cell-average defect and the independent centroid observation operator.

Let N = 44, u = (0,…,0,1,…,1)ᵀ, ḡ = Gvolume/μ₀, C = Pinterior,ZGpoint/μ₀, β = 1 − 1/μr, and S = diag(√(Vi/ΣV)), repeated for both components. Write SḡS⁻¹ = QΛQᵀ. The exact sphere self-field of uniform magnetisation is (2/3)μ₀M, so define d = ḡu − (2/3)u, e = Cu − (2/3)1, a = QᵀSd, and T = CS⁻¹Q. These depend only on geometry, the two field operators and μr; they do not use the solved magnetisation or its measured error.

The exact uniform magnetisation is M* = 3(μr−1)B₀/[μ₀(μr+2)]. Subtracting its discrete residual from (I−βḡ)M = βB₀u/μ₀ gives M−M*u = βM*(I−βḡ)⁻¹d. Since μ₀M*/Binterior = β, the relative centroid error is exactly βe + β²T diag((1−βλk)⁻¹)a in exact arithmetic. Applying the triangle inequality gives the closed form

E ≤ β‖e‖∞ + β² max_i Σ_k |T_ik a_k|/(1−βλ_k) + η, with η = 128 ε (2N) / min_k(1−βλ_k) as an explicit floating-point margin. The energy spectrum makes all denominators positive. The test computes this bound independently of the returned state, asserts that it stays below 1e-5, and compares the observed interior error against the computed bound. The 1e-5 ceiling is a rounded envelope of the largest derived bound, rather than a tolerance fitted to the observed interior error.
