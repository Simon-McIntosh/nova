The affine demagnetisation bound: the volume target rule, and the pinned operator

The excess sits in the volume potential's target rule, not in the affine basis and not in graded cells. On the 115-cell 16/80 mm octagonal shell the delivered rule reads 1.0000016; the same assembly with only that rule raised from order // 4 (six nodes per triangle) to twelve reads 0.999999996. The delivered rule crosses the bound on the uniform 160 mm and 40 mm shells too (1.0000078 and 1.0000072), so grading is not the cause: every mesh tested with 40 mm-scale cells or thinner sits at 1 ± 1e-5 and the rule decides which side, while the 20-cell 200 mm mesh keeps a 6.5e-4 margin.

The lead's 2026-11-19 ruling replaced the hand-back with a landing: the volume potential now integrates twelve nodes per triangle against the pairs inside eight least widths of each other and six elsewhere (424ec1958), which keeps every shell inside the bound at 2.7 times the delivered rule's wall instead of the global repair's 4.1 times. That matters because the repair moves the operator test_linear_shell_operator_pin pins by 1.4e-6 relative against a reproduction floor of 8.5e-12, and a global twelve-node rule triples the volume block's wall (236 s to 916 s on the 115-cell shell). The pin's control is re-baselined onto the repair revision (e7a5bd8a3), so the pin compares the working tree against the rule it now ships. WEST remains unmeasured, and the reason moved from the scheduler to a receipt: the H200 refuses a nested launch and its cards were fully allocated, and on the CPU route west_inputs() needs a provenance JSON (.../west-62751/mesh-pair/inputs/west-62751-solve-inputs.json) whose directory does not exist, beside a hex pickle that is present and digest-matching.

Which block carries the excess

The owner assembly is E = Eedge + Ecross + Evolume, contracted with the affine edge coefficient and the constant volume coefficient of linear_magnetisation_geometry. Splitting it in place (scripts/iron_operator_bound/localise.py) reproduces the owner's operator to 3.1e-16 relative on the delivered rule, so the split is the owner and not a second assembly. The largest eigenvalue's witness is reported in quotient units, so its three blocks sum to the quotient.

Two facts fix the diagnosis. Dropping the edge rule to eight nodes — a legal configuration of the same assembly — lifts the quotient to 1.0106 and inflates the witness's blocks by a factor of about 200: the excess rides on a mode whose blocks cancel to one part in a thousand, so it is a quadrature error in a nearly degenerate direction, not a structural defect of the affine basis. Raising the volume rule alone, with the edge and boundary rules untouched, removes it. The boundary rule is therefore not the cause: the repaired arm holds with twelve boundary nodes, and no higher boundary rule was needed.

Where it sits in the mesh (16/80 shell, delivered rule): the largest witness puts 0.284 of its 1.0 on one cell and 0.0275 on one pair. Both are in the outer 6 mm band of the grading: cell 110 spans R = 0.3800 to 0.3860 m and Z = −0.032 to 0.008 m, a 6 mm × 40 mm cell, cell 111 is the next cell along the band, and cells 105, 107 and 109 fill it too. The witness therefore sits where the volume self-term is thinnest, which is the geometry the delivered rule integrates worst; the coarse uniform meshes cross the bound by as much as the graded ones (7.8e-6 at 160 mm, 7.2e-6 at 40 mm), and only the 20-cell 200 mm mesh keeps a 6.5e-4 margin.

Every single-cell quotient is below the bound: worst 0.97295, a 6 mm × 40 mm cell of the graded 16/80 mesh; 0.97248 on the uniform 40 mm mesh; and 0.96030 for an isolated 8 mm × 40 mm cell at R = 6 m (scripts/iron_operator_bound/selfcell.py, receipt selfcell.log), so no self-cell term carries it: the excess is mutual, which is what the volume target rule integrates.

The rule, and its cost

linear_galerkin_cell_interaction integrated the volume potential at section_nodes(polygon, max(6, order // 4)): 36 points per cell at the default edge order 24. The integrand is the flux of the cell's own uniform volume current, logarithmic at the cell's own boundary, so that rule is a slowly converging quadrature of a self-term, and the same target points enter every cell pair. Twelve nodes per triangle (144 points per cell) is the value measured here; it is a repaired rule, not a converged reference, and it holds on every shell measured.

The rule now has a name, which is all the landed change does (23da5edaa, behaviour-preserving: the pin passes and the delivered shell rows are unchanged to the bit):

def _volume_section_order(order):
    """Return the section-rule order of the volume potential's target points."""
    return max(6, order // 4)

and the volume block calls section_nodes(polygon, _volume_section_order(order)). The repair is the one-line body change return max(12, order // 2), kept as volume-rule.patch in the receipts. The edge order, the boundary rule and the Gram blocks are untouched, so the constant-basis path and every coefficient are unchanged. Measured block walls on the 115-cell shell, JAX compile included: edge 24 4.4 s, boundary 12 84.4 s, volume 6 236.3 s, volume 12 915.9 s. The repair takes the shell assembly from 325 s to 1005 s, a factor 3.1.

The targeted rule: twelve nodes where the pair is near, six elsewhere

The twelve-node rule above repairs the bound by integrating the volume potential at twelve nodes on every pair, which triples the volume block's wall on the 115-cell shell (236 s to 916 s). The error it repairs is not everywhere: on that shell, 73.1 per cent of the whole six-against-twelve change to the assembled block sits inside one cell width, and only 26.7 per cent of it on the diagonal. The shipped rule therefore crosses its two rules by a geometric criterion, _VOLUME_NEAR_FACTOR = 8.0 least widths, and the near-field block is overwritten where the criterion fires (424ec1958).

The factor is the first one that leaves the operator match a margin rather than the first that looks large.
