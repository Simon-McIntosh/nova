§5 — Graded refinement of the piecewise-constant Galerkin iron route

Node icm-graded-refinement measures what a graded cell-size field buys on the piecewise-constant Galerkin iron route: the spacing is fine near the iron surfaces and grows to a stated coarse limit in the interior, with the sliver rule and the uniform grid untouched. It measures the analytic μr = 1000 closed shell at five uniform sizes and three graded gradings, and WEST 62751 at two graded levels. No formulation decision is recorded here. The result is that grading does not beat the uniform mesh per cell on this closed shell: the two families fall on one curve, and the fine graded arm reaches the 0.5 per cent bound only by carrying more cells than the uniform arm needs.

What changed

nova/biot/magnetisation.py gains a cell-size field. Given a fine spacing, a coarse limit and a set of iron surfaces, the spacing along each axis at distance d from the nearest surface is fine * (coarse / fine) ** (d / coarse), capped at coarse, and the thin-feature rule raises the spacing to max(width, fine) / cells_across where a part is thin enough for that to be finer. The rectilinear mesh is then clipped to the region as before, so the sliver rule and the cell construction are the ones already measured. _mesh keeps its uniform branch byte for byte: np.arange(r0, r1, size) plus the closing coordinate, so the default reproduces the recorded grid and the recorded solve bit for bit, and test_uniform_cell_size_reproduces_the_recorded_grid asserts that against a digest.

The graded field is passed to solve_magnetisation and to nothing else; no other public signature changed. Both the accuracy contract and the measurement script read one set of constants — GRADED_FINE = 0.002, GRADED_COARSE = 0.0048 and GRADED_CELLS_ACROSS = 20 — imported from tests/test_magnetisation.py, so the test and the receipt cannot disagree about the grading.

The operator the spectrum belongs to

The cell interaction is not symmetric on its own, because the axisymmetric volume measure 2 π r enters the energy form. The operator carrying the spectrum is the symmetric similarity V^(1/2) G V^(-1/2) / μ0 with V the repeated cell volume, and galerkin_spectrum reports that one. Reading eigvalsh off G alone reads one triangle of an unsymmetric matrix: on a 212-cell two-radius mesh it returns [−0.1131, 1.1041], while the volume-weighted spectrum is [2.35e-5, 0.99667] with a symmetry defect of 2.16e-17. test_galerkin_spectrum_is_the_volume_weighted_symmetric_range asserts both halves of that, and it is fast enough to run in the default lane. Every spectrum below is the volume-weighted one and stays inside [0, 1].

What the two families say together

Grading does not buy accuracy per cell here. At about four thousand cells the doubled-field graded mesh reaches 1.052 per cent worst-probe, while the uniform 8 mm mesh reaches 0.828 per cent at 4916 cells. At about nine thousand cells the graded mesh reaches 0.541 per cent at 9082 cells and the uniform mesh 0.548 per cent at 8630. Both families walk down one curve between h−1.4 and h−1.65, so the graded spacing does not change where the error sits for a given cell count; what it changes is the shape of the mesh, not the resolution the projection sees. That is the substantive measurement.

The consequence for the 0.5 per cent bound is that both families reach it, at different costs. The graded arm reaches 0.326, 0.397 and 0.291 per cent at 13 410 cells, and its doubled field misses the bound at 4017 cells (1.052 per cent), which is the negative control. The uniform arm reaches 0.548 per cent at 8630 cells, so it needs roughly nine and a half thousand cells to clear the bound, fewer than the graded arm's 13 410. On this closed shell, grading to a fine spacing at the surfaces and 4.8 mm inside costs about four thousand extra cells to reach the same accuracy.

Why the graded arms run on the CPU lane

The P100 admits a single HLO argument of at most 13 653 508 096 bytes. A mesh of n cells carries 3n unknowns and a dense factor near 8 (3n)^2 bytes: 11 000 cells needs about 8.7 GB and failed at 5.37 GiB more; 12 851 cells failed earlier at 11.09 GiB. The pinned graded arm measured 13 410 cells, 40 230 unknowns and a factor of about 12.9 GB before the interaction matrix is counted, which is the argument the device refuses. It runs on the CPU lane with 110 GB of host memory, assembling in 3660 s and solving in 237 s. No grading that meets 0.5 per cent at every probe both fits the card and stays inside the bound, so the choice was the lane, not a looser target: the 0.5 per cent bound and the doubled-arm negative control are unchanged.

Negative control and gate

The under-resolved arm is the same grading doubled everywhere through the shell; it must miss 0.5 per cent at one probe or more. The uniform-default mutant steps the uniform branch by one part per million and must fail the bit-for-bit grid digest. Both mutants fail on their own assertion: under-resolved exit 1 with the 0.5 per cent assertion message, perturbed-uniform exit 1 on the digest comparison. Verdict control holds, recorded in control/negative_control.json and control/negative_control.log.

The gate runs the three files the brief names with the slow marker excluded, one arm per revision, each in its own exported tree with an index over the source checkout's object store.
