The solved iron as one passive frame

The seam this node was for is landed and pinned: the affine magnetisation is inserted into the CoilSet as one passive frame whose currents the material solve writes, and Solve.include_iron adds the frame's field and flux to every target set the solve builds, through the same reduction a kernel batch's matrix uses. On a block fixture the zero state reproduces the air-core field bit for bit, the solved state's added field and flux equal BoundCurrents.field and .psi within 1e-12 relative, and the solved state moves the field.

The WEST-scale measurement this node was also for is incomplete, and this document reports why rather than presenting numbers it could not finish: the CoilSet-stage run that draws the figure and the tables did not complete in this allocation. The field values it would draw are verified against the pinned receipt to 4e-9 relative. Three further findings - a plasma-coupling wall, a reload defect that is fixed, and the 40 mm factor this allocation could not build - come with it.

One seam, and why it is a frame

Cell magnetisation is a bound current distribution, and Nova already turns a current distribution into every operator it builds: a source column set. BoundCurrents in nova.biot.magnetisation holds the affine state and exposes field_matrix, psi_matrix, field and psi. The field of a uniform sheet on an edge atom uses the closed-form sheet_edge_field, whose closure is fixed under refinement; the flux uses ring quadrature through traced_filament_greens, because no closed-form flux tile exists for a sheet current. MagnetisationSolution.field delegates to the same call on the linear basis, so the frame and the material solve cannot drift apart.

nova.frame.iron.insert_iron inserts that set as one passive frame labelled iron: a frame row with part="iron" and segment="iron", and one subframe row per affine unknown carrying Ic. The physics of those rows is an in-memory source column, iron_source, holding the BoundCurrents, so the CoilSet stores 10 170 currents at 80 mm rather than 10 170 polygons. Solve.include_iron reads that column, builds the per-target matrix in blocks of IRON_TARGET_BLOCK, and adds it where a kernel batch's matrix is added; check_segments skips the iron segment, so no kernel is launched for it. The frame's Ic is a bound-current amplitude, not an ampere-turn, and set_iron_currents is its writer.

The external term from a persisted coil response

The coils' field at the iron's 127 872 volume quadrature points is written once per mesh and per coil set and reused across slices. A second run of the same stage reports coil_response_reused=true with a maximum relative difference of 0.0 against a fresh direct evaluation, i.e. the stored response round-trips bit for bit, inside the plan's 1e-12 bar. A request naming another mesh raises the persisted coil response serves another mesh; one naming another coil set raises coil response …npz serves another coil set. Splitting the external term into coils plus plasma changes only the summation order: against the prototype's single quadrature set the Frobenius relative difference is 5.481e-16, while the maximum pointwise relative difference is 1.276e-10, at quadrature points adjacent to a plasma cell where the kernel is largest.

What did not run, and why

1. The WEST-scale CoilSet point solve. A point solve assembles the plasma closed coupling for its whole target set on every call, and on this CPU that assembly costs about 3.3 s per target per attribute. At 157 targets one attribute took 515.7 s (the progress receipt reads 2/6 [17:11<34:22]), so a single solve is about fifty minutes and the three the measurement wants are hours; at 247 targets one attribute did not finish in 28 minutes. The planes and diagnostics were therefore computed from the same external term the material solve consumes (the prototype coil and plasma quadrature pinned by west_inputs, air drift 0.0) plus BoundCurrents.

2. The frame on a reloaded CoilSet. Inserting the frame into the coilset reloaded from its stored cache raised KeyError: 'polysection_policy': a reloaded frame keeps attributes in its metaframe's additional table without carrying their defaults, and the rows the insert appends are filled from that table. The first repair seeded the subframe's metaframe only, and the reload still raised at framelink._build_additional, because the failing link is built from the CoilSet's frame metaframe. Seeding both closes it: test_reloaded_coilset_accepts_the_iron_frame stores a coilset, loads it, inserts the frame, and gets the bitwise zero control and the 1e-12 frame contract (1 passed in 252.89 s).

3. The 40 mm state. The 39 792-square dense factor (a 12.67 GB operator) could not be built in this allocation; the solver process was killed twice before the Newton iteration started, at a resident size of 13.0 GB. The 80 mm state solved instead: 1 695 cells, 10 170 unknowns, five Newton corrections in 27.1 s, mu_r 218.29 to 1692.49.

The apparent factor of 28 was a comparison error, and it is closed

This node first read its own iron field (6.780, 4.233 and 1.727 mT at the coilset's three probe radii) against 0.2438, 0.1628 and 0.0723 mT, which is the persisted 40 mm receipt's field_T, and reported a factor of 27.9 as a defect. It is not one: those receipt entries are the total field, air + iron, not the iron's contribution. The receipts store field_T = total (see scripts/west_iron_linear/measure.py line 193, air = external(probes), then total = air + result.field(...)).

Checked against the pinned state directly: on the 80 mm state whose residual history reproduces the pinned solve step for step (0.9977745775, 0.0011981286, 7.865737e-05, …, 3.690e-09), the seam's field plus the drive reproduces the pinned field_T to 4.04e-9 relative, and the drive minus the iron field matches nothing (a 55.9 relative residual). The two vectors are almost equal and opposite at those probes - a 7.02 mT drive against a 6.78 mT iron contribution - so the total is the 0.244 mT the receipt records. The iron shields at these radii; that is the physics the §4 record states as 242.6 µT against a 7024 µT air-core.

Two other evaluators were run on the same persisted state before the comparison error was found. tiledassembly.linear_cell_interaction (the untouched reference implementation) returns 6.77986, 4.23324 and 1.72697 mT, ratio 1.000 to the seam's matrix; the centroid kernel _interaction(mesh, points, plan) @ magnetisation returns 6.7898, 4.2338 and 1.7269 mT. So the seam's field is the value the package already computed, to four digits.

Residual honesty: the with-iron separation curve and the probe and flux-loop tables are still not published, because the CoilSet-stage run that draws them did not complete in this allocation (the plasma closed-coupling wall below). The field values are now verified; the figure and the tables are the next run's work, not a correctness question.

The census count pinned independently

tests/test_imas_iron_read.py::test_census_mesh_matches_previous_read compared two counts that both came from the production _mesh, so a change in the reader moved both sides together unseen. It now pins the production count itself (CENSUS_CELLS = 1694 at 0.08 m) and carries a negative control: the same reader on the same six outlines at 0.04 m returns a different count, so the pin is a measurement of that mesh rather than a constant the assertion could satisfy without reading the outlines.
