Positive derivative construction

Let an interval have width w > 0, H increment wδ > 0, and positive endpoint slopes p and q. For intervals with p,q ≤ 2δ the bounded Hermite cubic is retained. At normalised coordinate t in (0,1), its derivative divided by δ is affine in p/δ and q/δ. At the four vertices of their closed [0,2] square it equals, respectively, 6t(1−t), 2(1−t), 2t, and 2−6t+6t². All are strictly positive in the interior, the last being at least 1/2. The actual endpoint derivatives p,q are strictly positive too.

If either endpoint exceeds 2δ, choose:

f = min(1/4, δ / (2(p + q)))
v = [δ − f(p + q)/2] / (1 − f)

The derivative ramps linearly from p to v over length fw, stays at v for (1−2f)w, then ramps linearly from v to q over fw. Because f(p+q)/2 ≤ δ/4, v ≥ 3δ/[4(1−f)] > 0. Every value of each ramp is a convex combination of positive endpoint derivatives, and the plateau is positive. Integration gives two quadratics and a line, stored in the existing four-coefficient polynomial representation. The integrated increment is exactly the prescribed one:

∫ H′(B) dB = w [f(p+q)/2 + (1−f)v] = wδ.

The joining values follow by integrating the first ramp from the left table knot and the last ramp backwards from the right table knot. Both inner derivatives are v, and adjacent authored intervals share their fitted endpoint slope. Thus H and H′ are continuous at both inserted and authored knots. The origin slope is set to 1/(μ₀μr(0)) exactly in the coefficients. The final slope is set to H(Bmax)/Bmax, matching the unchanged held end-secant permeability outside the table. The derivative is Lipschitz across the finite polynomial joins, as required for local quadratic Newton convergence.
