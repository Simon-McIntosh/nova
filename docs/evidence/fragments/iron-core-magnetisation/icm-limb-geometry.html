WEST iron magnetic circuit — dimensions of the six return limbs and the horizontal arms

For the limb-geometry decision on docs/plans/iron-core-magnetisation.html (§8). Date 2026-11-07. Scope read-only investigation; no repository file was edited.

Headline. The machine description does carry the iron circuit, but only as an axisymmetric body: six poloidal cross-sections with no toroidal extent. Every radial and vertical dimension the decision asks for is available from it exactly, as stated vertex coordinates. The outer limb is 0.1875 m radially by 4.8006 m tall with its centre at R = 4.890 m; the horizontal arms are 1.200 m thick at their inboard end and thin as t·R = 0.9169 m² towards the limb; the inner column is 0.6140 m radially by 1.7480 m tall at R 0.050–0.664 m.

What the machine description cannot contain is the toroidal dimension. The iron_core geometry is a two-dimensional (R, Z) outline, so a discrete limb's toroidal width and toroidal angle are absent by construction, not by omission. No accessible published source supplies them either. What the section does fix is their product: six limbs of radial depth D and toroidal width W carry the same iron as the smear only if D·W = 0.960 m², and likewise h·W = 0.960 m² for an arm of constant vertical thickness h. Those are the two numbers to confirm against a machine drawing; everything else in this report is measured.

1. What was found, and how firmly

The iron circuit is six segments forming a closed C in the poloidal plane: an inner vertical column, two junction blocks, an upper and a lower horizontal arm, and one outer vertical return limb. Their sections are drawn below from the shipped vertex arrays.

How each value is known

Every row carries one of three markers. stated means the source prints the number; derived means it is computed from stated numbers by a relation displayed in this report. The third category, read from a figure, is empty: no dimension here was scaled off a drawing. Anything absent is marked not found and no estimate stands in its place.

4. The upper and lower horizontal arms

Each arm is a wedge in the axisymmetric section: a flat underside and a sloped top. Its thickness is 1.200 m at the inboard end and falls as t(R) = 0.9169 m² / R, reaching 0.1839 m where it meets the outer limb. The two arms are exact mirrors in Z about the midplane.

Read the 0.800 m as an inference, not a measurement. It rests entirely on the premise that the taper's inboard terminus fixes the real arm thickness. That premise is well supported — the 1/R law holds down to R = 0.764, where the thickness is exactly 1.2000 m, and it is the only reading consistent with the plan's "constant vertical thickness" — but a single machine-drawing measurement of either the arm thickness or its toroidal width would settle it. The same applies to any toroidal width for the limbs: nothing in the description constrains D and W separately.

6. The relation that constrains what is missing

The smear is not arbitrary. Thickness multiplied by radius is constant around the circuit, which is exactly the statement that the toroidally averaged iron area per radian is preserved when N discrete arms replace a continuous ring:

t(R) · R = 0.9169 m²  ⇒  N · A_arm = 2π · 0.9169 = 5.761 m²  ⇒  A_arm = 0.9602 m² for N = 6.

Both cases check out against the section independently: the limb gives 0.1875 × 4.890 = 0.9169 m², and the arm gives 0.3056 × 3.0 = 0.9169 m². Six arms at 0.9602 m² reproduce 5.761 m², the 5.8 m² that decision limb-geometry requires, and the ≈0.96 m² per arm that plan §0 quotes.

So the decision has two unknowns and one equation in each of the two places it needs numbers:

  • limb: D · W = 0.960 m², with both D and W unavailable;
  • arm: h · W = 0.960 m², with h = 1.200 m strongly implied (§4) and W = 0.800 m following from it.

8. Disagreements between sources

  1. Inner extents in the in-repo research record. G records Rmin = 0.0000 m with three vertices at R = 0 for five of the six segments, and lists 57410 among the files it read. The shipped files A and B both read Rmin = 0.0500 m. Either the files were re-curated after G was written or G read a different variant. The disagreement does not reach the limb: G gives segment 3 as R 4.7965–4.9840, Z ±2.4003, exactly as A and B do.
  2. "Three outer segments" versus six. imas-efit's docs/iron-model.html describes WEST as a "transformer yoke — 3 outer segments derived from iron_core IDS", while the IDS holds six. The reconciliation is visible in C: the retired XML collapses the six into three ironBoundary elements (upper yoke, outer limb, lower yoke) by absorbing the junctions into the yokes. The iron is the same; the segment count is a modelling convention.
  3. Plan §0 rounds. It gives the limb as 0.188 m thick (data: 0.1875), the invariant as 0.92 m² (data: 0.9169) and the per-arm area as ≈0.96 m² (data: 0.9602 m²). These are consistent roundings, not disagreements, but the decision should record the measured values so a later reader does not re-derive from the rounded ones.
  4. The plan's R ≈ 4.89 m is confirmed, not contradicted. It is the limb's radial mid-plane, 4.89025 m. The plan correctly distinguishes it from the plasma major radius.
  5. The premise itself. The only real disagreement is the one the plan already names: the axisymmetric description of §0/§1 against a discrete six-limb circuit. That is a modelling gap, not a source conflict, and this report supplies the axisymmetric side of it exactly.

10. What would settle the open values

  1. A machine drawing or CAD extract of the iron yoke — the definitive source, and the only one that gives D, W and the angular datum directly.
  2. The Hertout et al. 2005 PROTEUS paper (DOI above), which models this machine's equilibrium including iron saturation and is the likeliest place for a stated section.
  3. The 1983 Tore Supra poloidal field system paper, for the as-built circuit.
  4. CEA/IRFM internal note via J. Morales, who is already the named contact for the B–H table's measurement status in F; the same channel can ask for the yoke drawing.

Until one of those lands, §8 of the plan can proceed with the poloidal geometry exactly as measured here, and must treat D, W and the arm width as a single free parameter fixed by D·W = h·W = 0.960 m².
