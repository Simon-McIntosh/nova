Bound-current route and existing kernel owners

Each cell carries three scalar basis functions per component, 1, (r − rc)/h, (z − zc)/h, with h = sqrt(cell area): six unknowns per cell. Dividing a stored gradient coefficient by h recovers the physical gradient in A/m². The returned magnetisation remains the value at the area centroid; linear_coefficients carries the complete scaled basis.

The field uses the bound-current route. Along an oriented cell edge, Kφ ds = MR dr + MZ dz is linear in the edge parameter. In the cell interior, Jφ = ∂MR/∂z − ∂MZ/∂r is constant. The existing nova/biot/tiledassembly.py owns both the energy assembly and field evaluation; no parallel assembly module is introduced.

The Gram matrix and the material law

The local Gram is Hᵢⱼ = ∫cell φᵢ φⱼ 2πr dr dz. It includes off-diagonal first moments: an area-centred radial basis is not volume-centred. The reciprocal current energy E gives the field-coefficient operator G = H⁻¹E. The extended galerkin_spectrum(..., basis="linear") solves the generalized problem (E/μ₀) v = λ H v, rather than treating G as Euclidean symmetric.

The prescribed field is projected with the same volume rule. During Newton, the total Galerkin field polynomial is reconstructed at the section_nodes volume quadrature points, the existing B–H law is evaluated there, and its magnetisation is projected back through H⁻¹. The nonlinear law therefore sees the spatially varying reconstructed field, including its gradients. Local quadrature tangents are contracted to 6 × 6 cell blocks before multiplication by the dense interaction, avoiding a quadrature-points-by-global-unknowns Jacobian.

solve_magnetisation(..., basis="linear") selects the candidate. The default remains basis="constant". The constant operator, uniform mesh construction and default arithmetic are retained.
