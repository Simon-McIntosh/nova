Single-transfer staging and float64 factor ownership

The linear branch now calls _stage_linear_interaction once and reuses that device array, rather than constructing the dense interaction twice in operand tuples. On the graded 16/80 mesh, the first transfer starts with zero live device bytes and ends with exactly 9 903 310 848 bytes, one interaction. Every one of its 1 237 913 856 entries agrees exactly with the persisted source. The Cholesky comparison also records one transfer, with zero entry error. The constant branch retains its operand arithmetic.

The new route uses the full local volume Gram matrix Q and material differential A. With D = Q A, R = sqrt(D), and W = inv(Q) R, the Newton system has symmetric form B = (1 + λ) I − R G W. The inverse formula p = (b + W B⁻¹ R G b)/(1 + λ) avoids inverting R, so zero differential directions remain representable. Row tiles build B into one column-major buffer, donated to Cholesky; corrections are refined in the original float64 differential system. No float32 factor was used.

A negative diagonal already rules out positive definiteness. The stored vector independently gives a negative quadratic form, irrespective of how accurately the iterative eigenvalue estimate has converged. Its transformed vector gives (uᵀ Q G u)/(μ₀ uᵀ Q u) = 1.02585274855, while the material differential remains below its bound. Thus the measured interaction does not satisfy the bound assumed by the Cholesky-only ruling. The action comparison and known-SPD controls distinguish this from an incorrect row contraction or a full-size Cholesky backend failure.

The counterexample is retained as cholesky-diagnostic.json and cholesky-rayleigh-witness.npy under the GPFS evidence root. Possible continuations are to preserve G and use shifted Cholesky only as a preconditioner for an exact unshifted iterative Newton solve, or to repair the interaction under a separate decision that permits its values to change. Neither continuation has been silently substituted for the specified solve.
