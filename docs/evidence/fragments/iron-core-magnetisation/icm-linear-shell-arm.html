The linear shell criterion can fail: 80 mm passes, 120 mm misses

On the μr = 1000 closed shell the piecewise-linear basis at 80 mm spacing (76 cells, 456 unknowns) reads 0.136630 % worst-probe error against the dipole reference and meets the 0.5 % criterion. The next coarser spacing, 120 mm (38 cells, 228 unknowns), reads 0.557274 % and misses it. Every coarser mesh measured below misses by more. Clause (3) of §5b is met at the second rung: one arm of the linear basis misses the criterion.

Geometry, source, probes and reference are the existing shell measurement: scripts/iron_linear_magnetisation/controls.py holds the 0.9–1.0 m polygonal half-section, the one-ampere 50 mm loop at the origin, the probes (2, 0), (0, 2) and (3, 1) m, and exact_shielding() = 61.101838 for the dipole reference. Only the uniform spacing changes between rows; no operator, default or tolerance was altered. Errors are |Smeasured/Sdipole − 1| at each probe.

The error rises monotonically with spacing: 0.1366, 0.5573, 1.1046, 3.5020, 22.190394 and 31.780779 %, so the ladder is measuring spacing rather than run-to-run variation. The recorded 40 mm point of this same script, 0.0625439 % at 1,368 unknowns, lies below the 80 mm point: the coarse arm is above the previously measured fine arm, as a refinement curve should be. The finite-loop column is the independently transmitted reference and tracks the dipole column, so the crossing is not an artefact of the reference choice.

The mesher is the limit on going coarser, not the criterion. One logged run over twelve spacings records the admitted cell count and the refusal at each: every spacing from 10 mm to 480 mm is admitted, 480 mm at 6 cells and 36 unknowns, with the meshed cell area summing to 0.2984508339013301 m² at every admitted size. The 640, 960 and 1000 mm spacings are refused with exactly:

unresolved iron sliver; refine the cell-size field or reduce cell_size

So 480 mm is the coarsest uniform spacing this mesher admits, the criterion is already failed at 120 mm, and no coarser body was invented. Lane: the assembly cost is size-independent, and CPU cannot serve the sweep

An identical sweep was first submitted to rigel (16 cores, --mem=110G, job 1324235, one hour). It completed no row: the 80 mm interaction assembly had not finished when the job hit TIMEOUT at 60 min (receipt coarse/linear-0.08.json records the mesh and completed: false). A bounded in-allocation probe of the 480 mm, 6-cell assembly also exceeded 300 s. The same six spacings then completed on one Titan Tesla P100-PCIE-16GB in 39 min 30 s (job 1324373).

Assembly time is 361.9–370.5 s at every spacing from 480 mm down to 80 mm, while the unknown count falls from 456 to 36; the cost is a size-independent floor, not the mesh density. That floor is what the CPU lane could not pay. The walls quoted above are P100 walls and are not H200 timing claims.
