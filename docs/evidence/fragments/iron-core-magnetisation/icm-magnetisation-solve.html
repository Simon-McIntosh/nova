Mesh, law and Newton update

nova/biot/magnetisation.py owns the mesh, the material interpolant and the solve. Its public entry point is solve_magnetisation; the result exposes magnetisation, permeability, residual and accepted-step histories, termination and the iron field at new targets. A clipped square grid merges pieces below 0.15 of a full cell area into the neighbour sharing the longest boundary. Unresolved slivers are refused. Clipping duplicates separated only by floating roundoff are removed before forming sheet edges.

The interaction matrix is built only through nova.biot.tiledassembly.cell_interaction, also used for exterior fields. A common dilation of source and target coordinates leaves B/M invariant: the ring field scales inversely with length and the sheet arclength scales with length. An exact power-of-two dilation of 1024 therefore preserves the physical operator while reducing the tile's 1 nm edge-identity grid to 0.977 pm in the authored geometry. The external-field callback receives metre coordinates.

The material fit interpolates H(B) with cubic Hermite polynomials whose slopes are strictly positive and limited by the adjoining secants. Thus dB/dH is positive. The endpoint secant relative permeabilities are held outside the table. The residual is F(M) = M − c(|B|)B, with B = Bext + GM and c = (1 − 1/μr)/μ0. Dense jax.jacfwd forms the differential Jacobian, LU supplies the Newton direction, and fifteen halving grades require the residual to fall. State, operands, LU factor and step scale are runtime arguments of compiled functions. The law is single-valued and anhysteretic; no remanence or minor-loop response is represented.
