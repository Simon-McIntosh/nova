A disconnected numerical grid prevents merging

Each square's upper boundary is computed as r + size or z + size, while the adjacent square's lower boundary comes from np.arange. These expressions differ at roundoff. For example A ends at R = 0.61 m while B starts at R = 0.6100000000000001 m. The exact geometry operations therefore find no shared edge, even when the cells are physically adjacent. This prevents the intended longest-shared-edge merge. No authored iron needs moving or deleting.

A scratch-function probe makes each square use two consecutive entries of a common grid-edge array, so adjacent cells use identical endpoints. It retains the original area and clearance guards, and its exact polygon unions preserve the iron:

Clearance rule resolved

The coordinator clarified that 0.0186 m was the sphere census's observed clearance, not a bound. The acceptance threshold stays 0.01 * size.
