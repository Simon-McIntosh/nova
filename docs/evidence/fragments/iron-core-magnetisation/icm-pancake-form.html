What this node established

Node icm-pancake-form transcribes Urankar Part II (IEEE Trans. Magn. MAG-18(3), 911–917, 1982) eqs (26) and (27) into nova/biot/sheet.py and makes them the b_2 = 0 (flat, "pancake") route for both the flux and the field. The panelised surface-current integral the section already shipped is kept unchanged behind closed=True, so the two routes remain independently evaluable and one is held against the other at every target, as well as each against scripts/edge_sheet/reference.py.

Over the section's twelve fixed targets the closed form meets the 1e-10 floor with a worst relative deviation of 2.84e-13 on the flux and 1.68e-12 on the field, and agrees with the retained quadrature to 1.68e-12. Both worst cases are at the closest standoff, half a cell from the sheet's edge, which is where the arsinh remainder is steepest.

The transcription

With the paper's geometry — target at (r, z), source point at (r', z'), gamma = z' - z — and its abbreviations

c^2 = gamma^2 + r^2,   a^2 = gamma^2 + (r + r')^2,   k^2 = 4 r r' / a^2

eq (26) for the flat annulus is

A_phi0^(2)(pi/2) = r g_0^(2)(pi/2)
    - (r' / (2 a r)) [ (r^2 - r'^2 - 3 gamma^2) K(k) + a^2 E(k) ]
    - (c gamma^2 / (2 a r)) sum_{p=1,2} (-1)^p ((r' +- c) / (r -+ c)) Pi(n_p^2, k),

n_p^2 = 2 r / (r -+ c),   upper sign for p = 1,  lower for p = 2,

and eq (27) for the field is

H_r = (gamma / (a r)) [ 2 r' K(k) - sum_p (-1)^p c ((r' +- c) / (r -+ c)) Pi(n_p^2, k) ]

H_z = 2 g_0^(2)(pi/2) - (2 r' / a) K(k)

The module's normalisation is psi = mu_0 r A and B = (mu_0 / 2 pi) H, with the antiderivative differenced between the segment's two ends. Both were confirmed against the reference rather than assumed: the flux ratio is 1.000000 without any further factor.

The one arsinh remainder

g_0^(2)(pi/2) is the arsinh integral the paper leaves unreduced (its eq 12b remainder), here evaluated as

g_0^(2)(pi/2) = (1/2) int_0^pi arsinh( (r' - r cos phi) / sqrt(gamma^2 + r^2 sin^2 phi) ) d phi

The paper writes the integral over alpha = phi/2 on [0, pi/2]; the half-range factor of two is what reconciles the two, and it is why the transcribed r g term carries no extra constant. This is the only quadrature left in the closed form, and it is not a substitute for the closed form: the K, E and Pi structure of eqs (26) and (27) is evaluated in closed form, and the remainder integral is the term the paper itself leaves open.

The target set places eight of its twelve points on the sheet's own plane, at each end station, where gamma = 0 exactly. Three simplifications follow and all three are enforced in the code rather than approached numerically: the Pi term of eq (26) is O(gamma^2) and contributes zero; H_r is zero (the axisymmetric sheet puts no radial field on its own mirror plane); and the remainder's integrand becomes log-singular at phi = 0, which is what the quadrature below is designed for.

The remainder's quadrature

A double-exponential (tanh-sinh) rule on [0, pi]: phi = (pi/2)(1 + tanh((pi/2) sinh t)), step 0.05, nodes at |t| <= 3.0. The reach is capped there because tanh rounds to exactly 1.0 beyond it, which sends the denominator sqrt(gamma^2 + r^2 sin^2 phi) to zero and the integrand to infinity; at |t| = 3.0 the nearest node sits 3e-14 rad inside the interval, and 181 nodes then place the clustering exactly where the logarithm is.

The printed form that had to be read twice

The second branch of eq (27) was first transcribed with the paper's A symbol in place of a fraktur g: H_z = 2 A_phi0^(2)(pi/2) - 2 r' K / a. It is wrong, and the measurement says so unambiguously. With that reading, at the middle target half a cell from the sheet, the vertical field came out 3.16 times the reference value, while H_r — which shares the K term and nothing else — was already exact. The vertical branch is 2 g_0^(2)(pi/2) - 2 r' K / a: the arsinh remainder appears itself, not the whole potential. The 300 dpi page render cropped to the eq (27) region and re-rendered at 200% resolved the glyph. No misprint is recorded here: the printed form is consistent once the fraktur symbol is read as g, and the discrepancy was in the reading.

The negative control

The declared control is flip the sheet current sign on one edge: the antiderivative is differenced between the ends, so adding the two ends instead of subtracting them is exactly one edge's contribution reversed. Applied to the implementation, both the pancake flux and the pancake field comparisons fail through pytest over all twelve targets; the failing test ids are in the run's negative_control.log. The head arm passes the same comparison, so the gate is measuring the sign and not a tolerance.

One property worth recording because it is easy to lose: a flat segment has equal z at both ends, so the constructor's higher-end-first reordering never fires, and the antiderivative is therefore differenced in the sorted source radius. The sheet current runs azimuthally, so the direction the endpoints were given in carries no sign; ordering by endpoint instead produced a flux of the wrong sign on reversed segments, which the section's existing order-invariance test caught.
