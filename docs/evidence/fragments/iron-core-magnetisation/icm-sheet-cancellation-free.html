Pancake radial identity

Write the target radius as r, source radius as s, source minus target height as g, c²=r²+g², and d²=c²+s². Let H denote the endpoint radial field before multiplication by μ₀/(2π). Combining the two pole fractions under the defining integral, rather than subtracting their evaluated complete integrals, gives

H = g ∫₀^π cos φ (c² − sr cos φ)
          / [(c² − r² cos² φ) √(d² − 2rs cos φ)] dφ.

The angular addition theorem for Legendre polynomials yields ∫₀^π cos φ Pₙ(sin θ cos φ) dφ = π Pₙ¹(0)Pₙ¹(cos θ)/(n(n+1)). Since Pₙ¹(x)=−√(1−x²) Cₙ₋₁³ᐟ²(x), differentiating the source-radius integral of the ring potential analytically gives the following two convergent endpoint series. Neither series evaluates the cancelling pole sum.

For v=s/c ≤ 1/2, t=g/c:
H = −π (r g/c²) v³ Σₖ≥₀ v²ᵏ C₂ₖ³ᐟ²(0)
                         [C₂ₖ₊₁³ᐟ²(t)/t] / [(2k+2)(2k+3)].

For v=c/s ≤ 1/2:
H = −π sign(g) r/(c+|g|)
    −π (r g/c²) Σₖ≥₁ v²ᵏ C₂ₖ³ᐟ²(0)
                         [C₂ₖ₋₁³ᐟ²(t)/t] / [(2k)(2k+1)].

The outer series integrates the remaining rings from s to infinity. Its constant is the exact infinite-radius endpoint, rationalised as −π sign(g)r/(c+|g|). The odd Gegenbauer polynomial is carried divided by its argument through an even/odd recurrence, so g=0 never introduces 0/0.

For the intermediate source range, the near-axis cancellation is handled by expanding the already-combined pole integrand in q=2rs/d² and h=r²/c². Set cₙ=(2n choose n)/4ⁿ and Iₖ(h)=∫₀^π cos²ᵏ⁺²φ/(1−h cos²φ)dφ. Pairing the odd and even binomial coefficients before summation gives

H = (g r s³)/(c² d³) [−I₀(h)
    + Σₖ≥₁ c₂ₖ q²ᵏ⁻² (4r²/d⁴)
      ((2k/(2k+1))c² − s²) Iₖ(h)],
Iₖ(h) = Σⱼ≥₀ hʲ π (2j+2k+2 choose j+k+1)/4ʲ⁺ᵏ⁺¹.

The first coefficient is −s² before s³ is factored out. This is the term lost by subtracting the complete pole integrals. The near-axis series is used at h≤1/4. Outside these three regions the geometric ratios are bounded away from the cancellation limits and the direct complete-integral identity is retained. Each pancake series uses 32 terms per index.

Sloped gamma derivative identity

Let b=dr′/dz′, a=√(1+b²), and S=a b R+A, the bracket whose flux is μ₀r S/a². Along the source line the antiderivative satisfies (∂g+b∂s)S=a³s∫₀^π cos φ/D dφ. Differentiating this identity with respect to g commutes with its source-line derivative. Set g₀=g−s/b, c₀=√(r²+g₀²), t=−g₀/c₀, u=sign(b)/a, and v=sa/(|b|c₀). On the exterior branch |b g₀|>r the zero-source gamma derivative is zero. Integrating the differentiated addition theorem in source radius then gives

∂g S = π (a³/b) (r/c₀) (s/c₀)³
       Σₙ≥₁ vⁿ⁻¹ Cₙ³ᐟ²(t) Cₙ₋₁³ᐟ²(u) / [(n+1)(n+2)].

This is the derivative of the same closed antiderivative, with its cancelling root and arsinh contributions combined analytically. The factor s³ is explicit. The source integral is performed symbolically on each power; no numerical source or angular quadrature is added. The series is used at v≤1/2 on that exterior branch, and the regular complete-integral composition remains elsewhere. Eighty terms bound the geometric tail below double-precision rounding. The recurrence is (n+1)Cₙ₊₁³ᐟ²(x)=(2n+3)xCₙ³ᐟ²(x)−(n+2)Cₙ₋₁³ᐟ²(x).

The exterior qualification is essential. The printed primitive has an additional branch contribution when |b g₀|<r; applying the zero-source identity there changes the answer. Independent high-precision checks caught this in the standalone derivation, before implementation.
