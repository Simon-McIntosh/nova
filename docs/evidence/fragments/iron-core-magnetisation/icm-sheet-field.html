§2 — The conic sheet field, measured on the section's target set

Node icm-sheet-field adds the magnetic field B_R, B_Z of a conic toroidal sheet beside the flux that nova/biot/sheet.py already carries, sharing its elliptic setup. The field is the exact gradient of the same closed-form antiderivative, so the two components cost one extra quadrature pass each on the rule the flux already builds, and no parallel module was written. Both components hold the section's 1e-10 floor at all twelve fixed targets and all five slopes, and tests/test_edge_sheet.py asserts it: no target is excused, no tolerance was loosened.

Measured: worst relative error per slope

Geometry is the section's own: segments of length 0.1 m centred at r = 1.5 m, a cell width of 0.02 m, and twelve targets at half a cell, one cell, one segment and ten segments off the middle and off each end of the segment. The reference is the ring quadrature in scripts/edge_sheet/reference.py, panel-bisected to 1e-13. The worst relative error over both components at each slope is:

The cylinder branch against Part II eq (25)

The vertical branch (b_1 = 0) is held against Snow's closed form as an independent anchor rather than only against the ring reference. Part II eq (25), per end and per unit sheet current, is

B_r = a/(2r) [(1 + k'^2) K(k) - 2 E(k)]
B_z = gamma/a [K(k) + (r' - r)/(r' + r) Pi(n_3^2, k)]

with a^2 = (r + r')^2 + gamma^2, k^2 = 4 r r'/a^2 and n_3^2 = 4 r r'/(r + r')^2. The field is the difference of the two ends scaled by mu_0/(2 pi). It reproduces both components of the shipped field to 1e-10 at every one of the twelve targets, which is the test the anchor carries.

Part II eq (27), the thin pancake, was not transcribed. Its second branch is written in terms of the vector potential A_phi0^(2)(pi/2) of eq (20b), which lies outside the cropped page, and a trial transcription of the first branch against the reference missed by a factor of 9e4 at the middle station, so it was discarded rather than fitted. The flat branch is therefore held to the ring reference at 1e-10 and not to a closed form of its own. Part II itself states that eqs (25) and (27) agree with the standard forms derived from Biot-Savart, and the ring reference is that derivation carried out numerically.

Negative control

The declared mutation is the sheet current's sign. With sheet_current = -1 the field must miss every comparison, and test_flipped_sheet_current_fails_the_field_comparison asserts exactly that: at each of the five slopes the flipped field departs from the ring reference by more than the relative floor, and at pi/2 it departs from eq (25) as well. Both assertions run in the same gate as the positive ones, so a kernel that silently ignored the current's sign would fail there rather than pass everywhere.

What the derivation carries

Three terms decide whether the measure is reachable, and each was found by a measurement rather than by reading the algebra:

  • The segment line's own radius enters the height derivative. The antiderivative puts r_1 = r' - b_1 gamma, so at a fixed end r_1 depends on the target height and d(r_1)/dgamma = -b_1 multiplies the arsinh without the flux's field weight. Using the weighted integral there instead left the height component uniformly 0.33 to 0.6 relative out on all four sloped branches while the radial one was exact, which is the signature of a missing term rather than a scaling error.
  • The standoff is formed from positive terms. W = r_1 - r cos(phi) and the pole numerator both vanish with the standoff at phi = 0. Written as the difference of two O(r) values they lose every digit that the graded azimuthal rule buys, and three of the twelve targets stopped improving at 1e-9 no matter how many panels were added, even though the rule itself was converged to twelve digits.
  • A target on the segment's line stands off by rounding alone. The start and end stations lie on the segment's own extended line, so r_1 - r is 2e-16 rather than zero, and the graded nodes reach phi = 3e-17, below that standoff. The pole kernel there reads 1e16 and its weighted contribution is wrong in the second digit. Snapping a standoff within 1e-12 r to zero took the 22.5-degree branch from 8.1e-01 to 4.2e-12.

Each of the three was reproduced by an independent finite difference of the landed flux before it was changed, so the closed form is anchored to the flux it differentiates and not only to the ring reference.
