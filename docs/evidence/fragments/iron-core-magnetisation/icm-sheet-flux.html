§2 — The conic sheet flux, measured on the section's target set

Node icm-sheet-flux gives the poloidal flux of a conic toroidal sheet in nova/biot/sheet.py. Every inclination of the section's slope set is implemented, and the flat and vertical forms hold the section's 1e-10 floor at every one of the twelve fixed targets. The sloped form does not: at the three near standoffs its fixed panel rule falls short, reaching 9.2e-05 at half a cell width. The node is therefore delivered blocked on that measure, with the errors below, so the coordinator can choose between a singularity-subtracted azimuthal rule and reconstructing the paper's closed coefficients. Every fixed-set target asserts the 1e-10 floor; the 21 measured misses (seven per sloped slope) are recorded through pytest.xfail with their measured error in the reason, so the gate reports them as known shortfalls and a case that comes back changes state visibly. The rule was not adapted per target and no tolerance was loosened.

The section's graded arsinh rule was tried as the remedy and did not settle it. Reusing its grading width, two-panel sinh map and 128-node rule reproduces a brute-force quadrature of the arsinh integral to 3e-12 at an isolated endpoint, and takes the middle standoff at half a cell width from 9.2e-05 to 1.8e-10, but the start and end stations -- where the target sits on the segment's extended line and the denominator vanishes at an end -- read 1.5e-04 to 2.5e-04 at every standoff, worse than the plain rule's 2e-13 there. Those stations need the rule's endpoint-log removal, which was not ported, so the plain rule is what shipped.

The shortfall is the fixed rule's, not the reference's: the reference converged at every target (converged=True throughout), and the error is nearly identical at the three stations for a given standoff, which is the signature of a quadrature limit on the azimuthal integrand rather than of a geometry-dependent defect. At d = 0.1 m only the middle station misses (2.7e-10, 2.0e-10, 1.5e-10); the start and end stations hold 5.8e-11 to 8.8e-11. The azimuthal integrand is near-singular as the segment's foot approaches the target, and a uniform 8-panel by 16-node Gauss-Legendre rule cannot resolve it there.

The antiderivative that is used

Part II's expressions are antiderivatives in the source coordinate, not definite values: the finite segment enters as the difference between the two ends. Established by measurement — in the cylinder limit the eq (24) expression at one end alone misses the reference by 75 per cent, and the difference of the two ends reproduces it to 3e-16.

For a sloped segment, parameterised r' = b_1 z' + c_1, with gamma = z' - z, r_1 = c_1 + b_1 z and a_1^2 = 1 + b_1^2,

a_1^3 f(phi) = a_1 b_1 D + (r_1 + b_1^2 r cos phi) arsinh(beta_1)

with D = sqrt(gamma^2 + r^2 + r'^2 - 2 r r' cos phi), beta_1 = Gamma_1 / B_1, Gamma_1 = a_1^2 gamma + b_1 (r_1 - r cos phi) and B_1^2 = r_1^2 - 2 r r_1 cos phi + r^2 (1 + b_1^2 sin^2 phi), which reproduces the paper's eq (8) and reduces to eq (24) at b_1 = 0. The flux is psi = mu0 a_1 r [f(end_2) - f(end_1)], with the azimuthal integral on the fixed panelised Gauss-Legendre rule. The flat annulus evaluates the surface-current integral of the ring flux (nova.biot.greens.greens_psi) on the same rule.

Negative control

Flipping the sign of the closed form's sheet current fails 65 of the 71 tests (EXIT=1): every reference comparison, the flat pancake form and the flipped-current control. The six that still pass are the linearity and segment-order identities, which a global sign flip leaves true. The head arm passes all 71.
