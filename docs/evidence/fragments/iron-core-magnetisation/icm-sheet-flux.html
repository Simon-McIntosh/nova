§2 — The cylindrical limit lands; the conic form does not

Node icm-sheet-flux was to give the flux of a conic toroidal sheet by Urankar Part II eq (22a). The straight-cylinder limit, eq (24), is implemented in nova/biot/sheet.py and reproduces the section's 1e-13 ring-integral reference (scripts/edge_sheet/reference.py) with a worst relative error of 5.9e-15 over the five targets measured. The sloped case, eq (22a), is not implemented: SheetFlux raises NotImplementedError for a segment that is not parallel to the axis, so no caller can be handed a wrong number. The flat pancake, eq (26), raises for the same reason.

What the paper's expressions are, established by measurement

Part II's final expressions are antiderivatives in the source coordinate, not definite values. The finite segment enters as the difference between the two ends. This was not assumed: the eq (24) expression evaluated at one end alone misses the reference by 75 per cent, and the difference of the two ends reproduces it to 3e-16 relative. The same comparison fixes the normalisation, psi = mu0 r [F(gamma2) - F(gamma1)] with F the eq (24) right-hand side, and the third-kind convention, complete_pole(1 - n3^2, k'^2) with n3^2 = 4 r r' / (r' + r)^2.

The antiderivative itself was re-derived independently and checked against the paper's eq (8): with the line parameterised r' = b1 z' + c1 the segment integral is

a1^3 f(phi) = a1 b1 D + (r1 + b1^2 r cos phi) arsinh(beta1)

where r1 = c1 + b1 z (the segment's supporting line at the target height, the same at both ends), beta1 = Gamma1 / B1, Gamma1 = a1^2 gamma + b1 (r1 - r cos phi) and the squared denominator is B1^2 = r1^2 - 2 r r1 cos phi + r^2 (1 + b1^2 sin^2 phi). That B1^2 is derived, not transcribed: the printed page's second term reads as Gamma1^2, which is negative for the geometry measured here and produced nan on the first attempt.

With that antiderivative in place the flux as a one-dimensional azimuthal integral, psi = (mu0 a1 r / 2) integral cos(phi) [F(end2) - F(end1)] dphi, agrees with the ring reference at every slope tried — 7.5e-15 at slope 3 pi/8, and the same at pi/8 and pi/2. So the geometry, the normalisation and the endpoint rule are settled; what is missing is the closed-form reduction of that azimuthal integral.

Why eq (22a) is not implemented

Eqs (22a) and (24) to (27) are commercially printed scans. The coefficient block that feeds eq (22a) — eqs (13a) and (18a): S(k), M(k), N(k), P_phi(n_p) and the denominator of P_phi — could not be resolved to the print's own precision from the scan, and the assembled expression does not reproduce the azimuthal quadrature: at slope 3 pi/8 the elliptic combination comes out at -3.68 where the quadrature requires +0.226, a gap far too large to be a rounding difference. Every plausible reading of the (2r) factor and of the P_phi denominator was tried and none closes it. Shipping a formula whose error is unknown is worse than shipping a narrow one that is exact, so the cylinder limit is what landed.
