WEST 62751 on the Galerkin operator: two meshes converge from M = 0, and the +2 and +4 m probes carry a 13 to 17 per cent mesh-pair error bar

Both meshes converge through the production material path from a demagnetised state at full drive, in four and five Newton corrections with every accepted step exact. The far field at the plan's probes is then read off each mesh, and the pair separation is the discretisation error bar the lead asked for: 0.006, 26.3 and 16.6 µT at +1, +2 and +4 m, which is 0.0026, 13.1 and 17.2 per cent of the coarse value. The +1 m probe is mesh-independent to five significant figures; the +2 and +4 m probes are not, and that is the substantive result of this section rather than a shortfall of it.

Inputs and controls

The iron is read through StaticMachineDescription.read_iron_core at the DD pin its curation manifest declares (DD 4.1.0, six segments, one CEA B-H table, 25 rows), which is the same route tests/test_imas_iron_read.py exercises. The external field is rebuilt from the saved coil quadrature and the 1128 prescribed plasma currents. Two independent controls confirm that the source was reconstructed rather than replaced. The rebuilt field at the three probes reproduces the saved air field with a maximum relative difference of exactly 0 in both components, and the poloidal line of B magnitudes matches the recorded profile to a maximum relative 0.001090, below the 0.002 the earlier measurement asserted. The B-H table matches the recorded table bit for bit. A difference between the meshes therefore cannot come from a changed source. The measurement was also run twice, and both receipts name the commit whose measure.py blob they executed. The first attempt ran the tree at a7dac4ab5 carrying an uncommitted measure.py whose blob 4b4c5d74 was committed afterwards as 8e5baf8c8; the run reported here stamped 8e5baf8c8 and executed the error-bar fix whose blob ab1b1446 was committed afterwards as 211a595ca. The two blobs differ only in the error-bar assembly, which runs after every solve, so the solve path they exercised is identical code. Every physical quantity below, from probe field through converged permeability to the residual history, reproduced bit for bit between the two runs; only the wall clock moved.

The CEA table against a flat μr = 215

Most WEST iron sits on the steep low-field branch whose measurement status imas-efit records as unconfirmed, so the same solve is repeated with the table replaced by a flat μr = 215, which is the table's origin value. The flat solve converges in one correction on both meshes because the law is then linear, and at the coarser mesh its field reads 891.322255202, 594.225845090 and 254.107588583 µT at +1, +2 and +4 m.

The prototype recorded a far-field change of 14 per cent at one probe and 8 per cent at another, as a fraction of the air-core field. The Galerkin mesh pair measures 9.44, 9.12 and 8.89 per cent at the finer mesh, inside that recorded range, and the pair moves the figure by at most 0.21 percentage points. So the size of this modelling difference is a property of the constitutive choice, not of the discretisation: refining the mesh changes the probe field by up to 17 per cent while changing the table-versus-flat change by two tenths of a point. The flat-215 iron leaves the probes at 12.9 to 13.3 per cent of the air-core field; the table iron leaves them at 3.45 to 4.43 per cent.

Converged permeability distribution

Every cell exceeds μr = 100 on both meshes: the volume fraction above 100 is 1.0 exactly, because the table's origin permeability is 215. The fraction of iron volume above μr = 1000 is 6.564 per cent at 0.08 m and 6.652 per cent at 0.04 m, so the high-permeability core of the frame is a small and mesh-stable part of the iron, concentrated where the figure shows it at 0.04 m.

The law is anhysteretic

The constitutive law is the monotone H(B) fit with the one per cent saturation-tail cap. It is single-valued: the model carries no remanent magnetisation, so a drive that returns to zero returns to zero magnetisation, and no minor-loop permeability is represented after the central-solenoid swing. Both would change the shielding and neither is in these numbers. Hysteresis is out of scope by the lead's ruling of 2026-11-05.

Beside the former collocated roots A and B

The two former collocated roots were a branch disagreement, not a discretisation one: at +1 m they read 213.24 and 278.66 µT, 30 per cent apart. The Galerkin pair places the answer at 242.65 µT there, between them and stable to 0.003 per cent across the two meshes, which is the branch ambiguity resolved rather than inherited.

What the pair does and does not establish

The pair bounds the coarse mesh's error, and the 0.04 m mesh is the finest converged one available: a 0.02 m rung would be about 27000 cells, 53000 unknowns and a 22 GB dense operator against the 16 GB of the P100 this ran on, so refining further is out of reach on this device rather than merely untried. A third converged Galerkin solve exists at 3447 cells from the interaction study, and it reads 183.518310217 µT at +2 m and 85.629727291 µT at +4 m, each between the pair's own values, 200.369378227 and 174.104623884 µT at +2 m and 96.325592194 and 79.769382215 µT at +4 m, so both probes walk monotonically with refinement. It is context rather than a strict third rung: that study projected a piecewise-constant external field onto the saved cells instead of rebuilding the source quadrature, and its +1 m value, 241.429883919 µT, sits 0.5 per cent below both meshes measured here, which is the size of that difference. Two converged meshes on one source cannot establish an order of convergence, so the 13 and 17 per cent drop at +2 and +4 m is a bound on the coarse mesh's error rather than a measured rate, and the fine-mesh values at those probes are stated as carrying a residual error of a few per cent rather than as converged. The +1 m value, the change from the table to a flat permeability, and the permeability distribution are robust across the pair.
