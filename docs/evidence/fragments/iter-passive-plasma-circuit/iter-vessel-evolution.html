Evolving the ITER vessel currents through the DINA scenarios

landing: the sectioned passive set is driven by DINA's coil currents and the prescribed plasma, integrated with the exact zero-order-hold eigenmode propagation of nova.circuit.propagate, and measured against DINA's own pf_passive currents, the whole coupled-flux coil voltages and the VS3 psi-map residual. Aim is to predict ITER, not to match DINA: agreement with a second code on the same machine description is cross-code consistency, not hardware validation.

The ITER windings are superconducting, so their winding resistance is zero and each coil voltage is dPsi/dt alone; DINA's stored zero coil resistance is the physical value, not a gap in the data. The passive flux at VS3 is compared against exactly the source term the psi-map check subtracted, restricted to that kernel's passive columns, so a flux-model difference cannot hide inside the coil contribution.

Two shares, and they are not the same number. 537 of 1312 sectioned circuits, 41% of the circuits, fall inside a DINA loop cross-section. Those circuits carry 58% of the evolved current magnitude; the remaining 42% of the current sits in circuits DINA's pf_passive does not model and is unmatched by construction. DINA's passive cover totals 3.4 m2 in r[3.24, 9.72] z[-5.66, 5.68] while the sectioned set spans r[0.2, 14.4] z[-13.1, 16.0].

Tolerance is stated before comparison as peak residual ≤ max(10 kA, 25% of that loop's peak DINA current), reported per loop because the loops range over three decades in current. The 6 mm interval reproduces the 12 mm result: the same 90-91 loops, the same worst loop, and the same voltage and VS3 numbers to the reported digits, so the passive current is resolved on the 12 mm mesh.

On 105014/1 the passive coupling removes 64 V of the 857 V rms the bare coils miss, and 72-77 V of it lands on PF2-PF5 (PF2 168 → 93 V, PF5 242 → 178 V). The plasma term adds back 10 V of rms: driven by the evolved passive currents the plasma contributes its own dPsi/dt. On 105052/1 the same arms read 783, 593 and 591 V (PF2 388 → 177 V, PF4 780 → 301 V).

Cross-code consistency, not hardware validation. The 93-235 V rms agreement on PF2-PF5 is nova agreeing with DINA on a shared machine description; a shared geometry or a shared passive-structure assumption would leave both codes with a small residual and neither correct. The measured basis for the coupling uncertainty is JT-60SA's 5-8% on the mutual-to-self inductance ratio M/L, and that is what the 93-235 V is read against.

The CS modules, PF1 and PF6 are a different story. Their residual is 0.95-2.4 kV rms after the full coupling (CS1 2432 V, CS2U 985 V, CS2L 945 V, CS3U 1075 V, CS3L 1078 V, PF1 1045 V, PF6 1168 V) against 93-235 V on PF2-PF5 and 20 V on VS3. It is not a coupling error: DINA records converter-side voltage, pinned at the converter limit at initiation, while those coils discharge through their switching-network resistors, which sit apart from the converters (lead, 15 October 2026) — so nova's coupled-flux voltage and DINA's converter-side record measure different circuits there.

The anti-series sign of the VSU/VSL winding was taken from the machine description and tested; the residual does not resolve it (105014/1 separates the signs by 0.11 V rms, and 105052/1's minimum falls on the opposite sign despite a 2.5 V separation), so the sign is inherited rather than measured.

The VS3 psi-map residual does not close

An earlier check left a psi-map residual at the two VS3 sections of 0.88 and 1.08 Wb on 105014/1, and up to 2.52 Wb on 105052/1. Substituting the evolved passive flux for DINA's stored passive flux does not close it; it grows. On 105014/1 the evolved passive flux peaks at 8.40 and 10.08 Wb against DINA's 7.37 and 8.46 Wb, a difference of 2.0 and 3.5 Wb, larger than the residual itself. The residual is therefore not a passive-current modelling gap: DINA's own stored passive currents already reproduce the psi-map at VS3 to about 1 Wb. On 105052/1 the substitution moves 2.52 Wb to 8.47 Wb at the first section and 2.35 Wb to 16.32 Wb at the second. Removing the plasma drive leaves the substitution essentially unchanged (2.37 and 3.66 Wb move to 3.22 and 6.19 Wb), because the passive flux at VS3 is coil-driven.

Caveat: the receipt compares the peak of the residual against the peak of the flux difference, and those peaks fall at different times, so a common-time-base restatement is owed before these are read as per-instant closures.

Controls

The positive control is a single passive ring under a coil-current ramp, integrated with the same zero-order-hold scheme and compared with the analytic L/R solution; the propagated current recovers the analytic peak to a relative error of 1.4e-13, so the recurrence is exact for a piecewise-linear drive and the ring's decay time is set by its resistance, not its inductance.

Two negative controls, each logged. The first mutates the ring's resistance handling in a scratch copy, where that analytic decay test fails while it passes on the head arm. The second mutates the propagation itself — scaling the interval 2% inside integrate_eddy_ode, in a scratch copy outside the repository — and shows both halves of the guard: the propagation test reddens (analytic agreement moves from 1.4e-13 to 6.0e-3), and the comparison of two decay times that a test once computed for itself still passes under the same mutation, which is why it was replaced.
