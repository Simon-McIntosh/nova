The reconstruction arm's gap is one boundary polygon, not the coil cadence, and it lands on small cancelling modes

landing: the arm that misses its predeclared tolerance was localised by measurement rather than argued. Four presentations of one 105014/1 slice were driven through the full sectioned machine in one job, each differing from the generic presentation in one thing, and the difference belongs to a single input: whether the case's stored separatrix outline is offered to the boundary resolver at all. The coil-clock cadence the reconstruction-style presentation also applies moves nothing — its prescription is byte-identical to the generic one. And the 13 array elements that cross the predeclared relative bound are not conductors next to the plasma: they are small-amplitude passive modes of the plant's projection, 1.1 m to 13.4 m outside the boundary, whose relative sensitivity is a cancellation effect rather than proximity.

A correction to the earlier reading of the failing arm. No repair is available inside this node's fence, and the decision is the lead's. Nothing measured here is broken: the boundary resolver prefers a stored separatrix when one is offered and the reconstruction presentation offers none, which is what makes it a reconstruction; the coil clock does not enter the prescription at all; and the plant is a faithful function of its inputs, its section array agreeing to 0.0 Wb with a computation that shares no cache. The reference for the reconstruction arm cannot be moved on measurement alone: every non-DINA candidate stores the opposite flux orientation

landing: the lead redefined the reconstruction arm's third check, taking DINA out as its reference because DINA's flux functions and flux map disagree at the edge, and requiring instead an ITER equilibrium from a code whose flux functions agree with its flux map, chosen by measuring that agreement. Eleven non-DINA ITER source runs were surveyed and measured in one all_debug job, each opened through imas.DBEntry at the DD version its own IDS declares, with homogeneous_time checked. One candidate measures better than DINA on the absolute agreement test, and it cannot drive the arm: it stores the opposite flux orientation, which PlasmaSlice.prescribe refuses before any plant is built. Every non-DINA candidate the survey reached is in that same orientation. The arm's reference is restored to DINA, and the choice goes back to the lead with the table below.

Two findings decide the outcome, and one metric had to be thrown away. The column ∫jmap / Ip — the current the stored map implies through the Grad-Shafranov operator, against the stored plasma current — reads 225 to 300 in every case, DINA included. A factor that uniform across four unrelated codes is a unit or convention artefact of the operator used here, not a property that separates one code from another, so it is recorded and used for nothing. The lead's ruling of 2026-10-21 places it: nova reads DD3 data as COCOS 11 and DD4 data as COCOS 17 and uses COCOS 17 internally everywhere, so DD3 sources read through a COCOS 17 operator carry exactly this kind of constant factor. The consequence for this plan is that the edge-agreement criterion is not yet validly measured until DD3 data enters through a translator. What does separate the cases, and what is validly measured, is ∫jprof / Ip: the stored flux functions, read with the case's own 2π convention and integrated over the case's own resolved plasma polygon, against the stored plasma current. DINA comes to 0.959, CORSICA to 0.977–1.000, spiderInverse to 1.000, JINTRAC to 1.09–1.17.

The band residual the brief specifies as a fraction of the core value is the one metric that ranks DINA best, and its denominator is why. The stored profiles carry almost no edge current in any case: the band's mean profile density is 0.11 % of the core density for DINA, 1–3 % for CORSICA, and 32–75 % for spiderInverse and JINTRAC. Dividing a small band residual by a core value therefore measures how far each profile rolls off at its own edge rather than whether that case's map agrees with its functions, and the two rankings it produces are opposite, so it cannot choose the reference.

Why CORSICA 130506/6 was chosen, and why it cannot be used. On the absolute measure CORSICA 130506/6 is the best candidate: 0.999 of the plasma current, a stored last closed surface agreeing with its declared boundary flux to 1.94e-05, clean resolution through separatrix_x_point, and it carries equilibrium.h5, pf_active.h5 and wall.h5 — everything the plant needs. 130506/4 is the same equilibrium and carries no wall.h5, and neither is in the account's own imasdb, so both are reached through the shared tree's path= route.

Pointed at it, the arm fails somewhere new: PlasmaSlice.prescribe refuses “plasma current sign and psi ordering disagree with COCOS” (nova/imas/plasma_source.py:78), because the case's psi_boundary - psi_axis is -75.8 Wb where its plasma current is -15 MA, and the guard requires that product to be negative. That guard is not a defect: it is the orientation contract the plant path depends on, and every non-DINA case the survey reached is on the far side of it — CORSICA 130506/3–6, 103 and 305 (spans -75.8 to -76.7 Wb), spiderInverse 131000/60 and 121000/50 (-47.8 and -38.1), JINTRAC 134000/41 and 50 (-38.8 and -71.1) — against DINA's +16.3 Wb, which is why this arm has only ever run on DINA. Every one of those sources is DD3 data conforming to its own DD version's convention, so none of them is invalid: what the refusal exposes is that the guard encodes one fixed orientation rather than the source's DD-version convention, and the translator that fixes that is new work in a separate plan, not in this node. The job that ran the retargeted arm is scratch/reference_test.log: 1 failed in 9.73 s, on the refusal quoted above, with the other five files of the module untouched.

Outcome, as the lead ruled. The table above is the reference-selection evidence: CORSICA 130506/6 measured best on the one valid column (0.999 of the plasma current against DINA's 0.959), every non-DINA source is DD3 data in its own convention, and the map-current column is an operator artefact, so the edge-agreement criterion is not yet validly measured. The arm is restored to its DINA reference in 0c13b5c79, which reverts d2acc5b5d, and it is marked pytest.mark.xfail(strict=True) with the reason that its reference waits on the DD3 to COCOS 17 translator and that DINA's flux functions and maps disagree at the same edge. Strict is the operative part: the arm still has to fail on its own assertion, so it cannot be quietened by a precondition, and an unexpected pass reports as a failure. The translator and the reference case it enables are new work in a separate plan, not in this node.
