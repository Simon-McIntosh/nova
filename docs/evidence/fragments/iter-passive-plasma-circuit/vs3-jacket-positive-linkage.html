Quadrature is not the cause. Integrating the coil self-block at the same hybrid rule as the coil-to-jacket mutuals (removing the separate finite-source rule) moves the smallest eigenvalue by 1.5×10−13 H on the coil self terms and the largest generalised eigenvalue by 2×10−9, against the measured 2.2×10−5 excess. The separate finite-source rule contributes essentially nothing to the over-screening.

The corrected-geometry arm is validated. Inflating the jacket square to its outer diameter makes S more negative (−1.94×10−7 against −1.51×10−8), so "a bigger square outside" is not the fix. The repair that works is the IDS annulus itself, and the realisation matters. One circuit holding an outer disc at +1 and a core disc at −1, both at the section centre, is invalid: with a net-zero circuit current the observer weighting cancels and the jacket block computes as exactly zero, which would remove the negatives by removing the jacket's coupling rather than by relocating it. Splitting that pair into two circuits makes the 16×16 jacket block singular (smallest eigenvalue −1.38×10−8) because the two discs are nearly coincident. Discs cannot carry an annulus here; sectors can.

Each conductor and each jacket is therefore its machine-description annulus cut into N equal-sector quadrangle circuits with an equal area share, linked through the same polygon-section kernel (polygon_greens(n_panels=8, n_nodes=24)), and reduced to the two coil terminals and the eight jacket terminals. The reduction weight is 1/√N per sector, not 1/N: the returned linkage entry folds the source share once, and the physical port reduction applies the share to source and observer alike.

What ran is the geometry, not the discretisation. An earlier reduction weighted each sector by 1/N and reported L halving with every doubling of N. That was a reduction-normalisation error in the arm, not a property of the annulus: the correct weight makes the summed arm converge on the closed-form ring self-inductance, which is what the table above shows.

Both other knobs are inert on this block: section_n_max from 6 to 12 to 24 changes nothing to every printed digit (the sub-division is already capped by delta), and raising the polygon source rule from the linkage's 8-panel, 24-node call to the kernel's own 16×48 default moves the mode away from zero by 4 per cent (−5.672×10−7 → −5.894×10−7 H at 12 mm), converged there at 32×96. So the vessel negatives are an under-resolved observer quadrature on the thin vessel sections, not a geometry defect — and they are removable by refining the observer grid, which is the rule the repair node can apply to the whole joint matrix.

One caveat on how to read that table. The reduced fixture holds only the support rows, so median_section_scale over its rows is not the full build's and its matrix differs from the cached one by up to 1.6×10−6 H — comparable to the eigenvalues being tracked. What the measurement supports is the trend in the observer grid and the localisation of the mode, not the absolute values in the reduction; the cached block's own eigenvalue is −2.5279×10−7 H at 12 mm, and the 43-row restriction reproduces it to four significant figures, which is what licenses attributing the mode to those rows.
