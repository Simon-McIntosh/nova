The term-order scan. The production point program is cell-invariant: lowering production_point_batch on CPU gives 8,408,375 StableHLO bytes at 8 cells, 7,482,936 at 16, 6,461,067 at 64 and 6,088,070 at 132. A one-hexagon program is 5,440,521 bytes and eight hexagons 5,442,157 (+0.03%). The cell axis therefore carries one body already, and a fixed-capacity scan over that axis replaces one body with one body plus loop machinery. The inverse experiment shows the same thing from the other side: a mutation that unrolls the term order into one moment body per term lowers to 27,479,507 bytes at 8 cells, 5.05 times the shared form, so the 5.05 factor is what the shared form already avoids rather than what remains to be won.

The cheaper regular Hessian at corner heights. The near-corner stabilisation in polygonanalytic._packed_moments_height_tangent — two jax.jvp directions plus four shifted evaluations of the full 9-row moment kernel — owns 28.8% of the lowered graph: with that jvp replaced by the ordinary first-order direction in a fresh process the program lowers to 5,990,537 bytes against 8,408,375, and the 1e-5 shift constant disappears from the module. A 28.8% ceiling cannot carry a 217.9 s cold compile to 60 s, and dropping or retiming the block changes near-corner derivative entries, which the identity ruling forbids.

Where the equations are. attribute_graph over the point program at 8 cells reports 248,141 expanded equations, led by rangefunction._harmonic_pair_step at 15.97%, polygonanalytic._packed_analytic_moments_primal at 9.74%, rangefunction.evaluate at 5.21%, _product_sum_step at 4.93% and elliptic._pole_forward_step at 3.83%, with 21.9% unattributed fused internals. That is the packed recurrence graph the section's earlier nodes already ranked; none of it is a per-term body that a scan could collapse.
