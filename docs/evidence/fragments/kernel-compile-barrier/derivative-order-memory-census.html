The HLO count is the number of instruction definitions in lowered.compiler_ir("hlo").as_hlo_module().to_string(), before native optimization. StableHLO byte counts include debug metadata. The instrument counts instructions whose own metadata retains that scope, and distinct scope names among them. Outlined callees can carry their own metadata; the attributed-op count is not a transitive census of all descendants. Repeated calls to one outlined body and loop trips do not become distinct scope names. The Hessian has four traced primal calls but only three retained scope names in the saved HLO. An unused primal output can disappear during lowering. Thus the scope count describes retained traced bodies, not six Richardson samples or all inlined instances after XLA optimization.

Attribution and the smallest candidate change

Source inspection of nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py:3486–:3591 shows two stable tangent directions in a scan(unroll=2), an independent unchanged value expression, and six shifted stable-pole primal evaluations in one mapped height-sample body. The six shifts are ±1e-3, ±5e-4 and ±2.5e-4 m (±1, ±0.5 and ±0.25 mm). Richardson coefficients are 64, −20 and 1, divided by 45. The samples are calculated for all source-target entries; jnp.where chooses their result only for packed field channels at index 3 and above when the nearest corner height is within 1e-7 m (0.1 µm). This is selection after evaluation, not conditional sample execution.

The pole-product rule at nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py:1107–:1123 computes jax.jvp of the joint expression in full, then selects a finite slope at exactly zero root with jnp.where. The stable-pole tangent expression calls this rule through _regular_pole_contraction at :1161. Nested forward mode therefore differentiates both ordinary expressions and sample expressions before the selected field tangent is assembled. This is a mechanism to quantify, not proof that the finite-slope branch or Richardson branch owns a particular memory share.

Measured attribution: with fixed 24-cell source arrays and a single target, requesting a Hessian grows the lowered instruction count from 19,624 to 121,842 and causes compilation to exceed the 120G host allocation. A large wall batch is consequently unnecessary for this host-memory failure. The saved point program cannot allocate the full read's 1.07 TiB claim among derivative nesting, target batches and XLA optimization: no full-read comparison, batch-size sweep, completed Hessian executable or Hessian rule ablation exists. No percentage of that demand is attributed to either cause. The approximately 51.7% instruction-metadata share above cannot substitute for such a percentage.

Smallest candidate product change, named and not made: put the Richardson height-sample evaluation behind a scalar near-corner condition for each source-target entry, preserving the ordinary tangent for generic entries. The probe's masked rule sketches this using serial lax.map plus lax.cond. It could remove unnecessary execution and sample buffer demand away from corners, but conditional branches still exist in a compiled module, so neither compile-memory reduction nor unchanged higher derivatives follows without the missing measurements. Removing the Richardson rule altogether has no demonstrated precision justification. There is no evidence sufficient to name a change as removing the largest share yet.
