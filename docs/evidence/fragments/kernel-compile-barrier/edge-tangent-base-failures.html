The bound is not device-qualified, and on the H200 it sits below the reference derivative. A device-qualified bound would mean one device meets it and the other misses it in a fixed direction. The logs show both directions on the same test: [finite_part] fails on the CPU at 3.60 and passes on the H200 at 2.33, while [complete_pole] passes on the CPU at 2.13 and fails on the H200 at 9.89, and [cn_pole_moment] passes at 2.12 on the CPU and fails at 10.37 on the H200. The same program therefore moves by 4.6 and 4.9 times between the two devices, so no single bound expresses both. The same table also shows that on the H200 the bound is not reachable by a correct derivative at all. The reference JVP arm computes the same derivative a different way, and its own ratio to the primal is 8.99 for [complete_pole], 9.76 for [cn_pole_moment], 8.82 for [sn_pole_moment] and 3.01 for [harmonic_root_moments] — at or above three on all four failing terms. A bound of three on tangent-over-primal therefore asks the tangent to compile in a third of what the reference derivative costs on those terms.

The mechanism is the primal's own compile cost, not the tangent's. On the H200 the primal compiles in 0.111 to 0.272 s while both derivative arms take 2.2 to 2.6 s; on the CPU the same complete-pole primal takes 0.877 s and its two derivative arms 1.87 s and 1.85 s. The GPU makes the small primal 3.6 times cheaper while both derivative arms move the other way, the tangent from 1.87 s to 2.41 s, so the ratio moves from 2.13 to 9.89 on an unchanged program. The ratio is therefore measuring how small the primal is against fixed program overhead, and only secondarily how expensive the tangent is.

What the repair should do, by part. Two findings survive that reading. The instrument: for these terms the discriminating comparison is tangent against JVP rather than tangent against primal, because the JVP is the same derivative computed another way and is the only arm whose cost a correct tangent can be held to. Replacing the ratio's denominator, or recording a per-term bound derived from the JVP, is a test change and it is the lead's call. The product excess, whose size depends on the device: on the H200 the tangent is 9 to 10 percent above the JVP on the three pole terms — 2.412 s against 2.194 s, 2.535 against 2.318, 2.552 against 2.338 — while on the CPU the same tangents sit within 1 percent of the JVP (1.868 s against 1.853 s and 1.898 against 1.857, the sn row unmeasured). The tangent also carries 1035 equations against the JVP's 736, 1315 against 753, and 1336 against 765, which is where a per-device compile cost for the same equation set turns into that percentage. The tangents that carry it are _complete_pole_tangent (nova/biot/completeelliptic.py:617), _cn_pole_moment_tangent (nova/biot/elliptic.py:1561) and _sn_pole_moment_tangent (nova/biot/elliptic.py:1588): form each tangent through the intermediate products the JVP shares, so its equation count tracks the JVP's instead of exceeding it, then re-measure the four rows. _harmonic_root_moments_tangent (nova/biot/elliptic.py:1531) shows no such excess — 597 equations against the JVP's 593 and an identical 0.334 s — so its failure is the bound alone and no tangent change is indicated for it.

Classification and recommended repair

Class A — the 300 s default timeout, not a value (nine of the fourteen edge failures; eleven timeout observations on the CPU arm, since the two edge_terms rows time out there as well and carry a different cause on the GPU). Five test_tangent_compiles_within_bound rows, three test_truncated_tangent_fails_identity rows and three test_tangent_matches_base_jvp rows fail with a bare Failed: Timeout (>300.0s) from pytest-timeout: no assertion executed and no measured value was produced. The log carries XLA's own alarm for one of them, The operation took 7m52.731798174s, so a single CPU compile of the base JVP rule exceeds the whole per-test budget by 1.6 times. pyproject.toml sets timeout = 300, and the sibling module in the same suite already overrides it for the identical row: tests/biot/test_elliptic_tangent_recurrences.py:466 puts @pytest.mark.timeout(1800) on test_tangent_compiles_within_three_primals. The sibling module states the reason in its own comment: A compile row spawns 21 fresh processes; the repository's 300 s hang guard is for ordinary tests and cuts this one off before it asserts, so it gets its own bound. The edge module's compile row (test_edge_term_tangents.py:319-321) and its three SLOW_IDENTITY_ROWS carry no override, so they inherit 300 s while each runs 21 fresh cache-disabled compiles (seven repeats of three arms) or one 472 s compile. This is a test defect on both devices, not a CPU-slowness artefact: run at the same 300 s ceiling on the H200, test_tangent_compiles_within_bound[edge_terms] took 300.10 s and was cut off by the same Failed: Timeout (>300.0s) from pytest-timeout. The bound the row asserts is not implicated: with the lane's own default budget all fourteen ratios were measured at or below 3 on the H200 on 2026-10-08 (docs/evidence/fragments/converged-forward-solve/cfs-edge-term-tangents.html). Recommended repair: add @pytest.mark.timeout(1800) to test_tangent_compiles_within_bound and to the three slow identity rows, matching the sibling module; the gate is then the ratio, and a CPU arm that cannot finish is reported as cut short rather than as an assertion failure.

Class B — an exact bound asserted on a device that does not reproduce it (test_tangent_matches_base_jvp[second_residual]). The row measures 3.370e-12 against the 1e-13 EXACT_TOLERANCE, with 0.9996 of the 10 000 samples inside it: four samples of one domain. The same row is exactly zero on the H200 — 0.000e+00, covered fraction 1.0000, in the 2026-10-08 H200 record cited above — and the same fragment records this row's CPU behaviour as 1.720e-9 maximum with 603 samples over 1e-13. The bound is therefore attainable on one device and not on the other, and the CPU figure moves between runs because it is round-off. This is a device-qualified bound. Because the plan's decision of 2026-10-06 forbids relaxing the identity tolerance without the lead, the recommended repair is not a lowered number: run the exact rows where the exact bound holds (the H200 lane) and record the CPU arm's measured maximum beside them as a device-qualified figure, leaving the 1e-13 assertion intact on the device that reproduces it. Changing the CPU bound needs the lead's ruling.

Class C — a 1e-03 crossing that only the CPU arm sees (test_tangent_matches_base_jvp[flux_line_moments]). The row measures 1.021e-03 against 1e-09, with 232 of 10 000 samples over, and is exactly zero — 1.0000 covered — on the H200 at the same revision in the same hour. The device comparison was run to test the hypothesis that a 1e-03 excess is a product defect, and it rejected it: the same code agrees to the byte on the GPU. Nine orders of magnitude is nevertheless not round-off, so what the two arms measure is a discontinuity the backends resolve on opposite sides of, not a tolerance that a bound could admit. The samples that cross it are not identified here, which is the first measurement the next node owes. Between the 2026-10-08 record and this measurement the flux-moment primal was rewritten three times with no tangent line changed: b54a1af9b (2026-10-11, "accumulate flux moments in local source coordinates", 291 lines of nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py), c805c781a ("form only the elliptic combination used by flux") and 44345de22 (2026-10-12, "resolve angular peaks in polygon flux moments", the near-target graded quadrature in _flux_area_rule and _flux_area_integral). Each of the three touches zero lines of _EdgeScalar, _EdgeReductionTangent or any *_tangent function (git show <commit> -- nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py | grep -cE '_tangent|_EdgeScalar|_edge_' returns 0 for all three). The tangent under test is _edge_flux_line_moments_tangent at nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py:3769; it consumes the construction fields of the current _Edge/_Vertex while the identity compares it with jax.jvp of base revision 716d859f, so a construction or quadrature change alone breaks the row, but the H200 column shows it does not break it there, so the three rewrites are a candidate rather than the established cause. The classification is a device-qualified bound: the 1e-09 assertion is met on the same revision on the H200 and not on the CPU, where 2.3 percent of the domain crosses. Recommended repair, in order: attribute the excess by sample index first — the domain's near-endpoint and near-interior blocks are the places a branch would flip — and only then decide. If the crossing block is a numerical path that one backend evaluates differently, the repair belongs in _edge_flux_line_moments_tangent (nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py:3769) or in the construction it reads, not in the bound; if the blocks are the ones the near-edge graded quadrature covers, this row and Class D describe the same gap from two sides. Do not lower SCAN_TOLERANCE on the strength of this row: 1e-03 is not a tolerance.

Class D — a stale strict expected failure (test_near_edge_tangent_meets_reference). The test is marked @pytest.mark.xfail(strict=True) at test_edge_term_tangents.py:449 with the reason "the differentiated graded edge quadrature near endpoints leaves the exact integral's tangent (measured maximum 5.160e-7 relative)". At main it passes its own assert hand.max() <= SCAN_TOLERANCE, so strict xfail reports XPASS(strict) and the file is red on a guard that is satisfied. The 5.160e-7 in the reason is the 2026-10-08 figure from the fragment cited above, and the near-target angular quadrature repair 44345de22 (2026-10-12) is the change that would close exactly that gap. This is a test defect: the marker outlived its measurement. Recommended repair: delete the xfail marker so the row asserts the 1e-9 agreement it now meets, and carry the measurement history in the commit body. Do not relax SCAN_TOLERANCE.

Class E — a primal bit-identity difference seen only on the GPU so far (test_tangent_matches_base_jvp[edge_terms] and its truncation control). On the H200 these two rows do not reach their tolerance assertion at all. Both fail in _identity at tests/biot/test_edge_term_tangents.py:257, the assert left.tobytes() == right.tobytes() that requires the tangent's primal half to be byte-identical to the base revision's: AssertionError: edge_terms: primal bits differ, At index 52024 diff: b'\x1a' != b'\x04, one element of 52 024 differing in its last byte, in 5.67 s. Because the primal check precedes the identity check, the truncation control in test_truncated_tangent_fails_identity[edge_terms] fails at that same assertion too, so the declared mutation is never exercised for this row. The two values compared are the current _edge_terms_tangent's primal half (nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py:3912, whose tuple(a + b for a, b in zip(value, corner)) sums _edge_integrands_tangent's edge.terms(vertex) with _arsinh_terms_tangent's corner term) and the base revision's own _Edge.terms(vertex) + _Vertex.arsinh_terms(). The classification is product defect suspected; device reassociation not excluded until the CPU arm reaches the assertion. The CPU arm never arrives: on the CPU the same row is cut by the 300 s ceiling (Class A above), so this record holds no CPU measurement of the byte comparison, and two readings stay open that these logs do not separate — the tangent's primal half sums its two terms by a different association than the base program, or the GPU's rearrangement of the same sum moves one element's last bit. A last-bit difference at one element of 52 024 is not round-off scaling with magnitude, which argues against a tolerance question in either reading. Recommended repair: make _edge_terms_tangent form its primal half through the base program's own expression and association so the bytes agree, then re-run the two rows. If the last bit turns out to be attributable to the device rather than to the expression, the bit-identity contract needs the lead's ruling rather than a relaxed assertion, because the plan's decision fixes the primal as bit-identical.
