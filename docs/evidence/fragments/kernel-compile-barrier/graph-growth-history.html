Two commits tripled the point jet's lowered graph

The published per-pass profile of point_jet at size 4 recorded 3,043,945 bytes of StableHLO at revision 56097c60b (2026-10-27). At 7bd1d912c (2026-11-13) the same program in the same harness records 10,315,092 bytes, a factor of 3.39 on the graph. To find where that happened, one job on rigel (1313738) exported every commit on main that touches the point jet's source between the two revisions, traced and lowered both programs against each tree in a fresh process with the compilation cache disabled, and recorded the graph sizes. Tracing and lowering cost about fifteen seconds per program, so the whole history was measured without a single compile.

Two control rows hold the instrument. point_jet at 56097c60b lowers to 3,043,948 bytes against the published 3,043,945, and at 7bd1d912c to 10,315,095 against the published 10,315,092; both agree to three bytes on a graph of millions, so the CPU lowering measures the same graph the H200 profile compiled.

The growth is not gradual and it is not one step. Over 45 commits the lowered graph is flat except at two of them, and those two account for all of it: da28fc4e5 (2026-10-29) multiplies it by ×2.524, and cb42f656f (2026-11-06) by a further ×1.345, together ×3.39, which is where the point jet's 3,043,948 bytes become 10,315,095. The production point batch at size 132 follows the same two commits by ×2.137 and ×1.291, ×2.75 in total, so the lever is shared between the two programs rather than specific to the point jet's size. Two later commits move the graph by less than one per cent — fcd86472c adds 0.2% and 0f01394c4 removes 0.33% — and are not steps by the ten per cent threshold this record uses.

da28fc4e5 — Merge moment-field-hessian: a regular Hessian of the polygon moment field at corner heights

StableHLO grows ×2.524 for the point jet and ×2.137 for the production point batch. Its diff changes these functions in modules the point jet imports and evaluates:

  • packed_analytic_moments — the module gains the regular-Hessian path the point jet reaches through BiotMomentCoupling.value_gradient: the nested evaluate and difference closures that take the corner-height second derivative of the moment field, 115 added lines
  • packed_analytic_greens — one changed line on the neighbouring kernel entry point

cb42f656f — fix(biot): repair the polygon analytic tangents near edges and vertices

StableHLO grows ×1.345 for the point jet and ×1.291 for the production point batch. Its diff changes these functions in modules the point jet imports and evaluates:

  • _Vertex.against_root, _Vertex.root — take a stable keyword that routes the near-corner terms through the weighted residual
  • _Vertex._first_residual, _Vertex._second_residual — take a weight argument, the graded quadrature the weighted residual integrates over
  • _Edge.second — carries stable into the edge second-residual path
  • _vertex_against_root_tangent, _edge_flux_line_moments_tangent, _horizontal_flux_line_moments_tangent, _second_residual_tangent, _arsinh_terms_tangent — the tangent counterparts move onto the same weighted form, which is what the point jet's jacfwd differentiates through
  • _EdgeReductionTangent — its reduction carries the weighted terms

Both commits are correctness work in nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py: the first gives the analytic polygon moment field a regular Hessian at corner heights, where a Richardson-combined height tangent was being differentiated a second time, and the second takes the near-corner terms through a stable weighted residual so the full-domain tangent identities hold. Neither is a regression to revert — the first commit's own body records the cold compile rising from 94.6 s to 305.6 s and the lead accepting that as a cost paid once per machine shape. What this record adds is the attribution: the compile barrier is the shape of the analytic second-derivative path, not any single arithmetic term, and the two commits that define it are the place to look for a cheaper formulation.
