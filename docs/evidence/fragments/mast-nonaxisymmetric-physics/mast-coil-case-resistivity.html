MAST PF coil-case resistivity source check

The claim-by-claim receipt records the 0.90 µΩ·m provisional bulk stainless ceiling from mast_seed_parameters.py. Original MAST's five non-solenoid PF pairs sit in stainless steel cans, but the paper names no alloy grade (Edlington et al., p. 421).

A reference austenitic steel measures 0.811 µΩ·m at 300 K, 0.890 at 400 K and 0.923 at 450 K; interpolation crosses the seed ceiling near 415 K (142 °C). This is a reference specimen, not identified MAST stock (NBS SRM 798, Table 4). MAST reported 200 °C designed and 140 °C achieved bake-out temperatures, neither a measured case temperature during the fitted shots (Akers et al., Table 1).

Explanation for the fit reaching the ceilingVerdict from independent sources
Higher-resistivity gradeUnsourced: the MAST can description says stainless steel without a grade (Edlington et al., p. 421).
Hotter steel during the fitted shotsUnsourced: the material threshold is physically possible for the reference alloy, but the published MAST temperature is a bake-out value (NBS Table 4; Akers Table 1).
Toroidal insulating break or segmentation of the caseUnsourced: published insulating breaks belong to diagnostic supports; separately measured case currents establish conduction but do not resolve the case closure path (Edlington et al., p. 422; Berkery et al., §3).
Unmodelled parallel vessel pathSupported as topology: the cans connect electrically through supports and feeder bellows to the vessel; a 3-D model explicitly couples vessel and cases. Its bias in this fit is unmeasured (Edlington et al., p. 421; Berkery et al., §3).

No independent source establishes a greater-than-0.90 µΩ·m bulk case value for the held-out shots or a quantified segmentation multiplier. The seed stays unchanged; vessel-coupled current paths are a sourced model-extension candidate, not a fitted parameter (Edlington et al.; Berkery et al.; NBS Table 4).
