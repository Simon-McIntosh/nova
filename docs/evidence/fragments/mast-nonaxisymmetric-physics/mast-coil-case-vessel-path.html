Discrete vessel connections leave the axisymmetric ring current unchanged

The connection hypothesis cannot explain an n = 0 resistivity saturation in the axisymmetric passive model and needs a 3-D current model to test. This conclusion applies to toroidally closed, uniform rings with axisymmetric linked-flux derivatives. No passive parameter was fitted or promoted, no product module changed, and no cohort pass was run.

The discrete network solves Kirchhoff current conservation and Ohm's law on two rings with total resistances 2 and 5 Ω and loop EMFs 3 and −4 V. Their isolated currents are 1.5 and −0.8 A. Each ring segment's resistance and uniform EMF scale with its angular fraction. Connections have positive resistances 0.05, 1 and 20 Ω; these are synthetic test values, not sourced contact resistances for MAST. One gauge potential is fixed; all other potentials and branch currents are solved, including single-connection self-loops and two-connection parallel arcs.

For angular fractions fs, segment KVL reads R fs Is = Es − ΔVs. Summing around a closed ring cancels every potential difference, so its n = 0 current is Σ fsIs = Eloop/R. Summing current conservation over a ring gives Σ Ibridge = 0, the zeroth Fourier coefficient of the localized transfer currents. Uniform angular EMF permits constant potentials on both rings, so every bridge current vanishes even with unequal spacing. Unequal spacing alone does not generate transfer. A zero-integral angular EMF supplies the nonzero-transfer control.

The digest-verified geometry checkpoint cited by the spectrum receipt contains 24 modeled case rings: eight each for the upper and lower P3 cans, and one each for the upper and lower P2, P4, P5 and P6 cans. Those are poloidal-section discretization counts, not physical connection counts per can. Edlington, pp. 421 and 423 documents can-to-vessel paths through supports and feeder-bar bellows, and detectors around six support legs of the P2 and P3 coils. It does not give a complete connection count and angular map for every can. Berkery, section 3 models the conducting structure in 3-D before deriving effective segment resistances.

Toroidal variation in material, conductor cross-section, driving fields or inductive coupling can mix harmonics. This resistor-network result does not exclude such a real 3-D effect, and does not identify a bridge resistance: every tested bridge resistance gives the same n = 0 currents.
