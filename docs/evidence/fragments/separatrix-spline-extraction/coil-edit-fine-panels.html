Coil-edit panels drawn from a fine display map

The coil-edit panel drew its contours from the lattice raster alone, so a 20 mm lattice showed as a 20 mm staircase and no reading finer than the solve grid was available. The panel now draws the flux a stored state's own currents produce, evaluated at 10 mm. The coupling is built in the benchmark from the machine the state was solved on: the external term is the stored circuit fields contracted with the panel's prescribed currents, and the plasma term is the uniform, radial and vertical moment triple over the operator's own cells, taken from the operator's normalised-current route at the solve's target current. That route is asked for its moment triple directly, because the builder's own normalised branch traces a clipped support that an operator carrying point moments and no moment geometry cannot provide; the render receipts name the observation entry point of the same route. nova/equilibrium/display_flux.py is unchanged.

The map is pinned against the operator that produced the state. A re-solved state's panel map is compared with operator.raster_image at the lattice nodes and must reproduce it to 1e-10 relative, and it asserts the drawn map's own reproduction_relative_error rather than a value it recomputed. On the real MAST states the two lanes record 6.78e-12 and 7.20e-12, so the coupling frames agree with the operator's to eleven digits rather than merely passing the floor. That check pins the coupling's arithmetic and says nothing about the solve: raster_image is not the map the solve iterates, so eleven digits of agreement with it is not agreement with the terminal state — the term the display leaves out is measured below.

Each of the four figure sets was re-solved on the lane its receipt names, twenty 2% warm edits from a cold solve, one job per set. Every job reached edit 20 with the compilation cache warm after the first edit, and every job's terminal marker is EXIT_STATUS=2, which is the benchmark's own non-converged verdict rather than a crash. The terminal pair for each set is edit 12 (fraction +0.06, residual 5.73e-09, converged) and edit 9 (fraction 0.00, residual 6.61e-03, active set settled).

The converged set moved between the revision the sweep was first run at and the one the panels are drawn at. The display builder reproduces the wrong reference. The map the solve iterates is the trip boundary of the constrained reduced solve, nova/equilibrium/reduced_newton.py:926: it reads the moments at the trial flux (_current_moments, reduced_newton.py:353, normalised when the solve carries a target current), images them with external + operator.current_moment_image(moments) plus the registered rows' compensating flux (_image, reduced_newton.py:572), and then passes the promoted nodes through unchanged (mapped = jnp.where(promoted, state, image), reduced_newton.py:976), where promoted is the residual-shadow mask at that state against the frozen one. The raster the display checks against, ForwardFluxOperator.raster_image (nova/equilibrium/forward_operator.py:2938), is only external + grid.internal(moments): it carries neither the constrained row's compensation nor the promotion, so the display can reproduce it to eleven digits while sitting away from the state the solve terminated on. The operator does expose the right map, as ForwardFluxOperator.__call__ (forward_operator.py:3946) and flux_map (forward_operator.py:3986): external plus the plasma image, then the residual-shadow pass-through.

Measured at the two terminal edits of the mast-centroid archive, the terms are these, all at the 33 by 33 lattice nodes. The archive's field is not the state's own flux: it stands 2.786e-02 Wb from the terminal state on the converged edit, 2.1% of the 1.3155 Wb field peak, and 3.505e-02 Wb on the non-converged one. That gap is the residual-shadow promotion — 46 nodes on the converged edit, 25 on the non-converged one — where the trip boundary passes the state through instead of imaging it. The archive is the operator's raster of the terminal state's normalised moments plus the constrained row's compensating flux: subtracting external + grid.internal(moments) from it leaves 2.405e-03 Wb (converged) and 3.438e-02 Wb (non-converged one) — the compensating current the vertical-centroid row spent, which the sweep folds into the prescribed vector before it images and which the archive does not record separately. The operator's stored external current contributes 0.0 Wb on both panels, since its thirteen conductors are all zero and the display passes a zero current, so no conductor term is missing. Across the two moment routes the term is 3.894e-02 Wb (converged) and 0.608e-02 Wb (non-converged), and the three sum to F - raster(unnormalised moments) to 1.1e-16, so nothing is left over. On this state the trip boundary maps it to 1.60e-02 Wb from itself while the normalised and unnormalised raster routes sit 2.79e-02 and 3.89e-02 Wb from it: the two missing terms are structural, not a scalar, so no value of the normalisation amplitude closes either gap.

The normalisation is a real term, but a small one: 1.6e-3 relative, third in size behind the promotion on the converged panel and behind both the promotion and the compensation on the non-converged one. A reading that took the normalisation reciprocal for the whole gap is consistent with the numbers only while the comparison is made against the raster; against the state, which is the quantity the solve terminates on, it accounts for about 6% of the converged difference.

What the display would need, therefore, is the trip boundary rather than the raster: evaluate the operator's own map at the terminal state — ForwardFluxOperator.__call__ or flux_map, which carry the plasma image and the residual-shadow pass-through — and add the constrained rows' flux_delta. That in turn needs two things the archive does not carry. It stores the 33 by 33 raster and the prescribed current vector, not the terminal state vector (1126 entries at this operator: the lattice plus its wall and direct-sample rows), and it does not store the compensating current the vertical-centroid row spent, which the solve folds into the prescribed vector before it images. A second, independent defect sits in the panel path itself: it recomputes the moments from the archived raster (benchmarks/coil_edit_latency.py:1466) and never reads the archive's own cell_current_i column, which is the true state's already-normalised moment triple and the only quantity in the archive taken from the solve rather than from an image of it.
