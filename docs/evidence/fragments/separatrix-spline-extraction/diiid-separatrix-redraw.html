§8 — The predicted separatrix on the DIII-D frames

The forward bank's three polished, divertor-class frames now draw the separatrix assembled from their own display field, and each one reports its symmetric distance to the EFIT boundary. The fourth frame is marked unavailable rather than drawn from a substitute. Every frame is solved at its own terminal current and its own polished saddle value; nothing here re-solves a frame, and no other frame's field is borrowed.

The display grid is the display map's own

The conductor response is accumulated over the points the display map itself carries, taken from DisplayFluxMap.radius and .height, so the assembler and the panel read one grid. The producer no longer keeps a local grid: the operand store now holds a 55980-point response (response.npy, c8075ff7…) built once and reused by every frame, which is what lets a frame's separatrix be reconstructed from its persisted operands without touching the solver.

Frame 146's saddle converges and its field admits no closed branch (closed_candidate_count 0), so it is drawn without a separatrix and captioned as such; frame 144 has no persisted display map and is marked unavailable in the panel.

No frame reports a converged forward solve. That is the bank's own terminal state, not a redraw artefact: the separatrix is assembled at the polished saddle of each frame's terminal field, and the caption carries the convergence flag beside the boundary so the reader sees which state the contour came from.

Branch capacity

The assembler sizes its branch slots from the grid it is given (1 << (2 * (radius.size + height.size) - 1).bit_length()). At the legacy 256-slot capacity every frame empties: the candidate branches overflow before a closed cycle is formed, which is why the panels carried no separatrix at all. The negative control pins the capacity back to 256 through a sitecustomize on PYTHONPATH, leaving the worktree untouched, and test_a_persisted_frame_assembles_its_separatrix_from_its_own_field then fails at assert drawn — no frame assembles a separatrix from its own field — in 436.5 s with EXIT=1 (negative-control-256.log).

Gates

tests/test_diiid_forward_gs_match.py and tests/test_frame_operand_artifacts.py are run at the base revision and at the node head, on the CPU lane with JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu, one process each, whole files. The base arm is 18 passed (EXIT=0, 28 s). The head arm adds the persisted-frame test described below. The negative control, its test id and its exit status are in negative-control-256.log; the gate logs are base-gate.log and head-gate.log in the run directory.

test_a_persisted_frame_assembles_its_separatrix_from_its_own_field loads one banked frame's persisted operands and conductor response, reassembles its separatrix at the persisted axis, and requires a finite rms and sup distance to that frame's labelled LCFS. It reads only the labelled LCFS columns, so it costs seconds rather than a solve.
