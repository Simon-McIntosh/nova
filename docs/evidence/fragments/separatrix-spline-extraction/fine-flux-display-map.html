Fine-grid display flux map

A display flux map is the flux a state's own currents produce, evaluated wherever a panel wants to read it. The builder takes the coupling the producer already owns as a pair of callables over target points -- the conductor and prescribed-response rows on one side, the plasma cell uniform, radial and vertical moment columns on the other -- contracts them against the state's own cell-current moments, and adds no kernel of its own. The lattice spline stays the solve's authority; the map reports its own polished saddle beside the lattice spline's, and the largest node mismatch to the state.

The build cost is the producer's coupling cost, not the builder's: each chunk calls the caller's Green's columns once per plasma cell, so a 62k-target map over a MAST wall bounding box scales with the number of cells and the chunk count. The builder holds no per-point state beyond the map itself, so peak memory is one chunk of coupling plus the map array.

Negative control

One conductor current in the fine-map build is scaled by 0.99 and the reproduction gate is re-run: the map at the lattice nodes then misses raster_image by a relative 5.45e-4 and the test fails. The guard measures the map, not the suite.

What was not produced

The MAST CPU centroid state's X-point region figure was not drawn inside this node's budget: it needs the prescribed-circuit response matrix rebuilt at the fine targets (938 sections against every display point), and that build was not run. The saddle and mismatch numbers above are measured on the manufactured operator. Drawing the MAST panel from this builder belongs to the panel sections that follow, which already own the producer path and its response cache.
