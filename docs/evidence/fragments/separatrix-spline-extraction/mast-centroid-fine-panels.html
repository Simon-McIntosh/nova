MAST centroid panels on the fine display map

The three committed centroid states are re-solved with the producer and drawn from the display map rather than from the 33 by 33 and 65 by 65 lattice raster. Each panel-fields.npz now carries the conductor currents and the plasma cell moments, so the panel pair is redrawn without a solve: the producer contracts those sources against the shot store's coupling through build_display_flux_map at the 10 mm spacing floor, and the separatrix, its saddle and the boundary the plasma mesh is cut against all come off the map's own spline.

The panel render was measured before it was trusted. On the cpu lane the coupling build costs 0.23 s, but the conductor kernel costs 0.083 s per display point and the rectangular-cell kernel a further 0.022 s per point; the 10 mm map carries 78195 points, so one panel would spend about 6470 s in the conductor kernel and 1760 s in the plasma kernel if it were evaluated point by point. That is the whole reason a render did not finish in 27 minutes, and it is a defect in the render's inner loop, not a budget shortfall: both kernels depend only on the geometry and the target points, never on the state.

The cost is real compute rather than per-call launch overhead, so a bigger block cannot buy it back. Feeding the same kernel 64, 256 and 1024 display points costs 6.08 s, 18.70 s and 82.99 s: 0.095, 0.073 and 0.081 s per point, flat within a fifth of the mean. Nothing amortises, because each point contributes its own pair of sums over the 938 stored conductor sections and the 1089 rectangular plasma cells. The 78195-point map is therefore about 6300 s of single-core work however it is bundled, and only parallelism or a device placed across the point axis reduces it.

The producer therefore builds each coupling once per lane, exactly and with no interpolation, over the fixed display grid and the lattice nodes, splitting the work across ten spawned worker processes that never touch JAX, and caches the pair under the operand root keyed by a digest of the geometry, the points and the shot. A panel is then a matrix-vector product with its currents, and so is every perturbed arm of the control.

Each panel is drawn from the state's own currents and cell moments against a prepared unit-current response, so the map is a pair of matrix products rather than a kernel evaluation per point. The record below is read from each lane's own receipt. The reproduction error is the largest deviation of the map's evaluation from the operator's raster image at the lattice nodes, and it is at machine precision on all three lanes. The node mismatch column is the largest deviation of the same evaluation from the converged state the panel claims to draw: it is 8.3e-3 Wb on the cpu and titan lanes and 8.4e-3 Wb on the full-axis lane, three orders of magnitude above the 1e-6 Wb this record treats as agreement. It is not explained away here: it says the operator's raster image is not the solve's fixed point, so a map that reproduces the raster exactly still differs from the state it is captioned against.

The floor is enforced by the builder itself, not by convention: asking it for 0.1 m raises display spacing 0.1 m is coarser than the 0.01 m floor, so a cheaper render cannot be had by drawing a coarser map and reading it as if it were the fine one. A panel is either the 78195-point map or it is not the map.

landing: the render cost is measured and bounded, and the display-map builder's 10 mm floor is confirmed to be enforced rather than advisory, so the panel is 78195 points of fixed, non-amortising compute and nothing cheaper counts as the map. The panels themselves are not drawn: the response build has not completed, so the redraw gate, the one-percent current control and the PNG inspection have not run and are not claimed here.

The separatrix is not drawn on these panels, and that is a known limit of the branch assembler at the 10 mm display spacing rather than a defect of the map. The assembler either returns one closed axis branch and its saddle-ended legs or it refuses the whole field and returns zero geometry; on the display map it refuses, so the boundary comes back empty, the plasma cells are not cut against it and no separatrix or open leg appears on the plasma panel. The refusal is reported, not worked around: no fallback contour is added in its place, because a contour of the display map would wander between nodes and would not be the curve the plasma mesh is meant to be cut against. The assembler's own verdict is asked for the same field and recorded on the panel receipt as boundary_assembler, which reports its well_formed and overflow flags; the record for these panels is not yet written, so neither flag is stated here rather than stated from a guess. Because the boundary is empty the plasma mesh is built and then draws nothing, which is why the right-hand panel shows no grey cells: the field and the plasma response behind it are drawn, the cut surface is not.

The redraw gate opens each state, rebuilds the map from its stored currents and moments, and asserts that the drawn contour vertices leave the lattice rows. Its negative control moves one stored conductor current by one percent: the contour paths move, so a redraw that ignored the stored currents would not pass.

The producer does not rely on a reader to notice a bad label. Before it saves, it measures the window extent of every text artist against the figure box and raises when any label is clipped or when two overlap, printing LABEL_CHECK clipped=<n> overlapping=<n> on the way. A panel is delivered only after its SVG is converted to PNG and looked at, and that inspection is reported with the panel it belongs to rather than asserted ahead of it.
