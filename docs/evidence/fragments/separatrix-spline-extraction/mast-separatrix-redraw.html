The MAST centroid panels draw the assembled separatrix

Every MAST centroid panel now draws the separatrix it was computing and not showing. The three committed states are redrawn from their stored conductor currents and cell moments, and the right-hand panel of each lane carries the closed lobe around the magnetic axis and both open legs through the admitted X-point, with the hexagonal plasma mesh cut against that lobe and filled inside it. The boundary is the display map's own branch assembly: the panel reads branches and its well_formed verdict from nova.equilibrium.display_flux rather than asking the assembler a second time with a hand-composed argument order.

The defect was reproduced before it was repaired. On the receipts committed at c2e4fd211 every lane recorded boundary_assembler: {available: true, overflow: true, well_formed: false} with boundary_branches_drawn: {closed_drawn: 0, open_drawn: 0} and boundary_vertex_count: 0, so the panels drew the field and the coil set but no boundary, and the plasma mesh was built and then had nothing to be cut against. The mechanism was the assembler's fixed 256 branch slots: a 10 mm display map needs 677 segments on the cpu and titan lanes and 675 on the full-axis lane, so the assembly overflowed and, by construction, refused the whole field rather than returning a partial boundary.

The redraw is read from each lane's own receipt. Segment counts are the map's assembled branches, the vertex count is the boundary the plasma mesh is cut against, and the saddle offset is the polished saddle's distance from the lattice saddle the seed came from.

The cpu lane is drawn twice, and the two files are different pictures rather than the same one twice: the plain panel is drawn at the producer's 4000-cell default, delivering 4286 cells (md5 a9f168eef415757c8f8f521dd96a6466), and the -fine panel at the 12000 cells its receipt records, delivering 12353 (md5 0f1f60461833579beb5f21679d0ad08e). The finer map reads as a finer mesh at full size, and its clipped-cell count rises from 1453 to 4250 with it. A test compares the two committed files' digests, so a redraw that collapsed them to one picture fails rather than passing silently.

The contour population is drawn at #929292 with a 0.6 pt stroke. At the ink style's 0.35 pt default the strokes antialiased to about #b8b8b8, roughly 1.9:1 against white, which is below the ~3:1 a data population is asked to hold and is the level the earlier review rejected as too faint. The separatrix is drawn in the same ink and heavier, so the boundary reads as one of the drawn contours rather than as a second overlay in another colour.

Each receipt's invocation now names the repository producer, benchmarks/mast_centroid_solve_panels.py panels, so a reader can replay the receipt from the repository alone. The previous receipts named a run-local wrapper outside the repository, which no reader could open. A test asserts the receipt's argv[0] is the producer.

Every PNG was rendered and looked at at full size. The separatrix runs through the red X-point marker and encloses the axis marker in all three lanes; the legs leave the saddle and exit the map rather than closing across the private flux region. The producer measures its own labels before saving and prints LABEL_CHECK clipped=0 overlapping=0 on every lane.

The cpu-lane receipt records display_map.reproduction_relative_error as exactly 0.0, where the same lane read 1.79e-16 before and the other lanes read about 2e-16. That exact zero is not agreement; it is the comparison reading one array twice, and it is a property of this producer's stand-in operator rather than of the display map. The map compares its evaluation at the lattice nodes against operator.raster_image, and this panel builds its operator as _PanelOperator onto the same DisplayCoupling the map is drawn through, so both operands are the same contraction of the same prepared response and the difference cancels bit for bit when both evaluations take the cached block. The non-zero readings on the other lanes are that same identity evaluated along a path that reassociates the sum, not a real disagreement. The figure therefore carries no information either way about whether the map reproduces the solve's own raster, and no claim is made from it here. The check with teeth on these panels is max_node_mismatch_wb, which compares the map against the stored state itself rather than against a copy of its own arithmetic.

The redraw gate ran as one job through the lane launcher: slurm job 1306726 at revision 5f91ff873, exit status 0, 11 passed in 2178.98 s. The base arm ran the same command at revision 2fd5d5b58 in slurm job 1306717, exit status 0, 10 passed in 2899.39 s. The head carries one test id the base does not, and neither arm carries a failing id, so the gate shows zero added failures against the base. Both arms ran on the reserved H200.

The gate was then re-run at the final head 8e6ea80b2, because the revision the evidence was written against had moved. Both the gate and its control were submitted at once and both landed on the titan rung after the H200 reservation refused them at that moment. That placement cannot serve the full-axis lane: the P100 holds 16 GB and the lane's JAX program asks for 2.46 GiB beyond it, so all three full-axis ids failed on RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED rather than on any assertion about the separatrix, and the gate ended 3 failed, 8 passed, exit status 1 in job 1306786. That is a placement defect, not a regression: the same tree re-submitted to the reserved H200 alone is job 1306805, and the revision is only reported as gated once that job's summary is read. The lesson is worth recording rather than smoothing over — a lane launcher that falls back from H200 to titan falls back onto a device this lane does not fit, which is the opposite of what the rung list is for.

The control for the new guard lives in the test module itself and is re-runnable from the tree: NOVA_PANEL_256_SLOT_CONTROL=1 makes it pin the display assembler back to 256 branch slots on the one call display_flux._branches makes, so the well_formed guard the panels now depend on must fail. Run at the same head as the gate, it goes red exactly where it must: 5 failed, 6 passed, exit status 1, with test_committed_panel_state_renders[cpu-lane] and [titan-lane] failing on assert assembler["well_formed"] and reporting closed_segment_count: 0 — the overflow refusing the whole field, which is the state these panels shipped in. Those two ids pass in the gate run at that head, so the guard is what separates the two runs rather than an incidental failure. The remaining three failures in the control run are the full-axis lane's device-memory failures described above, which that placement carries in the gate run too and which are therefore not what the control is measuring; the earlier H200 control run (logs/negative-control-final.log) reddened all three render ids on the same assertion, which is the complete form of the control.
