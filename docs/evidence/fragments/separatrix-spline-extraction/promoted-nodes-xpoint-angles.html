Separatrix directions at the promoted coil-edit X-point

Re-runs the converged MAST 22086/43 boundary-coil edit (fraction +6.000e-02 on circuit 8, target 933034.8750 A) on the assembler as it stands after the display-field capacity repair, so the separatrix this fragment measures is the one nova.equilibrium.display_flux._branches actually assembles rather than the truncated one the earlier record had to report unassembled. The solve closes at 5.730e-09 Wb with converged reading True, and promotes 60 of 1089 lattice nodes plus 14 of them on the wall, out of 1126 physical nodes of which 37 are wall coordinates.

Both separatrices are well formed: True and True. The state's trace carries 585 closed vertices over an arc of 5.2586 m and the field's 5417 over 5.2578 m. The two counts are not comparable, and neither is wrong: the state's separatrix is assembled on the solve's own lattice (0.0928 m cells) while the field's is assembled on the display map (0.0100 m), and each trace is sampled at a fixed number of points per cell of its own grid. The counts therefore differ by exactly the ratio of the two grids (9.26 against 9.28) while the two curves agree in length to 0.813 mm: they are the same separatrix at two resolutions, and the median spacing between the state's samples is 8.76 mm against the field's 1.24 mm. Every branch direction below carries that difference, and the state's chord is read at the limit of its own sampling.

The X-point itself is polished twice, on each field's own spline. On the solve's lattice the two nulls coincide at 1.73e-06 mm and their Hessians agree to 4.3e-08 relative; on the display map they sit 0.3545 mm apart, with Hessians agreeing to 2.4e-02 relative. The 0.3545 mm the earlier record quoted is the display-map figure, and it is a real property of the reconstructed field at that resolution, not of the solve's lattice. The state's null on the lattice is (0.578763, 1.204704) m at -1.307e-01 Wb.

The Hessian at the null has eigenvalues -1.384875 and +1.403806 Wb/m^2, a ratio of 1.013669, and its trace is +1.893e-02 Wb/m^2. The eigenvectors are the principal axes at +101.84° and -168.16°; they are orthogonal by construction and are not the separatrix asymptotes, which are the directions d with d'Hd = 0. In the eigenbasis that is l1 cos^2 t + l2 sin^2 t = 0, so each asymptote lies 45.1945° from the major axis — 45° only when the two eigenvalues are equal and opposite — and the two asymptote lines open 90.3889° at the null.

The trace fixes the local toroidal current density. With Delta* psi = psi_RR - psi_R/R + psi_ZZ and psi_R = 0 at a null, Delta* psi there is the trace itself, and mu0 J_phi = -Delta* psi gives -1.506e+04 A/m^2. A traceless Hessian would mean no local toroidal current and asymptotes at exactly 90°; this one is not traceless, so the asymptotes open slightly wider than a right angle and the four arms are not evenly spaced.

The mechanism is the paste at nova/equilibrium/reduced_newton.py:985, mapped = jnp.where(promoted, state, image), inside _reduced_kernels.trip_boundary: on a closing trip the state carried into the next trip is the map, so at a promoted node the terminal state is the pre-paste state and image - state there is exactly the residual the shadow rule exists to hide. The source tells a reader which mask selected a node; it does not tell a reader which region of the flux map the node sits in, and that is what decides the size of the hidden residual.

The worst near-zero group is the upper-half interior flood (private flux) nodes (24 of them), at 1.058e-09 Wb. The operator's own column reads 1.061e-09 Wb on the same nodes, so the state and the field of the currents genuinely agree there and the near-zero column is a true agreement rather than an artifact: the flood selected nodes whose residual is already at the solve floor, so the paste changes nothing at them. The reported upper-half column is nevertheless not uniformly zero: the 7 private-wall band nodes in the same half read up to 2.003e-03 Wb, and the operator's raster carries no wall coordinate to check them against. So the half's aggregate column averages two selections and is not a single quantity; the split by source is the first reading that separates them. It is not the last: the lower half's flood group carries the residual in its own right, and the next section is what that group is.

The lower interior-flood pocket

The two interior-flood groups are the same rule applied to two very different places. The upper group sits in the upper private-flux wedge, 56.5 to 608.2 mm from the separatrix, its normalised flux running 0.744 to 0.997, and there the field of the currents already reproduces the frozen state to 1.058e-09 Wb. The lower group sits in a pocket the solve never resolves, and its frozen state is 2.621e-02 Wb from the field of the currents.

That pocket lies below a second saddle that the solve did not admit. Polishing the same lattice spline from a seed at (0.60, -1.20) m converges to a saddle at (+0.55359, -1.18150) m with flux -1.474e-01 Wb, and the field of the currents puts its counterpart at (+0.56155, -1.16421) m at -1.458e-01 Wb. With the axis at 0.342984 Wb and the admitted boundary flux at -0.130668 Wb, the lower saddle's normalised flux is 1.0354 — it lies 0.0354 outside the boundary level, and the receipt's admitted set carries the single upper X-point and not this one. The 22 lower interior-flood nodes sit beneath it: every one is at least 193.5 mm below it in height (up to 568.5 mm) and 193.7 to 640.3 mm from it in the plane, their flux is 0.0274 to 0.1478 Wb above its flux, and their normalised flux runs 0.723 to 0.977 — entirely on the plasma side of the boundary value. They are 21.9 to 191.3 mm from the wall, measured to the wall polyline's segments rather than to its vertices: the nearest of them sits 21.9 mm off the first wall, and a vertex-only distance reports several times that, because the wall's vertices are far enough apart that a node alongside a straight run is nowhere near the closer of the two.

So the pocket is a closed-flux lobe below an unadmitted saddle, bounded on one side by the wall and cut off from the axis on the other by the narrow band around that saddle where the flux falls below the boundary level. The flood labels exactly those cells: classify_domains assigns PRIVATE_FLUX to every cell that is closed but not in the axis-connected component (nova/equilibrium/domain.py:213), and connected is the saddle-aware flood grown from the magnetic axis in axis_connected_component (nova/equilibrium/domain.py:185). The mask surfaces as DomainMasks.private_flux (nova/equilibrium/domain.py:117) and is returned as the first shadow component by ForwardFluxOperator.residual_shadow_components (nova/equilibrium/forward_operator.py:3712, returned at nova/equilibrium/forward_operator.py:3758). Nothing in the pocket carries plasma current: profile participation is the complement of the private-flux and material masks (nova/equilibrium/domain.py:128), and the same cells are removed from the residual. No equation therefore constrains the flux there, and the paste is what holds it at whatever value the iterate carried in — which is why the field of the currents does not reproduce it.

The worked example is node 335 at (+0.6662, -1.3750) m, the worst of the group: its frozen state is -1.103e-01 Wb, the field of the currents gives -1.365e-01 Wb, and the difference of -2.621e-02 Wb is confirmed by the operator's own raster at -2.621e-02 Wb. The held region changes no boundary geometry. Its nearest node is 302, at 354.09 mm from the state's assembled separatrix, and the farthest is 756.17 mm away; the pocket touches the trace at no point, and every boundary reading in this fragment is taken at the admitted upper X-point, which the pocket never approaches. The held lower region is a defect in the flux map, not in the boundary.

Does the promotion move the near-X-point geometry?

Every local reading at the null says no more than the grid it was taken on. On the solve's lattice, where each field is polished on a spline of its own values, the two nulls coincide to 1.73e-06 mm and the four arms agree to 0.000001°. The 0.3545 mm the display grid carries is not a displacement of the solve's null: that comparison is between two different interpolants sampled on the same finer grid — the state's lattice tensor spline evaluated at the display coordinates (display_state_flux, built from the 0.0928 m lattice nodes) against the reconstructed display map itself (display_flux, from build_display_flux_map at 0.0100 m). The 0.35 mm is the difference between those two interpolants, carried by the lattice spline being evaluated off its own nodes; the display map is the finer grid, not the coarser one. On that grid the arms differ by 0.606° and the traced chords by at most 0.895°. The promotion therefore perturbs nothing that the solve resolves. The rule a repair would change is the paste itself: the exclude-shadow branch of ForwardFluxOperator.residual_shadow_mask in nova/equilibrium/forward_operator.py decides which nodes keep their pre-paste state, and _reduced_kernels.trip_boundary in nova/equilibrium/reduced_newton.py is where that decision is applied. Narrowing it so a node is kept only where the residual the paste would hide is large enough to matter, rather than wherever the flood or the wall band reaches, would shrink the excluded set without changing the closing field it exists to protect — and would remove the 22-node lower pocket, whose held residual is 2.621e-02 Wb and whose nearest node is 354.1 mm from any boundary the solve assembles. Nothing in this node's evidence says the change is needed; it says the mechanism by which the widened set shows up.
