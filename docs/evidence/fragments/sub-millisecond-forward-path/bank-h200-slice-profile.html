Sequential-pulse latency, independent-pulse throughput and the memory stop

Under TORAX coupling, time slices within one pulse are causally sequential. Batch width therefore means independent pulses or ensemble members, each advancing its own current time slice; later slices of one pulse cannot be treated as concurrent batch members. The fixture measures a homogeneous independent-member population and does not run the TORAX coupling itself. A batch width of 64 gives 370.069 ms batch latency for those concurrent solves and 5.782 ms/slice throughput cost, not 5.782 ms latency for a sequential slice of one pulse.

Ranked changes toward 1 ms throughput across independent pulses

At 64 concurrent independent pulses or members, the throughput cost needs a further 4.782 ms/slice reduction. The ranking below targets aggregate throughput at that concurrency while preserving causal progression within each pulse. It does not claim 1 ms single-pulse latency: that latency starts at 39.880 ms, and the same repairs must also be measured separately at width 1. The following are measurement-led engineering targets, not observed speedups. Their optimistic expected-saving budget totals 4.79 ms/slice, leaving about 0.99 ms/slice; achieving that requires the large reductions stated below, and none is established by this profile alone. No saving is credited by removing a qualification check or freezing continuous geometry.

  1. Share and fuse primal/tangent quadrature for booking and assembly. current.book, book_tangent_channels and step_jacobian together cost 181.186 ms/batch, or 2.831 ms/slice at width 64. Reuse booked primal quantities at the unchanged iterate, retain the runtime bank operands, and fuse the populated ring/strip reductions and tangent channels. Expected throughput saving target: 2.40 ms/slice (about 85%). Verify with the same fresh-process width-64 profile, an exclusive booking/assembly budget at most 0.431 ms/slice, unchanged fresh residuals, and tangent agreement against the existing fresh-read derivative checks.
  2. Reuse current-state geometry between trial testing and the full certificate, and compact local fragment work. Full plus carried geometry costs 124.917 ms/batch, or 1.952 ms/slice. Share coefficients, curvature, fragment areas and edge intervals already evaluated at the exact same trial state, while still running full connected qualification; batch only the live strip/fragment work. Expected throughput saving target: 1.70 ms/slice (about 87%). Verify full-versus-carried moved-state equality and rebuild refusals, unchanged terminal certificates, executed strip-kernel counts and an exclusive geometry budget at most 0.252 ms/slice.
  3. Reduce Krylov reductions and avoid dense matrix traffic. Block GMRES, balancing and update scaling cost 31.243 ms/batch, or 0.488 ms/slice. Apply the analytic Jacobian across independent members in a compact batched form, carrying each pulse’s own preconditioner between its causal steps only where measured contraction certifies it; do not assume that factor carry wins when it adds reads. Expected throughput saving target: 0.35 ms/slice. Verify a budget at most 0.138 ms/slice, condition-estimate × Krylov-residual at most 1e-8, terminal fresh residual at most 1e-10, and no extra nonlinear reads that erase the saving.
  4. Fuse stationary tracking and keep the stream’s small control state on device. Tracking costs 17.084 ms/batch (0.267 ms/slice), other/fused kernels 6.655 ms (0.104 ms/slice), and measured copies/fills 1.357 ms (0.021 ms/slice). The native wall exceeds total traced device activity by only about 0.12 ms/slice; the 0.383 ms/slice profiled gap is inflated by profiling and cannot all be credited as a native saving. Expected throughput saving target: 0.34 ms/slice across this combined surface, using fixed-shape scalar operands, fused stationary iterations and a device-resident graph that advances each pulse sequentially while batching only independent members. Verify with unprofiled end-to-end walls, transfer-byte counts, stationary-point precision and complete kernel accounting. Finally remeasure all four changes together at width 64 for the 1 ms throughput gate and at width 1 for causal single-pulse latency; summed isolated targets do not prove either result.
