The 3.77e15 ITER bank second derivative belongs to the certified bank and comes from the stable-pole derivative rule, not from the moment rows

38 node-cell pairs carry a second derivative above 1e9, and every one of them is a moment-1 psi_RR entry whose target node sits within 6e-13 m of a vertex of its own source cell. The certified bank 81ee8fdf carries them. Its two DINA rerun rebuilds are bit-identical to each other and reproduce the certified entries to 1.1e-5 absolute with the same pair count at every bound, so the rerun did not introduce the blow-up. Reverting the corner entries alone to their pre-repair values lowers the largest magnitude from 3.77e15 to 3.36e9 and the count above 1e9 from 38 to 1, so the number is carried by those corner entries.

Evaluating one blown-up pair's row function under three derivative arms puts the cause in the stable-pole contraction inside the derivative rules rather than in the value graph. With the stable-pole contraction present the entry is -3.7747e15; with the same substitution disabled the identical call returns 1.92e6; and the shipped path agrees with the fully stable-pole path to twelve significant digits. A probe of the booked radial gradient at four offsets from the vertex shows no discontinuity: the signed difference between the two sides falls as the offset falls, so the row value is smooth there and a second derivative of this size cannot come from it. The moment-row repair does not remove the entry. psi_RR is built from rows 0:3 and 6:9, and the repair rebuilds rows 3:6, which feed dpsi/dZ, psi_RZ and psi_ZZ only.

The row value is continuous at the vertex

The booked radial gradient is 2 pi R rows[6:9], so a second derivative of 3.77e15 would be visible as a discontinuity or a very steep slope in that gradient at the vertex. The probe steps the target radius by +h and -h about the vertex for h = 1e-3, 1e-6, 1e-9 and 1e-12 m and records the gradient on each side. A jump that holds its size as the step shrinks is a discontinuity in the first derivative; a difference that falls with the step is a smooth row value.

A genuine second derivative of 3.77e15 across the 2e-9 m stencil would require the row value to change by about 3e5 over that spacing, against the 6.0e-11 measured. The booked psi_RR is a property of the derivative rule, not of the value graph it differentiates.

The stable-pole arm is the exception, and it locates the branch. Its row value at +1e-9 m is -1.0386e-6 and at +1e-12 m it is -1.4716e-7, while the shipped arm reads -1.0398e-6 at both steps. The substitution's own threshold therefore falls between those two steps, which is the branch whose derivative the booked entry carries.

The moment-row repair would not remove it

The candidate explanation under test was that the moment-1 and moment-2 Z-gradient rows are built independently of their flux rows. The measured layout rules it out as the cause of this entry. psi_RR is 2 pi R times the radial derivative of rows[6:9], and the row assembly carries rows 0:3 as the flux moments, rows 3:6 as the radial field and rows 6:9 as the vertical field, so psi_RR depends on rows 0:3 and 6:9 and never on rows 3:6.

The arms confirm that separation: the shipped and stable-pole arms share psi_RR to twelve significant digits while their psi_RZ differ, and the certified bank differs from the shipped arm in dpsi/dZ and psi_RZ while agreeing in psi_RR. Rebuilding the moment rows therefore changes dpsi/dZ, psi_RZ and psi_ZZ and leaves the 3.77e15 psi_RR where it is. The candidate's premise holds, and the blow-up is simply not in the Z-gradient rows. The corner construction, and its stable-pole derivative rule, is what has to change.

Method and artifacts

Each measurement ran as one scheduler job submitted from this worktree, and every job log ends in EXIT=0. The census job compares six bank stores directly and records pairwise agreement. The attribution job lists every pair above a bound with its node, cell, moment, position and distances, and counts both masks. The kernel job rebuilds one node's packed rows under three derivative arms and differentiates them exactly as the bank does, swapping only the row function. The jump job steps the target radius about a vertex and records the booked radial gradient on each side.

The four JSON records sit under the shared cache root, with the job logs in the run directory. This page is rendered from those records by scripts/prototypes/bank_hessian_blowup/fragment.py. No product source was changed.
