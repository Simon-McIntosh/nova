The 3.77e15 ITER bank second derivative belongs to the certified bank and comes from the stable-pole derivative rule, not from the moment rows

38 node-cell pairs carry a second derivative above 1e9: 37 are moment-1 psi_RR entries and one is a moment-2 psi_RR entry, and every target node sits within 5.7334e-13 m of a vertex of its own source cell. The certified bank 81ee8fdf carries them. Its two DINA rerun rebuilds reproduce the certified entries to within 1.06e-5 in absolute terms and are identical to each other, with the same pair count at every bound, so the rerun did not introduce the blow-up. A rebuild of the same inputs reproduces the pre-repair entry exactly at the 20 pairs both are recorded at, while differing from the certified bank there by up to 3.77e15. It lowers the largest magnitude from 3.77e15 to 3.36e9 and the count above 1e9 from 38 to 1, so the number is carried by those corner entries.

Where the offending pairs sit

A bank row is a coefficient of a linear operator, not a solved flux field: it carries no poloidal flux contour map and admits no stationary points, so there is no null set to mark and no flux panel to draw. What the figure can show is the geometry the coefficient is computed from, and where the offending pairs sit in it.

Is it a jump or a smooth row

The booked rows of node 110, cell 16 evaluated at points straddling its own radius, by +/- each offset. A step that holds its size as the offset shrinks is a discontinuity; a step that falls with the offset is a smooth row.

The booked second derivative therefore belongs to the stable poles derivative rule, whose own first derivative steps across the probe radius: every arm's value is continuous there, so the blow-up is a jump in that rule's derivative rather than in a value graph the arms share.

How it was measured

Four records carry every number on this page: the census of each bank's second-derivative magnitudes, the attribution of the pairs above the bound, the jet comparison of the row programs, and the offset profile. They are written under /home/ITER/mcintos/.cache/nova-bank-hessian-blowup.

Nothing here re-implements the repository's machinery. The point-to-edge distance is nova.equilibrium.boundary_comparison._point_to_segment_distances; the jets are nova.equilibrium.fixed_points._source_jets, called with its row function swapped for the arm under test; and the cell geometry is read once by scripts.prototypes.bank_hessian_blowup.geometry and shared by every script in this directory.
