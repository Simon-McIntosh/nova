A uniform rectangular source at a horizontal edge recovers the density jump to 2.60268e-07 relative at 100 nm, and 5.32595e-09 after distance extrapolation. This is the positive control for the jump instrument.

The Grad–Shafranov equation constrains ψRR + ψZZ − ψR/R = −2πμ0RJ. Across a smooth edge the Hessian jump is the forcing jump times n⊗n, where n is the edge normal. Thus ψZZ carries the nZ² fraction of that jump; ψRZ carries nRnZ. At a corner, the second angular harmonic of the piecewise source density can produce logarithmic curvature. The opposing ψRR and ψZZ logarithms cancel in the trace. Treating every corner as a horizontal smooth edge therefore gives the wrong jump test.

A separate local-wedge calculation predicts the measured increments per decade of decreasing distance. Over the 79 active-corner records, the relative norm differences are 1.28e-5/9.71e-6 for ψRZ/ψZZ at state 220, 1.11e-5/1.18e-5 at 333, and 1.21e-5/9.79e-6 at 336. At node 943 and state 220 the predicted increments are 1.481364 and 3.099123 Wb/m² per decade; the measured increments are 1.481347 and 3.099104. This checks the logarithmic interpretation against the source geometry, independently of either bank Hessian.

The nominal source corner and its rounded stored node are distinct. The corner approach sequence reaches 100 nm; at node 943 this is far above the 0.542 pm separation between the stored coordinate and the source corner. It diagnoses the nominal corner limit, while the preceding arbitrary-precision correction estimates the derivative at the actual stored coordinate. Neither interpretation makes the interior discrepancies disappear.

An unperturbed interior reference arm is accepted. Multiplying its ψZZ values by 1.000001 produces a relative error of 1.000000e-6 at each state and is refused. The respective discrimination thresholds are 1.53034e-8, 1.39477e-8 and 1.36917e-8: the larger of the 1e-8 bank budget and four times the reference envelope. This is the declared instrument control, not a claim that either bank route passes the fidelity budget.

The one-sided experiment approaches every boundary-group node from below and above. Each derivative stencil is wholly contained in the same source-cell membership as its displaced centre, checked against every authored polygon. First-derivative fields are evaluated in extended precision, then differentiated with four central steps and eighth-order Richardson extrapolation. At an active corner, only its nearby source cells are reevaluated; the independently measured smooth remainder is held at the target. Consequently finite-distance totals omit the smooth remainder's O(distance) variation, while retaining the limiting corner behaviour. At nodes without an active corner the complete active-source field is reevaluated. Coil Hessians are held at the target in both cases. No terminal forward solve or timing lever is produced by this measurement.
