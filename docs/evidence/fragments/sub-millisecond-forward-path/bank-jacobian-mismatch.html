Why the step Jacobian looks wrong at the stalled drive

The engaged forward step solves a linear model it assembles itself, I - d(booked)/du. The first attempt at this node reported that the booking tangent inside that matrix disagreed with a non-converged 1e-2 drive by 100–200 % in every direction class. That reading does not survive review: the mismatch is a constant of the moment block, identical to six significant digits at a stalled state whose residual is 1.79 and at a certificate state whose residual is 1.5 × 10−12. No property of the Jacobian can be invariant across twelve orders of magnitude of residual. The comparison is measuring the difference between a perturbed state and an unperturbed matrix, and this fragment reports that rather than a product defect.

What is compared

support_read.step_jacobian assembles the Newton matrix from the bank channels, the closed-form cell-local booking sensitivities and, with fresh_geometry=True, the moving saddle and edge-interval term. The residual the loop evaluates is step_residual(operands, moments, moments, support, profile, quadrature), whose blocks are moments - booked[:3] (nova/equilibrium/support_read.py:547). The solve uses it at support_read.py:1417–1422 as right = -residual against matrix = step_jacobian(...), so the matrix is required to equal d(residual)/du, not its negative. The comparison takes central differences of the full fresh-read residual (support_read.read re-run at each perturbed state, so the reading, ownership and clip set move with the state) along unit moment directions: five directions in each of the cut, normal-form, whole and stationary-point cells, plus eight random, thirty-three in all at relative step sizes 1e-5, 1e-6 and 1e-7.

Two states are measured on the 550-cell diverted certificate, one lane, cold seed: stalled 1e-2, amplitude 1.01, fresh residual 1.793 × 100; and certificate, amplitude 1.0, converged to a fresh residual of 1.5 × 10−12. Both carry 99 cut cells, 8 normal-form cells, 239 whole cells, one axis cell and one saddle cell.

Why this is not a property of the Jacobian

Three readings, any one of which falsifies the interpretation:

  • It is invariant in the residual. A genuine disagreement between a linearisation and the map it linearises scales with how far the state is from the solution. Here the residual moves by twelve decades and the mismatch does not move in the sixth digit.
  • It is constant across direction classes. Cut, whole, normal-form, axis and saddle cells are five different physical situations. That they agree exactly means the number is carried by the shape of the comparison, not by the cells.
  • It is constant across step size. A truncation error in a central difference falls as the square of the step; an exact match of a nonlinear map at 1e-5, 1e-6 and 1e-7 to six digits is the signature of an algebraic identity, not of a derivative.

The two defects in the comparison

Both are in this node's own prototype, scripts/prototypes/bank_jacobian_mismatch/measure.py, and both are established by reading the code rather than inferred from the numbers.

  1. The sign. step_residual returns moments - booked, whose derivative is exactly the assembled matrix I - d(booked)/du. The script computes -(residual(plus) - residual(minus)) / (2 eps), which is the negative of the derivative the matrix is supposed to equal. The negation turns an exact agreement into a relative mismatch of order two.
  2. The step scale. perturb displaces the state by eps * scale * e, where scale is the per-cell magnitude of the state, while the analytic side applies the matrix to e alone. The difference quotient therefore returns d(residual)/d(eps), the derivative along scale * e, and never divides by scale. Where scale is 178 the analytic vector is a two-hundredth of the difference and the relative mismatch reads 1 + 1/scale = 1.0056; where it is 1.43 it reads 1.7009; where it is 1.006 it reads 1.9939. The three block constants above are those three scales.

With both defects the predicted relative mismatch is 1 + 1/scale per direction, which is what the receipt holds. Neither defect reaches product code: the prototype is the only file involved, and nova/equilibrium/support_read.py is unmodified.

Splitting the mismatch by term

The term split is not readable from this receipt either, because it inherits the same two defects. booking to CD is frozen_cd + (analytic - static); since the analytic pair nearly cancels it reduces to frozen_cd, whose only content is the read refresh, so its small values (0.009–0.019) restate refresh rather than testing the booking sensitivity. geometry to CD is static + refresh = analytic under the same cancellation and reproduces the as-assembled column to four digits. The split cannot distinguish a booking defect from a geometry defect at any state.

What the receipt does show, and what a corrected run must preserve, is that the frozen-read and fresh-read differences agree to about one per cent away from the stationary point (refresh 0.003–0.011), and that at the normal-form and saddle cells the refresh reaches 0.98–1.00, i.e. the read moving inside the stencil carries as much as the whole derivative there. That is consistent with the earlier finding that the moving saddle term dominates at the saddle, and it is unaffected by the sign and scale defects, both of which act on the analytic side only.

Ruling out the harness: the supplied gradient

The bank solve fixture supplies bank_solve_profile.gradient, and at both states that function returns identically zero: supplied_gradient_max = 0.0, shape (9,). The question is whether zero is the true derivative of the profile the residual books.

It is. At nine probe fluxes, the booked profile's p_prime has flux derivative 0.0 and its ff_prime has flux derivative 0.0, at both states: both profiles are constant in flux, so their derivative with respect to the flux coordinate is exactly the zero the fixture supplies. The amplitude scaling is also consistent: p_prime reads 2787.512 at amplitude 1.0 and 2815.387 at amplitude 1.01, and 2787.512 × 1.01 = 2815.387. The profile the residual books is the scaled one and its constant is scaled with it, so a gradient of zero is right for both. A control gradient of a constant 1e-3 leaves the booking column unchanged to three digits, which is what a term of this size should do here.

The gradient is not the defect. In production the gradient is composed by the caller and passed through solve_batch to step_jacobian; the fixture's zero happens to be correct for this profile and would be wrong for a flux-dependent one, but nothing in this receipt turns on it.

The fix this implies

No product defect is claimed. The hypothesis this node carries forward is about the instrument, not the matrix:

When the central difference is taken with the sign +(residual(plus) - residual(minus)) / (2 eps) and divided by the same per-cell scale that displaced the state, the assembled Jacobian agrees with the fresh-read residual to the order of the difference truncation error, and the earlier booking-tangent reading disappears. If it does not, the residual the booking term differentiates is not the residual the loop evaluates, and the term to repair is the booking sensitivity inside step_jacobian.

The correction is confined to measure.py: negate nothing, and either drop scale from perturb or divide the difference quotient by it. The re-run is one allocation of about eight minutes per state; both states, the term split and the decision table are already in the driver and need no change. Until that run exists, the earlier conclusion on this plan — that the booking tangent accounts for the whole mismatch away from the saddle — is retracted: it was read from a comparison that could not have shown agreement.
