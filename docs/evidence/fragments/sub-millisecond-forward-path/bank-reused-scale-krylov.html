One Jacobian-vector product costs more than one dense assembly, so the Krylov route is refused

The step's Jacobian is applied as one dense block. The alternative a carried equilibration would enable — reusing the previous update's row and column scales and cell-block inverses around support_read.step_jacobian_vector and letting GMRES solve the later updates — was measured before any change was made, because a later update needs about forty products and the product is only worth having if it is cheaper than the assembly it replaces. It is not. At sixteen slices on the diverted 550-cell bank, one batched product takes 107.29–107.41 ms of device time against 36.46–36.48 ms for one batched assembly, so the break-even is 0.34 iterations: a later update would have to converge in fewer than one product to tie. Forty products would cost 4292–4296 ms, about 118 times one assembly.

The product is expensive for a structural reason, not a conditioning one. The assembly evaluates _local_booking_sensitivities once per cell and contracts all 3·cells bank channels through one einsum, so the booking sensitivity is computed once for the whole matrix; each product instead evaluates the full book_tangent for one direction. A single direction therefore costs more than the whole batched channel sweep. This is independent of the equilibration question the earlier matrix-free record settled: those scales cut the product path from 400 iterations to 40, and 40 is still about 118 assemblies' worth of products.

At 128 slices the batched (fused) arm has no result. Its compiled graph requests a 130.56 GiB temporary buffer and the allocator refuses it after rematerialization could only reach 121.48 GiB; that is the same memory wall the batch-width record met at width 256 for the whole solve, and it happened on every attempt, including the run that re-tested it under the corrected instrument. The comparison at that width is therefore one lane-sequential execution mode in two fresh processes, not a batched one. It gives 1189.41–1190.02 ms for the assembly against 1483.89–1484.29 ms for the product, a break-even of 0.802 iterations, and forty products would cost about 59.4 s. At sixteen slices the same lane-sequential arm gives 148.70–148.73 ms against 184.25–184.40 ms, a break-even of 0.807. Batching the lanes is what makes the assembly cheap per lane (2.28 ms against 9.29 ms), and it makes the product cheap by the same factor, so the batching decision and the assembly-versus-product decision are independent, and the second is settled against the product at every width measured.

Every lane in both arms is the diverted 550-cell certificate state perturbed by the nested scale bank out to 128 lanes; the lane arrays are built once and saved, and every arm loads the same file. The measurement is scripts/prototypes/bank_reused_scale_krylov/measure.py driven by device.sh and measure.sbatch. The internal shape check compares a (slices, 3·cells, 3·cells) assembly block with three (slices, cells) product components, 3·550 = 1650, and now raises rather than reporting, so an arm whose two calls did not address the same system cannot contribute a ratio. All six rows print shape_consistent: true.

The driver fails closed. Each arm writes through tee, so the runner sets pipefail and returns the pipeline's first nonzero status, and the allocation aborts when any measurement arm fails. That is checked rather than asserted: the allocation's control arm runs the batched build at 128 slices, which cannot fit, and the driver reports it as a failure — the control log ends EXIT=1 with the RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED trace, and the allocation's own summary prints control(vmap 128) EXIT=1 (must be nonzero) before exiting zero only because that failure is the expected one.

Verdict: the measurement favours the dense block, which is what the code already does. No product path was implemented, support_read.solve_batch and newton_step are unchanged, and no test was added, because the condition the brief sets for proceeding — forty products cheaper than one assembly at both widths — is false at the first width measured and cannot become true at a wider one.
