Fast-route convergence and oracle precision beyond the diverted seed

The fast route converges on all four measured certificate rows — both cases at 550 cells and at 2000 cells — and all four stay inside the locked precision budget on every clause, the 2000-cell rows several orders tighter than the 550-cell ones. The limited rows' terminal residuals sit at their own numerical floor rather than short of what the route can reach.

Each row runs the fast route from the certificate seed — the analytic read, booked — to a fresh-read residual, then runs the default present route on the same certificate row. Geometry is judged against the analytic oracle under the locked precision budget: magnetic axis and X-point within 5e-4 of the minor radius, boundary flux within 1e-4 of the flux span, and a fresh-read solved residual of at most 1e-10. The present route is judged only on its own convergence status and terminal residual, since §4 holds it to no regression rather than to convergence.

Every X-point gap between the routes is stated with the representation it was read on. On the solve lattice a gap above a few micrometres is real; on a display map the gap is reported beside that map's own interpolation offset.

The diverted row converges in three updates and its stationary points land 6.80e-6 m from the analytic axis and 6.80e-6 m from the analytic X-point on a 0.7343 m minor radius, with the boundary flux 9.29e-7 Wb inside a 0.03587 Wb span. The axis and X-point are about fifty times inside their budget and the boundary flux is 3.9 times inside its own — the row's closest approach, and the clause that would give first on a coarser bank. A repeat of the diverted row alone at the final revision reproduced this: three updates and 1.592e-12 Wb fresh residual with every budget clause passing, so the numbers are stable to about 3e-13 between runs rather than to four significant figures.

Both limited rows pass both clauses. Against the analytic flux on a 2.0318 m minor radius, the 550-cell row places its axis 2.35e-5 m out (1.158e-5 of the minor radius, 43 times inside the 5e-4 budget) and the 2000-cell row 4.67e-6 m out (2.297e-6, 218 times inside). The boundary flux is judged against the limiter's designed contact, the midpoint of the wall's midplane-crossing edge at the largest major radius. The analytic flux at that contact is zero to rounding (2.2e-14 Wb), so the clause measures the flux the route's own boundary point carries: the 550-cell route places it 8.344e-3 m away at (7.8014, −0.008344) m, where the analytic flux is −2.683e-4 Wb, and the 2000-cell route places it 2.433e-5 m away at (7.8014, 2.433e-5) m, where it is −2.281e-9 Wb. The error comes out at 2.683e-4 Wb on the 550-cell row and 2.281e-9 Wb on the 2000-cell row, which is 5.373e-6 and 4.567e-11 of the 49.941 Wb span against a 1e-4 budget — 19 times inside on the 550-cell row and 2.2e6 times inside on the 2000-cell row. Neither row has a magnetic island, so no X-point ratio applies; axis and boundary are the only two clauses they carry, and both pass at both resolutions.

A peer node found the miss this section previously reported was in this measurement's own oracle, not in the route. The earlier row_oracle took the boundary reference as the wall vertex carrying the largest analytic flux. The wall's 121 vertices straddle the midplane at the outboard edge, so that argmax picks vertex 0 at (7.8014, 0.11294) m, where the analytic flux is −0.049155 Wb, while the flux maximum lies between it and its neighbour, on the edge the plasma is designed to touch. The reference is now the oracle fixtures' own designed_contact, which finds that edge from the polygon geometry alone, and it returns (7.801395789307387, 0) m with an analytic flux of zero. Imported rather than re-implemented, so this measurement cannot drift from the point the certificate is built against. Against that reference both limited rows pass, and the flat 9.9e-4 ratio the earlier measurement reported was the flux drop across one wall segment carried by the vertex sample, not error at a shared point. Attribution: the defect was found by the bank-limited-contact node, whose measurement of the same rows identified the vertex argmax as the source; the corrected clause above is recomputed from this node's own archived states with no new allocation. The table and the panel carry the corrected boundary reference; the archived oracle ratios in results/limited-oracle.json were recomputed for the boundary clause from the saved states.

The clause is reproducible from the repository, not from the run directory. Both archived limited states are committed beside the instrument that reads them — scripts/prototypes/bank_route_rows/archived/limited-550-oracle.npz (sha256 74ee9e08…) and …/limited-2000-oracle.npz (sha256 42a849be…) — with the recorded clause in archived/boundary-clause.json. One command re-derives both rows and asserts them against that record, exiting nonzero if an archive is missing, its hash moved, the designed contact moved, or either ratio drifts: JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu python -m scripts.prototypes.bank_route_rows.boundary_gap --check. It prints CHECK=PASS. Two properties the clause depends on are worth naming, since they bound what it can claim. The archive holds the converged route's node coordinates, its solved flux, both stationary points and the wall, but not the route's own reported boundary-flux value, and its boundary point is a wall point rather than a lattice node — so the clause carries the analytic flux at the route's own boundary point, and the route's own terminal residual (9.64e-11 Wb at 550 cells, 6.64e-11 Wb at 2000) is the archived bound on how far its booked value sits from it. Separately, the flux span is taken between the analytic axis and the designed contact, which is 49.941 Wb; the earlier 49.990 Wb figure came from the same vertex contact this correction removed.

The limited residuals sit at their own noise floor. Moving each converged moment by one to four representable steps, with the landed noise-floor instrument's nudged imported unchanged and sixteen patterns per row, leaves the 550-cell fresh residual with a median of 1.219e-10 and a maximum of 2.547e-10 against a converged baseline of 9.641e-11, and the 2000-cell row with a median of 8.049e-11 and a maximum of 1.201e-10 against 6.639e-11. Every perturbed read stayed valid. Against the steps the routes actually recorded, the floor is comparable to the terminal residual but not larger than the last accepted gain: on the 550-cell row the last accepted step moved the residual from 6.039e-10 to 9.641e-11, a gain of 5.07e-10, while the largest shift any single-rounding move produced is 1.58e-10 — about a third of that gain. On the 2000-cell row the last accepted step moved 9.177e-5 to 6.639e-11, six orders larger than its own 5.4e-11 noise span. So the terminal residuals are not evidence that either row stopped short of what the route can reach, and the noise is the same order as the certificate: a rounding step is worth about a third of the 550-cell row's final gain and lands inside the 1e-10 limit's neighbourhood, exactly as the diverted lanes behaved before the saddle-curvature repair. The 550-cell row's baseline of 9.641e-11 lies between the smallest perturbed residual it produced (9.459e-11) and their median (1.219e-10), and its floor straddles 1e-10 — the residual limit stays inside this row's numerical noise, so the 9.64e-11 pass at 550 cells is a coefficient of the arithmetic rather than a margin the route controls. The 2000-cell row's floor sits lower, from 4.638e-11 to 1.201e-10 around a baseline of 6.639e-11, and reaches past the limit at its maximum as well.

The present route does not converge on either row. Its own status is cited from the committed certificate receipts at the base revision (docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/gate-c-resolve/parts/), because the present arm could not be re-run in this allocation: the H200 jobs stayed PENDING on a resource reason past ten minutes and were cancelled, and the titan fallback asked for 11.92 GiB of one P100 and failed with CUDA_ERROR_OUT_OF_MEMORY on a card that shares its memory with the node's other jobs. The receipts are the same benchmark, the same cases and the same sizes: the diverted -500 row realises 550 cells and ends unconverged at 0.1938 Wb after 109.8 s, and the limited -500 row realises 555 and ends unconverged at 0.08986 Wb after 144.1 s. Against a stalled present state there is no converged X-point to compare on the lattice — its own topology read admits no X-point on the diverted row, reporting class limited — so the fast-against-present X-point gap and the asymptote angle difference are recorded as not measurable rather than as zero.

The flux gap over unpromoted nodes is not measurable on these rows for a second reason: the two routes carry different node sets, and nearest-neighbour matching of the 1741 fast lattice nodes against the present receipt's 2226 coordinates finds no pair closer than 4.8e-5 m and a median nearest distance of 6.8 mm, so any difference read across them would be an interpolation artefact. Reading it needs the present route's own promoted mask and an interpolation onto one node set, which is the measurement the promoted-node record already carries for its own case rather than one this node can assemble from a stalled state.

The 2000-cell rows did build and converge; the 5000-cell row did not fit. Only the diverted 550-cell bank is stored, so every other row builds its own operator matrix through build_jet_matrix. At 2000 cells the diverted row realised 6172 lattice nodes and the limited row 6176, and each build took about 626 s on the H200 to write a 1.78 GB matrix — under twice the 550-cell wall of 328.8 s, not the three-and-a-half times this node first estimated from node count alone. Both fast routes then converged in two updates. The diverted 2000-cell row is the most accurate row measured here, with its axis 1.53e-6 m and its X-point 1.95e-6 m from the analytic ones: four times tighter than the 550-cell row on each stationary point and seven times tighter on the boundary flux. That is the bank's build-and-resolution trade in one comparison, and it is why the 550-cell pass should be read as the coarse end of a trend rather than as the route's precision. No 2000-cell row's present arm was measured: the diverted row's died when the certificate benchmark's analytic-flux process pool broke under contention with the other job on the node, and neither limited row reached one. The limited rows' own oracle ratios and residual floors come from a second allocation that rebuilt both limited banks and re-ran them with the repaired row_oracle, 329 s at 550 cells and 636 s at 2000 cells; their boundary clause was then recomputed against designed_contact from those archived states on the CPU, with no further allocation. At 5000 cells the matrix alone is 7.2 GB and the build was projected at about five times the 626 s wall, which did not fit the allocation; that row is reported unrun rather than dropped.
