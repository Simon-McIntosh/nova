Why the slow lanes of the batched bank solve take several times the median step count

Every lane of the sixteen-lane batch is within two orders of the tolerance after two Newton steps. The remaining 2 to 26 steps are not Newton convergence at all: once the fresh residual reaches the freeze threshold the active set locks, and from then on the residual and the update sit on a two-value ladder, alternating between 1.6e-7 and 3.2e-10. The iteration is a period-2 cycle that exits only when an iterate happens to land on the low level and clear 1e-10 on the same step. The lane step count is therefore the length of a random walk on a two-element set, which is why it moves between runs.

Does the count repeat?

Two solve_batch calls inside one allocation are bit-identical: the per-lane step counts match exactly and the sixteen terminal residuals compare equal byte for byte, in both allocations that ran the pair. Across allocations the count is not stable. The same revision, seed and call gave [5,14,7,11,18,9,5,12,12,11,6,9,5,6,5,11] (max 18, median 6) in job 1306031 and [5,7,9,10,26,12,10,4,6,28,6,17,12,5,5,13] (max 28, median 9.5) in job 1306065. Both are internally repeatable, both converge, and every lane certifies; only the count moves. A slowest-lane figure is therefore a draw from a distribution, not a property of the seed, and the batch wall follows it.

The mechanism

The first two iterates are a Newton solve: the residual falls 1.2e-1 to 3.9e-4 to 1.6e-7, roughly 2.4 decades per step, in every lane. At iterate 2 the fresh residual is below the freeze threshold, so start_freeze holds, the discrete decision — the clipped cut-cell membership, the normal-form set and the selected fragment — is copied into cut_set, normal_set and locked_fragments, and every later step is taken with that frozen decision while its residual is measured on the fresh read.

From that iterate the residual and the update take only two values, and they alternate. Lane 10's update is 2.19e-7 on the high level and 3.19e-10 on the low one, and its residual is 1.6e-7 and 3.2e-10 on the same iterates; lane 5 shows the same two numbers. The values agree to three significant figures across lanes, which is the signature of a fixed geometric feature rather than of arithmetic: a clipped cell whose edge interval is about 2.2e-7 wide in the update unit. The state moves to one end of that interval and the next Newton step, built from the frozen decision, moves it back. The correction is not shrinking toward a root — it is a constant pair, so the iteration is a period-2 cycle, not a slow contraction. Because the cycle is driven by the continuous read (the clipped cells' edge intervals and fractional membership) while the lock freezes only the discrete decision, selection_changed never fires: the cut set, normal-form set and fragment are identical at both ends of the cycle. So drift stays false, the lock is never released, and the driver's own unfreeze counter is zero in all sixteen lanes.

Acceptance is the only exit, and it needs a fresh residual at or below 1e-10 on the same iterate as no drift. The low level of the ladder is 3.2e-10, above the tolerance, so a lane is accepted only when an iterate lands below both. Whether that happens on the fourth step or the twenty-eighth is a coin flip per cycle, which is a geometric waiting time with a long tail — lane 10 waited 26 cycles. That tail is the whole of the difference between the median and the slowest lane, and it is also why the count moved from a maximum of 18 to one of 28 between two allocations at the same revision.

The change in support_read.py that removes it

Release the lock when the fresh residual fails to contract, instead of only when the discrete selection changes. In solve_batch's advance, the unfreeze term is changed, which is frozen & selection_changed(...). The iteration's own contraction ratio is already computed one block later as ratio = current_norm / previous_norm, against the same contraction_limit that governs the factor reuse. Extending the release to

changed = frozen & (
    selection_changed(fresh.reading, cut_set, normal_set, locked_fragments)
    | ~jnp.isfinite(ratio) | (ratio > contraction_limit)
)

releases the decision at the first non-contracting iterate. In this trace that iterate is 3 for seven lanes and 4 for eight; lane 8 never stalls and needs no release. The next step then rebuilds its Jacobian from the fresh read and takes an unlocked Newton correction, which this trace measures at 2.4 to 2.5 decades per step.
