At sixteen slices one Newton step per iterate is almost the whole iterate: the tangent assembly inside it costs 1594 ms of a 1879 ms per-iterate stage sum, 85 %, and it grows 12.6 times from one slice (127 ms) while the whole read grows only 2.8 times. The stage sum does not close on the whole-solve wall: it overstates it by 89 % at one slice and 209 % at sixteen, and that residual is reported below rather than absorbed into a remainder stage.

Per-iterate stage wall at one and sixteen slices

One H200 (job 1306018, partition betelgeuse, reservation gpu_0003_grpA, one card, two cores) at revision 2a21f478f. Every stage is its own jitted call at the solve's shapes — one or sixteen lanes of the diverted 550-cell bank, each lane a per-lane perturbation of the same seed — warmed, timed with block_until_ready and reported as the median of 12 repeats. Nothing is timed inside the solve's while_loop: a timer there syncs the device and reports the barrier rather than the stage.

The read is split at the boundaries the product code has. Read census is stationary_stencil over the batch, the stationary-point solve that supplies the null and saddle census. Read geometry is the whole-mesh fit (nodal_jets and field_from_jets) plus the terminal _support_at_level call the read makes — the clipped edge intervals, the fragment areas and the fractional membership at the admitted level. Read decision is read_at_stationary_points less that terminal call, and so carries the cut-cell membership, the normal-form set, the curvature stencil, the wall events, the candidate loop and the fragment selection; it is a difference of two measured programs, not a separate program. Step residual is step_residual counted twice, once for the solve's own magnitude and once inside the Newton step. Step assembly is step_jacobian, factor is _equilibrate and lu_factor, and back-solve is lu_solve with the update's scaling and linear residual.

The whole solve is timed as the product runs it: solve_batch over the same states, 12 repeats, divided by the steps it took (3 at one slice, 15 at sixteen, the slowest lane's count).

The stage sum does not close on the whole-solve wall, so the 15 % closure requirement is not met. At one slice the sum is 197.55 ms against 104.27 ms per iterate, an overstatement of 89 % or 93.27 ms; at sixteen it is 1878.61 ms against 608.85 ms, an overstatement of 209 % or 1269.76 ms. The residual is not reported as a remainder stage, because it is not work the loop does: it is the difference between summing separate programs and running one fused program, and it has the sign that difference predicts.

Cross-check: the same code as the fused units the loop contains

The fused read costs 53.63 ms against the 54.54 ms the census, decision and geometry stages sum to, and 151.19 ms against 151.82 ms — so the read's split is faithful to within 1 % at both batch sizes, and nothing is lost to the separation there. The fused step costs 137.80 ms against the residual, assembly, factor and back-solve stages' own 143.00 ms at one slice and 1730.05 ms against 1726.79 ms at sixteen, again within 1 %. The split is therefore not what overstates the iterate. What the cross-check does not do is explain the residual. One fused iterate body — a read, a step, the candidate's read and the magnitude — costs 251.15 ms, which is 1.00 times the sum of its parts: no fusion benefit at all inside that one program. Yet the loop's own iterate is 104.27 ms — 84.5 ms in its steady state, measured a step at a time — against the 196.49 ms its three units cost separately, so the loop is 2.33 times cheaper than its own parts. Two readings survive this node: one compiled while_loop body shares work between the read and the step that a chain of separate jitted calls re-derives and re-transfers, or newton_step inside the loop is not doing all the work the standalone call does. The step arms rule out a third reading — that the loop hides one very expensive first iterate — because the marginal step is cheap too. This node does not choose between the two that remain, and it does not claim the first.

What one more step actually costs

A per-iterate wall taken as the whole solve divided by its step count assumes every iterate costs the same. Pinning the loop's step ceiling below and at convergence measures the cost of a step directly, as the difference between two arms run on the same seed (job 1305960, one lane, 12 repeats, revision 6889117f5). The receipts in this fragment ran at 2a21f478f and 6889117f5; every commit between them touches only scripts/, so the product code measured is identical.

One iteration costs 144.6 ms and each further step adds 84.5 ms, so the loop holds one moderately dearer first iterate and a steady state of 84.5 ms; the per-iterate walls above, taken as the whole solve over its step count, are that average. The 196.5 ms the same iterate's units cost as separate programs is 2.33 times the steady state, which is the closure residual stated as a ratio rather than as a sum, and the first iterate's 144.6 ms accounts for only 60.1 ms of it.

Two caveats on this receipt, both visible in its table. These arms run the batch of one lane at its unperturbed seed, which converges in 4 steps, so a ceiling set above it converges in the same 4 steps: the last row is a repeat of its predecessor rather than a longer run, and the two walls agree to 0.4 ms, which is a reproducibility check rather than a measurement. The stage receipt's one-lane batch is that seed scaled by 1 + 2e-5 and needs 3 steps, so the two receipts are not the same state; their per-step walls, 104.3 ms and 99.5 ms, agree to within a few per cent, which is how far the seed moves the cost of a step.
