What four reads imply

At sixteen slices, a read costs 27.703 ms of attributed device work and its fresh-residual certification costs 7.990 ms: 35.692 ms per read together. An update costs 8.119 ms for its residual, 25.483 ms for the Jacobian, 8.820 ms for GMRES and update work, and 2.444 ms for equilibration: 44.865 ms per update. These are means over the two fresh processes. The residual is split by compiler call scope: newton_step owns update evaluation; solve_batch.magnitude owns certification.

The observed three updates and four reads imply 4 × 35.692 + 3 × 44.865 ≈ 277.365 ms per batch, or 17.335 ms per slice. The two process subtotals are 277.267 and 277.463 ms. The target is 16 ms per sixteen-slice batch; this subtotal alone exceeds it by 261.365 ms, or 16.335 ms per slice. Even the four reads alone cost 142.769 ms per batch, or 8.923 ms per slice.

This is a lower accounting floor for the measured implementation, not an irreducible hardware bound or a latency prediction for a redesigned solver. It excludes 14.376 ms of other/shared device activity and copies, plus gaps. Device durations can overlap: subtracting all measured overlap from this subtotal gives the conservative floor 275.936 ms per batch, or 17.246 ms per slice. Profiling affects the execution; the observed unprofiled wall remains the 303.291–303.659 ms range above.
