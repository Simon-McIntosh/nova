bank-step-batch — batching the warm step does not help it, because the vmapped booking tangent costs four times the unbatched stage

The warm bank Newton step was run over independent slices in one compiled program at the diverted 550-cell certificate state on one H200. The batched program is support_read.step_batch: it reads the batch through read_batch, which forms one rebuild decision for the whole batch outside the map, and applies the step as a vmap over the lanes. Each lane is a distinct state, a small multiple away from the converged moments, so a batch that broadcast one lane across the axis would not reproduce the single-lane result.

The headline is negative: batching does not help the step as built. The batched read does fall with batch size, from 53.81 to 12.87 ms per slice between one and sixteen slices, because the read's per-call launches are shared. The booking tangent and the assembly it feeds do not fall at all — 108.97 and 110.55 ms per slice at sixty-four slices against 121.24 and 125.32 at one — and the unbatched booking tangent at the same state costs 27.87 ms. The vmapped form is therefore four times dearer per lane than the stage it replaces, at every batch width.

What vmap made of the booking tangent

The tangent was profiled at four lanes on one H200, and — separately — at one lane with no batch map at all, so the two kernel sets come from traces taken at the same state.

Nothing changed hands. The five largest kernels are the same five in both columns — no sort appeared, no cond became a select, no scatter replaced a gather, and no kernel was dropped or merged. What vmap changed is how many times each one runs: the unbatched tangent launches each fusion twice per call, the batched tangent launches the same fusion nine times, and each of those launches processes the lane axis as real data. So the batched form pays 4.5 times the launches and 4.5 times the work per kernel for each lane it adds: every row of the batched column is 4.5 to 4.6 times its unbatched counterpart per lane, except input_reduce_fusion_2 at 2.8.

The mechanism is the channel axis. _local_booking_sensitivities builds a direction basis of samples + 8 channels with jnp.eye, jnp.minimum and jnp.clip, and under vmap the batch dimension is pushed under that basis, so the fusions carry a channels × lanes × cells × samples array. XLA unrolls the channel axis into repeated loop bodies (loop_concatenate_fusion is that gather chain, executed nine times a call) and each body now also spans the lanes. That last fact fixes the same arithmetic and only the lane axis grows, so the per-lane cost does not fall with the batch — it is at best flat, measured here at 121.24 ms per slice for one lane of the batched program against 27.87 ms for the unbatched stage the launch study measured.

This node does not attempt a fix. The direction for a later one is to stop mapping the batch under the channel basis and instead hoist the basis out of the map, or to batch over cells rather than over slices.

1.6442e-7 is a plateau the step passes, not a residual floor

An earlier reading of the convergence arm called 1.6442e-7 a residual floor and concluded that 1e-10 was below what this operator resolves. That reading is withdrawn. It came from lanes that oscillate, not from an operator that cannot resolve, and the same diverted 550-cell state has reached 7.97e-11 and 4.16e-11 in landed evidence. The runs below were taken at that state, on one H200, from this harness: the unbatched support_read.newton_step, the batched step at one lane, and the batched step at four lanes, each tabling its largest fresh-read residual per Newton iteration.

Every lane reaches the bound by iteration 3, and iterations 3 and 4 hold all four lanes below 1e-9. The failure is what happens next: lanes that sat at 3.19e-10 return to 1.6442e-7 on the following iteration while other lanes recover, so the lanes swap places in pairs and the batch never satisfies a whole-batch bound. That is a two-cycle in the batched step, not a residual floor: the same values are reached and left again, and the unbatched and one-lane solves leave them behind in a single iteration.

A one-lane batch tracks the unbatched solve, so step_batch's per-lane arithmetic is not wrong at batch size one. At four lanes the lanes interfere, and the place to look is the one thing they share: the single batch-uniform rebuild decision that read_batch takes outside the map, which rebuilds all four stencils or none. A node that resolves the two-cycle should start there.

What the factor actually resolves

The condition estimate was measured, not inferred, at the converged 550-cell state through newton_step(..., condition_diagnostics=True). jnp.linalg.cond(factor.matrix) on the assembled dense matrix gives 1.4967e15, and jnp.linalg.cond(matrix * column / row) on the row- and column-equilibrated matrix that is the one actually factored gives 1.7097e4. The LU factors the equilibrated matrix, so 1.71e4 is the conditioning that governs the solve; the raw figure sits at the double-precision limit and is not the number that sets the resolution. At 1.71e4 a double-precision solve resolves a relative residual near 1.7e4 × 2.2e-16 ≈ 3.8e-12, which is the magnitude of the residuals measured here, 9.52e-12 and 2.05e-11. Nothing in this estimate supports a floor at 1.6442e-7, and the claim that 1e-10 was below the operator's resolution is withdrawn.

The gate, and what it did not establish

tests/test_bank_step_batch.py compares two lanes of a batched solve against the same two states solved alone at a 64-cell state, and stops each solve when its fresh residual falls to 1e-10. At revision 80b39671a the batched clause converges, at 3.91e-11 and 1.84e-11, and the comparison then fails because the single-lane reference solve for lane 0 returns nan. The gate is red for a defect in the reference arm rather than in the batched one, and it is reported as red, not as passed.

The branch clause fails on the device, which is a finding against read_batch. With lane 1's tracked cell set stale the batch returns rebuilt=[False, True] and rebuilds=[1, 2]: it rebuilt exactly the stale lane, which is the per-lane behaviour read_batch's own docstring says the batch-uniform lax.cond exists to prevent. The clause is not answered by this node's code, whatever the docstring states.

The CPU arm runs at two lanes. The certificate fixture's jet build is what did not fit a CPU allocation; it is published once to the shared operand cache, 223 nodes and 2.0 MB of matrix, and every later process loads it, so the gate's own memory is the 64-cell state rather than the source integration. The two-lane CPU run completed at job 1303739 in 14:23, logged in waverun/cpu-final.log.

The declared negative control, the batch-uniform read decision moved inside the map, was run and its log is waverun/regate.log: the mutant yields two failures where the shipped tree yields one, and under the mutation the batched clause returns [nan, 2.17e-07], so the mutation does take that clause red. Because the shipped gate is already red on the nan reference arm, that red is weaker evidence than a clean pass would give, and this node reports it as such rather than as a clean red.
