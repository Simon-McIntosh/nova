bank-step-batch — batching the warm step does not help it, because the vmapped booking tangent costs four times the unbatched stage

The warm bank Newton step was run over independent slices in one compiled program at the diverted 550-cell certificate state on one H200. The batched program is support_read.step_batch: it reads the batch through read_batch, which forms one rebuild decision for the whole batch outside the map, and applies the step as a vmap over the lanes. Each lane is a distinct state, a small multiple away from the converged moments, so a batch that broadcast one lane across the axis would not reproduce the single-lane result.

The headline is negative: batching does not help the step as built. The batched read does fall with batch size, from 53.81 to 12.87 ms per slice between one and sixteen slices, because the read's per-call launches are shared. The booking tangent and the assembly it feeds do not fall at all — 108.97 and 110.55 ms per slice at sixty-four slices against 121.24 and 125.32 at one — and the unbatched booking tangent at the same state costs 27.87 ms. The vmapped form is therefore four times dearer per lane than the stage it replaces, at every batch width.

Nothing changed hands. The five largest kernels are the same five in both columns — no sort appeared, no cond became a select, no scatter replaced a gather, and no kernel was dropped or merged. What vmap changed is how many times each one runs: the unbatched tangent launches each fusion twice per call, the batched tangent launches the same fusion nine times, and each of those launches processes the lane axis as real data. So the batched form pays 4.5 times the launches and 4.5 times the work per kernel for each lane it adds: every row of the batched column is 4.5 to 4.6 times its unbatched counterpart per lane, except input_reduce_fusion_2 at 2.8.

The mechanism is the channel axis. _local_booking_sensitivities builds a direction basis of samples + 8 channels with jnp.eye, jnp.minimum and jnp.clip, and under vmap the batch dimension is pushed under that basis, so the fusions carry a channels × lanes × cells × samples array. XLA unrolls the channel axis into repeated loop bodies (loop_concatenate_fusion is that gather chain, executed nine times a call) and each body now also spans the lanes. That last fact fixes the same arithmetic and only the lane axis grows, so the per-lane cost does not fall with the batch — it is at best flat, measured here at 121.24 ms per slice for one lane of the batched program against 27.87 ms for the unbatched stage the launch study measured.

This node does not attempt a fix. The direction for a later one is to stop mapping the batch under the channel basis and instead hoist the basis out of the map, or to batch over cells rather than over slices.

Freezing a converged lane

step_batch now takes an optional per-lane converged flag and zeroes a flagged lane's update, so a lane that has met the residual bound holds the state that satisfied it while the other lanes continue. The driver's loop also tests the bound before stepping rather than after: the earlier arm read the bound from the pre-step state and then took one more step off it, which is why a lane that had reached 9.5e-12 was reported at 1.64e-7 at exit.

What the factor actually resolves

The condition estimate was measured, not inferred, at the converged 550-cell state through newton_step(..., condition_diagnostics=True). jnp.linalg.cond(factor.matrix) on the assembled dense matrix gives 1.4967e15, and jnp.linalg.cond(matrix * column / row) on the row- and column-equilibrated matrix that is the one actually factored gives 1.7097e4. The LU factors the equilibrated matrix, so 1.71e4 is the conditioning that governs the solve; the raw figure sits at the conditioning limit and is not the number that sets the resolution. At 1.71e4 a double-precision solve resolves a relative residual near 1.7e4 × 2.2e-16 ≈ 3.8e-12, the magnitude actually reached (2.01e-11, 2.74e-11, 9.52e-12). No floor at 1.6442e-7 is supported.

The rebuild decision, and what it was doing

read_batch took its branch on one batch-uniform flag but reported a per-lane verdict: a batch with one stale lane returned rebuilt=[False, True] and rebuilds=[1, 2], the exact per-lane behaviour its docstring says the batch-uniform lax.cond exists to prevent. The decision is now computed once as a scalar that the branch, the reported flag and the counter all read, so no lane can carry a verdict of its own. The clause in tests/test_bank_step_batch.py requires a batch with one stale lane to report the rebuild on every lane and to advance every counter by exactly one.

The gate

The gate now runs at the diverted 550-cell certificate state, not at 64 cells. At 64 cells the fixture's converged moments are not a fixed point of that mesh's operator: the diagnostic arm measured a joint residual of 13.4 at iteration 0 there, with every stage finite — the read's stationary positions, the booking, the residual and the update all finite — and the iteration then walked away from the state rather than toward it, which is what ended in the NaN the record previously reported. That is a fixture defect, reported as such; the solver was never returning a non-finite value from a finite reading.

With the freeze in place the batched clause converges at 550 cells. The re-run under the restored bound (job 1303787) printed batched residuals: [1.3358e-11, 1.8943e-11] after 25 steps, both lanes at or below the 1e-10 bound, and the branch clause passed. The batch is not faster to converge than the single solve — the operator alternation of the section above is what sets the step count — but it now stops moving on a lane that has met the bound, which is the property the clause asserts.

The gate is red at delivery, and on the comparison rather than on a residual. The convergence clause failed its confluent-flux check at rtol=1e-10, atol=0: lane 0's vertical flux disagreed in 7 of 550 cells, and every one of those cells is zero to round-off — the two solves returned 1e-30 and 1e-26 where a relative tolerance divides by nothing. The largest absolute disagreement was 1.7e-13, which is the scale on which the unbatched reference's own residual alternation moves the flux. The comparison was given atol=1e-12 with the relative tolerance unchanged (commit 6a3d7084a) and re-run as job 1303826. That run converged both lanes ([2.9701e-11, 7.2660e-11] after 22 steps) and still failed, now on a single density-flux cell: 4.9494e-05 against 4.9494e-05, an absolute difference of 3.2e-10 and a relative one of 6.4e-6. The measured agreement between a batched lane and that lane's own solve is therefore 3.2e-10 absolute on a 5e-5-scale component, and rtol=1e-10 was never earned by the measurement — it was asserted. The tolerance is left as measured rather than widened at the last minute, so the gate stays red and the number behind the red is the number above.

The uniform rebuild decision costs nothing measurable at the read. The sixteen-slice read re-measured at the repaired revision is 12.90 ms per slice against 12.87 ms before it (job 1303785, waverun/h200-stage.log), and the one- and four-slice reads reproduce at 53.80 and 20.32 ms. The timing headline stands unchanged.

The negative control was re-run against the repaired code in the same job. Its mutation moves the batch-uniform read decision inside the vmap, and the mutated batch takes the convergence clause red: NEG_EXIT=1, with lane 0's radial flux disagreeing with that lane's own solve in 75 of 550 cells at a maximum relative difference of 5.2e-8 and an absolute one of 2.0e-8 — four orders above the 1e-12 floor the comparison now carries, so the tolerance change does not weaken the control. The batch still converged under the mutation (1.97e-11 and 4.53e-11 in 13 steps), which is the point: the mutation is caught by the flux, not by failure to converge. Its log is waverun/regate.log (job 1303787), which carries the gate result and the mutation arm in one stream.

At sixteen lanes the batch does not meet the bound in the window it was given. The four-lane series converges at 20 steps, but the sixteen-lane run reached iteration 12 inside a thirty-minute allocation and the job was cancelled by the wall clock before it could print an exit line (waverun/trace.log, job 1303790). Nine of the sixteen lanes were frozen at or below the bound by then — 4.07e-11, 4.51e-11, 1.93e-11, 3.66e-11 and 6.26e-11 appear unchanged from iteration 7 through 12 — while seven lanes were still cycling at the operator's 1.64e-7/3.19e-10 plateau. The freeze works lane by lane; what sixteen lanes do not get is enough iterations to carry all of them through the plateau, because stepping sixteen lanes of a 550-cell bank is what spends the allocation. The claim this delivery supports is the four-lane one; the sixteen-lane convergence is not measured here.
