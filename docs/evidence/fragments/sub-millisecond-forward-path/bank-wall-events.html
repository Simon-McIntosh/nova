Read-count variation and normalized cost

Reads vary between fresh head processes as well as between arms. Base processes report 8 and 8 reads per lane; head processes report 7 and 8. Per-lane Newton updates change between fresh processes of both arms. Within each process, both uninstrumented timed calls have identical counters and terminal-state bytes. Each diagnostic solve preserves its corresponding uninstrumented update/read counters, and its independently replayed wall outputs match the captured outputs bit for bit.

The runtime-input fingerprints locate a difference before Newton iteration: across base and head, only the three initial current-moment arrays differ; the operator bank, cache, seeds and previous-read state match. Between the two fresh processes of either arm, only the initial cell-current array differs. At the first read, before Newton updates, the two paired processes already differ in Bell coefficients by up to 1.05346e-12, wall coordinates by 2.58682e-14 m and wall values by 6.10623e-16 in native flux units. Across common read ordinals, the largest coordinate/value gaps are 3.30846e-14 m / 2.12677e-15. All nonfinite masks agree.

Both wall implementations, independently compiled and evaluated on each identical captured field, produce identical bits for all 496 lane/read states: point and value differences, mask mismatches and bit-difference counts are all zero. The same comparison instrument detects the nonzero cross-arm gaps tabulated above. Thus the premise needed for identical Newton paths was absent: independently built fixtures did not supply bit-identical starting states. The measurements reproduce process-dependent trajectories and read counts without changing the solve, and expose small perturbations near the unchanged 1e-10 stopping threshold. They localize the variability to fixture current-moment construction; they do not isolate the individual GPU primitive responsible, or prove that this alone caused each extra read in the original pair.

Using the mean of each process’s two unprofiled timed calls, whole-solve wall divided by reads falls from 251.283 to 214.578 ms/read (14.61%) in the first pair and from 272.030 to 201.314 ms/read (26.00%) in the second. The second pair has eight reads in both arms and whole-solve means of 2176.237 and 1610.512 ms, a 565.725 ms saving. This normalization includes factorization and update work, whose per-lane counts remain visible; it is not an isolated read-stage timer or a forced identical-trajectory comparison. For the original seven-versus-nine-read pair, the same division gives 265.974 versus 207.123 ms/read (22.13% lower), while its directly measured whole solve still did not improve.
