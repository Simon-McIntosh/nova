bank-warm-start — reads and updates of a warm-started bank slice

Measures §4's warm start on the 550-cell diverted certificate: sixteen lanes carried through eight slices at three stated profile-amplitude steps, every slice seeded from its stream's previous converged state through support_read.solve_batch unchanged. One H200, allocation 1307763, three processes — a builder that converges slice 0 and saves the stream arrays, then two fresh interpreters on a warm compilation cache that load those exact arrays.

What a slice is driven by

The bank solve's runtime input is the profile: one RotatingDomainProfile travels with the call while the operator bank and geometry are fixed operands. A slice's drive is that profile's amplitude — p_prime and ff_prime scaled together, which at a constant-gradient source is the plasma-current knob. The three steps are relative amplitude changes per slice, chosen against the certificate's ~1 MA plasma on a 1 ms slice boundary:

stepphysical reading
1e-5slow flattop slice, about 10 A of plasma current
1e-3fast ramp slice, about 1 kA of plasma current
1e-2deliberately coarse time grid, about 10 kA of plasma current

The batch carries one drive, not sixteen. solve_batch takes a single profile and a single operator bank for the whole call, so sixteen lanes of one batched solve are sixteen distinct cold seeds on one shared ramp — not sixteen independently driven streams. A batch of shots that differ in drive is not expressible through this entry point today; that is a property of the entry point, not of this measurement.

Does a warm slice reach one update?

No. At the slowest step a warm-started slice takes two updates and three reads — the seed read, one trial read and the terminal certificate — and 215 ms of the batch's wall, against 304 ms cold. A slice never converged in one update in any of the 21 warm slices measured at the three steps, and never in zero. The seed's own fresh residual, before any update, is 1.8e-3 at a 1e-5 step and 1.8e-1 at 1e-3: the warm start buys about two orders of magnitude of residual, not a converged state.

At a 1e-3 step the slice needs 3–6 updates and 4–12 reads, and at a 1e-2 step the solve stalls outright. Five of the seven coarse-step slices (1, 4, 5, 6 and 7) run every lane to the 60-update ceiling and finish with no lane converged. Three of those five end with the residual exactly where their seed left it (slices 5, 6 and 7, at 1.73, 3.46 and 5.20): the search takes no step at all, which is the non-smooth residual plateau the driver documents, now reached from a warm seed. Slice 1 ends above its seed (1.80 to 2.24) and slice 4 ends below it but far from convergence (1.73 to 0.242). The remaining two coarse slices fail on one lane each or none: slice 2 converges 15 of 16 lanes, and slice 3 is the only coarse slice that converges fully (9 updates, residual 6.0e-12).

Where the 1 ms gap is. One warm batch read costs about 72 ms here (215 ms over three reads; 76 ms per read cold), which is 360× §1's 0.2 ms per-slice read bound. The step count is not the binding term at a realistic slice step: even the three-read slice is 215 ms, so the target cannot be reached by converging faster — it needs the read's own wall to fall by the same factor. At the coarse step both terms fail together, and no read count helps.

Precision against the analytic oracle, and what the oracle can and cannot say

The terminal fresh residual meets the locked residual clause on every slice that converged: 2.6e-12 cold, at most 2.9e-11 at a 1e-5 step and 6.1e-11 at 1e-3, all at or under the 1e-10 budget.

The geometry clause needs an oracle solve of each slice's own drive, and the cheap construction of one — the unit-drive analytic solution scaled by the slice's amplitude — is refuted by measurement. A cold solve at amplitude 1.07 does not land on 1.07× the unit-drive state: it runs to 60 updates and 436 reads and ends 95% away in relative moment, with a fresh residual of 17.4, because the drive scale also moves the equilibrium rather than merely scaling it. The fixed analytic axis and X-point are therefore not the solution of a scaled drive, and the axis drift this run measures against them — 5.9e-5 of the minor radius at a 1e-5 step, 5.1e-3 at 1e-3, on a budget of 2.7e-4 m at a minor radius of 0.544 m — is a measured drift of the equilibrium, not an accuracy verdict on the solve.

What this node can and cannot certify, stated plainly: 15 of the 21 warm slices converge in every lane and pass the residual clause; the other six carry at least one unconverged lane and are counted as failures of the clause, with terminal residuals of 2.24, 1.80, 0.242, 1.73, 3.46 and 5.20. The geometry and boundary-flux clauses are not certified for a scaled drive, because the analytic oracle at that drive was not constructed. The slow-step drift sits inside the budget (5.9e-5 against 2.7e-4) and the fast-step drift does not, and the difference tracks the accumulated amplitude as the refuted scaling law predicts.
