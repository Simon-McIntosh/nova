A carried cell is reused only on the geometry its decision was read on

The carried-read reuse mask certified a cell from its signed coefficients alone. A cell whose carrier changed while the same coefficients were retained was reused with its stale fragments, so the reuse decision named a polynomial and never named the polygon it was evaluated on. The topology read now records the carrier it was built from (cell_vertices, cell_vertex_count, cell_centre, cell_pitch), and _fragment_reuse_mask adds _same_carrier_geometry to its conjunction: a cell is reusable only while its live vertices, vertex count, centre and pitch all match the read's record, whatever its coefficients say.

The moved-geometry case names a move that provably moves its cell

The first version of the moved-geometry case fixed one displacement per field — for vertices, a 1e-2 * pitch shift of slot 0 — and asserted that the moved cell's reference fragment differed from the stale one. An independent landing measure showed that assertion is not a control: on the fixture it selected, cell 8's vertex shift moved no fragment at all, so the test could pass only by accident of the fixture and failed outright in a fresh process (0.0 > 0.0). A displacement that does not move the cell cannot demonstrate that a geometry change refreshes it.

The case now chooses its move by measurement. For the field under test it enumerates candidates — every live vertex at four displacement scales, or four scales of centre offset, or three scales of pitch scaling — and evaluates each candidate's effect with the same quadratic_cell_fragments decomposition the mask itself uses, on a cut conic cell of the borrowed decision. It keeps the first candidate whose fragment area changes by more than 1e-6 of that cell's full area, asserts the move's provenance before the refresh runs, then requires that exactly that one cell refreshes and that the masked result equals a forced full refresh. A fixture that supports no such move fails loudly rather than passing quietly.
