tracked-read-h200 — one cell rule, the H200 wall and the trial-state error

The cell selection has one owner

support_read.stationary_cells re-implemented the nearest-generator cell selection that fixed_points._CachedField._location already owned, so the cell a tracked read recorded and the cell the cell-local Bell fit evaluated in were two rules that could drift apart. The rule now lives once, in fixed_points.nearest_cell, vectorised over any leading position dimensions: a single point returns one index and a batch of points positions returns points indices. _location, BellField.value and the tracked read all call it, and stationary_cells is deleted. tests/test_tracked_read.py passes 4 of 4 over the change.

Which stage carries the trial-state error

Every reference is a direct Newton solve on the analytic flux of the certificate state. A second solve runs on the field the read differentiates — the Bell interpolant over the nodal jets it contracts — so the distance from the read to that field is the solve's own error and the distance from that field to the analytic extremum is the derivative source's.

The warm wall on the H200

One job on 98dci4-gpu-0003, an NVIDIA H200 NVL, at the 550-cell diverted state. The bank is loaded through support_read.read_bank from /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/nova-assembled-step-bank (digest sha256:6ebe02e0…, 1,741 nodes, 162.2 MB), so nothing is re-derived from source jets. Every row is the minimum of five warm repeats of one compiled step:

The steady read is 1.225 ms, six times the section's 0.2 ms bound. The launches table says where they go: the stationary solve is 55 launches and 0.634 ms, and the remaining 750 launches cost the other 0.591 ms. Those are not the fit — a steady step pays no whole-mesh fit — but the whole-mesh containment scan topology._inside_cells evaluated at each tracked point, the Hessian eigenvalues that classify it, and the tracking predicate. Even a perfect fusion of the remainder leaves the solve's own 0.634 ms, three times the bound, so the bound is not reachable by fusing the read: the warm-started solve has to get cheaper, which is §3's subject.

The rebuild row is reported as measured but the instrument is suspect: 35.65 ms against 1.649 ms reported for the same call on a P100 is twenty-two times slower on the faster device, where the steady row is only 1.25 times faster. A number that moves the wrong way by an order of magnitude is a claim about the instrument before it is a claim about the code, so this row is not used to price a rebuild step.
