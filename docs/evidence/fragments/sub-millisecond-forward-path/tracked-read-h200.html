tracked-read-h200 — one cell rule, the H200 wall and the trial-state error

The cell selection has one owner

support_read.stationary_cells re-implemented the nearest-generator cell selection that fixed_points._CachedField._location already owned, so the cell a tracked read recorded and the cell the cell-local Bell fit evaluated in were two rules that could drift apart. The rule now lives once, in fixed_points.nearest_cell, vectorised over any leading position dimensions: a single point returns one index and a batch of points positions returns points indices. _location, BellField.value and the tracked read all call it, and stationary_cells is deleted. tests/test_tracked_read.py passes 4 of 4 over the change.

Two clauses keep it one owner. The first refuses a second nearest-generator rule: support_read must carry no stationary_cells, and both support_read.read and fixed_points._CachedField._location must reach the shared rule. The second is behavioural, because a source check cannot see two correct implementations that disagree at a boundary: it takes the two generators nearest each other in the mesh, puts a point exactly between them - on the surface where two cell choices are equidistant - and requires the read's cell and the field's cell to be the same index. Two owners drift at exactly the points this one is chosen from.

Which stage carries the trial-state error

Every reference is a direct solve on the analytic flux of the certificate state, and none of them passes through the read it judges. The axis and the diverted boundary are Newton solves on the analytic flux seeded from the certificate; the limited boundary is the wall point where that flux is extremal on the plasma side, found by sweeping the wall polygon and refining the segment with a ternary search; the flux span is the difference of those two analytic values. An earlier version of this row compared the read against topology.read on the analytic field, which is the same read, so the reference decided its own clause; that is what the numbers below replace.

Each state is measured twice: on the moments it books (the trial state books the sampled field, the converged state books the analytic one), and on the analytic field substituted for those moments. A third column is the distance from the read's own derivative source - the cell-local Bell interpolant of the booked nodal jets, solved directly - to the analytic extremum, which is the interpolant's own share.

The booked field carries the miss, and the substitution shows it. Hand the read the analytic field's moments and both trial states land inside every axis budget: the diverted X-point at 3.88e-6 m against 6.81e-4 and the limited axis at 8.40e-4 m against 2.00e-3. Hand it the trial booking and the diverted X-point goes to 8.82e-4 m and the limited axis to 4.12e-3 m. The interpolant is not the carrier on the diverted state - solved directly on the analytic moments it sits 3.88e-6 m from the analytic axis - while on the limited state it is already at 8.40e-4 m, inside the 2.00e-3 budget but the larger of the two shares. What moves either state out of budget is the column of booked moments, which is the booking's error, upstream of the read.

The solve is not the carrier either, and the iteration count settles that: every point terminates with status=0 on a position tolerance of 1e-10 of the pitch, so where the solve stops is a stationary point of the field it is handed, not a Newton that ran out. Widening the iteration budget cannot move these numbers, and a converged Newton solve has forgotten its seed.

At the converged state the same instrument lands at 3.88e-6 m on the diverted axis and 8.40e-4 m on the limited one with the boundary flux inside 1.5e-5 and 8.3e-5 of the span, so the read reproduces the analytic state it is handed. The gate runs as tests/test_tracked_read_accuracy.py over the four states, with the two cell-owner clauses, and reports 3 failed, 8 passed: the limited trial axis at 4.12e-3 m against 2.00e-3, the diverted trial X-point at 8.82e-4 against 6.81e-4, and the diverted trial boundary flux at 2.21e-3 of the span against 1e-4. Both converged states pass every clause. Those are the recorded defect reproduced against a reference the read does not decide, and they are left failing rather than relaxed: the budgets are the section's, and a test that stopped asserting them would report a read that meets them.

The gate is controlled. The declared mutation averages the Bell fit across the three nodes of the triangle a point occupies instead of contracting each node's own jets, applied to an exported copy of this revision and run on the same device: it reports 6 failed, 2 passed in 419 s, and the ids it adds to the three above are both converged cases, which the head passes. A mutation that no clause catches would leave this gate's failures indistinguishable from a gate that is simply too strict.

The shared store publishes the diverted certificate's bank only (receipt.json reads "kind": "diverted"), so binding the limited case to it fed that case another state's field and the read reported invalid with a boundary-flux error of 259 in span units. A case the store does not hold is built from its own rows; the numbers here are each certificate measured on the field it books.

The warm wall on the H200

One job on 98dci4-gpu-0003, an NVIDIA H200 NVL, at the 550-cell diverted state. The bank is loaded through support_read.read_bank from /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/nova-assembled-step-bank (digest sha256:6ebe02e0…, 1,741 nodes, 162.2 MB), so nothing is re-derived from source jets. Two further jobs on the same device repeated the table at the head: the steady read came back at 1.237 ms and 1.268 ms with 805 launches, so the steady figure is stable to about 2 %, while the solve-alone row moved between 0.594 ms and 0.817 ms across the three runs. The bound is missed by every reading and the conclusion below does not turn on which; the spread is stated so the solve figure is not read as tighter than it is.

The steady read is 1.225-1.268 ms across three head revisions, six times the section's 0.2 ms bound. The launches table says where they go: the stationary solve is 55 launches and 0.634 ms, and the remaining 750 launches carry the other 0.591 ms, over 21,000 of the 24,178 HLO instructions. The remainder is not the fit — a steady step pays no whole-mesh fit — and the stages it contains are read from the code rather than timed separately: the whole-mesh containment scan topology._inside_cells evaluated at each tracked point, which tests every cell's edges for each point, the Hessian eigenvalues that classify it, and the tracking predicate. Even a perfect fusion of all of it leaves the solve's own 0.634 ms, more than three times the bound, so the bound is not reachable by fusing the read: the warm-started solve has to get cheaper, and that is §3's subject. The stage split is left as measured for the solve and structural for the remainder, which is stated rather than presented as a measurement it is not.

The rebuild row is reported as measured but the instrument is suspect: 35.65 ms against 1.649 ms reported for the same call on a P100 is twenty-two times slower on the faster device, where the steady row is only 1.25 times faster. A number that moves the wrong way by an order of magnitude is a claim about the instrument before it is a claim about the code, so this row is not used to price a rebuild step.
