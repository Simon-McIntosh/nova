tracked-read-steady-batch — the batch carries the steady branch, and what it costs per slice

The batched read was measured at 54.61 ms per slice at one slice and 5.82 ms at sixty-four, against 1.27 ms for the same read called once with its tracking carried, and the previous record left the cause open between the batch axis and the rebuild conditional. The tracking state does reach the batch, and no lane asks for a rebuild: the per-lane decision reads false for every lane at every batch size. What the batch paid was not the branch it took but where the decision was made. A jax.vmap of support_read.read carries the read's lax.cond inside the mapped function, and the cost barely moves when the conditional is removed altogether: the arm with the decision carried and no branch at all costs 54.65 ms at one slice against 54.36 ms with the conditional present. support_read.read_batch forms the per-lane decision from batched stationary stencils and takes one lax.cond on whether any lane needs a rebuild, outside the map. The batch held the tracking state, and did not rebuild

The cause is settled at the state the bound is stated at rather than inferred from a wall. Every arm is handed the same stacked tracking state, one entry per lane, and the per-lane decision (support_read.rebuild_needed over the batched stencils) is printed for each batch size: at one, four, sixteen and sixty-four slices every lane reads false, so the tracking state does reach the batch and the batch's own decision agrees with it. A batch whose lanes hold the converged and the trial state of this certificate also reads false in every lane and takes no rebuild, because at this geometry the two states resolve the same cells. The printed flag is therefore a real decision and not a constant: the gate drives a lane whose cell is stale and requires the batch to take the reload branch.

What separated the arms was not which branch ran. With the decision carried and no branch in the program at all, the vmapped read still costs 54.65 ms at one slice, 0.5 % more than the arm that keeps the conditional. Placing the decision inside the mapped function is what the batch paid for, and taking it once outside the map is what removes it: the same flags, the same state, the same device, 54.65 ms against 2.07 ms.

The bound is met at sixty-four slices and missed by 15 % at sixteen

Sixteen slices read at 0.230 ms per slice, against the 0.2 ms bound; sixty-four read at 0.133 ms and meet it. The per-slice wall is a fixed per-call cost plus a marginal per-slice cost, and the four points give both: the marginal from four to sixty-four slices is (8.528 − 2.539) / 60 = 0.0998 ms per slice, and the fixed share is 2.539 − 4 × 0.0998 = 2.14 ms per call. At sixteen slices that fixed share is 2.14 / 16 = 0.134 ms per slice against 0.0998 ms of arithmetic, so the miss at sixteen is the per-call cost and not the work per lane. At sixty-four the fixed share falls to 0.033 ms and the same arithmetic gives 0.133 ms. Batching beyond sixty-four therefore approaches the 0.0998 ms arithmetic floor, which is two times the bound and the part no batch size removes.

The batched program's own floor is visible at one slice: 2.068 ms against 1.333 ms for the same state read once with its tracking carried, 0.73 ms of per-call cost that the single read does not pay, which the one lax.cond and the batch's extra dispatch are what is left of once the stationary stencil is counted.

Kernel launches and device time are not reported, and the zero is the instrument's rather than the path's. Each program was traced with jax.profiler.trace and the xplane read back with jax.profiler.ProfileData, and every arm at one slice — including the arm that takes 54 ms and therefore issues thousands of kernels — reported zero device events, which is the positive control for the instrument and the same failure the previous record saw at one slice. The wall series is what the attribution above is made from. Copy bytes are zero by construction: the timed region returns device arrays and nothing converts to host between its timestamps.

The gate

tests/test_tracked_read_steady_batch.py reads a batch of two distinct states, one per lane, through read_batch and compares each lane's stationary position, status, cells, boundary flux and verdict against the same state read once. It then carries the batch's tracking state and requires that no lane reports a rebuild and that the rebuild counter does not move, which is the clause a batch that lost the tracking state fails. A second clause gives one lane a stale cell and requires the batch to take the reload branch for all lanes and still match each state's single read, because the branch is taken once for the whole batch.

The declared mutation drops the tracking state from the batched call in a scratch copy of the test, so every lane rebuilds; the steady clause refuses it. At the shipped revision both tests pass in 548 s and the mutant fails in 545 s on the steady clause alone (test_batched_steady_read_equals_the_single_read_lane_by_lane), which the head passes, so the clause is what separates the two arms.
