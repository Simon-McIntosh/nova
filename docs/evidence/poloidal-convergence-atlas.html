Poloidal convergence atlas

Every committed frame of the MAST twelve-row bank and the five-frame DIII-D demonstration route as one poloidal scene: full-map flux contours, the governed wall, the magnetic axis, every qualified X-point with the out-of-vessel members distinguished rather than dropped, the chosen boundary beside the EFIT reference surface, and the shadowed wall segments drawn as shadow. Low and high plasma current, converged and non-converged, closed and missing boundaries, and the lower-centre-column anchor are all in the grid rather than curated away.

Grid and coverage

17 panels, two machines, two current levels, one grid. The corrected bank reads every committed row as diverted (each row agrees with EFIT on class), so the class axis carries the honest corrected reading; the four rows whose read returned no closed boundary are marked unclassified rather than hidden. MAST low current is 0.80 MA (low) (21978, 21983) and high current 0.93 MA (high) (21985, 21986, 21989, 22086); DIII-D low current is 1.02 to 1.28 MA and high 1.6 to 1.63 MA. One frame converged to machine precision (22086/43 mixed, residual 1.74e-14); 22086/43 pure did not (residual 4.79e-4, active set settled), and the rest are the honest non-converged states the sprint chased.

Showcased failures rather than a curated set. The non-converging frame (21986/46 pure, residual 5.9e-3), the no-closed-boundary frame (DIII-D 00000c4a7b:179, four such frames in the route), the centre-column-anchor frame (21983/35, wall anchor at R 0.28, Z -1.64), and the converged healthy reference (22086/43 mixed) are tagged in their captions. A frame from the reversed-current block, where every slice fails axis admission for campaign-sign reasons, is not in the committed poloidal corpus: no reversed-current flux raster exists in this tree, so it is recorded as a follow-on rather than fabricated.

Data, method and deviations

Panels are rendered from the committed operands of docs/figures/topology-visual-corroboration (MAST mast-topology-operands.npz at base revision e6a83802; DIII-D diiid-topology-operands.npz) plus the DIII-D source parquet's EFIT psirz raster, by benchmarks/poloidal_convergence_atlas.py. The render ran on the debug partition with a per-job TMPDIR; measured 0.593 s per frame mean and 8.34 s cold-start max over 17 panels. Machine-readable receipt: docs/figures/null-identification-authority/convergence-atlas/convergence-atlas.json. Renderer revision 52ca3c4e5a.

Two deviations from the painter set are deliberate and stated. First, nova.media.poloidal.draw_nulls is not used for the X-points because its containment filter drops out-of-vessel candidates, and showing that population is the atlas's purpose; the dedicated null painter here keeps every qualified candidate and draws the wall exterior with a distinct marker and a per-panel tally. Second, the production shadow rule is proposed & private_wall, and the committed store persists neither the per-wall-node private flux nor a private-flux raster on the MAST rows, so the dark shadow is drawn only where the committed domain labels carry private-flux cells and the broad light band is labelled as the saddle-height footprint. The qualified X set is recovered from the raw 30-slot candidate table by the visual proxy of lying within the operator's X-qualification radius of the admitted or EFIT saddle, because the committed store persists only the raw table and the single admitted selection.

Coverage limits and follow-ons

Two requested frames have no committed flux. The limited-class frame (the pre-correction reads of 21986/46 and 21989/55 and the relabelled limited corpus survive only as classifications, not flux rasters) and the reversed-current-block frame (shots 22475 to 22626) both require a solve that persists a flux raster, which is H200 relabel work outside this node's CPU budget. The atlas therefore covers one class (diverted) on two machines at two current levels and the four failure classes that the committed operands can draw; the limited-class and reversed-current panels are the recorded follow-ons.
