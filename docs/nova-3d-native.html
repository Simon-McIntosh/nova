§1 — Objective and substrate

Point the nova physics spine at a 3D-native implementation. The substrate already exists (user direction 2026-07-24): the unified Biot tier solves the x, y, z components of field and vector potential for true 3-D elements — arc, bow, line and beam kernels, validated by test_biotsavart_3d (39/39) and the analytic 3-D element tests — and the per-angle Python-level stacking in the arc/bow kernels is now vectorised (limit-point axes broadcast through scipy's incomplete elliptic functions; the zeta midpoint quadrature runs in grouped blocks — bow grid solves 2.2× faster, values within a couple ulp of the element-wise reference). What is missing is not capability but assembly: the collapsed, vectorised 3-D field predictors are not yet exposed as exact coupling operators between arbitrary 3-D conductors and 3-D observation loops, and their outputs are not yet folded back as toroidally averaged metrics for the axisymmetric reconstruction path.

§2 original analysis (superseded by the landed record)

Question (user, 2026-07-24): can 𝔍(α) = ∫₀^α arcsinh β₁(α′) dα′ (Urankar eq. 9b) be solved directly? Assessment: a classical closed form does not exist, and Urankar's "evades an analytical treatment" still stands — but the cost can be cut by an order of magnitude without giving up exactness guarantees.

  • Why the closed form fails. Integrating by parts removes the arcsinh: 𝔍(α) = α·arcsinh β₁(α) − ∫₀^α α′ (d/dα′ arcsinh β₁) dα′. The remaining integrand is algebraic — rational in cos φ, sin φ over the two radicals √(γ² + r² sin²φ) and √(γ² + B²(φ)) — and each unweighted term reduces to incomplete elliptic integrals F, E, Π by the standard u = cos φ substitutions. The obstruction is the explicit α′ weight: it produces integrals of incomplete elliptic integrals (∫ F(φ,k) dφ terms), which lie outside the Legendre family — elliptic-polylogarithm territory. Every other term of the Urankar Part III/V antiderivatives reduces; this one is genuinely transcendental in the classical special-function basis.
  • Option A — spectral quadrature (recommended first step). The integrand is smooth (analytic away from the conductor surface), so fixed-order Gauss–Legendre converges spectrally: 16–48 nodes replace the current ~500 midpoint panels per unit α at equal or better accuracy — a further ~10–30× on the zeta term, and fixed node count means fixed shapes, exactly what the JAX/vmap path wants (the existing JAX Zeta already uses fixed-count trapezoid; Gauss–Legendre strictly dominates it). Near-conductor targets (β₁ gradient spike) get a tanh-sinh or split-interval fallback. Gate: match the midpoint reference at ≤1e-12 relative across a scan of (rs/r, γ/r, α); re-baseline the greens ulp fingerprints to tolerance gates where they pin quadrature internals rather than physics.
  • Option B — by-parts hybrid. Evaluate the boundary term α·arcsinh β₁(α) exactly, reduce the elliptic-reducible pieces of the remainder to F/E/Π (already computed for the neighbouring terms), and quadrature only the small elliptic-polylog residue — a smoother, smaller integrand needing very few nodes. More derivation work; combine with A if profiling still shows zeta hot after A.
  • Option C — Chebyshev surrogate ("build the special function"). Nondimensionalised, ζ depends on three reduced variables (rs/r, γ/r, α); a precomputed Chebyshev tensor grid evaluates to ~1e-12 in a handful of fused multiply-adds, fully vectorised and jit-friendly. Best asymptotic cost; adds a table artifact + build step. Appropriate once the 3-D coupling operators make zeta the dominant remaining term.
  • Option D — harmonic expansions (cylindrical/toroidal harmonic series à la Walstrom/Selvaggi): analytic term-by-term integrals, excellent in the far zone, poor convergence near the conductor — useful as a far-field fast path only, not a replacement.

Done-when: a quadrature decision is locked; the chosen fast path lands with a tolerance-gated equivalence suite against the midpoint reference (and the greens corner fingerprints migrated from ulp-pinning to physics-tolerance gates); bow/arc solve cost re-measured and recorded.

§3 — Exact flux couplings: 3-D coils and 3-D loops

Expose the kernel tier as coupling operators: the exact mutual flux between any 3-D conductor (arc/bow/line/beam paths, finite section) and any 3-D observation loop, via the vector-potential line integral M = (1/I) ∮ A·dl over the loop path — reusing the already-solved (Ax, Ay, Az) predictors so no new physics enters, only a loop-path quadrature and the reduction machinery. Validation oracles: coaxial circular pairs against the Maxwell/Neumann analytic mutual inductance; the ITER sub-inductance goldens recovered as the axisymmetric special case; reciprocity (M_ij = M_ji) and translation/rotation invariance as property tests. This is the general replacement for axisymmetric-only inductance assembly and the basis for non-axisymmetric sensor modelling (saddle loops, partial Rogowskis).

Done-when: a coupling API (loop ↔ coil, coil ↔ coil) passes the analytic oracles at kernel-golden tolerances; reciprocity and invariance property tests green; axisymmetric limit reproduces the existing inductance matrix.

§4 — PF coil offsets and tilts

Rigid-body perturbations of nominally axisymmetric coils: each PF/CS coil takes an optional (Δx, Δy, Δz, tilt-axis, tilt-angle) transform applied to its source geometry before kernel evaluation — an axisymmetric coil becomes a general 3-D arc ensemble, which the kernels already integrate exactly. Couplings from §3 then give exact perturbed flux at sensors and loops, supporting error-field and as-installed studies with the metrology inputs norma produces. Batched evaluation (leading perturbation-ensemble axis, spine §5's vmap machinery) makes Monte-Carlo tolerance studies cheap.

Done-when: perturbed-coil couplings agree with the unperturbed path at zero offset/tilt to machine precision; first-order sensitivity against analytic small-displacement expansions for a circular loop; a batched perturbation ensemble evaluates through vmap with fixed shapes.

§5 — Toroidally averaged metrics for axisymmetric reconstruction

Fold the 3-D predictions back into the axisymmetric world: toroidal (n = 0) averages of the 3-D fields on the poloidal grid — averaged Green's functions, averaged sensor responses, and averaged flux couplings — supplied as drop-in replacements wherever the reconstruction stack consumes axisymmetric operators. A perturbed or genuinely 3-D coil set then supports an axisymmetric reconstruction with the 3-D content correctly averaged rather than ignored, and the n ≠ 0 residual is available as a diagnostic sideband.

Done-when: for an unperturbed axisymmetric machine the averaged operators equal the native axisymmetric ones to quadrature tolerance; for a tilted-coil case the averaged operator matches a brute-force dense-toroidal-sampling reference; the reconstruction seam accepts either operator source unchanged.

§ Decisions

Zeta fast path (§2): Gauss–Legendre spectral quadrature first (recommended — smallest change, fixed shapes for JAX, ~10–30× on the zeta term), the by-parts hybrid (more derivation, fewest nodes), or go straight to the Chebyshev surrogate (best asymptotic cost, adds a table artifact)? Options compose: A now, B/C if profiling still shows zeta hot after the §3 coupling operators land.

Landed 2026-07-25 (develop c4d4b8ce) with a scan-forced refinement: the integrand is logarithmically singular at the interval ends whenever the target lies in the source-corner plane (gamma->0), so GL alone never reaches 1e-12 there at any order (measured boundary: GL48 fails below |gamma|/r=0.075). Production is GL-48 for |gamma|>=0.2r plus tanh-sinh 177 below (2.7x reserve, test-pinned); JAX path takes tanh-sinh branch-free. Worst case 5.5e-14 over the 5,672-case scan; bow solve 2.3x, zeta share 13.6x, rectangle kernel 6.3x. The by-parts hybrid and Chebyshev surrogate are unneeded: zeta is now 10% of the bow solve. Bonus finding: the old midpoint rule was an accuracy defect (~3.4e-3 in-plane), not just a hot spot.

Coupling formulation (§3): vector-potential line integral M = ∮ A·dl over the observation loop (recommended — reuses the solved A predictors, one quadrature) or the Neumann double line integral (symmetric by construction, but re-integrates the source and duplicates kernel work)?
