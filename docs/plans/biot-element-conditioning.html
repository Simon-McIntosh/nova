§1 — Why: three measured losses, and one that raises a warning

The arc reduction has already had this treatment once — its two residual terms wanting opposite by-parts constants were found and fixed by working the algebra rather than by adding nodes. Nothing equivalent has been done for the rest of the element set, and the evidence says at least three sites are losing digits now.

The small-k² bracket, measured 2026-08-08. K − E is a difference of two numbers both near π/2 whose value is of order k². A target near the axis or many ring radii away therefore loses digits at a rate set by the arrangement, not by where K and E came from: the Cephes route and the Bulirsch descent stand together at 1e-3 of the field scale by k² ~ 1e-9. Changing the special function cannot help; splitting the pole off the bracket can.

The same file already knows the technique. nova/biot/greens.py:210-214 carries a comment doing exactly this for the other bracket — "a² − R² − dz² is −d² + 2a(a − R) and a² + R² + dz² is d² + 2aR, both exact, and what is left over d² is the divergence itself". The method is established in the codebase; it has simply not been carried across every bracket.

A live warning at greens.py:220. The B_R bracket (big_e − big_k + 2·ar·r/gap·big_e) raises RuntimeWarning: invalid value encountered in add — an inf + (−inf) as gap → 0 at the coincident limit. The result is masked afterwards by np.where(r < _R_FLOOR, 0.0, br_full), so the warning is not obviously wrong output; but computing a NaN and selecting it away is the pattern that hides a real conditioning problem, and it fires in ordinary test runs and benchmark sweeps.

A residual every route shares. Across every source-side route measured — filament, exact polygon, corner rule, banded — the reduced self-inductance sits at the same 4.32e-04 from the double integral. A number common to all four is not a property of any of them: it is the target frame's own subdivision residual, and §4 is where it is addressed.

§2 — The audit: every element, one method

Each element in nova/biot/ that evaluates a kernel gets the same pass. The list is the Solve.generator registry and what it reaches: arc, beam, bow, circle, cylinder, line, polybeam, polybow, polysection, and the kernels beneath them — greens, polygon, polygonanalytic, completeelliptic, incompleteelliptic, incompletemoments, symmetricelliptic, elliptic, zeta, arcamplitude, toroidalharmonic, sectionaverage, bandedcoupling.

For each: identify every difference of like-sized quantities and every division by a quantity that can approach zero; state the limit in which it degenerates; measure the achieved accuracy against an arbiter the element does not share a code path with (extended precision, an independent quadrature, or an analytic limit); and where a loss is found, fix it by rearrangement — splitting the pole off, factoring the difference, or a series in the small parameter — rather than by adding nodes. Adding nodes to a cancelling bracket buys nothing, which is why this is algebra work.

Every element ends the pass with a test pinning its accuracy in its worst regime, and no element is allowed to compute a NaN it then selects away: a branch that cannot be evaluated is guarded before evaluation, not after.

§3 — Settle the source-side route on the measurement

The coil section promotion (2026-08-08) measured four source-side routes on an ITER pair and a twelve-coil machine. polysection shipped as the default on accuracy and on two properties the filament lane lacks — reduced terms invariant under mesh refinement to 1e-9 where the banded lane drifts above 1e-4, and reciprocity held to 7.6e-5 where the filament lane breaks it by 2.6e-3.

But exact-everywhere was not measured as the best point on the curve. polysection-banded lands 6.08e-06 from the exact lane — three orders inside the filament lane's error — at 2.64 s against 9.52 s for exact on the machine grid, only 1.7× the filament route. The remaining options are standoff (a two-way exact/filament split, which the module's own docstring notes does not converge for a full ring at any standoff), quadrature order reduction, and the cylinder corner rule, which already serves the undivided axis-aligned rectangle exactly and cheaply.

The decision below settles which is the shipped default, on the audit's numbers rather than on this one sweep.

§4 — The target-side rule: what dcoil is actually for

With the section integrated on every pair, the source side no longer depends on the mesh: a tiling of sub-sections sums to the whole section's integral identically, which is why the reduced coil-coil terms hold 1e-9 across dcoil −2 to −20. What dcoil still buys is the target-side area average — the flux a conductor links is the mean over the area its current occupies, and the force it feels is set by the mean field there. Uniform subdivision is a midpoint rule for that average, and it converges like one.

The better rule already exists in the package and is not wired into the operator. nova/biot/sectionaverage.py supplies exactly this: a tensor-product Gauss-Legendre rule on a signed fan decomposition of the section, nine nodes a triangle — 36 nodes for a rectangular filament, 54 for a hexagonal one. It is currently used only as the reference the elements are measured against. Reaching the same average through 36 Gauss nodes instead of hundreds of uniformly subdivided filaments is the cheaper and the more accurate route, and it would decouple accuracy from dcoil in the same way integrating the section decoupled the source side.

That is a measurement, not an assumption: the rule's convergence has to be shown against the same double-integral arbiter, on sections that are not axis-aligned rectangles (the hexagonal plasma cell and the wall-clipped one are the hard cases), and the cost compared at equal accuracy. dcoil keeps whatever role survives — it still sets where force is resolved within a coil, which is a different question from what the coil links.

§5 — The GPU route does not currently reach this kernel

Worth stating plainly because it bears on the cost argument. The tiled assembly in nova/biot/tiledassembly.py carries the device path — batched evaluation, a compilation cache, and devices splitting pair blocks across local devices — but its geometry is "ring" or "arc" only, and nothing in the element registry routes through it; its sole importer in nova/ is the geometry-Jacobian helper. So promoting the polygon section did not move coil assembly onto a GPU, because no GPU route to that kernel exists yet.

The kernel itself is not the obstacle: polygon.traced_pack_section is xp-threaded and a geometry Jacobian already reaches through it. Whether to extend the tiled assembly to polygon sections is a scoping question this audit should answer with a cost number, since it changes what the exact route costs and therefore what §3 should default to.

Against that, the cost is paid once. Verified 2026-08-08: a solved operator reloads from its netCDF cache in 0.199 s against 36.4 s to build, a 183× saving, with the mutual inductance bit-identical across the round trip. For a static machine the exact route is a one-time build; the routes only separate for work that rebuilds operators repeatedly — a geometry solve, a mesh sweep, or a per-pulse wall.

§ Decisions

Which source-side route ships as the default for a coil section, given banded lands 6e-06 from exact at a fraction of the cost?

Does the operator's target-side area average move from uniform subdivision onto the section quadrature that already exists?

§ Followups

Fix the two known bracket faults, then sweep the element set

Two sites are already identified with evidence: the K − E bracket, where both special-function routes lose digits together at small k², and the B_R bracket at greens.py:220, which forms inf + (−inf) at the coincident limit and masks the NaN afterwards. Fix those two by the rearrangement the same file already documents for its sibling bracket, which establishes the method, then apply the pass to the rest of the registry.
