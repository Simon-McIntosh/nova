§1 — The defect, exactly, and how it was found

The free-boundary moment map's convergence barrier was chased through an exclusion chain that ended in three decisive measurements, all banked in the moment-map evidence record. First, the consistent support quadrature (evidence) replaced the two-path source seam (degree-3 interior stencil vs locally linearised clipped integral) with one formula — exact moments of the same cubic nodal interpolant over the actual support polygon, closed Green/simplex edge moments through degree four. It is verified at the property level: unclipped support reproduces the interior stencil bitwise, clipped error is bounded by 4.1× the missing-area fraction, both-sided derivatives agree with smoothing switched off, and the polygon integration matches independent adaptive quadrature at 2e-13. The kink is removed by construction, so the entire smoothing branch retires as symptom treatment.

Second, the four-quadrant test (evidence) crossed both source schemes with both the production first-moment coupling and a converged piecewise-linear reference coupling on the 566-cell coarse fixture at the analytic Solov'ev seed. The coupling axis moved the flux error by less than the reference's own refinement change while the source axis carried a 10.9 Wb shift (source error 7,598× the coupling error in RMS), so second-moment coupling blocks are refuted and the coupling stays first-moment.

Third, the per-cell localization (evidence) attributed the shift completely. Of 447 nonempty supports, 96 lack a degree-3 stencil — boundary-ring cells whose neighbour rings are incomplete. Those 96 cells carry 4.59% of the plasma current (−689 kA) yet 84% (L1) of the flux shift; every one of the ten leading flux cells is stencil-unavailable, and the moment source's total current is −14.384 MA against the exact-support −15.005 MA — 0.62 MA of edge current lost at the availability seam. The ψ normalisation constants are secondary (0.87%). The interior agrees with the analytic integral; only the ring is wrong:

The falsifiable prediction this plan starts from: repairing only the 96 ring-incomplete supports with boundary-local nodal quadrature, keeping the 351 stencil-available cells unchanged, removes 11.281 Wb of the 10.935 Wb sup shift at the argmax target, leaving ≈ −0.345 Wb — the moment source would then beat the legacy centroid scheme (0.826 Wb) by roughly 2.4×.

§ Decisions

Which nodal set feeds the cubic interpolant on ring-incomplete supports?

Deferred by the owner's 2026-08-18 ruling pending the audit; the audit then exonerated the attribution formulas and convicted the availability zero. Owner steer 2026-08-18: evaluate own-nodes first — sample on the hex nodes + cell centroid and construct current and moments from there. Why the current path samples outside the cell: trial flux lives only at cell centres, so vertex values are themselves ring reconstructions (SharedNodeFluxStencil, runtime step 3), and the interior cubic additionally takes fitted first-moment constraints from the neighbour ring (step 8) — 10 dof exceeds 7 in-cell samples. own-nodes drops the cubic's ring constraints on the boundary: the seven in-cell samples support an in-cell quadratic, the shared-node vertex values already exist on ring cells wherever vertex-sharing neighbours exist, and the clipped-moment integration is the audited closed-form machinery. The two quantified risks are scored in isolation by the §5 ladder: the psi_norm interpolation gate (quadratic reconstruction vs the exact quartic oracle) and outermost-vertex availability.

Owner ruling 2026-08-18: sample only the cell's own geometry — six hex vertices plus cell centroid — with vertex flux carried as direct grid-free targets, never reconstructed from out-of-cell ring fits. Architecture: build one-off evaluation matrices (the existing nova.biot Solve/Target machinery PlasmaGrid.solve already uses for cell-centre Psi/Br/Bz and gradients) over the union of all cell centres and all unique hex vertices, evaluate every node in a single matmul pass as currents change, then select each cell's seven-sample adjacent set by precomputed indexing. Fully vectorized and traceable, hence performant and GPU-batchable. Verified in code: PlasmaGrid's tricontour triangulates cell centres, so vertices are new target rows through existing machinery, not new machinery. This removes the shared-node quadratic reconstruction error (±≈1.2–1.5 mWb on the quartic oracle) by construction and dissolves the availability seam — every nonempty cell owns its seven samples.
