§1 — Why the forward solve needs constraints it can be handed

The free-boundary forward solve prescribes the coil circuit currents and the flux-function profiles and asks for the flux map. Along at least one direction that problem is ill-posed by physics: an elongated plasma's vertical position is fixed by the prescribed currents only through a near-singular balance. The measurement on solver-convergence-regression put the smallest singular value of the frozen-mask Jacobian at three to four ten-thousandths of the largest on 21986/46 and 21989/55, with the direction 70 to 74 percent the rigid vertical shift; along it the residual barely changes while the plasma moves, Newton walks, and every damping layer we had was holding that walk. The real machine does not run on prescribed currents either. Its position controller reads a measurement and drives a circuit. The lead's ruling (2026-09-04, decision vertical-mode-elimination-by-constraint on solver-convergence-regression) is to constrain the position as an inner problem with a compensating radial-field current and then solve the position away with the outer condition that the compensating current vanishes, so no reference position is ever imposed.

That construction is not specific to the vertical mode. Every diagnostic that knows where the plasma is carries placement authority the currents cannot supply, and every one of them enters the solve the same way: one scalar (or small-vector) constraint on the state, paired with one compensating unknown whose flux image the solve already knows how to form. Where a diagnostic is trusted, its constraint is imposed and the compensating unknown becomes an output that says what the prescribed inputs missed. Where nothing is trusted, the constraint is used as a change of variables and solved away. The solver must be built so that adding such a pair is a wiring step, never a solver change. The lead's direction of 2026-09-04 names three constraints to wire, in the order of their immediate applicability, and asks that the Shafranov shift be among them.

§2 — The constraint interface

A constraint is a pair. The residual half is a functional g(ψ, u) of the flux state and of the vector u of compensating unknowns, differentiable through the same Jacobian-vector product the Newton–Krylov step already uses, fixed-shape under jit and vmap. The unknown half is one compensating quantity per scalar constraint whose effect on the state is a known flux image: a circuit current (its column of the response matrix), a profile amplitude (the source term's derivative with respect to it), or a multiplier when the constraint is a projection rather than a physical actuator. The augmented system [F(ψ, u); g(ψ, u)] = 0 is square, and newton_krylov solves it as it solves F today: the Krylov space sees the extra rows and columns through the augmented JVP, the merit ladder, best-iterate retention, the own-mask and pre-settlement acceptance and the trusted-Krylov admission are unchanged, and the receipt gains one row per constraint carrying its residual, the compensating value, and the constraint's projection onto the Jacobian's smallest singular direction so a constraint that fixes a soft mode is seen doing so.

Three invariants decide whether the interface is right. With no constraints registered the solve is bit-identical to the current one. Registering N constraints changes the compiled program's shape once, statically, and never traces per constraint at run time. And the same registered constraint can be imposed (its target supplied) or eliminated (its compensating unknown driven to a prescribed value by an outer scalar solve) without the inner solve knowing which. The elimination mode is the general form of the vertical route: an outer root-find over the constraint's target whose equation is that the compensating quantity equals its prescribed value, secant or Newton on that scalar with a bounded iteration count and the inner solve warm-started between outer iterates, whose derivative is the stiffness the inner problem cannot resolve.

§3 — The constraint library, in order of applicability

Vertical position from the plasma itself (immediate). The current centroid height, or the axis height, with the up-down antisymmetric combination of the radial-field circuits as the compensating current. Its inner form is the prototype in flight on solver-convergence-regression (commit 6ae142d5, held) and its eliminated form is followup f-scr-003 there. This plan's first deliverable is to route that pair through the interface of §2 rather than leave it a special case in ForwardProfile.solve.

Current centroid from magnetics (immediate where the reconstruction publishes it). When the magnetics know the plasma's current centroid well, both components, R and Z, are imposed with the radial-field and vertical-field circuit combinations as the two compensating currents. The compensating currents then measure the inconsistency between the prescribed circuit currents and the field that holds the plasma where the magnetics say it is, which is the DIII-D question (diiid-vertical-force-balance) asked as a solver output rather than a tare study.

Thomson scattering edge crossings (more abstract, immediately applicable). A Thomson channel is a point on a line of sight where the electron density and temperature are measured; the channel at which the profile falls to the edge locates the separatrix's crossing of that chord, on one or both sides of the plasma. Each measured crossing point x_c gives the constraint ψ_N(x_c) − 1 = 0, differentiable through the normalised flux, with a shaping circuit current or a profile amplitude as the compensating unknown. Two channels on one chord fix the plasma's width and position along that chord; chords at different heights fix the elongation and the vertical position; the constraints have remarkable placement authority because they read the plasma edge directly rather than inferring it from external field moments. The development target is the DIII-D competition dataset (lead direction 2026-09-04): the competition provides Thomson scattering channels as one of its signals, so the constraint is developed on those channels, on the competition's own chord geometry, with the goal of placing the competition equilibria from the signals the competition supplies. imas-ambix owns the corpus reading and the challenge conventions, so the Thomson channel signals, their chord geometry and their units cross the boundary as typed crossing points in COCOS 17 and Nova consumes them; the first node is a scout that inventories what the competition dataset carries for Thomson (channels, chord geometry, quantities, time base, edge definition) through the ambix reader. MAST's bank, which carries EFIT's LCFS per row, supplies synthetic crossings along MAST's chord geometry as the calibration against a known answer before any measured profile is used, and stays the secondary target.

Shafranov shift, βp + li/2 (the one the lead asked for with a smile). The Shafranov integral of the external magnetics fixes the combination of poloidal beta and internal inductance, which is a constraint on the scale and shape of the pressure and current profiles rather than on position. Its compensating unknown is a profile amplitude, the pressure gradient scale or the ff' scale, whose derivative the source term already carries. It closes the loop between the flux-function extraction and the magnetics without fitting anything.
