Why this plan exists

On 2026-10-01 the lead ruled that nova's topology read is rebuilt as one authority, meaning one module that is the sole source of every topology verdict: a contour tree of the poloidal flux, computed on the hex plasma cells, with no raster step, no normalised flux in any topology decision, and the plasma-current sign carried explicitly in every definition. The ruling answered the open decision shadowed-contact-withdraws-the-wall-or-only-the-candidate on null-identification-authority. That decision asked which of two pinned tests' verdicts to keep, with the other test's assertion rewritten to match: the 9×9 grid fixture in tests/test_forward_operator_axes.py, which needs a finite class margin of −0.319, and the MAST row-10 fixture in tests/test_limiter_spline_minimum.py, which needs the wall operand withdrawn. Both verdicts were already ruled discretisation artifacts, and every offered option picked which artifact to keep.

The class read in nova/equilibrium/connectivity_boundary.py has four defects that one authority removes together:

  • The normalisation is arbitrary as well as circular. The read divides by psi_out, the extreme flux on the edge of the computational grid, so u = (ψ − ψ_axis) / (ψ_edge − ψ_axis) and class_margin = u_wall − u_x (line 2019) change with the size of the solve box. Normalising by the boundary flux instead would be circular, because the boundary flux is what the read selects.
  • Reachability is judged on a raster. A wall node is reachable only if it touches a flood from the axis over u ≤ u_x − offset on the connectivity raster (_axis_component_before_level, line 721), and has a straight sightline from the axis that clears the wall polyline (_select_reachable_wall_limiter, line 1020). The sightline is a geometric heuristic, not a topological fact.
  • A shadowed contact sets the wall level to infinity (line 1861), so a diverted plasma 1 mm from the centre column and one 20 cm from it read the same margin.
  • One field is discretised four ways. The null census polishes on the global spline, reachability floods a raster, the boundary contour is traced separately, and the limited level comes from the wall-target flux. The stored X-point coordinate and the flux beside it once disagreed by 2.25e-2 Wb; a MAST 27079 limited boundary floated 10 cm off its own anchor; and on MAST 21983/35 the class selector took a saddle outside the vessel.

A contour tree removes all four. It is the graph whose nodes are the critical points of the flux and whose edges are the regions between them, so the plasma, the private regions and the order of critical events as the level falls from the axis are read off one combinatorial object rather than reconstructed from floods and distances. Data Dictionary 4 already carries this object: equilibrium.time_slice.contour_tree (added in 4.0.0, lifecycle alpha) stores node(:) with critical_type (0 minimum, 1 saddle, 2 maximum), r, z, psi and the saddle levelset, plus edges as pairs of node indices. The authority writes its tree into that structure against DD 4.1.0 exactly (the structure itself dates from 4.0.0), beside the other DD fields listed in §1.

plasma-cell-topology-authority (shipped, S18) already moved the saddle-aware flood, the sign-change census and the private mask onto the plasma cells. This plan replaces those reads and the remaining raster class read with the tree, and it is the input that null-identification-authority's remaining read now waits on. The overlapping sections of topology-read-containment-and-rebank (class-free containment and the saddle detector) are to be re-scoped against this plan before they are dispatched.

§1 — Conventions and definitions

A module docstring and one result type (a dataclass returned by the read) carry the contract every later section implements. They state:

  • Sign. σ is computed from the state's declared COCOS sign tuple and the sign of the plasma current through nova.io.cocos, never hard-coded, so that the current-carrying magnetic axis is a local maximum of σψ. It need not be the global maximum; §4 selects which local maximum is primary. Every comparison of levels is a comparison of σψ in webers. The existing polarity arguments in nova/equilibrium/topology.py derive from this same σ.
  • Units. Raw poloidal flux in webers. No normalised flux enters any topology decision. Once §4 has chosen the boundary, normalised flux may be reported, referenced to that boundary level, for consumers that ask for it.
  • Domain. The closed vessel region, tested by true polygon containment over the multi-unit wall, never by a bounding box. The wall polyline is the domain boundary, and its vertices belong to the domain as boundary vertices.
  • Carrier. The lattice of hex-cell centres: one flux value per hex plasma cell, at its centre. Nothing in the read resamples onto a raster.
  • Output. A mapping onto DD 4.1.0, written under COCOS 17 as that version declares:
    • the tree as contour_tree.node and contour_tree.edges, with each node's psi stored as raw ψ in webers under COCOS 17 and its critical_type classified on raw ψ. Under σ = −1 the current-carrying axis is therefore a minimum of raw ψ (critical_type 0); under σ = +1 it is a maximum (2);
    • the class as boundary.type (0 limiter, 1 diverted);
    • the boundary level as boundary.psi;
    • the axis level as global_quantities.psi_magnetic_axis;
    • the wall clearance as boundary.closest_wall_point, with its r, z and distance.

The section also draws the explanatory figure for the plan: one single-null Solov'ev state with its line contours, both null sets, the wall, and the tree's nodes and edges overlaid. The figure follows the repository plotting rules.

Done when: the docstring and typed result exist and are cited by §2 to §5; σ is derived through nova.io.cocos, and a state whose plasma current is flipped without flipping ψ raises a named convention error before any tree is built (negative control: the test asserts that exception); and the figure is committed under docs/figures/contour-tree-topology-authority/ with an SVG beside the PNG.

§2 — The contour tree on the hex carrier

The tree is built combinatorially, so its topology cannot depend on a tolerance:

  • Mesh. Hex cell centres form a triangular lattice. Triangulate the adjacent centres (the three-direction mesh), clip that triangulation to the vessel polygon, and add wall vertices on the polyline. Each wall vertex's flux is interpolated linearly on the triangle that contains it, from the same piecewise-linear field the tree is built on, so §2 needs no smooth field. §3's smooth representation refines node positions and values; it never changes the tree's topology.
  • Trees. Compute the join tree of σψ (superlevel sets, descending from the axis) and the split tree on that piecewise-linear field, then merge them into the contour tree. Break ties by simulation of simplicity, so that no two values are ever equal.
  • The wall. Connect every wall vertex to one virtual outside node valued below every interior level. The moment a region first meets the wall then appears in the join tree as its join with the outside node, at the level of the highest wall vertex in that region: the first one met as the level descends.
  • Tracing. The construction is fixed-shape and traceable under jit and vmap, with the node and edge capacities stated. An overflow is recorded as a named refusal flag on the receipt that marks the state's topology unqualified. Nothing raises inside the traced read, so one overflowing state never aborts a batch. The only exception that can be raised is §1's host-side convention check, which runs before tracing.

Done when:

  • on the Solov'ev certificate fixtures and the MAST rows named in §4, the tree matches an independent brute-force connectivity count of the superlevel sets at every critical level, sampled finely enough to resolve each event;
  • the node count minus the edge count is one on every fixture;
  • batched and per-state trees are identical under vmap;
  • a fixture whose capacity is exceeded reports the refusal rather than truncating the tree.

§3 — Critical-point geometry

Each interior tree node is located and valued on a smooth field representation: Newton-polished, Hessian-classified, and contained in the vessel polygon. The node's polished type must agree with its combinatorial type, and the polished levels must keep the tree's order. A disagreement is never repaired silently: it is written as a named receipt field, and the state's topology is marked unqualified. This replaces the sign-change census and the raster census as the source of critical points. The own-node quadratic polish stays one of the candidate representations, because on the hex carriers it admitted the saddle on 9 of 9 rungs and the axis on 15 of 15. The choice of representation is the open decision below, and the one chosen is the only route in production.

Done when:

  • on the Solov'ev fixtures, the axis and every X-point sit within the certificate position bound of the analytic ones, and the flux at each node agrees with the field evaluated at its coordinate to 1e-9 Wb on the same representation;
  • on MAST 21983/35 no out-of-vessel saddle becomes a node;
  • replacing the vessel polygon with its bounding box makes that saddle a node again (negative control);
  • panels show the solved and analytic null sets with the draw_nulls vocabulary, the wall, and line contours on shared levels.

§4 — Primary selection, regions, class and margin

Everything below is read off the tree:

  • Primary O-point: among all in-vessel local maxima of σψ (not necessarily the global maximum), the one whose tree branch holds the largest share of the plasma current. The current is summed over the hex cells of that branch down to its first event, which is the higher of its first interior saddle and its first join with the outside node. A tie is recorded as a named refusal flag on the receipt and marks the topology unqualified, like every refusal inside the traced read.
  • Plasma region: the superlevel component of σψ that contains the primary O-point, followed down the tree from it. The magnetic axis is the primary O-point by definition.
  • The first event is read only along the primary O-point's own path down the join tree. "First" always means the highest σψ level, the first one met as the level descends from the axis. It is the larger of two levels:
    • the highest in-vessel join saddle on that path (the primary X-point), whether it lies above or below the wall contact;
    • the first join of that path with the outside node, i.e. a wall contact reached without passing through any saddle (the limiter).
    If the first event is the saddle, the class is diverted and the boundary passes through the X-point. If it is the wall contact, the class is limited and the boundary touches the wall.
  • Private regions: the other branches that join at the primary X-point. A wall contact inside a private region can never be a limiter, which is the precise meaning of "shadowed".
  • Margin: σψ_x − σψ_lim in webers. σψ_lim is the highest σψ over the wall vertices that do not lie in a private region of the primary X-point. For a limited state this is the limiter level itself. For a diverted state it is the level at which the plasma would first touch the wall outside the divertor's private flux. σψ_x is the primary X-point's level as defined above, so a limited state with an in-vessel saddle on its path still has one. The margin is positive for diverted states and negative for limited states whose path carries an in-vessel saddle; it is finite wherever it is defined, and independent of the solve box. A state whose path carries no in-vessel saddle at all is limited and has no margin: the receipt records margin: none (no in-vessel saddle) instead of an infinity.

Done when:

  • the 9×9 grid fixture reads limited with a finite negative margin;
  • MAST row 10 reads diverted with a finite positive margin, and its gap to the centre column is reported in millimetres;
  • MAST 27079 row 16 reads limited, with the traced boundary within one hex-cell pitch (the centre spacing at the contact) of the wall, and the boundary flux equal to the field sampled at the contact point;
  • every Solov'ev certificate row reads its analytic class;
  • a limited state whose path carries no in-vessel saddle records margin: none (no in-vessel saddle) rather than a number or an infinity;
