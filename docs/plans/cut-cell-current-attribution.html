§4 — One discretisation: the flux read and the current placement from the same clipped geometry

The chord clip and the exact clip both take their edge roots and density coordinates from the same flux interpolant (shared_node_flux, support_flux_coefficients), and the current moments feed the polygon coupling (coupling_current_moments into current_moment_image) with zeroth, first and second moments. Exact moments under a boundary located by a linear edge interpolation, or a boundary located exactly under moments computed on a chord, are two halves of two different discretisations, and the solve finds the fixed point of whichever pair it is given. This section makes the pair one: the boundary the read returns, the region the current integrates over and the polynomial space the density is evaluated in are the same object per cell, so that with the analytic flux supplied the operator returns the analytic current image and the analytic boundary, and with the analytic current supplied the solve converges to the analytic equilibrium. The unified clip-and-moment path on uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling is where this is built; this section owns its acceptance and the design constraint, which the S19 discriminator established: the clipped geometry enters the flux read and the current placement together or exact moments alone move the answer away from the reference.

The design question the lead decides after §1 is which representation carries the read: the six-point ring quadratic extended to the clipped support, the global tensor spline the extraction route already fits, or the cellwise polynomial the moments are evaluated in. Each is a candidate for one reason and disqualified for one reason today, and §1's paired differences are what makes the choice an evidence question rather than a preference.

Lead, 2026-09-10: the global tensor spline, unless an alternative capable of the same accuracy and shared fidelity shows a significant performance benefit. Any challenger must match accuracy and shared fidelity and beat it materially on speed before it is considered.

The coupling geometry is frozen; the clip enters through the current moments

<p>Gate B found a one to two percent smooth error when clipped first moments were imaged through the frozen linear-basis blocks, and the held line answered it by rebuilding polygon-analytic blocks over each clipped polygon every trip. That answer changes the Biot geometry per trip, which is neither cacheable nor batchable nor differentiable, and it is not the design.</p>

The interaction matrix is built once per mesh and cached. The clipped cell's current is represented by its moments about the atomic centroid up to second order (amplitude, gradient, curvature), which move the effective centroid; the image is frozen blocks times moments, a matmul that traces, batches and scales to 2500 cells. The frozen-current oracle decides how many orders are needed to match the clipped-polygon accuracy of 0.000266 of span at 110 cells, and why the first-order route measured worse than zeroth order.

reference-exterior-from-analytic-clipped-moments

Fast: one cached array per mesh, no runtime cost. Accurate and discriminating: the map's fixed point differs from the analytic flux by the booking and coupling errors alone, both measured and both falling with order and cells, so a good clip and a bad one read differently and the iteration is judged separately by self-consistency at 1e-12. Posing the exterior from the production booking would make every booking a fixed point by construction and is admitted only as the pure iteration probe in the oracle-start node; a coil-set exterior has no closed form because the Solov'ev exterior is not a vacuum field.

census-ring-is-the-cell-vertex-ring

Lead, 2026-09-14, on the unadmitted-cells-110 panel: the census ring must be the cell's vertices around its centroid, not neighbour centroids around a centroid.
