§2 — Correction: restore geometric clipping of the profile support, then re-verify every consumer

Three separate questions, measured separately (lead direction 2026-09-10). The allocation of current to cells, the coupling that turns cell currents into flux, and the iteration that finds the fixed point are three different problems, and a solve loop measures their sum. Each gets its own gate on the oracle. Gate A, allocation: with the oracle's flux supplied at the mesh nodes, the clip books each cell's current; compare it per cell with the oracle's current integrated over the exact plasma region of that cell. §3 — The X-point cell: saddle vertex as a clip vertex, branch-paired chords, per-branch supports

The single-chord clip cannot represent a cell the separatrix enters and leaves twice. The mechanism recorded on the boundary-ring audit and the SOL demonstration is carried here unchanged in shape and made concrete: the fitted saddle position is inserted as a vertex of the clipped polygon; the four edge roots are paired by branch (the two roots each branch connects through the saddle) so the clip yields the core-side wedge, the private-flux wedge and the two common-SOL wedges as separate polygons of fixed vertex capacity; and the current moments integrate per wedge with the profile of that wedge's region (the confined profile on the core wedge, zero or the SOL closure elsewhere). The complement-clip machinery already exists (common_support), and the general ordered spline-chain clip on the held stack (0372d3fc) already inserts every edge root in polygon order, so the X-point cell is the case where two chains meet at one interior vertex. Fixed shapes are kept by padding: every cut cell carries capacity for four roots and one interior vertex, with unused slots exact zero, so the traced program does not branch on the cell's crossing count.

§4 — One discretisation: the flux read and the current placement from the same clipped geometry

The chord clip and the exact clip both take their edge roots and density coordinates from the same flux interpolant (shared_node_flux, support_flux_coefficients), and the current moments feed the polygon coupling (coupling_current_moments into current_moment_image) with zeroth, first and second moments. Exact moments under a boundary located by a linear edge interpolation, or a boundary located exactly under moments computed on a chord, are two halves of two different discretisations, and the solve finds the fixed point of whichever pair it is given. This section makes the pair one: the boundary the read returns, the region the current integrates over and the polynomial space the density is evaluated in are the same object per cell, so that with the analytic flux supplied the operator returns the analytic current image and the analytic boundary, and with the analytic current supplied the solve converges to the analytic equilibrium. The unified clip-and-moment path on uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling is where this is built; this section owns its acceptance and the design constraint, which the S19 discriminator established: the clipped geometry enters the flux read and the current placement together or exact moments alone move the answer away from the reference.

The design question the lead decides after §1 is which representation carries the read: the six-point ring quadratic extended to the clipped support, the global tensor spline the extraction route already fits, or the cellwise polynomial the moments are evaluated in. Each is a candidate for one reason and disqualified for one reason today, and §1's paired differences are what makes the choice an evidence question rather than a preference.
