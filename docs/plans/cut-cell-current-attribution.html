Why this plan exists

Three measurements made on 2026-09-09 and 2026-09-10 do not fit together, and the misfit is the plan. First, the production support (whole-cell booking by centroid side, as §1 established that evening, not a chord clip) attributes current to only 16 of the 43 cells the analytic separatrix cuts on the weak-rotation Solov'ev case at 110 cells, and every participating cut cell's zeroth, first and second moments are wrong against the exact density over the exact clipped region; substituting exact cut-cell moments into the linear flux image removes 98.6 percent of the squared flux error (uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling, cut-cell moment discriminator). Second, putting that exact participation into the production solve does not improve it: the weak and moderate static certificate rows terminate 63 and 11 times further from their fixed point, and when the two cells that resisted the general clip are placed back on chord moments the solve converges to 6.2e-13 but to a different equilibrium, its magnetic axis 0.533 m outboard of the analytic axis, while the never-converged chord baseline sits within 1.8 mm (the exact-placement discriminator, receipts under docs/figures/uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling/exact-participation/discriminator). Third, on the single-null case the solved flux already matches the analytic field to 1e-17 at 300 cells, yet the topology read admits no X-point at 110 or 300 cells, so the row fails joint qualification while its gradient and Hessian error concentrate around the X-point and along the legs.

The lead's reading, 2026-09-10, is the rule this plan is built on: an improved component that reduces acceptance is evidence that the model is not correct, not evidence against the component. A discretisation that lands near the reference only because its clip error cancels an error elsewhere is compensating, and what the certificate has been scoring is the compensation. The acceptance of this plan is therefore convergence to the reference with exact inputs, never a residual or a pass count on its own. The X-point cell mechanism that sol-current-demonstration recorded as a prerequisite and deferred while confined current made it a 0.2 A effect (the saddle cell is crossed four times by the analytic separatrix and production draws one chord with up to 15 mm endpoint displacement; evidence anchor saddle-gap-current-check) is now first-order for the read and is carried here as §3. The time is now.

§2 — Correction: restore geometric clipping of the profile support, then re-verify every consumer

Three separate questions, measured separately (lead direction 2026-09-10). The allocation of current to cells, the coupling that turns cell currents into flux, and the iteration that finds the fixed point are three different problems, and a solve loop measures their sum. Each gets its own gate on the oracle. Gate A, allocation: with the oracle's flux supplied at the mesh nodes, the clip books each cell's current; compare it per cell with the oracle's current integrated over the exact plasma region of that cell. This is the unit-amplitude census and the read-representation discriminator run on the exact-input state; its acceptance is the per-cell and total agreement of the booked current with the oracle's, and it names which clip and which boundary representation are at fault by how much, with no iteration involved. Gate B, coupling: with the oracle's per-cell currents frozen (the exact zeroth, first and second moments over the exact clipped regions), form the Biot flux prediction on the mesh and compare it with the oracle flux map, as an absolute flux error panel and by cell class. Whatever error remains is the coupling's own: the polygon kernel, the moment truncation order, the second-order compatibility terms. The cut-cell moment discriminator already measured this as a fraction (exact moments remove 98.6, 99.9999 and 99.99999 percent of the base squared image error on weak 110, moderate 110 and weak 300); Gate B states the remaining absolute error and its map so the coupling is judged on its own number. Gate C, iteration: only with A and B passed does the Newton-Krylov loop carry a correct map, and its convergence from the cold seed is then a solver question about damping, frozen geometry per trip and seeding, judged by residual and by position against the reference. The rung A re-solve divergence (below) is a Gate C result and says nothing about A or B.

The fix has two rungs and one rule. Rung A, reinstate the clip. The profile support clips each cell against the signed flux of the current iterate, as it did before c9a89e58: traced_clip(signed_flux) on the confined side, with candidacy decided by the clip geometry itself (a cell participates when any part of it lies on the confined side of the level, whether or not its centroid does) and the profile participation label used only to name which region a clipped piece belongs to, never to exclude a cell. The unit-amplitude census showed why this matters: on the committed terminal state both whole-cell booking and the exact clip drop the same outboard-midplane cells (106, 104, 108, 83, 116, together over 0.8 MA of analytic current) because candidacy came from the label and the read's boundary sits inside the analytic one there, so a clip that inherits the label inherits the hole. The clip is the committed straight chord through the two edge roots of the cell, which is exact for a cellwise-linear boundary and second order for the current moments, and it is the smallest change that makes the operator clip at all. The ledger follows the clip: a cut cell's current is booked to the side its clipped polygon lies on, not to its centroid's side, so the common scrape-off-layer ledger on the transport machine carries only the current the profile actually places outside the separatrix. Rung B, the exact clip behind the flag. The general ordered spline-chain clip on the held stack (0372d3fc, general clip with per-cell confinement; 28a49bcb, the discriminator and the clip-mode switch) lands with the chord as the committed default and the exact path selectable, so both can be measured against the same analytic totals on the same rows and the exact path is promoted to the default only by the acceptance rule of §5. The rule: the plasma current amplitude at the seed and terminal states is reported on every row and is within one percent of unity for whichever clip is in production; an amplitude that departs from unity is the signal that the support is booking the wrong current, and it is never absorbed by the normalisation.

Re-verification. Restoring the clip changes the physics of every forward solve made since 27 August, so no consumer bank is expected to be bit-identical and each is regenerated and compared, delta recorded, never asserted identical: the sixteen Solov'ev certificate rows (residual, axis error, X-point error, joint qualification, amplitude, one H200 job, rows sequential, contour panels per row against the committed panels); the twelve MAST bank rows and the DIII-D gate frames (terminal residual, axis and boundary displacement against EFIT, one H200 job each, device-qualified); the transport machine's scrape-off-layer fraction (the strict expected failure on tests/test_transport_evolved_state.py is expected to turn red and its marker is then removed); the cut-cell moment oracle at 110 and 300 cells on the weak and moderate rows (participation now every cut cell, moments compared to the exact density); and the playable labeller's frame receipts on one MAST shot as a smoke of the consumer path. §3 — The X-point cell: saddle vertex as a clip vertex, branch-paired chords, per-branch supports

The single-chord clip cannot represent a cell the separatrix enters and leaves twice. The mechanism recorded on the boundary-ring audit and the SOL demonstration is carried here unchanged in shape and made concrete: the fitted saddle position is inserted as a vertex of the clipped polygon; the four edge roots are paired by branch (the two roots each branch connects through the saddle) so the clip yields the core-side wedge, the private-flux wedge and the two common-SOL wedges as separate polygons of fixed vertex capacity; and the current moments integrate per wedge with the profile of that wedge's region (the confined profile on the core wedge, zero or the SOL closure elsewhere). The complement-clip machinery already exists (common_support), and the general ordered spline-chain clip on the held stack (0372d3fc) already inserts every edge root in polygon order, so the X-point cell is the case where two chains meet at one interior vertex. Fixed shapes are kept by padding: every cut cell carries capacity for four roots and one interior vertex, with unused slots exact zero, so the traced program does not branch on the cell's crossing count.

The read follows the clip. With the saddle carried as a vertex of the cut, the cell partition on the plasma cells (the saddle-aware flood of plasma-cell-topology-authority) sees the X-point cell split into its four regions, and the null census has a cell whose geometry states where the saddle is instead of one that straddles it; the no-admitted-X-point verdicts at 110 and 300 cells on the single-null case are the first test of whether that is sufficient, and if the read still refuses, the refusal names its reason per candidate.

Done-when. On the single-null certificate case at 110, 300 and 500 cells, one all_debug oracle job reports the X-point cell's core-side current within 1e-6 relative of the analytic wedge integral and exactly zero private-flux and common-SOL current for the confined profile; one H200 job (one card, rows sequential, receipts and contour panels persisted per row) re-solves the four diverted certificate rows with the treatment on and reports the admitted X-point at 110 and 300 cells with position error inside one characteristic pitch, the joint-qualified diverted count against the committed 1 of 4, and every terminal residual beside its committed baseline with no regression. The four limited rows are re-run in the same job as the negative control and must be bit-identical, since they have no saddle to cut.

§4 — One discretisation: the flux read and the current placement from the same clipped geometry

The chord clip and the exact clip both take their edge roots and density coordinates from the same flux interpolant (shared_node_flux, support_flux_coefficients), and the current moments feed the polygon coupling (coupling_current_moments into current_moment_image) with zeroth, first and second moments. Exact moments under a boundary located by a linear edge interpolation, or a boundary located exactly under moments computed on a chord, are two halves of two different discretisations, and the solve finds the fixed point of whichever pair it is given. This section makes the pair one: the boundary the read returns, the region the current integrates over and the polynomial space the density is evaluated in are the same object per cell, so that with the analytic flux supplied the operator returns the analytic current image and the analytic boundary, and with the analytic current supplied the solve converges to the analytic equilibrium. The unified clip-and-moment path on uniform-cell-clip-and-coupling is where this is built; this section owns its acceptance and the design constraint, which the S19 discriminator established: the clipped geometry enters the flux read and the current placement together or exact moments alone move the answer away from the reference.

The design question the lead decides after §1 is which representation carries the read: the six-point ring quadratic extended to the clipped support, the global tensor spline the extraction route already fits, or the cellwise polynomial the moments are evaluated in. Each is a candidate for one reason and disqualified for one reason today, and §1's paired differences are what makes the choice an evidence question rather than a preference.

Done-when. The certificate is re-run with exact participation and the consistent read in one H200 job, and on every row at 300 cells and finer the solve converges to the analytic axis within one characteristic pitch with terminal residual at or below 1e-12, the diverted rows additionally admitting the X-point inside one pitch; arm b of the exact-placement discriminator is re-run as a preregistered test and lands on the analytic axis; the joint-qualified count is reported before and after; and every remaining error term is recorded by cell class with its magnitude, none retained because it improves a score. The chord clip is retired as the production default only when this done-when holds on all sixteen rows; until then it stays the default and the exact path stays behind the benchmark flag.

Acceptance for this plan and for every plan it informs is convergence to the reference with exact inputs: position of the axis and of every admitted X-point inside one characteristic pitch, terminal residual inside the locked recovery bound, and the joint qualification rule of null-identification-authority, reported per row. A pass count is a summary of that, never the gate. A component that is closer to the analytic answer on its own oracle and regresses the certificate is retained as evidence of a remaining inconsistency and is never reverted to recover the score. § Decisions

What is the acceptance for cut-cell attribution work: a terminal residual, a pass count, or convergence to the analytic equilibrium?

Lead, 2026-09-10: a 100 percent pass rate is not a hard gate, and an improved model that reduces acceptance is an indication that the model is not correct. Does the deferred X-point cell mechanism (saddle vertex as a clip vertex, branch-paired chords, per-branch supports) get built now or stay a prerequisite for the SOL closure?

Lead, 2026-09-10: we developed a plan to treat this correctly when the time comes, and the time is now. The single-chord X-point cell is first-order for the topology read on the single-null certificate rows (no admitted X-point at 110 and 300 cells while the flux is exact) and a lot of the visible error is driven from the placement of the primary X-point.

Which representation carries the flux read that the current placement must be consistent with: the six-point ring quadratic extended over the clipped support, the global tensor spline, or the cellwise polynomial the moments are evaluated in?

Lead, 2026-09-10: the global tensor spline, unless an alternative capable of the same accuracy and shared fidelity shows a significant performance benefit. A key feature of the global spline is its shared state, which makes downstream processes such as flux-surface averages much easier. Any challenger must match accuracy and shared fidelity and beat it materially on speed before it is considered.
