Why this plan exists

nova ingests plasma data from the IMAS Data Dictionary. The lead ruled on 21 October 2026 (decision d-ippc-dd-cocos on iter-passive-plasma-circuit): nova reads DD3 data as COCOS 11 and DD4 data as COCOS 17. It uses COCOS 17 internally everywhere, and it treats data that does not conform to its own DD version's COCOS as invalid. DD3 data enters through simple, reusable translators.

The ruling followed a measured defect. nova/imas/plasma_source.py::PlasmaSlice.prescribe hard-codes one flux orientation: it refuses whenever ψ_boundary − ψ_axis has the same sign as the plasma current, which is the σ_Bp = −1 convention of COCOS 13 and 17. That guard was calibrated on DINA 105014/1. Measured over every slice above 1 MA, that case is stored as DD 3.38.1 yet carries σ_Bp = −1 in all 732 slices, so it does not conform to DD3. The guard therefore refused every conforming ITER source surveyed: CORSICA 130506, spiderInverse 131000 and 121000, and JINTRAC 134000, all COCOS 11 as DD3 requires. Of the DINA cases, 105052/1 and 105069/3 conform. 105013/1 carries σ_Bp = +1, but its σ_ρθφ contradicts COCOS 11 on every slice above 1 MA (b0 stored at +2.65 T with q > 0), so it does not conform either.

nova already owns one COCOS algebra: the stdlib-only nova_cocos package (packages/nova-cocos, plan cocos-standalone-distribution), with identify_convention, convention_transform(source=, target=) and its single factor table. This plan builds on it and adds no second table. imas-ambix owns its own COCOS conventions for the challenge corpus, and it consumes nova, so nova must not import ambix. The design review's prior-art scout reads ambix's COCOS handling only for reuse of ideas, not of code.

§1 — The census: every IDS reader and the convention it assumes

The census lists every place nova reads a COCOS-dependent quantity from an IDS: ψ and its derivatives, the plasma current, B0 and F, q, p′ and FF′, and the flux-loop and pick-up signals whose sign follows ψ or B_p. For each, it records the file and function, the quantities read, the convention the code assumes (explicitly, or implicitly through a sign test such as the plasma-source guard), and the DD versions its tests exercise. Each assumption is checked against nova_cocos.identify_convention applied to a fixture of the data that reader is tested on.

The census also lists every convention sign nova hard-codes, in writers and internal consumers as well as readers: a bare negation or normalisation marked # COCOS (today nova/imas/extrapolate.py lines 301 and 345, nova/imas/pulsedesign.py lines 531, 544 and 581, nova/equilibrium/inverse.py line 245, nova/biot/contour.py line 198) and defaults such as cocos: int = 11 in nova/io/ingest.py. It names the owners that already exist, which later sections extend rather than duplicate: nova_cocos for the one digits table and the factors; nova/io/sourcemap.py for per-quantity conversion (SourceSignal.convention_factor) and refusal (BlockedSignal); nova/scripts/identify_source_cocos.py::determine_digits for deriving the four digits from observables; nova/equilibrium/convention.py for the internal sign chain; and nova/database/filepath.py::canonical_key for cache keys. Reads from a non-DD archive, such as the MAST path in nova/imas/mast_solve_inputs.py with its measured source convention 3, are listed with their declared convention; the DD ruling governs IDS reads.

Done when, recorded in docs/research/dd-cocos-census.html: (1) the census table covers every reader found by a search for the IDS paths above and every hard-coded sign found by a search for COCOS comments and fixed-orientation sign tests, under nova/, scripts/ and packages/, with both search commands and their hit counts stated so the table's completeness can be checked; (2) each row names the convention it assumes and whether that matches the DD version of its test data, with a fixture-backed verdict; (3) the internal consumers (solve, topology read, transport, circuit) are listed with the convention each now works in, which is the migration list for §4. Negative control: a reader whose guard is inverted in a scratch copy must change its census verdict.

§2 — One reusable DD3 translator, with DD4 passthrough and a conformance refusal

The translator takes an equilibrium-bearing IDS read at DD3 into COCOS 17 and passes DD4 through unchanged, and it is built by extending the owners §1 names, not beside them.

  • Digits from observables. nova/scripts/identify_source_cocos.py::determine_digits already derives σ_Bp from sign(ψ_edge − ψ_axis) · sign(Ip), cross-checked against p′, and σ_ρθφ from sign(q) · sign(Ip) · sign(B0). The same script measures e_Bp separately, through flux_loop_two_pi_ratio, reduced in its caller. nova_cocos stays standard-library only, as its packaging contract requires, and owns only the four-digits-to-convention lookup. determine_digits uses numpy, so it moves out of the script into nova/io/sourcemap.py, the conversion owner, which already imports nova_cocos. Readers call that one validated derivation, and the e_Bp measurement stays with the archive reader that needs it. For a DD read, e_Bp = 1 and σ_RφZ = +1 are fixed by the DD version, since COCOS 11 and COCOS 17 share both; σ_Bp and σ_ρθφ come from the data. The data conforms when nova_cocos.identify_convention on those four digits returns the DD version's convention.
  • One digits table. cocos_from_digits in the same script carries a second transcription of the Sauter and Medvedev table. It is deleted, and its callers call nova_cocos.identify_convention: the production caller in the same script (the verdict line after the e_Bp reduction) and the tests tests/test_io_cocos.py and tests/test_mastcocos.py. The compatibility shim nova/io/cocos.py retires with it; nothing imports it any more, so it is deleted.
  • Conversion and refusal through the sourcemap. A DD equilibrium read declares its source convention, 11 for DD3 or 17 for DD4, and target 17 as SourceSignal rows in nova/io/sourcemap.py, whose convention_factor already comes from nova_cocos.convention_transform. Data whose digits do not identify its DD version's convention is refused through the sourcemap's refusal path, by name, stating the DD version, the expected COCOS and the measured digits. If the sourcemap cannot carry an equilibrium IDS read, the node records why before adding a module, and that module adds only the entry point.
  • One declaration per reader boundary. The MAST archive read in nova/imas/mast_solve_inputs.py keeps its measured source conventions, 3 and 13, through the same SourceSignal mechanism. A reader that accepts both IDS and archive data declares each source once, at its own boundary, and never a second time downstream.

Done when: (1) CORSICA 130506/6, the imasdb pulse at /work/imas/shared/imasdb/ITER/3/130506/6 written at DD 3.41.0, translated through the sourcemap, matches quantity by quantity the analytic COCOS 11 → 17 mapping of the same slice, and the DD3 run of the same case that does share times with the repository's DD 4.1.0 validator layers, ITER 130506/403, agrees with them in sign at those shared times; 130506/6 itself shares no times with the layers. Those layers are iter_corsica_130506 in tests/data-manifest.json, a validator input and reference pair rather than a conversion of the pulse. As the DD4 fixture they pass through untouched: every factor is exactly 1.0 and the samples are bit-identical; (2) over every slice above 1 MA, DINA 105014/1 (σ_Bp = −1) and DINA 105013/1 (σ_ρθφ = −1) are each refused by name with the contradicting digit stated, while DINA 105052/1, 105069/3 and CORSICA 130506/6 are accepted; (3) the surface balances: readers have one entry point, documented in its docstring with the two DD conventions, and cocos_from_digits and nova/io/cocos.py are deleted, with a search for either name under nova/, scripts/ and tests/ stating its hit count before and after (zero after), and finding a copy of either name planted in a scratch file as its positive control. Literal negative control: negate ψ on a conforming DD3 slice and the read is refused.

§3 — The readers go through the translator

Every reader in the census takes its data through the §2 module, starting with nova/imas/plasma_source.py. The plasma source then works in COCOS 17 whatever DD version it was read from. Its guard in PlasmaSlice.prescribe refuses a slice unless span · I_p < 0, which is σ_Bp = −1. That is COCOS 17's own σ_Bp, so the guard is the right check for nova's internal convention. Its defect was the data that reached it: untranslated COCOS 11 DD3 slices, which it refused, and the non-conforming DINA 105014/1, which it accepted. So the guard stays, re-expressed as the internal COCOS 17 check. Its σ_Bp is taken from nova_cocos.convention(17) rather than written as a literal, and a slice that fails it is refused by name. The message keeps the substring psi ordering disagree that test_mast_flipped_psi_is_refused matches. The readers translate into COCOS 17 before any slice is built.

The guard also protects a route the DD refusal never reaches. PlasmaSource.from_mast feeds the same prescribe from the MAST level-1 archive, whose declared and measured convention is COCOS 3. That route reads through the MAST frame reader nova/media/sources/mast_efit.py::read_frame, which rescales flux by TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR with no declared conversion. COCOS 3 and COCOS 17 share σ_Bp but differ in two digits: e_Bp, which puts the 2π on flux, and σ_ρθφ, which flips q. nova_cocos.convention_transform from 3 to 17 gives 2π for ψ-like rows, −1 for q-like rows and 1/(2π) for d/dψ rows. For the flux rows read_frame already converts, the declared factor equals today's TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR. So the reader declares exactly those flux rows through the sourcemap, and their values are unchanged for every caller, from media to the parity gate to transport. read_frame passes p′, FF′ and any q row through raw today. The node records, for each of those rows and each caller, whether the caller converts it to COCOS 17. A raw COCOS 3 p′ or FF′ reaching COCOS 17 code would be a 2π error, which is reported as a finding for §4 rather than changed here. COCOS 3 and COCOS 17 share σ_Bp = −1, so a conforming MAST slice passes the internal check and a reversed flux family is still refused. tests/test_plasma_source.py::test_mast_flipped_psi_is_refused flips ψ on a slice after the read and calls prescribe, so it exercises exactly the internal check and stays green as written.

Four green tests prescribe DINA 105014/1, which §2 refuses, so they turn red once the reader translates. The fourth is tests/test_coupled_plant.py::test_reconstruction_route_into_the_plant_meets_the_stated_tolerance; the other three are:

  • tests/test_plasma_source.py::test_dina_generic_source_reproduces_existing_prescription
  • tests/test_coupled_plant.py::test_dina_case_through_the_generic_source_is_unchanged
  • tests/test_prescribed_plasma.py::test_polygon_flux_change_on_a_real_slice

Each is re-pointed to the conforming DINA 105069/3, and its changed expectation is named with the pulse change that caused it. The refusal of 105014/1 is asserted in a test of its own. A fifth test drives DINA 105013/1, which §2 also refuses: tests/test_unseen_source_full_plant.py::test_unseen_source_drives_the_full_plant_with_zero_rebuilds, whose DINA_CASES holds (105069, 3) and (105013, 1). Its 105013/1 case is replaced by the conforming DINA 105052/1, so the test still drives two distinct sources, and the refusal of 105013/1 is asserted beside that of 105014/1.

Done when: (1) PlasmaSource.from_imas prescribes CORSICA 130506/6 and DINA 105069/3, and refuses DINA 105014/1 and 105013/1 by name, each with the digit it contradicts. The one module that owns the MAST flux conversion is nova/media/sources/mast_efit.py. §4 — nova's internals run in COCOS 17

The internal consumers the census lists (the forward solve, the topology read, transport, the circuit tier and their caches) work in COCOS 17. A consumer still in another convention is converted at its own boundary, never by a sign flip at a call site. The internal convention already has an owner: nova/equilibrium/convention.py fixes the total-flux and current-density sign chain and exports TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR to its importers. §4 states COCOS 17 there once. The census's hard-coded sign sites in writers and internal consumers (the pulsedesign.py writer, inverse.py, biot/contour.py, and the display negation in extrapolate.py::Extrapolate.plot_2d_masked at line 345; the read in Extrapolate.update moves in §3) take their convention from that owner or from nova_cocos, and none keeps a bare negation. The cache key has an owner too: nova/database/filepath.py::canonical_key and hash_attrs. The key that must gain the convention is SignalStore.cache_key in nova/io/signalstore.py, which today hashes only the URI and the DD version. nova/io/ingest.py writes cocos into the dataset attributes, which are not a key input.

Done when: (1) every internal consumer in the census works in COCOS 17, each shown by a test that fixes a known COCOS 17 state and checks the consumer's sign-sensitive output; (2) the certified forward-solve rows and the analytic oracles keep their published magnitudes, by delta against the base. A consumer converted to COCOS 17 flips its sign-sensitive outputs, so every flipped expectation is named with its consumer and the COCOS factor that flips it, and no other expectation changes; (3) cached artefacts whose stored fields change convention carry the convention in their key through canonical_key, starting with SignalStore.cache_key, and an entry written before the change is refused or translated, never served silently. Literal negative control: leave one converted consumer in its old convention in a scratch copy, and its COCOS 17 test fails on its own assertion.

§5 — A valid measure of edge consistency between flux functions and flux map

The lead's criterion for a reference equilibrium is that its flux functions agree with its flux map at the edge. The survey that ranked the ITER sources could not measure that: its map-implied current carried a uniform factor of 225 to 300 in every case, DINA included, which is an operator artefact. This section builds the measure properly by extending the one nova already reports. nova/imas/mast_parity_chain.py::_profile_reproduction_residuals computes the RMS of the profile current minus the current the structured-grid Grad-Shafranov operator reads from the flux, over interior in-limiter cells with a complete stencil. It assembles Δ* inline by central differences over the plasma flux and recovers the current density as −Δ*/(TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR μ0 R), with TOTAL_FLUX_FACTOR imported from nova/equilibrium/convention.py. §5's algebra stays consistent with that inline operator and that factor. §5 takes that residual to the IDS reads, in COCOS 17 after translation, in a band inside the boundary. It reports three forms. The first is the parity chain's own figure, unchanged: numerator and denominator over the parity chain's scored cells of the whole slice, RMS(j_φ,profile − j_φ,grid) / max|j_φ,grid|, so each row reads directly against the parity chain's figures. The second and third are the band forms: the absolute current density of the residual in the band, and that residual relative to the local profile current in the band, which is what the edge criterion asks. The band forms never borrow the slice-wide denominator. The whole-slice form is judged against the bound the parity chain already registers for that field, PROFILE_RESIDUAL_RMS in nova/imas/parity_tolerances.py. The edge criterion is judged on the band form relative to the local profile current. That threshold is not a scorecard field: parity_tolerances.py admits exactly the ScorecardField members. It is a named constant in the module that implements the measure, with its basis stated beside it and derived from the analytic state's discretisation error, and it is fixed before any IDS row is read.

Done when: (1) on the analytic Solov'ev-family state CerfonFreidbergSingleNull in nova/equilibrium/analytic_single_null.py, whose grad_shafranov_source method gives the exact strong-form source, the residual is zero to the discretisation's stated order, and a 1 % FF′ perturbation is detected; (2) the measure is reported for the conforming sources, DINA 105052/1 and 105069/3 and CORSICA 130506/6, in a table in docs/research/dd-cocos-census.html; (3) the reference choice of iter-passive-plasma-circuit §6 is confirmed or revised on that table. Literal negative control: perturb FF′ only in the edge band of the analytic state, leaving its flux map unchanged, and the measure reports that state inconsistent beyond the fixed threshold; a measure that confirms the perturbed state cannot confirm a reference.

Lead ruling, 2026-10-23. nova/equilibrium/convention.py books j_φ = −2π(R p′ + FF′/(μ0 R)), which makes nova's p′ equal to −dp/dΦ.
