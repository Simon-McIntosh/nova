§1 — Three observables survived the fix that explained everything else

Applying the 69 registered terminal-observable bounds to the six held-out cases returns 66 of 69 bounds passing and 407 of 414 case-observable evaluations passing. The three that fail are the subject of this plan, and the reason they deserve their own plan rather than a line in someone else's is that they are not explained by the mechanism that explained everything else on this map.

Read the magnitudes before reading the ratios, because the ratios mislead. Two of the three failures are at float64 resolution: volume and major radius each differ by two units in the last place, against bounds that are respectively zero-width and one ULP wide. Only divergence_j carries a large ratio, and it carries it on the relative envelope of a quantity whose absolute difference is 1.16e-10 and inside its own absolute bound. A reader who takes "3.9 times its bound" as the size of the disagreement will mis-scope the repair. The registration itself says so: the 69 envelopes are declared frozen empirical envelopes for subsequent aligned-seed evaluations, not physics tolerances, and criterion-family.json records terminal_observable_envelopes_are_physics_tolerances = false.

The first argument that these are independent: they outlived the repair, and they are the wrong size to be inherited from it. Iteration-local Krylov conditioning collapsed the compiled-versus-eager terminal flux difference from 1.1646900802908533e-05 to 3.5787553476218648e-12 on these same six cases — a 3.3-million-fold reduction. The residual state disagreement is therefore 3.58e-12, yet volume and major radius disagree at 2.15e-16 and 3.21e-16 relative: four orders smaller than the state difference they would have inherited. A quantity computed from two states differing at 3.58e-12 should differ near 3.58e-12, not near one ULP. So these two are not state-inherited at that scale; they are what two evaluations of an effectively identical state look like when layout, accumulation order or kernel selection differ. divergence_j is the one that could still be either: its absolute difference is 1.16e-10 and its 3.925 utilisation comes entirely from relative normalisation of a near-zero quantity. Which of the three readings applies to which observable is exactly what §2 measures, and this plan does not assert it in advance.

The second argument: they do not track amplification severity. If the failures were residual fallout from the non-normal amplification, the worst-conditioned cases would fail worst. They do not, and the corrected per-case table makes the absence sharper than the original reading did. The mildest case, 21989/55 at a maximum projected Krylov condition of 173.01, fails two observables. The worst, 21978/35 at 3674.07, fails one. The only case that fails all three, 22086/43, sits mid-range at 1061.04 — below 21978/35 and 21985/51 — while 21986/46 at 775.05 and 21983/35 at 582.48 fail nothing at all. There is no monotone relation in either direction, which is what one expects if the cause is a property of the observable rather than of the trajectory that reached it.

The third argument, and the one that decides the priority: a labeled catalog emits exactly these quantities. The throughput plan's product is not a flux map, it is a store of boundaries, moments and conservation diagnostics across 1,341,435 slices. Terminal flux agreement at 3.58e-12 does not make that store trustworthy if the moments it publishes differ between the batched and per-slice routes that produced them. Every argument made about batched-equals-per-slice reproducibility has so far been made about the state; the labels are where it actually lands for a consumer.

The honest caveat, stated because it bounds the first node rather than the plan. These observables were measured on a route that does not yet converge — the six held-out cases reach a reference-native terminal residual of 7.322007721740656e-5, above any candidate criterion. A non-converged state's derived quantities may legitimately differ between routes more than a converged one's. That is precisely why §2 exists and why it comes first: the discriminator separates the two readings on data that already exists, before any repair is attempted.

§2 — Separate a computation difference from a state difference

The discriminating question is sharp and answerable without new physics: given two terminal states that agree to a measured tolerance, does the observable's own evaluation path produce different answers from the same input? Evaluate each failing observable on a single shared terminal state through both routes' evaluation paths. If the answers differ, the computation differs and the state is exonerated. If they agree, the disagreement is inherited from the residual state difference and scales with it — testable by evaluating across cases whose state agreement spans a range.

moments.volume failing an exact-equality bound is the sharpest probe in the set and should be attacked first. Exact equality cannot fail by accumulation of rounding in a well-posed shared computation; it fails when the two routes evaluate structurally different things — a different cell count, a different mask, a different clip, or a reduction performed in a different association. Whatever explains it is likely to explain the other two, because the other two are continuous quantities computed over the same support.

Done when each of the three failing observables carries a verdict of COMPUTATION_DIFFERS or STATE_INHERITED, with the evidence that separated them quoted: for the first, the two evaluations of a single shared state and their difference; for the second, the observable difference plotted against state agreement across the six cases with a stated correlation. The receipt names the first structurally differing operation for any observable reaching the first verdict. No repair is attempted in this section.

§3 — Repair what the discriminator convicts

Scope follows the verdict rather than preceding it, which is why this section carries no predetermined mechanism. An observable convicted as COMPUTATION_DIFFERS is repaired at the differing operation so both routes evaluate one definition — reusing the derived-quantity chain that equilibrium-metric-parity established rather than authoring a second one. That plan proved every derived quantity agrees when computed from the same flux map; this one is the complementary question of the same quantity computed through two routes, so its machinery is the natural home and any new component needs a stated reason the existing one does not fit.

An observable convicted as STATE_INHERITED is not repaired here. It is recorded as a consumer of the convergence work and its bound is re-examined once the state difference falls — because a bound written against a non-converged state may itself be the thing that is wrong.

Done when the 69 registered bounds are re-applied to the six held-out cases and the pass count is quoted against the banked 66 of 69 and 407 of 414, with any remaining failure carrying its verdict and its reason for remaining. A repair that moves a bound rather than a computation is refused unless the bound's derivation is shown to be the defect.

§4 — Make label parity an acceptance condition, not an afterthought

The throughput ladder's reproducibility argument is currently expressed on the state: a one-map bound at sixteen epsilon and terminal agreement conditioned on seed alignment. Nothing in it obliges the labels to agree, and the labels are the deliverable. This section closes that by making the registered observable bounds part of what a throughput rung must demonstrate.

It carries one measured constraint from the parity work that must not be forgotten here: a semantically zero representation change — a matrix widening carrying no numerical difference — was measured to move a reference root by 10.744 orders on this map. A batched path differs from a per-slice path in layout, accumulation order and kernel selection, all changes of that class. So label parity established at one batch size and device count says nothing about another, and this section's contribution is an acceptance condition each rung re-runs rather than a certificate issued once.

Done when the observable-bound check is expressed as a reusable acceptance the throughput rungs invoke, demonstrated at more than one batch size, with the per-batch pass counts quoted and any batch-size dependence stated explicitly.

§ Decisions

Should any terminal observable carry an exact-equality bound across two execution routes at all? moments.volume does, and it fails. Exact equality is the strongest possible statement and it is either exactly right — the quantity is discrete and must be identical — or it is a category error on a floating-point pipeline where layout and accumulation order legitimately differ. Answer it before repairing, since the two readings imply opposite fixes.

Decided on measurement, not argument. The discriminator evaluated moments.volume twice from ONE shared terminal state, through the scalar ForwardProfile.observe entry and through a jitted vmap of that same entry, on both platforms: the two paths are EXACTLY equal on CPU - a constant-zero difference vector across all six cases, which is why its correlations are undefined there - and differ by 4.440892098500626e-16 on the H200 at observe_moments.volume_reduction. A quantity that is bitwise identical on one execution platform and two units in the last place apart on another is not discrete, and an exact-equality bound on it is not a strong statement about the physics but a claim about a reduction order that the pipeline does not guarantee. The registration agrees: criterion-family.json records terminal_observable_envelopes_are_physics_tolerances = false and the registration itself declares the 69 envelopes frozen empirical envelopes measured for aligned-seed evaluation. So the bound is replaced by a derived tolerance. The tolerance must be DERIVED and not fitted: the section's own refusal rule and the method requirements forbid taking it from the achieved value of the quantity it scores, so it comes from the floating-point summation error of the reduction itself - a term proportional to the element count times the active-dtype epsilon times the value scale - which is a property of the algorithm and the dtype rather than of any measurement. This does not excuse the two continuous observables from repair: all three verdicts are COMPUTATION_DIFFERS and the named first-differing operations remain the repair surface. § Followups

Run §2 first and let its verdict scope §3 — do not assume the repair

§2 BEFORE §3, and the ordering is the point rather than a formality. The three failures could be defects in how each observable is computed, or they could be inherited from a terminal state difference that has not yet fallen because the solve does not converge. Those two readings imply OPPOSITE fixes — repair the evaluation path, or leave it alone and re-examine the bound once convergence lands — so any repair attempted before the discriminator runs has an even chance of being the wrong one. §2 needs no new solve and no new physics: it evaluates each failing observable on a single shared terminal state through both routes' paths, on data already banked.

ATTACK moments.volume FIRST. It fails an EXACT-EQUALITY bound, which cannot fail by accumulated rounding in a shared computation — it fails when the two routes evaluate structurally different things, such as a different cell count, mask, clip, or reduction association. It is therefore the sharpest probe in the set, and whatever explains it will probably explain the two continuous quantities computed over the same support.

THE OPEN DECISION IS LOAD-BEARING AND SHOULD BE ANSWERED BEFORE THE REPAIR, not after. exact-equality-bounds asks whether any terminal observable should carry exact equality across two execution routes at all. If a discrete quantity must be identical, the failure is a genuine defect; if it is a category error on a pipeline where layout and accumulation order legitimately differ, the bound is the defect. Repairing under the wrong reading produces a plausible-looking change to working code.

ONE MEASURED CONSTRAINT THAT MUST REACH §4: a SEMANTICALLY ZERO representation change — a matrix widening carrying no numerical difference — was measured to move a reference root by 10.744 orders on this map. A batched path differs from a per-slice path in layout, accumulation order and kernel selection, all changes of exactly that class. So a label-parity result established at one batch size and device count says nothing about another, which is why §4's deliverable is a re-runnable acceptance rather than a certificate.
