§1 — What replay is, and why the horizon finding demands it

No pulse has ever been replayed through the coupled forward model. The transport ladder is validated on a synthetic fixture (2.5 ms windows, longest coupled evolution ~11 ms) and DIII-D so far is per-slice: vacuum gate, differencing gate, Newton-Krylov free-boundary solves on individual labeled frames. The capability the ladder exists for — given a machine's actuator record, march the plasma state forward through time — is undemonstrated on any real machine.

The fixture's confinement horizon is the argument for replay, not against it. The time-parallel experiment's qualified negative measured that an uncontrolled equilibrium under sustained transport evolution leaves the confined branch in ~11 ms — with arbitrary fixed coil currents no controller ever chose. A real DIII-D discharge record inverts that premise: the recorded coil currents are the closed-loop currents (locked doctrine, DIII-D onboarding), the machine's real control actions are in the data, and replaying them supplies the feedback that kept the real plasma confined. What replay does not supply is correction of our model's drift relative to the real trajectory — the recorded currents corrected the real plasma, not our simulation of it. That residual open-loop drift is precisely the quantity this plan measures, and precisely what an ensemble filter later absorbs. Replay is therefore both the first real-machine demonstration and the baseline any assimilation work is scored against.

§2 — Segment replay, honestly costed

One diverted flat-top segment of one development-split shot, on the order of 100 ms (several labeled frames at the catalog cadence). Initialize the flux functions from the segment's first labeled frame through the landed differencing/extraction machinery; drive every window with the recorded coil-current waveforms interpolated onto the window time base; march coupled windows end-to-end with the shipped gate and damping defaults; carry the full receipt chain (per-window iterations, gating and all-field norms, ledger closures, branch class with causal selection history). Corpus access goes through the ambix-homed read/COCOS authority — nova carries no challenge-convention modules; this plan consumes conditioned inputs exactly as the onboarding plan's solve gates do.

Cost is stated, not hidden: at the measured ~66–82 s per 2.5 ms window on CPU, 100 ms is ~40 windows ≈ 45–55 CPU-minutes; the same-tree H200 lane (5.25× per iteration pair) brings a segment under ten minutes once the current-tree H200 measurement lands (transport plan followup). Full-shot replay is a stretch rung gated on the acceleration followups (sample/member batching, warm starts), not a promise of this plan.

Done when the segment marches to completion (or fails typed, which is a result: a replay that loses confinement under the real coil record is a measured model defect, not a crash), and the evidence records time-trace figures of nova-marched versus labeled q95, βN, boundary metrics and per-frame ψ RMS over the segment, every row tree-stamped.

§3 — The drift horizon

The replay analogue of the fixture's confinement horizon: how far does the marched state stay within a pre-registered tolerance band of the labeled trajectory before drift exceeds it? Pre-register the band (per-metric, from the per-slice solve residuals the onboarding plan banked) before the first scored run; report the horizon in milliseconds and in labeled frames; attribute the first band exit (flux-function drift, boundary drift, or transport-source mismatch) from the receipt chain. This number is the honest statement of what the physics forward alone gives an assimilator to work with — the forecast-skill floor an ensemble filter must beat to earn its keep.

§ Decisions

Replay a full pulse immediately, or a labeled flat-top segment first?

Owner direction 2026-08-22 to replay a pulse through the forward models, scoped by the measured window cost: a segment demonstrates the capability and measures the drift horizon at ~40 windows; a full shot at current wall times would spend hours before the first finding. Escalate scope with the measured acceleration, not ahead of it.
