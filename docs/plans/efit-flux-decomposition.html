§1 — There is ten times the plasma current outside the plasma

Masking the recovered current density to the stored LCFS did not discard the exterior signal — it quantified it. On shot 21978 slice 46 the 2783 operator-interior cells outside the boundary carry a Δ* signal of −9.605046 MA signed, 10.080226 MA absolute, against a plasma current of 0.925 MA. That is an order of magnitude more current outside the plasma than inside it.

The ordinary explanation is that this is real and correctly recovered. The poloidal field coils sit outside the LCFS and inside the grid; Δ*ψ evaluated there returns their current density, and MAST's coil set carries ampere-turns of exactly this order. On that reading the exterior signal is a validation of the operator, and reconstructing the external field from the recorded conductor currents should cancel most of it.

The superseded unmasked probe suggests it may not cancel. That run — not valid evidence for the masked contract, and reported here only as the reason this plan exists — measured an external term at 1.2726 of the stored flux span and a residual sup-norm at 1.2156 of it. If a masked, mask-consistent repeat reproduces figures of that size, our external field does not reproduce the reference's, and every free-boundary forward solve built on it inherits the error. That is the vacuum-field problem this plan exists to confirm or clear.

§2 — The operator round trip, which carries a bound

Before any residual can be read, the coupling operator must be shown to invert its own forward map. Derive j_φ from the stored flux via delta_star, mask it to the stored LCFS interior, drive nova's plasma-current-to-grid coupling with it, and require the reconstructed plasma flux to differ from the masked round trip by at most 1e-6 of the stored flux span. This is a pure self-consistency identity with no physics in it: exceeding the bound means the coupling operator, the grid interoperation, or the convention conversion is defective, and a residual computed on top of a defective operator is meaningless.

Grid interoperation is a named risk rather than an afterthought. The store's live plane is 65×65 on efm/gridz × efm/gridr and nova's grid need not coincide; the superseded probe reported an interpolation error of 4.16e-02 of the flux span, which is larger than most effects this ladder is trying to resolve. Any interpolation must therefore be named and its own error reported separately, so an interpolation artefact is never attributed to a missing conductor.

§3 — The external term, from the fitted currents

Reconstruct the external contribution on the same grid from the recorded conductor currents, reusing the described-response machinery in nova/imas/mast_vacuum_response.py and the prediction form already proven in nova/calibrate/gain_check.py — that path forms an exact vacuum prediction as recorded currents times a described response row and is validated at sensor locations, so this rung extends a proven operator to a grid rather than writing a second vacuum-prediction path.

The external field is driven from EFIT's fitted currents, efm/fcoil_c, and this is not a firewall breach. The firewall exists to stop EFIT's solution leaking into a fit that is then scored against EFIT, which would be circular. Nothing here is fitted: the question is whether nova's forward solve, given identical sources and currents, produces the same field. Since the stored map was produced with fcoil_c, driving our external field from the experimental fcoil_x instead would confound a solver difference with an input difference — the experimental vector makes the comparison weaker, not more independent. The DINA demonstration that already succeeded used its reference's own declared currents for the same reason.

Report the fcoil_x figure beside it where that is cheap. The difference between the two vectors quantifies how far EFIT moved the currents from experiment, which bears directly on the vacuum-field question this plan exists to settle: a residual that shrinks under fitted currents but survives under experimental ones is a statement about the current measurement rather than about our field model.

These routines have value beyond this diagnosis, which raises the bar on how they are written. Separating the plasma term from a stored flux map is what yields flux-surface-averaged geometry, and that geometry is the input to training one-dimensional transport equations for the competition entry — the same record flux-function-forward-transport consumes as a public output contract of ForwardProfile. So the decomposition should be built as a reusable capability with the reference read behind an adapter, not as a one-slice diagnostic script that happens to answer this question.

§4 — The residual and the current it implies

Report stored − masked plasma − external as a sup-norm and a root-mean-square, each as a fraction of the stored flux span. These figures carry no threshold and none may be applied. A large residual is the informative outcome of this plan and must not be reduced by fitting, by masking beyond the stated LCFS mask, or by excluding regions.

Then apply delta_star to the residual. Because Δ* returns a current density from a flux, the residual's operator is a map of the current our model does not carry: report its peak in A/m², its integral in A, and the R,Z coordinates of its largest magnitude relative to the named conductors. A residual current that localises on a known conductor is a current-magnitude or turns error; one that localises on the vessel wall is a passive-structure omission; one that localises where no conductor exists is either a geometry error or an artefact, and the interpolation figure from §2 is what discriminates the last case.

Done-when: the round trip meets its 1e-6 bound or the operator defect is named; the external term and the residual are reported as fractions of the flux span; and the residual's implied current is quantified and localised, with a stated verdict on whether the external field is fit to serve as a free-boundary boundary condition.

§5 — Handing a plasma-subtracted map to the geometry solve

The repair route already exists and has been waiting for this input. coil-geometry-inversion defines two problem classes, the second of which is "flux-map-space (currents fixed, match a plasma-subtracted flux map)" — and a plasma-subtracted flux map is exactly what §2–§4 produce. Its Green's kernels are already verified autograd-able with respect to coil geometry (jacfwd against finite differences to 1e-6), so if the residual localises on a conductor, solving for that conductor's position or current is a gradient optimisation on machinery that is already built.

Trigger: a residual that survives §4 with an implied current localised on or near a named conductor. The handoff is the plasma-subtracted map plus the residual current field; the solve itself belongs to that plan, not this one. If instead the external reconstruction cancels the exterior signal and the residual is negligible, this section closes as unnecessary and that is the better outcome — it would mean the vacuum field is sound and the forward demonstration can proceed directly.

Two neighbouring plans are informed by whatever this measures, and neither duplicates it. vacuum-signature-extraction extracts instrument terms from vacuum time-windows in sensor signals; its archive sweep is currently held because its observable "cannot separate an acquisition-state change from a drive or pair-state change or from a response-model change", and this plan measures that third confound independently, from a different instrument and on a 2D map. mast-misfit-toroidal-harmonics built source-free harmonic maps of the magnetics misfit; the Δ* residual here is the same idea applied to a flux map, and a conductor implicated by both is implicated twice over.

§ Decisions

Should the residual carry a pass/fail bound like the other gates in this programme?

The round trip in §2 is an operator identity and carries a real bound at 1e-6 of the flux span. The residual is not of that kind: its magnitude is the measurement this plan exists to obtain, and a pre-registered bound would create an incentive to reduce it by masking or region exclusion — the exact contamination the LCFS-mask decision was made to prevent. A large residual holds downstream work by being reported, not by failing a threshold.
