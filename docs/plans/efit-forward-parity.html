§1 — The demonstration nova owes, and the one it made instead

nova is a forward physics model. Its locked class matrix puts prescribed sources to equilibrium flux in ForwardProfile, measurements to a fitted profile state in ReconstructProfile, and shape targets to conductor currents in InverseDesign. The parity claim that matters for a forward model is therefore: given a reference equilibrium's own flux functions and conductor currents, does the free-boundary Grad–Shafranov fixed point reproduce its field?

That has never been run. The existing chain drove ReconstructProfile.least_squares_map(...) through newton_krylov — a whitened least-squares fit to measured magnetics with a hard measured-current equality. It inferred the flux functions rather than being given them, which is why it needed no reference profiles, and it scored a fitted state against EFIT. Its converged_fraction = 0 across all six frozen shots is a fit failing to converge; it is not evidence about the Grad–Shafranov fixed point. The two metrics that passed — the CurrentDiffusion flux ledger and throughput — are the two that never depended on the fit closing.

§2 — What the solve is given

ForwardProfile.solve(initial_flux, *, route="newton_krylov", current=...) takes a trial flux and conductor currents, with the prescribed flux functions carried on its source state. Every input exists in the reference file, censused directly rather than assumed:

397 rows across the frozen six carry the complete forward input set — flux functions, a finite map, and both conductor vectors — which is exactly the usable geometry count, so requiring full forward inputs costs no coverage.

Free-boundary means the boundary condition is the external field, which is why this plan depends on efit-flux-decomposition. If our conductor model does not reproduce the reference's external flux, the fixed point is being asked to close against the wrong boundary and a deviation cannot be attributed to the solve. The decomposition's verdict on whether the external field is fit to serve as a boundary condition is this plan's entry gate.

§3 — Seeding, and not smuggling the answer in

Seeding from the reference's own flux map would weaken the claim. A fixed point started at the answer can converge in zero meaningful iterations and reproduce it trivially; the demonstration would then show only that EFIT's map is a fixed point of our operator to within the convergence tolerance, which is a weaker statement than reproducing it from elsewhere. Conversely a neutral seed may not converge at all on an axisymmetrically unstable state — the measured map eigenvalue on a diverted reference case is 1.25 to 1.40, which is why newton_krylov is the default route and no relaxed route rescues it.

So the seed is a declared axis of the demonstration, not an implementation detail: report the comparison from a reference seed and from a moment-seeded or neutral start, and state the iteration counts for each. The reference-seeded run bounds how close the operator's fixed point sits to EFIT's map; the independently-seeded run is the one that carries the parity claim.

Two registered metrics do not carry over, and saying so is part of the correction. The whitened raw-magnetics residual and the qualified-magnetics bar exist to referee a magnetics comparison, and a forward solve makes none — no measurement enters it. They stay valid for whatever reconstruction work owns that comparison; they are simply not part of a forward parity verdict. profile_residual_rms also changes meaning: with the flux functions prescribed rather than fitted, the cross-substrate current reproduction it measures becomes a check on the source evaluation rather than on a fit.

The calibrated expectation comes from the demonstration that already worked. ForwardProfile reproduced DINA 135011/7 slice 353 on a production 1587-cell hexagonal ITER mesh to I_p −1.12%, β_p +2.24%, l_i −0.135% and axis 41.2 mm, with the residual growing under refinement and all four candidate causes since excluded by measurement. This plan is that same pattern pointed at a second reference, so a deviation of order a percent is the expected outcome rather than a failure — and the DINA experience says the interesting question will be whether the residual grows under refinement here too.

Done-when: the frozen six are scored on the metric set above from both seeding conditions, per-slice and per-shot, with pass/fail against the carried-over registered tolerances and reported deviations for the new field and moment metrics; the verdict states whether the free-boundary fixed point reproduces the reference within those bounds; and the GPU catalog is unblocked or held on that verdict rather than on the reconstruction result it is currently held on.

§5 — What this replaces, and what it does not

This plan supersedes the frozen-six reconstruction gate as the parity claim for nova. That gate's result is not withdrawn — it is a real measurement of an inverse fit, and its banked record stands — but it is not evidence about the forward model and must not be cited as such. In particular the accelerator hypothesis raised from it, that accelerator-primary's synthetic self-consistent locking evidence may not generalise to real data, was drawn from a least-squares fit's convergence and cannot be tested by one; if the forward fixed point converges here on the same machine and slices, that hypothesis is substantially weakened without the accelerator being touched.

What this plan does not do: it does not fit profile coefficients, infer a state from diagnostics, or claim anything about reconstruction quality. Reconstruction against measured magnetics is a real capability with real value — it is simply Ambix's side of the seam in the intended architecture, and ReconstructProfile remains nova's implementation of it for callers who want it.

§ Decisions

Which seeding condition carries the parity claim: the reference's own flux map, an independent moment seed, or both reported side by side?

Owner ruling 2026-08-21: the reference-seeded solve carries the initial parity demonstration, and the independent-seed claim upgrades through the dual-basin strategy rather than through seed engineering - where both basins exist, solve a diverted AND a limited version side by side (cheap on the batched GPU route) with the selection made afterwards from history (hysteresis), which also blocks the in-solve topology switches that crash convergence. The dual-basin machinery is owned by the dual-basin-solve plan (S8); this plan consumes it when the independent-seed parity claim is made.

Do the whitened raw-magnetics residual and the qualified-magnetics bar form part of a forward parity verdict?

Owner direction 2026-08-13: nova is a forward physics model, not an inverse solver. A forward solve consumes prescribed sources and conductor currents and reads no magnetics, so a whitened magnetics residual has no input to score and the cross-source budget that referees it has nothing to referee. Both stay valid for reconstruction work and are retained in the registry for that use; they are excluded from this plan's verdict rather than deleted.

normalization-anchors

Owner ruling 2026-08-21, forced by the DINA finding: a stored reference's profiles are tabulated against ITS declared psi_axis/psi_boundary, and those declarations can disagree with the reference's own flux map (DINA 135011/7: 0.411 Wb, half a percent of span - the drift that consumed the recovery programme until convicted). Reproduction runs therefore normalize psi_norm AND bound the plasma support in the reference's own declared coordinate (the prescribed-anchor mode); nova's own topology read stays authoritative for free-standing solves. Gauge discipline binds: nova's Biot-Savart composition sets its flux gauge, and no comparison may mix nova-gauge amplitudes with reference-gauge constants. Every stored reference is checked for the declared-boundary-vs-map-saddle inconsistency class before any deviation is attributed.

Which flux-function source drives the stored DINA reproduction: the declared profiles_1d, extraction from the stored map, or both?

Owner directive 2026-08-21: use the existing infrastructure to extract the flux functions from the flux maps (the affine per-surface projection) in addition to the profiles the dataset lists (equilibrium IDS profiles_1d, paths located with the imas-dd tools; read with imas-python at the written DD version). The two routes bound the reference's internal consistency in the profile domain; neither is discarded, and route disagreement is reported rather than reconciled.
