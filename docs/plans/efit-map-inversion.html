§1 — Read the reference before scoring against it

The parity chain built so far does not test nova's forward model. run_parity_chain drives ReconstructProfile.least_squares_map(...) through newton_krylov: it forms target = measured − source_to_sensor @ source_current, whitens by a per-sensor scale, and carries a hard measured-current equality. That is an inverse solve which infers the flux functions from magnetics. nova's own locked contract puts that outside the forward class — flux-function-forward-equilibrium §2 requires the forward solve to have "no magnetic measurements, no whitening matrix, no priors and no least-squares coefficient update". So the frozen-six verdict measures a reconstruction, and its converged_fraction = 0 is a least-squares fit failing to converge, not the Grad–Shafranov fixed point failing.

The demonstration that belongs in nova is the forward one: give the solve prescribed flux functions and conductor currents, find the free-boundary fixed point, and compare the field to the reference. That needs the reference's own sources as inputs, which the chain never had — which is precisely why it fitted them instead.

This plan is the first rung: prove we can read a stored EFIT equilibrium correctly. Every quantity here has a known-good answer already in the file, so each check is a comparison against truth rather than against another solve. If we cannot recover EFIT's own current density and flux functions from EFIT's own flux map, then no comparison built on top of that reading means anything — and the geometry metrics in the existing gate are uninterpretable for the same reason.

§4 — Current density and flux functions on the LCFS interior

The Grad–Shafranov relation run backwards recovers the source from the field. nova/equilibrium/conservation.py supplies delta_star with the convention documented in place: μ₀ j_φ = −Δ*Φ / (2π R). Multiplying the current-density expression by R makes it affine in R², so per flux surface a straight regression returns both flux functions:

R j_φ = R² p′(ψ) + FF′(ψ)/μ₀ — slope p′, intercept FF′/μ₀.

The prototype measurement already landed, and it establishes the rung. On shot 21978 slice 46 (t = 0.244999990 s), the stored 151-vertex LCFS partitions the 61×61 operator interior into 938 plasma cells and 2783 exterior cells. Over 24 fixed bins with zero exclusions:

The LCFS mask is what makes this work, and its effect is measured rather than argued. The same prototype without the mask admitted 1421 cells and closed the plasma current at −42.88%; masking to the stored boundary admits 938 and closes at −0.0275% — a factor of about 1560. §5 — Whether two terms are enough

They are not, in about half the bins. Refitting each bin with a third R⁴ column resolved that coefficient above its own standard error in 11 of 24 bins, reducing scatter by a median 1.171796e-02. An R⁴ term in R j_φ is the signature of a pressure that depends explicitly on major radius — toroidal rotation, for which nova already carries a locked isothermal closure and banked rotating references.

But three readings fit that evidence and they are not equivalent, so this section is a separation, not an implementation. The term may be physical (EFIT fitted a rotation or otherwise R-dependent source); it may be numerical (thin bins near the axis and near the boundary hold few cells over a short R² lever, so a third column absorbs noise); or it may be geometric (near-boundary bins clipped by the mask sample an asymmetric part of each surface). The scatter distribution is consistent with the second: the maximum two-term scatter fraction is 0.571 against a median of 0.014, so a small number of bins dominate.

Done-when: the 11 bins are identified by flux-surface position and cell population, and the physical reading is separated from the numerical one by a measurement — refitting under a binning that equalises cell population, and testing whether the recovered R⁴ coefficient is consistent in sign and magnitude with a single rotation profile across bins rather than scattering as noise would. If it is physical, the recovered rotation parametrisation is reported as a third forward input; if numerical, the two-term model stands with the bin-population floor recorded.

§ Decisions

Over what domain are plasma-side quantities — the flux-function regression and the plasma-current integral — computed from a recovered current density?

Owner direction 2026-08-13: mask the plasma current with the given LCFS for both the Δ* solve and the flux-function coefficient calculation, so missing conductors cannot pollute the solve. Measured: the unmasked prototype admitted 1421 cells and closed the plasma current at −42.88%, while the stored-LCFS mask admits 938 and closes at −0.0275%. The mask does double duty — it keeps conductor current out of the plasma-side fit, and it isolates the exterior Δ* signal as a quantified missing-conductor diagnostic rather than discarding it. A normalised-flux threshold is not equivalent: it selects on a value, and private-flux and exterior regions can sit below one.

The efm file declares no COCOS. Data-derived digits are compatible with source COCOS 3, but σ_RφZ is undeterminable from the stored scalars. Do we declare COCOS 3 externally on the strength of the derived digits, or gate the ladder on documentary confirmation of the frame?

Owner declaration 2026-08-13: MAST is COCOS 3. The census's data-derived digits agree (e_Bp = 0, sigma_Bp = -1, sigma_rho_theta_phi = -1 on all six shots), so the declaration and the derived evidence are consistent and sigma_RphiZ is settled by the declaration rather than left undeterminable. ONE CAUTION CARRIED WITH IT, and it is not a formality: check for convention changes with respect to the SIGN OF PLASMA CURRENT rather than assuming the declaration fixes it once. An Ip sign flip between the machine's recording convention and COCOS, or between campaign eras, would silently invert the plasma term and every quantity derived from it while leaving magnitudes plausible. MAST has already demonstrated era-dependent convention hazards in this programme: 19 magnetics channels carry discrete unnormalised acquisition scales stepping by 1/2, 1/sqrt2, sqrt2 and 2 across shot blocks, which produced phantom campaign physics until they were divided out on the read path. A sign convention that moved across eras is the same class of trap. The check is therefore per shot, not once: agreement in sign between plasma_current_x, plasma_current_c, the delta_star-derived masked integral, and the declared COCOS 3 frame, reported for every frozen shot.

The store carries conductor currents twice: efm/fcoil_x as experimental input and efm/fcoil_c as EFIT's own fitted output. Which drives our external field?

Owner direction 2026-08-13: use the fitted EFIT currents. This is not a firewall issue. The firewall exists to stop EFIT's SOLUTION leaking into a FIT that is then scored against EFIT, which would be circular; here nothing is fitted, and the question is whether nova's forward solve, given identical sources and currents, produces the same field. Driving from fcoil_x would confound a solver difference with an input difference, because EFIT's stored map was produced with fcoil_c - so the experimental vector makes the comparison weaker, not more independent. It also matches the DINA demonstration that already succeeded, which used that reference's own declared coil currents. A secondary motive stands on its own: the flux-map decomposition routines have value beyond diagnosing this failure, as the plasma-term separation feeds flux-surface-averaged geometry for training one-dimensional transport equations in the competition entry. Where it is cheap, report the fcoil_x figure beside the fcoil_c one - the difference quantifies how far EFIT moved the currents from experiment, which is itself relevant to the vacuum-field question.
