§1 — Why the current quadrature is the wrong instrument

The exact clip books each cut cell's current by integrating the profile's current density over the clipped plasma polygon of that cell. The production integrator (nova/equilibrium/clip_quadrature.py, clipped_support_current_moments and clipped_support_field_integrals) does this by fanning the clipped polygon into triangles from a sampled boundary arc and applying an eight-by-eight Gauss product rule on every triangle, which comes to about 196,480 evaluation points per cut cell. The lead's ruling of 2026-09-16 is that this is out of proportion to what the moments are for: the coupling that consumes them images each cell's zeroth, first and second moments through polygon-analytic kernel blocks, so the integrator only has to deliver those moments to the accuracy the second-order coupling can use. A method should return accuracy comparable to the scheme it supports, not four orders more evaluation points than the scheme can exploit.

The cost is not academic. The exact-clip solve at 1000 cells spends its device memory in one 8.4 GB allocation, which is this workspace evaluated for every cell at once (peak 8.0 GiB on the weak 1000 row, the memory node's receipt under docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/exact-clip-memory/); the compiled program carries the quadrature body once per clipped moment path; and the solve wall of every exact-clip row so far, about 1250 s at 135, 341 and 1072 cells alike, is compile dominated by the same body. The whole-cell route compiles its trip to 25 ms; the exact clip has never had its warm execution wall measured because the compile hides it. The measurement node in flight (cca-exact-clip-warm-cost-and-workspace) supplies the baseline this plan is measured against.

Where the flux inside a cut cell comes from settles how many points the integral can possibly need. On the hex certificate carriers there is no global spline: the flux in a cell is its own six-coefficient quadratic (support_flux_coefficients) fitted to the centroid value and the six vertex sample values, the same interpolant the clip uses for its edge roots, and the audit of 2026-09-14 found the global tensor-spline projection ill-conditioned on hex data (4e-4 to 1.8e-3 of span residual at the data points) so it is not used there. On rectangular lattices the level arc may come from the tensor-spline patch, which the connectivity read, flux-surface extraction and the coefficient carrier rely on (fit 0.22 s on CPU); the certificate path does not. The information content of the flux in a cut cell is therefore six numbers, the density is a low-degree polynomial of that quadratic, and a rule of a handful of nodes per edge integrates its moments exactly; the fan's 196,480 points add evaluation, not information.

§2 — Polygon-exact moments at the coupling's order

The clipped region is a polygon with at most 24 vertices whose boundary is straight edges plus the traced level arc, and the density is the profile evaluated on the cell's local flux representation, a low-degree polynomial in the local coordinates once the level arc is replaced by its chord-and-sagitta expansion at the order the coupling uses. Moments of a polynomial over a polygon are boundary integrals by the divergence theorem, exact with a Gauss rule of a handful of nodes per edge, and the same polygon-analytic machinery already used for the kernel blocks (nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py, polygon_analytic_flux_moments) integrates the geometric moments of the region exactly. This section replaces the fan quadrature by that boundary integration: per clipped polygon, the zeroth, first and second moments of the density times the toroidal volume weight, evaluated to the same order as the coupling blocks, with the level arc represented consistently with the clip. Done-when: on the four analytic rows at 110, 300 and 1000 cells the new moments agree with the fan quadrature's to the fan quadrature's own convergence floor (measured by doubling the fan density on the 110 row, stated as a number per moment order), the gate B frozen-current flux image is unchanged to that floor, the evaluation-point count per cut cell falls from about 196,480 to under 200, and the per-cell workspace is bounded by the vertex capacity rather than by a sample fan.

§4 — Size the cut-cell polygon capacity to the realised support

§ Decisions

How is the traced level arc represented inside the polygon-exact moment integral: as its chord (first order), as chord plus sagitta (second order, matching the coupling), or by the spline itself with a per-edge Gauss rule?

Lead 2026-09-16 (1c): the traced level arc enters the moment integral as the clip's own sampled arc, each of its fixed 128 segments a straight edge carrying a per-edge Gauss rule, so the region integrated is exactly the region the clip books. Evidence: with pointwise density this reproduces the fan to the last bit on weak 110, while chord plus sagitta with the same density moves the first moments by 2.5e-3 and the frozen image by 1.0e-4 of span, larger than the coupling's own second-order image error (1.2e-4 to 3.2e-5 of span), and the density model contributes only 1e-6. Two conditions from the lead: the shape is fixed (the arc sample count, the straight-edge capacity and the per-edge node count are compile-time constants, padding exact-zero, no data-dependent branching), and the accuracy budget is sized relative to the other error sources on the same row rather than set absolutely: the moments and frozen image must agree with the fan to within one tenth of the smallest other error term measured on that row (the second-order coupling image error, 3.2e-5 of span at 300 cells, sets 3e-6 of span there), stated per row in the receipt with the error terms it is measured against; the per-edge Gauss order is the lowest that meets that budget, and the evaluation-point bound is the fixed edge count times that order, expected under 500.

moment-integration

Lead 2026-09-17, superseding the per-edge Gauss rule of decision arc-representation (1c) after the order study: the source flux is a quadratic fit on seven nodes and the destination is a second-order current distribution, so a sixth-order quadrature exact to 1e-12 pays for precision the interpolant cannot support. The density is a known quadratic in local coordinates, so its area, first and second moments over a polygon with straight edges are exact closed forms per edge (Green's theorem), with no quadrature order to argue about; the only approximation is the polyline arc, whose vertex count is sized to the quadratic fit's own O(h^3) error rather than fixed at 128, gated per row by the locked budget rule (one tenth of the smallest other error term on that row). Consequences: the per-edge Gauss order constant and the 500-evaluation ceiling are retired; the fixed-shape condition of 1c stands (arc vertex capacity a compile-time constant, exact-zero padding); memory and trace cost are first-class measures of the landing beside the moment agreement.

What fences the five rows whose other error term sits at the fan's refinement floor?

Measured by the fallback-floor node 2026-09-19. Three candidates were exercised. One tenth of the fan's own refinement floor lands at 2.9e-17 to 5.1e-16, below any polyline's round-off floor of 1.5e-15 to 2.5e-15 at 128 vertices, so no arc count can meet it. The closed form's own round-off envelope, now measured at 2.038e-16 current, 2.060e-15 radial and 4.789e-16 vertical, is a real floor but the five rows are still unmet against it. The node's own follow-on argues the fence should instead be the retained fan's refinement floor expressed in the moment relative L2 norm, which is the norm the rows are judged in, rather than either of the two above.

How is the traced polygon vertex capacity set for a cell whose level set enters twice?

Measured by the capacity record-repair node 2026-09-19 on the diverted single-null row at 110 cells. The derived bound is a one-arc bound, 128 arc samples plus the straight chain, giving 162 for that row.
