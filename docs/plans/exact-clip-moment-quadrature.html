§1 — Why the current quadrature is the wrong instrument

The exact clip books each cut cell's current by integrating the profile's current density over the clipped plasma polygon of that cell. The production integrator (nova/equilibrium/clip_quadrature.py, clipped_support_current_moments and clipped_support_field_integrals) does this by fanning the clipped polygon into triangles from a sampled boundary arc and applying an eight-by-eight Gauss product rule on every triangle, which comes to about 196,480 evaluation points per cut cell. The lead's ruling of 2026-09-16 is that this is out of proportion to what the moments are for: the coupling that consumes them images each cell's zeroth, first and second moments through polygon-analytic kernel blocks, so the integrator only has to deliver those moments to the accuracy the second-order coupling can use. A method should return accuracy comparable to the scheme it supports, not four orders more evaluation points than the scheme can exploit.

The cost is not academic. The exact-clip solve at 1000 cells spends its device memory in one 8.4 GB allocation, which is this workspace evaluated for every cell at once (peak 8.0 GiB on the weak 1000 row, the memory node's receipt under docs/figures/cut-cell-current-attribution/exact-clip-memory/); the compiled program carries the quadrature body once per clipped moment path; and the solve wall of every exact-clip row so far, about 1250 s at 135, 341 and 1072 cells alike, is compile dominated by the same body. The whole-cell route compiles its trip to 25 ms; the exact clip has never had its warm execution wall measured because the compile hides it. The measurement node in flight (cca-exact-clip-warm-cost-and-workspace) supplies the baseline this plan is measured against.

§2 — Polygon-exact moments at the coupling's order

The clipped region is a polygon with at most 24 vertices whose boundary is straight edges plus the traced level arc, and the density is the profile evaluated on the cell's local flux representation, a low-degree polynomial in the local coordinates once the level arc is replaced by its chord-and-sagitta expansion at the order the coupling uses. Moments of a polynomial over a polygon are boundary integrals by the divergence theorem, exact with a Gauss rule of a handful of nodes per edge, and the same polygon-analytic machinery already used for the kernel blocks (nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py, polygon_analytic_flux_moments) integrates the geometric moments of the region exactly. This section replaces the fan quadrature by that boundary integration: per clipped polygon, the zeroth, first and second moments of the density times the toroidal volume weight, evaluated to the same order as the coupling blocks, with the level arc represented consistently with the clip. Done-when: on the four analytic rows at 110, 300 and 1000 cells the new moments agree with the fan quadrature's to the fan quadrature's own convergence floor (measured by doubling the fan density on the 110 row, stated as a number per moment order), the gate B frozen-current flux image is unchanged to that floor, the evaluation-point count per cut cell falls from about 196,480 to under 200, and the per-cell workspace is bounded by the vertex capacity rather than by a sample fan.

§3 — Land it in the solve and measure the cost

With the moments exact and small, the exact-clip solve's memory and compile should be set by the interaction matrix and the topology read, not by the clip. Done-when: the memory instrument (benchmarks/exact_clip_memory_scaling.py) reports the 1000-cell exact-clip peak and largest allocation before and after, the warm-cost instrument reports the compiled trip wall and the compile wall before and after at 110, 300 and 1000 cells, the twelve-test exact-clip module and every module importing the changed files pass one per fresh CPU process with zero added failures, the weak 110 and 1000 exact-clip rows re-solve from the repaired seed to the same fixed points (agreement to within 1e-10 of span stated), and the whole-cell certificate rows and the single-null control remain bit-identical because they never traverse the clip.

§ Decisions

How is the traced level arc represented inside the polygon-exact moment integral: as its chord (first order), as chord plus sagitta (second order, matching the coupling), or by the spline itself with a per-edge Gauss rule?

Polygon-exact clipped-cell moments at the coupling's order, gated on the fan quadrature's own floor

Replace the triangle-fan Gauss product rule in clip_quadrature.py with boundary integration of the density moments over the clipped polygon at the order the second-order coupling blocks use, decide the level-arc representation (the plan's open decision), and gate on agreement with the fan quadrature to its own convergence floor on the four analytic rows at 110, 300 and 1000 cells, the frozen-current flux image unchanged, and under 200 evaluation points per cut cell.
