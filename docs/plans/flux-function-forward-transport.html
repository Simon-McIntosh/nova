§1 — Authority and capability

ForwardTransport joins ForwardProfile as Nova's second deterministic physics forward. Given a flux-surface-averaged geometry record, an initial 1D profile state, boundary and actuator waveforms, and a declared source and transport-model selection, it advances the flux-surface state over an interval and reports conservation receipts — the flux-consumption ledger, the plasma-current ledger, and the achieved boundary conditions. It takes no measurements, contains no least-squares update, and never rescales its inputs silently. Fidelity is an explicit model selection carried in the result provenance, not an approximation the consumer must reverse-engineer.

The authority split with Ambix is unchanged from the flux-function seam: Ambix owns inference, closure corrections, uncertainty and causal provenance; it conditions the state that enters this solve and consumes the state that leaves it. Nova owns the deterministic evolution operator, exactly as it owns the free-boundary fixed point. The evolved p′ and FF′ hand back through the same typed FluxFunctionState contract that feeds ForwardProfile — transport is a producer at that seam, not a private pipeline.

§2 — Fidelity ladder

Transport fidelity is a ladder of declared rungs behind one public contract. Each rung is a physics forward with identical input and receipt types; selecting a rung changes the physics carried, never the seam.

The native rung stays permanently: it is the dependency-free, fully differentiable floor, already verified to <1% RMS against TORAX itself on shared geometry, and it is the correct vehicle where a learned or fitted closure is the object of study. The embedded rungs buy the verified physics surface Nova will not reimplement — coupled heat and particle transport, neoclassical closures, the source catalogue, adaptive nonlinear stepping, pedestal and sawtooth models. Evidence and the full capability-gap inventory: transport-forward-contract.

The coupled rung's contract unit is the window, and its exchange is a waveform. Equilibrium and transport advance at different native rates, so the coupled solve works on a declared time window with each side consuming the other's trajectory: transport integrates against the interpolated geometry waveform, the equilibrium solves at its coarse sample times against the interpolated source waveform, and the exchange iterates to a fixed point (waveform relaxation). Conventional staggered co-simulation is this contract truncated at one iteration, so both schemes are points on one measured axis rather than competing designs. A window carries four declared knobs — window length, per-side sample grids, iteration cap and convergence tolerance — and returns convergence receipts: iterations used, a contraction estimate, exit residual per exchanged field, and any damping applied. Window length is a policy the receipts justify — short where shaping evolves quickly, long in flat-top — with the contraction estimate as the adaptive signal. A window that cannot converge raises; it never returns a silently degraded state.

Innovation purity is the principle that assigns the fixed point to Nova. The assimilation layer upstream learns from model–measurement innovation, and an unconverged seam would confound that signal with numerical splitting error — teaching corrections to compensate the solver rather than the plasma. Nova therefore converges the window to its declared tolerance and surrenders the convergence behaviour as typed receipts, which the consumer classifies beside the innovation: slow contraction is itself physics. Gradients cross the window implicitly — a converged fixed point differentiates by the implicit-function adjoint without unrolling the iteration, so learned closures train through the coupled solve exactly when it converges. The window machinery is public at two granularities: the converged solve_window with receipts and implicit gradients is the default, and the single-sweep equilibrium/transport waveform primitives remain public pure functions for declared compositions that own their own convergence receipts.

§5 — Sequencing and exclusions

The dependency gate is first and alone; it is small but it gates every other item. The FSA record completion and COCOS tests can proceed against the native rung in parallel once the environment resolves. The coupled loop lands last, after ForwardProfile's static absolute-source contract is green in the sibling plan, which this plan depends on.

Excluded: developing turbulent-transport or pedestal models (TORAX owns its physics; Nova validates the boundary); any measurement fitting or profile identification inside the forward solve; field-aligned/poloidal SOL current (deferred with the same boundary-current reasoning as the equilibrium sibling); porting TORAX's geometry construction to jnp for end-to-end differentiability, unless the loop-differentiability decision is revisited with a load-bearing gradient consumer. Consumer-side migrations — lifting the ambix estimators off whole-run TORAX calls and retiring the torch donor — belong to the ambix integration plan, not here.

Which route hands the Nova FSA record to TORAX? Does the coupled equilibrium–transport loop need end-to-end gradients? Recommendation: Picard fixed-point coupling first — gradients flow through transport steps but not through geometry construction — deferring a jnp port of the geometry builder until a specific gradient consumer (design optimisation or whole-loop calibration) is named, because the port is real work with no current load-bearing user.

Resolved by the windowed-waveform design: the converged window differentiates by the implicit-function adjoint without unrolling, so learned closures train through the coupled solve exactly when it converges. The jnp geometry adapter built directly from the traced FSA record bypasses TORAX's host-side geometry builder and keeps that gradient path intact; a port of TORAX's own builder stays unnecessary until a consumer needs gradients through TORAX's reader path itself.
