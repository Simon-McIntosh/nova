§1 — Authority and capability

ForwardTransport joins ForwardProfile as Nova's second deterministic physics forward. Given a flux-surface-averaged geometry record, an initial 1D profile state, boundary and actuator waveforms, and a declared source and transport-model selection, it advances the flux-surface state over an interval and reports conservation receipts — the flux-consumption ledger, the plasma-current ledger, and the achieved boundary conditions. It takes no measurements, contains no least-squares update, and never rescales its inputs silently. Fidelity is an explicit model selection carried in the result provenance, not an approximation the consumer must reverse-engineer.

The authority split with Ambix is unchanged from the flux-function seam: Ambix owns inference, closure corrections, uncertainty and causal provenance; it conditions the state that enters this solve and consumes the state that leaves it. Nova owns the deterministic evolution operator, exactly as it owns the free-boundary fixed point. The evolved p′ and FF′ hand back through the same typed FluxFunctionState contract that feeds ForwardProfile — transport is a producer at that seam, not a private pipeline.

The coupled rung's contract unit is the window, and its exchange is a waveform. Equilibrium and transport advance at different native rates, so the coupled solve works on a declared time window with each side consuming the other's trajectory: transport integrates against the interpolated geometry waveform, the equilibrium solves at its coarse sample times against the interpolated source waveform, and the exchange iterates to a fixed point (waveform relaxation). Conventional staggered co-simulation is this contract truncated at one iteration, so both schemes are points on one measured axis rather than competing designs. A window carries four declared knobs — window length, per-side sample grids, iteration cap and convergence tolerance — and returns convergence receipts: iterations used, a contraction estimate, exit residual per exchanged field, and any damping applied. Window length is a policy the receipts justify — short where shaping evolves quickly, long in flat-top — with the contraction estimate as the adaptive signal. A window that cannot converge raises; it never returns a silently degraded state.

Innovation purity is the principle that assigns the fixed point to Nova. The assimilation layer upstream learns from model–measurement innovation, and an unconverged seam would confound that signal with numerical splitting error — teaching corrections to compensate the solver rather than the plasma. Nova therefore converges the window to its declared tolerance and surrenders the convergence behaviour as typed receipts, which the consumer classifies beside the innovation: slow contraction is itself physics. Gradients cross the window implicitly — a converged fixed point differentiates by the implicit-function adjoint without unrolling the iteration, so learned closures train through the coupled solve exactly when it converges. The window machinery is public at two granularities: the converged solve_window with receipts and implicit gradients is the default, and the single-sweep equilibrium/transport waveform primitives remain public pure functions for declared compositions that own their own convergence receipts.

§7 — FSA moment accuracy

The independent reference located a defect in the kernel, not the seam. Against TORAX reading the same real EQDSK, the record disagrees by 18–26% on g2g3_over_rhon — the ψ-diffusion coefficient itself — 10–37% across the ∇ψ moments, 10–17% on enclosed current, and 10–22% on triangularity, while scalars, F and ψ are accurate to well under a percent and the three-way COCOS check is bit-clean. Until these close, a transport solve on this record is solving a measurably different problem from the one TORAX would solve from the same file, so §6's remaining items — the real coupled window and the physics figures — wait on this section rather than producing numbers nobody should quote.

The kernel discards information the discretisation already carries. ψ is known at grid nodes; jnp.gradient evaluates ∇ψ at those nodes; the Gaussian-CDF shell weight is evaluated at those nodes; and each cell's entire 2πR dr dz volume is attributed to its own node's ψ. Every surface average is therefore a node-sampled midpoint rule against a smeared indicator, and two errors compound: a cell is wholly in or out of a shell according to one sample, and the bandwidth (1.25 × surface spacing) then smears across neighbouring shells. The extrema are worse still — min and max over selected nodes, which quantises R_in, R_out, elongation and triangularity to one cell.

The integrators for this already exist, and this record is the one place that reinvented them. Nova carries exact traced clipped-cell polygon quadrature in nova/equilibrium/separatrix_clip.py — TracedClippedSupports with per-cell area, full_area, first_area_moment, second_area_moment and contour_area, padded_polynomial_current_moments over a complete polynomial basis, and _traced_clip keeping it jit/vmap-safe — built and validated for the li and I_p metrics and the edge-current representation. Nova also carries a second, independent flux-surface read in nova/equilibrium/flux_surface_geometry.py, which traces contours with axis refinement: the same class of method TORAX uses. So the transport record's Gaussian shells are a third integration method for a problem two landed ones already solve, and the fix is to migrate onto them rather than to author a fourth.

  1. Exact clipped-cell surface integrals. Replace the Gaussian shell weight with the exact clipped area each cell contributes between a shell's two bounding surfaces, taken from TracedClippedSupports, and carry the integrand's within-cell variation on the clip's own area moments rather than a single node sample. This uses everything the discretisation holds — corner values, the edge crossings the clip solves for, and the centroid the moments are formed about — and it removes the bandwidth parameter entirely rather than tuning it: there is no smearing left to trade against binning noise.
  2. Extrema from the clip's own crossings. R_in, R_out and the top and bottom of each surface come from the clipped polygon vertices, which are already the interpolated level-set crossings along cell edges. Grid quantisation is the whole of the shape error, so this falls out of item 1 rather than needing its own machinery.
  3. Cross-check against Nova's own contour read. Compare the migrated record against nova/equilibrium/flux_surface_geometry.py's contour-traced extraction as well as against TORAX's reader. Three independent routes over one equilibrium localise any residual disagreement to a method rather than leaving it unattributed — and a persistent two-against-one split is itself the diagnosis.
  4. A higher-order surface representation, and the extremum it unlocks. A chord represents the surface inside a cell as a straight segment, so its topmost point can only be an edge crossing. Near its own turning point the surface is locally parabolic in R, which makes the radial error of an endpoint scale as √(Δz/κ) — a square-root rate, and the likely whole of the 10–22% triangularity error, since no amount of area accuracy touches it. Reaching O(h²) requires the reconstruction itself to carry curvature.
  5. Why the exact bilinear arc is not that reconstruction — proved, and measured. This section first specified clipping against the exact bilinear level set, assuming its arc carried an interior turning point. It does not. For bilinear ψ the level set is Z = (k − a − bR)/(c + dR), so dZ/dR = −(bc + d(k − a))/(c + dR)² — and that numerator is a constant. Every branch is therefore monotone, its extrema are necessarily its endpoints, and if the numerator vanishes the branch degenerates to a line of constant Z. So the square-root behaviour belongs to the bilinear reconstruction, not to the chord. The correction was implemented exactly regardless and validated against a 500×500 subcell raster to 1.57e−5, and it measured worse: ITER coefficients barely moved (g2g3 2.628→2.549%) while STEP degraded on every decisive field — vpr 14.60→25.75%, g1 16.04→22.83%, area 3.51→4.25% — and clipped-versus-contour vpr went 16.46→27.59%, away from both reference routes. The lesson generalises: exactness on a poor reconstruction is worse than a compensating error on it.
  6. Tensor bicubic, replacing bilinear as the moment authority. Represent ψ per cell by a tensor bicubic, take clipped area and moments by Green's theorem along the in-cell arc with a fixed Gauss–Legendre rule, and locate the extremum as the stationary point of that level set within the cell. Three things make this the principled choice rather than merely the remaining one: both reference routes already use this representation class and agree with each other to 0.16% on Φ while bilinear disagrees with both, so two independent implementations outvote ours; a bicubic is C¹ with non-constant in-cell gradient, so its level set carries curvature and can hold an interior stationary point at all; and Nova already reads flux this way on the equilibrium side, where flux_surface_geometry.py builds a RectBivariateSpline and takes Newton steps on the spline Hessian — adopting the house method rather than importing a foreign one. Reuse rather than author: the traced pytree interpolants in nova/linalg/interpolant.py keep the assembly jit and vmap safe, and nova/biot/arcbandedcoupling.py::_arc_rule is the existing unit-interval Gauss rule. The bilinear arc path is retired rather than kept beside it, and nova/equilibrium/separatrix_clip.py stays unmodified because concurrent plans own it.

§9 — Low-aspect-ratio support (MAST)

The STEP negative is promoted from recorded limit to required work: the second part of the Sophelio competition includes MAST (A ≈ 1.3–1.8, the same regime as the STEP stress case), so a spherical-tokamak-accurate record is a consumer requirement, not an option.

Why the coarea estimator degrades at low A — the identity is exact; the discrete estimator's error is (within-shell variance of the integrand) × (shell allocation error), and low aspect ratio maximises both. (1) One surface spans R_in≈0.3 m to R_out≈1.3 m, so 1/R² varies ~20× and |∇ψ|² similarly along a single surface — which is why the ∇ψ and 1/R² moments fail (g1 16–27%) while Φ, volume and area hold 2–3% on the same maps. (2) Uniform-ψ̂ bins concentrate: thin shells outboard, fat inboard, measured as a maximum single-cell coarea weight of 0.105 on STEP against 0.069 on ITER — one cell carrying a tenth of a shell. (3) High elongation puts much more arc length near-tangent to grid axes, degenerating the single-ordinate parameterisation (minimum ordinate derivative 0.00218 vs 0.00321). (4) The confirming paradox: exact spline integrands made STEP worse (g1 15.4→27.4%) because corner-bilinear values had been cancelling allocation error — a compensating error propping up a structurally biased estimator, the session's recurring lesson.

The fix is an estimator change, not new machinery. The two referees that agree with each other sample the integrand ON the surface. The service already traces per-cell arcs at every level with Gauss nodes and spline gradients; summing ∮(X/|∇ψ|)dl and ∮(1/|∇ψ|)dl over those in-cell segments is orientation-free (no global ordering), fixed-shape, jit/vmap/grad-safe, and yields vpr and every ⟨·⟩ directly — no between-shell volume differencing for the averages, while volume, Φ and area keep their exact clipped-cell integrals.

§ Decisions

Where does FSA extraction live and what is its public contract?

One machine-agnostic extraction service in nova/equilibrium: a structured grid of flux values in, the FSA record out, with the TORAX comparison pinned at exactly this seam. Non-structured solves (hex StencilMesh, triangulations) enter by evaluating flux exactly onto the extraction lattice through their own grid-free forward operator — interpolating node values at the seam is rejected outright after the accuracy this plan paid for. The forward solve's exact cell machinery (separatrix_clip) remains the authority for li, Ip and source moments.

Which surface representation is the geometry authority, after the local Catmull-Rom bicubic measured worse than the smeared kernel it replaced on STEP?

A native global C2 tensor B-spline in nova/linalg, fit by two banded 1-D solves composed from the existing Bernstein pytrees, differentiable with respect to the gridded values, validated against RectBivariateSpline. Both reference readers use this representation class and agree to 0.16% on toroidal flux while the local reconstruction disagreed with both; per-cell Bernstein coefficients are a static linear map of the control net, so the fourth-order clip machinery is retained and only its coefficient authority changes.

Which engine produces global contour information for shape, given marching squares ports poorly to GPU and contourpy requires host-side regular grids?

Separate topology from geometry: the existing flood-fill selects the axis-connected core (fixed-shape associative scans, GPU-native, no accuracy demanded of it), the global spline supplies within-cell geometry through the salvaged clip machinery, and shape extrema are masked argmax-with-payload reductions over core cells — no ordered polyline exists on device because nothing in the record needs one. Locked with the standing instruction to inspect sprint-S7 cell-moment progress so the service consumes that evolving API rather than forking it; contourpy remains the host-side referee.

Which FSA record fields carry gradients with respect to the flux map?

Every field, shape included: coefficients and fluxes through the Green quadrature, extrema through implicit differentiation of the constrained Newton solve with subgradients at measure-zero argmax ties. Chosen so learned closures and design optimisation may differentiate through shaping, and enforced by an acceptance gate that differentiates through at least one shape field rather than only the coefficients.
