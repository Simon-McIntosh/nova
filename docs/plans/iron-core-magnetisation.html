nova.biot · machine description · WEST first

Iron core magnetisation

nova has no ferromagnetic material. For WEST that is not a small omission: a prototype of the method this plan builds says the axisymmetric iron core leaves less than a tenth of the air-core field beyond a metre outside the equatorial coils, where imas-efit's own iron model leaves about two thirds. This plan turns the prototype into a production kernel, a material solve and a validated source, and then corrects the axisymmetric picture for the six discrete return limbs.

Why this plan exists

The stray poloidal field outside WEST was computed in this session from nova's Biot-Savart model (figure below, committed as docs/figures/west-stray-field/west-62751-stray-field-nova.svg): coils from the machine description, currents from pulse 62751, and the plasma current carried on nova's hexagonal cells from the NICE flux map and flux functions. On the midplane the field falls through 5 mT 1.75 m outside the Fh/Fb outer edge and through 1 mT at 5.7 m. That calculation is air-core. WEST carries an iron magnetic circuit, and its outer return limb sits 0.3 m outboard of the same coils.

The machine description encodes the iron as six axisymmetric segments whose thicknesses carry the toroidal fill factor: the horizontal yokes thin as 1/R and the outer limb is 0.188 m thick, so that the product of thickness and major radius is 0.92 m² along the whole circuit (about 0.96 m² for each of six arms). That smear preserves the flux density in the iron, so saturation and the reluctance along each arm are right for the toroidally averaged field. It does not represent the field between and behind the discrete limbs, which matters within roughly 1 to 2 m of the legs, exactly where the stray-field question begins.

§1 — Prototype: edge sheets, two analytic controls, and WEST

Method. Each iron cell carries a uniform poloidal magnetisation (MR, MZ). In axisymmetry the bound volume current of a uniform cell vanishes, so its field is that of toroidal sheet currents on its edges, Kφ = MZnR − MRnZ. Each edge is therefore a thin conic sheet, the element Urankar's Part II treats in closed form. The prototype integrates each edge with 12-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over nova's exact ring Green's function (nova.biot.greens.greens_bz_br), collocates at cell centroids, and solves the material law M = c(|B|) B with c = (μr − 1)/(μ0μr) by damped Newton on the differential permeability. Code: scripts/prototypes/iron_magnetisation/; receipts under the GPFS evidence root /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-core-magnetisation/prototype/.

Uniform-field sphere. A linear sphere in a uniform field has a uniform exact magnetisation, which piecewise-constant cells represent exactly. The interior field matches 3μr/(μr+2)B0 to between 1e-11 and 4e-5 for μr = 10 to 1000, and the exterior dipole to 1e-6 (the 256-gon approximation of the sphere). μr = 1 returns B0 exactly. The error grows as cells shrink because the edge quadrature is near-singular for sliver cells, which is the case for the closed-form sheet kernel of §2.

Closed shell around a dipole. This is the control that matters for WEST, because the axisymmetric WEST iron is a closed high-μ box around every coil and the plasma. A small loop inside a linear spherical shell (a = 0.9 m, b = 1 m) has its exterior field reduced by the exact factor S = ((2μ+1)(μ+2) − 2(μ−1)²(a/b)³)/(9μ).

WEST. With the six iron_core segments meshed at 8 cm (1696 cells, 10.1 m²), the CEA B-H table from the same IDS, and the coils and hex plasma of the stray-field solve as sources (which reproduce the nova CoilSet field to 1.1e-3 beyond 0.3 m), the Newton solve converges in four iterations with μr between 216 and 1667. Beyond about 1 m from the coil edge the iron leaves 8 % of the air-core field; a linear μr = 215 leaves 14 %. Inside the box, between the coils and the limb, the iron raises the field, as an image current would.

Disagreement with imas-efit. imas-efit's axisymmetric iron, which uses the same geometry and B-H table, leaves about 65 % of the air-core field. Coil filaments (imas-efit) against exact sections (nova) explain a few per cent within 0.3 m of a winding and nothing at a metre, where only the dipole moments matter. The working hypothesis, untested, is that the difference sits in the iron representation: imas-efit carries B-spline boundary currents with the permeability sampled at its observation points. Neither model is checked against measurement: in-vessel magnetics fit equally well with and without iron (BPOL and BTNG residuals change by under 3 %; receipt /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-core-magnetisation/imas-efit/magnetics-residuals.json). The imas-efit closed-shell control (§6b) tests that hypothesis and §9 seeks a measurement that discriminates.

Defects found and fixed in the prototype. A fixed-permeability update oscillates without converging, because the CEA curve rises from 215 at B = 0 to 1692 at 1 T; Newton on the differential law converges. Square cells clipped to the sloped yoke left slivers whose centroids sat on their own edges, which read 10.9 T; merging slivers into a neighbour removed them. The PCHIP fit of the B-H table extrapolated past 6 T to a negative permeability; the law now holds its end values.

§2 — The edge sheet: a thin Part V strip, or the Part II closed form

A magnetised cell's edge carries a thin conic sheet: a straight segment in the poloidal plane swept through 2π with a uniform azimuthal sheet current. nova already owns an exact route that may deliver it: a polygon strip of width ε carrying K/ε through the Urankar Part V closed form in nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py, which the production PolySection route already runs on the device (nova/biot/AGENTS.md). Its model error falls as ε² while the cancellation between the strip's two near-coincident long edges grows as the rounding error over ε, so measure first where the two cross. If the strip reaches 1e-10 relative at every target at least half a cell width from the segment, the sheet ships as a Part V strip and no new kernel module or tile is written. Otherwise implement Urankar Part II (IEEE Trans. Magn. MAG-18(3), 911–917, 1982) eqs 22a and 23a in nova/biot/sheet.py: K, E and Π of modulus k² = 4rr′/a² through the existing owners complete_kind and complete_pole in nova/biot/completeelliptic.py (with the cached constants in nova/biot/constants.py) and incomplete_pole in nova/biot/incompleteelliptic.py, plus the smooth integral 𝔤(π/2) = ∫0π/2 sinh−1β1(α) dα on the fixed-node rule the Part V module uses for its arsinh remainders. Case n = 1 (slope ψ ≥ π/4) is derived; case n = 2 follows from eqs 7, 16 and 23b.

The reference is a Gauss–Legendre integral of greens_psi and greens_bz_br along the segment, refined by panel bisection until two successive refinements agree to 1e-13 relative at that target, with the panels clustered at the target's foot on the segment. The target set is fixed: at every slope, targets at half a cell width, one cell width, one segment length and ten segment lengths, both off the segment's middle and off each end. A target in this set whose reference does not converge fails the node; it is never dropped. Done when (1) the Part V strip's error against that reference is measured for ε from 1e-2 to 1e-8 of the segment length, at slopes 0, π/8, π/4, 3π/8 and π/2, over that target set, and at every slope the error curve turns over strictly inside the sweep (the ε² model error and the rounding-over-ε cancellation cross between its ends), with the best attainable error and the ε that gives it recorded per slope. Production uses one strip width, a fixed fraction of each edge's length, so the strip is chosen only if one such fraction meets 1e-10 at every slope; that fraction is the one recorded, with the rounding term at that fraction per slope (the curve's rounding-over-ε branch fitted on the small-ε side of each turnover and read at the production fraction); (2) the chosen route meets 1e-10 relative against that integral over the same targets, and at slopes π/2 and 0 reproduces Snow's cylinder and pancake forms (Part II eqs 24 to 27) to the same 1e-10 relative, while a negative control with the sheet current's sign flipped on one edge fails that comparison; (3) the choice and the measurement behind it are recorded in the sheet-route decision.

§3 — The cell interaction matrix on the device

Build the cell interaction matrix G (BR and BZ at the cell centroids per unit MR and MZ, square, 2N by 2N for N cells, the operand of the §4 solve) through the existing tile plan, padding, device placement and persistent compilation cache in nova/biot/tiledassembly.py, never a bespoke driver. The sheet-route decision chose the Part II closed form, so a sheet tile evaluating nova/biot/sheet.py is added beside the polygon, moment and arc tiles (_fill_tile, _fill_moment_tile, _fill_arc_tile). The tile wraps the route §2 landed rather than re-deriving it: sheet_flux and sheet_field in nova/biot/sheet.py evaluate the closed form by default at every slope (the pole-reduced sloped form, Snow's pancake form through nova.biot.zeta.zeta, eq 25 for the cylinder), traced in JAX and jitted at about 3 ms per field evaluation, with the graded quadrature kept as an explicit cross-check. A cell's columns are signed sums of its edge sheets, so a shared edge between two cells appears once with the net sheet (M1 − M2)×n. The same program, given a different target set, gives ψ and B of the magnetised iron at any external points (the coils, the plasma grid, the probes), which is what §6 and §7 consume; that is a second target set, not a second program. The mesh is an input: §3 measures on the §1 prototype mesh (mesh in scripts/prototypes/iron_magnetisation/magnetisation.py, 1696 cells for WEST), and §4 and §5 rebuild G on their production meshes through this program without changing it. The tile plan pads every kernel call to one (block, nodes) shape and scans over blocks, so the sheet tile compiles once per build at a size set by the block rather than by the cell count; that is why one compile bound holds at both counts. The ledger's 300 to 2500-cell rows that miss 60 s are whole-closure programs whose size grows with the cell count, the form the tile route avoids.

Done when (1) the device build and the §2 host route agree in float64 over the 1696-cell prototype WEST mesh against its own centroids, entry by entry, to within 1e-13 measured relative to the sum of the absolute values of the per-end antiderivative terms that make up that entry, rather than to the net entry or to the per-edge contributions. A cell's four edge sheets nearly cancel at a distant target, and each edge's contribution is itself the difference of its antiderivative at the two segment ends, so an entry near 1e-14 can be the remainder of terms near 1e-13; only the absolute sum of those per-end terms, the largest magnitudes the evaluation carries, bounds what two correctly rounded evaluations of the closed form can differ by. Every entry is finite on both sides; a NaN in either is a failure, not an excluded entry. A negative control that reverses one cell's edge normals in the device build breaks that agreement; (2) on one H200, in a fresh process whose NOVA_COMPILATION_CACHE_ROOT points at a new, empty directory the job creates for itself (never the shared pre-warm root /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/jax-cache/nova-prewarm, which nothing here clears), the build at 1696 cells and on the prototype mesh at half its cell size (count recorded, near four times as many) compiles in at most 60 s with an executable of at most 50 MB and at most 64 GB of host memory, the bounds kernel-compile-barrier owns, with the warm execute time recorded, and a second fresh process hits the cache entry the first one wrote; (3) both builds are entered in docs/research/compile-cost-evidence.html: as program rows with the columns that table carries (program, cells, platform and host, compile wall, StableHLO or HLO instructions, executable bytes, persistent-cache result, job, source), and with their peak host memory as rows of its memory-ladder table keyed by function and scale, so a production mesh's cost and memory are read from that ledger rather than re-measured. Heavy gates run on betelgeuse under gpu_0003_grpA, one job.

§4 — Iron frame and the material solve

Read the iron from the machine description: each iron_core segment's outline, its material and its B-H table. The read extends the machine-description owner, nova/imas/machine.py (ImasGeometryReader and StaticMachineDescription), and the two script readers in scripts/prototypes/iron_magnetisation/west.py and scripts/west_stray_field/solve.py are moved onto it rather than joined by a third. The mesh, the material law and the solve live in one module, nova/biot/magnetisation.py, whose public surface is the solved magnetisation of a meshed iron region for a given external field; nothing else in the package sees the mesh or the law. Mesh it without slivers (quality triangles or merged quads; the prototype's merge rule is the floor), and fit the B-H relation as a monotone law with dB/dH > 0: fit H(B) through every table point, and beyond the last point Bn blend C1 over a width δ ≤ 0.02 μ0Mn, so that M rises by at most 1 % of Mn, then hold M constant with dH/dB = 1/μ0. Build the interaction G by Galerkin cell averaging: the 2πr-volume-weighted average of each source field over each target cell, so that G is symmetric in the volume inner product, the symmetric spectrum of G/μ0 lies in [0, 1], and the demagnetising operator is positive semidefinite; with the monotone law the discrete problem is then the stationary point of a convex energy and has a single root. Do not sample at centroids, which admits spurious roots and folds. Average the external field the same way, and judge accuracy by refinement rather than at a fixed mesh. Solve F(M) = M − c(|B|)B = 0 with B = Bext + G M by Newton on the differential permeability, written so that the state, operands and factor are runtime variables of one compiled update (the rule in nova/equilibrium/AGENTS.md), on the pattern of nova/equilibrium/reduced_newton.py: an external field plus a fixed response matrix, a dense jax.jacfwd Jacobian and backtracking grades.

Hysteresis is out of scope (lead, 2026-11-05). The law is anhysteretic and single-valued, so the model carries neither remanent magnetisation nor the lower incremental permeability of a minor loop after the central-solenoid swing. Both would change the shielding, and the receipts say so. Most WEST iron sits at 0.1 to 0.3 T, on the steep low-field branch of the CEA table (215 at B = 0, 1692 at 1 T), whose measurement status imas-efit records as unconfirmed; the receipt therefore also reports the far-field change between the table and a flat μr = 215 (14 % against 8 % of the air-core field in the prototype).

This extends the §3 tile program with a Galerkin cell-average assembly (galerkin_cell_interaction beside cell_interaction in nova/biot/tiledassembly.py); the centroid program remains for evaluating the solved field at points. Done when (1) the law and the operator hold their contracts: the law tests assert the fit exact at every table point, a rise beyond Bn of at most 1 % of Mn and M constant beyond the blend, and a secant-resuming mutant fails them; G is symmetric in the volume inner product with the symmetric spectrum of G/μ0 inside [0, 1] on WEST and on the analytic meshes, and a centroid-sampling mutant fails that test; the closed-shell errors at μr 100 and 1000 fall at least 2.5× from 40 to 20 mm, with the 20 mm error at each probe at most its collocated 20 mm value plus 0.5 percentage points, probe by probe (μr 100: 0.447/0.517/0.349 %, ceilings 0.947/1.017/0.849 %; μr 1000: 2.7/3.0/2.0 %, ceilings 3.2/3.5/2.5 %), and the sphere interior error lies within its derived discretisation bound while that bound itself is at most 1e-5; (2) WEST 62751 converges from M = 0 at 0.08 and 0.04 m in at most ten Newton iterations, or by a drive ramp of at most ten per step, with every μr inside the table; starts from different initial states reach one root; a frozen-permeability Picard run on the same case, with the same continuation (direct from M = 0, or the same drive ramp), is recorded with its iteration count, and Newton needs fewer corrections than it does (Newton is kept for its quadratic rate, which steep saturation would otherwise erode); and the same Picard iteration on the centroid-sampled operator of the same mesh is recorded failing to converge (the negative control showing that the Galerkin operator, not the iteration, is what makes the solve well posed); (3) the iron read extends nova/imas/machine.py and both script readers (scripts/prototypes/iron_magnetisation/west.py, scripts/west_stray_field/solve.py) call it, with no third iron reader in the tree. The receipt, docs/evidence/fragments/iron-core-magnetisation/icm-west-62751-galerkin-pair.html with large files under /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-core-magnetisation/west-62751/, states the mesh pair, the iteration record, the converged permeability range and the far field with its mesh-pair error bar; the Picard iteration-count comparison and the centroid-operator failure are recorded in docs/evidence/fragments/iron-core-magnetisation/icm-west-picard-control.html.

§5 — Accuracy for thin high-permeability parts

Piecewise-constant magnetisation converges at first order and slowly for a thin high-μ shell: 2.8 % at μr = 1000 with 2 cm cells across a 10 cm shell. The WEST outer limb is 0.188 m thick at μr near 400 and the yokes reach 1600, so the far-field shielding is exactly the quantity that converges slowest. Measure the candidates on the shell and on WEST: graded refinement through thin parts, piecewise-linear magnetisation, and a boundary formulation in the reduced scalar potential that keeps unknowns on the iron surfaces only, using the same sheet kernels. Record the choice in the formulation decision below.

Done when (1) the chosen route reaches 0.5 % on the μr = 1000 shell, and a deliberately under-resolved arm of the same route (the cell-size field doubled everywhere through the shell for the volume routes, graded or uniform, half the panel density on the iron surfaces for the surface route) misses 0.5 %, so the measure can fail for whichever candidate is chosen; (2) the WEST midplane field at 1, 2 and 4 m outside the coil edge changes by less than 1 % under one further refinement; (3) the comparison table of the candidates, with their cost on one H200, is in the plan's evidence record.

§6 — Iron as a field source

Let the solved magnetisation contribute ψ, BR and BZ through one seam: the edge sheets are inserted into the CoilSet as one passive frame whose currents the material solve writes, so every existing grid and point solve, the stray-field figure, the forward map and any probe read include the iron with no change to a solve path. The sheet-route decision chose Part II, so the frame carries the sheet section evaluated by nova/biot/sheet.py. Regenerate the WEST stray-field figure with iron beside the air-core curve.

Done when (1) docs/figures/west-stray-field/ carries a with-iron figure whose curves come from the production path; (2) the iron contribution at the WEST in-vessel probes and flux loops is tabulated from the same seam; (3) a control with the frame's magnetisation set to zero reproduces the air-core field at every grid and probe point to round-off, while the solved magnetisation changes it, so the seam is shown to carry the iron rather than to pass the air-core field through.

§6b — The imas-efit closed-shell control

Run the closed-shell control through imas-efit's iron model and report the result to the imas-efit owners. imas-efit reads its iron geometry through IronCoreBuilder from the iron_core IDS, with its B-H curve (src/ironCoreBuilder.cpp), and falls back to an XML ironBoundaries element only when the IDS carries no iron core (src/tokamakData.cpp); WEST's configuration/machines/WEST/iron.xml holds only a constant relativePermeability and no boundary. So the shell geometry is authored as one new iron-boundary element: imas-efit's IronBoundary is a single closed iron-air path in (R, Z) with R ≥ 0 (src/ironBoundary.h), and a spherical shell's poloidal section is a half-annulus with no hole, closed along the axis, so its contour runs the outer arc, the axis segment, the inner arc reversed and the other axis segment, as the §1 prototype builds it (scripts/prototypes/iron_magnetisation/shell.py); a contour along the outer arc alone would be a solid iron sphere. The permeability the boundary selects must be 1000: with the IDS withheld, imas-efit reads magneticPermeabilities from the same XML document, and WEST's iron.xml defines only id 1 at 215, so the configuration adds an entry at 1000 and points the boundary's material at it. The element's required fields (geometry/category, the knotSet and the observationPoints blocks) are those checkData in imas-efit src/ironBoundary.cpp enforces, since no instance exists under configuration/ to copy; the iron_core IDS is withheld, and the permeability is constant: the exact shielding factor S of §1 is a linear-μ result, and a run with a B-H table would be compared against the wrong reference. imas-efit's own iron solve is converged before any verdict is drawn. Its relativeError (1e-2 in configuration/machines/_common/solver/iron-solver.xml) is an absolute threshold on the squared change of the summed boundary currents (usermanual/xml/ironModel.xml), capped by maximumIterationCount = 100 at relaxFactor 0.5, so convergence is shown by the result rather than by the setting: lowering relativeError a hundredfold, with the iteration cap raised until the solve reports converged, must move S by under 0.5 %, and so must one further refinement of the boundary resolution (the knotSet and observationPoints counts of the element), so a miss cannot be under-resolution. The control falsifies in one direction: if imas-efit misses S, its representation is implicated in the §1 WEST disagreement; if it reproduces S, its representation is sound on that body and the disagreement stays unexplained, which §9 then carries. It is a cross-code comparison with its own owners, so it lands separately from the §6 seam. The receipt, including a copy of the message sent, is written under /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-core-magnetisation/imas-efit/closed-shell/.

Done when (1) at μr = 1000 and a/b = 0.9, where the exact S is 61.10, the converged imas-efit shielding factor is recorded beside nova's, and it is scored as reproducing S if within 2 % of 61.10 and as missing S otherwise; (2) the same configuration at a/b = 0.5, where the exact S is 195.1, reproduces that value to the same 2 %, the control that the shell geometry is read as intended (a solid body, a wrong radius or a wrong orientation moves S with a/b; μr = 1 would not, since S = 1 for every geometry there); (3) the result is sent to the imas-efit owners with the message recorded in that receipt directory, and the one-directional verdict that rule gives (a miss implicates the representation; a reproduction leaves the §1 disagreement unexplained) is written into the plan for §9 to read.

§7 — Iron inside the forward solve

The iron responds to the plasma and the plasma to the iron, so a forward equilibrium with iron updates the magnetisation inside the Grad–Shafranov Newton step rather than once before it. The iron block of the Grad–Shafranov Jacobian, dM/dψ and dM/dIcoil, is obtained by implicit differentiation at the material solve's fixed point (dM = −(∂F/∂M)−1 ∂F/∂Bext dBext with F as §4 defines it, ∂F/∂M being the §4 Newton factor that is reused), not by re-running the inner solve per outer iteration. Enter through the public forward-solve seam (ForwardSolveRequest in nova/equilibrium/solve_request.py, landed as forward-solve-api §1), which this section waits on.

Done when (1) a WEST forward state with iron converges through the seam on the H200 to the forward solve's own residual tolerance, in at most twice the Newton iterations of the air-core state; (2) the shift of the magnetic axis and of the boundary flux against the air-core forward state is reported beside a frozen-magnetisation arm (M solved once before the step and held), whose shift must differ from the coupled one, the control that the coupling acts; (3) the implicitly differentiated iron Jacobian block agrees with central finite differences to 1e-6 relative.

§8 — The six discrete limbs

The axisymmetric smear is right for the toroidal average far from the legs and wrong near them. Magnetise six limbs of their real section in the axisymmetric field and evaluate their field with nova's existing 3D route and its toroidal average in nova/biot/toroidalaverage.py (toroidal_points, frame_magnetic_field and average_toroidal_field), giving |B| along a radial line behind a limb and between two limbs; no new 3D or averaging code is written. The limbs span R 4.80 to 6.00 m (centre R ≈ 5.40 m, their own radius, not the plasma major radius; the limb-geometry decision), so their toroidal spacing is 2πR/6 ≈ 5.7 m and the n = 6 departure from the average decays over roughly R/6 ≈ 0.9 m; the correction matters within about 2 m of the legs.

Done when (1) φ-resolved midplane |B| is reported behind and between limbs from the coil edge to 6 m; (2) its toroidal average agrees with the axisymmetric §6 result beyond 2 m from the legs to within 2 %; (3) the limb dimensions used are the ones recorded in the limb-geometry decision, and a control with the six limbs replaced by their axisymmetric smear shows no behind-versus-between difference above round-off, which the discrete limbs must exceed.

§9 — Validation against measurement

Nothing yet checks any iron model against WEST data. The strongest available case is the pre-magnetisation of pulse 57410, where the central solenoid carries 29 kA per turn (5.7 MA-turns) with no plasma: the iron response is largest and no plasma current confounds it. Done when (1) the 57410 vacuum probe and flux-loop residuals are reported for air core, axisymmetric iron and the limb-corrected iron; (2) the comparison names which model the data prefer and by how much, and the preference is shown to vanish when the probe positions are permuted, the control that it comes from the probes' geometry; (3) any external measurement used is cited by its source.

§ Decisions

Which magnetisation formulation goes to production for thin high-permeability parts?

Piecewise-constant cells converge slowly on the μ r = 1000 shell (2.8 % at 2 cm). §5 measures the candidates; decide after its table exists.

What are the real dimensions of the six outer return limbs and of the horizontal arms?

The machine description carries only the smeared section, about 0.96 m² per arm. §8 needs each limb's toroidal width and radial depth, and the arms' constant vertical thickness; their product with six must reproduce 5.8 m².

Derived as the lead directed: the six arms are toroidally contiguous at their inboard join and keep that toroidal width outward, and the arms and limbs keep a constant section, as the lead stated the real iron does. The WEST iron_core IDS (pulse 57410; search report /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/reports/nova/icm-limb-geometry/limb-geometry.html) represents the six discrete arms as one axisymmetric slab whose vertical thickness tapers as t(R) = 0.9169/R: that taper is the smear, a constant 0.960 m^2 arm section (2 pi 0.9169/6) spread over the toroidal pitch 2 pi R/6, and it equals the arm's real thickness of 1.200 m only at R_j = 0.9169/1.2 = 0.764 m. Six contiguous arms at R_j give a toroidal width w = 2 pi R_j/6 = 0.800 m. Each arm is therefore 0.800 m wide by 1.200 m thick along its whole radial run (the IDS taper is dropped, not applied to the discrete arm), carrying 0.960 m^2 everywhere, and each limb carries the same 0.960 m^2 as 0.800 m wide by 1.200 m deep, so the section is continuous through the arm-limb joint and the six arms total 5.76 m^2. The IDS limb slab's radial extent (R 4.7965 to 4.9840 m, 0.1875 m) follows the same rule, 0.9169/4.89 = 0.1875 m; the search report reads that extent as exact, and this decision overrides that reading because a 0.1875 m deep limb of constant width 0.800 m would carry 0.150 m^2, a sixth of the arm feeding it. The limb's inner face is held at R 4.7965 m, which keeps the 0.3 m clearance outboard of the Fh/Fb coils, and its 1.200 m depth extends outward to R 5.9965 m; the arms extend radially to the same outer radius. Heights are the IDS values (limbs Z +/-2.4003 m; arms Z 2.4024 to 3.6024 m and mirror). Provenance: no source found states the limb depth, the toroidal width or the arm width; D = 1.200 m and W = 0.800 m follow from the lead's constant-width rule and iron conservation, not from a drawing. The limbs' toroidal angular datum is likewise unsourced and is taken as one limb at phi = 0.

Does a thin Part V polygon strip deliver the edge sheet, or does it need the Part II closed form?

The strip reuses the production polygon route and adds no module or tile; Part II adds nova/biot/sheet.py and a sheet tile. §2 measures the strip's best attainable error and decides on a 1e-10 relative threshold.

Measured (052fa853c, fragment icm-strip-error-sweep): the thin Part V strip against a 1e-13 ring-integral reference bottoms at 6.7e-8 worst case at eps/L 1e-4 (per-slope bests 1.3e-8 to 6.7e-8), every slope turns over inside the 1e-2 to 1e-8 sweep, all 60 references converged, and no single edge-length fraction meets 1e-10. An independent attempt on a 1.0 m segment floors at 3.1e-8 (/work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-core-magnetisation/strip-sweep-attempt-1/strip-error-sweep.json). The strip misses the threshold by about two decades, so the edge sheet is the Urankar Part II closed form in nova/biot/sheet.py.
