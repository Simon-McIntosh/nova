Iron core magnetisation

nova has no ferromagnetic material. For WEST that is not a small omission: a prototype of the method this plan builds says the axisymmetric iron core leaves less than a tenth of the air-core field beyond a metre outside the equatorial coils, where imas-efit's own iron model leaves about two thirds. This plan turns the prototype into a production kernel, a material solve and a validated source, and then corrects the axisymmetric picture for the six discrete return limbs.

Why this plan exists

The stray poloidal field outside WEST was computed in this session from nova's Biot-Savart model (figure below, committed as docs/figures/west-stray-field/west-62751-stray-field-nova.svg): coils from the machine description, currents from pulse 62751, and the plasma current carried on nova's hexagonal cells from the NICE flux map and flux functions. On the midplane the field falls through 5 mT 1.75 m outside the Fh/Fb outer edge and through 1 mT at 5.7 m. That calculation is air-core. WEST carries an iron magnetic circuit, and its outer return limb sits 0.3 m outboard of the same coils.

The machine description encodes the iron as six axisymmetric segments whose thicknesses carry the toroidal fill factor: the horizontal yokes thin as 1/R and the outer limb is 0.188 m thick, so that the product of thickness and major radius is 0.92 m² along the whole circuit (about 0.96 m² for each of six arms). That smear preserves the flux density in the iron, so saturation and the reluctance along each arm are right for the toroidally averaged field. It does not represent the field between and behind the discrete limbs, which matters within roughly 1 to 2 m of the legs, exactly where the stray-field question begins.

§1 — Prototype: edge sheets, two analytic controls, and WEST

Method. Each iron cell carries a uniform poloidal magnetisation (MR, MZ). In axisymmetry the bound volume current of a uniform cell vanishes, so its field is that of toroidal sheet currents on its edges, Kφ = MZnR − MRnZ. Each edge is therefore a thin conic sheet, the element Urankar's Part II treats in closed form. The prototype integrates each edge with 12-point Gauss–Legendre quadrature over nova's exact ring Green's function (nova.biot.greens.greens_bz_br), collocates at cell centroids, and solves the material law M = c(|B|) B with c = (μr − 1)/(μ0μr) by damped Newton on the differential permeability. Code: scripts/prototypes/iron_magnetisation/; receipts under ~/.cache/nova-iron-research/.

Uniform-field sphere. A linear sphere in a uniform field has a uniform exact magnetisation, which piecewise-constant cells represent exactly. The interior field matches 3μr/(μr+2)B0 to between 1e-11 and 4e-5 for μr = 10 to 1000, and the exterior dipole to 1e-6 (the 256-gon approximation of the sphere). μr = 1 returns B0 exactly. The error grows as cells shrink because the edge quadrature is near-singular for sliver cells, which is the case for the closed-form sheet kernel of §2.

Closed shell around a dipole. This is the control that matters for WEST, because the axisymmetric WEST iron is a closed high-μ box around every coil and the plasma. A small loop inside a linear spherical shell (a = 0.9 m, b = 1 m) has its exterior field reduced by the exact factor S = ((2μ+1)(μ+2) − 2(μ−1)²(a/b)³)/(9μ).

WEST. With the six iron_core segments meshed at 8 cm (1696 cells, 10.1 m²), the CEA B-H table from the same IDS, and the coils and hex plasma of the stray-field solve as sources (which reproduce the nova CoilSet field to 1.1e-3 beyond 0.3 m), the Newton solve converges in four iterations with μr between 216 and 1667. Beyond about 1 m from the coil edge the iron leaves 8 % of the air-core field; a linear μr = 215 leaves 14 %. Inside the box, between the coils and the limb, the iron raises the field, as an image current would.

Disagreement with imas-efit. imas-efit's axisymmetric iron, which uses the same geometry and B-H table, leaves about 65 % of the air-core field. Coil filaments (imas-efit) against exact sections (nova) explain a few per cent within 0.3 m of a winding and nothing at a metre, where only the dipole moments matter. The difference sits in the iron representation: imas-efit carries B-spline boundary currents with the permeability sampled at its observation points. Neither model is checked against measurement: in-vessel magnetics fit equally well with and without iron (BPOL and BTNG residuals change by under 3 %). §6 runs the closed-shell control through imas-efit and §9 seeks a measurement that discriminates.

Defects found and fixed in the prototype. A fixed-permeability update oscillates without converging, because the CEA curve rises from 215 at B = 0 to 1692 at 1 T; Newton on the differential law converges. Square cells clipped to the sloped yoke left slivers whose centroids sat on their own edges, which read 10.9 T; merging slivers into a neighbour removed them. The PCHIP fit of the B-H table extrapolated past 6 T to a negative permeability; the law now holds its end values.

§2 — The thin conic sheet kernel (Urankar Part II)

Implement the full-turn field and vector potential of a thin conic sheet, a straight segment in the poloidal plane swept through 2π and carrying a uniform azimuthal sheet current, in nova/biot/sheet.py. Urankar Part II (IEEE Trans. Magn. MAG-18(3), 911–917, 1982) gives it in eqs 22a and 23a: complete elliptic integrals K, E and Π of modulus k² = 4rr′/a², and one smooth integral 𝔤(π/2) = ∫0π/2 sinh−1β1(α) dα that Urankar evaluates numerically. Case n = 1 (slope ψ ≥ π/4) is derived; case n = 2 follows from eqs 7, 16 and 23b, so every slope from the cylinder (ψ = π/2) to the pancake (ψ = 0) keeps the same accuracy. Reuse nova's complete-integral routes (Bulirsch's cel on the complement, as the Part V reduction in nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py does) and the same fixed-node rule the Part V module uses for its arsinh remainders.

Done when (1) the kernel matches a converged Gauss–Legendre integral of greens_psi and greens_bz_br along the segment to 1e-12 relative for targets at least one segment length away, across slopes 0, π/8, π/4, 3π/8 and π/2; (2) at slope π/2 and slope 0 it reproduces Snow's cylinder and pancake forms (Part II eqs 24 to 27) to round-off, and a negative control with the sign of b1 flipped fails that comparison; (3) a Part V polygon strip of width ε carrying K/ε converges to the sheet as ε², measured over three decades of ε.

§3 — Sheet tiles on the device

Add a sheet tile to nova/biot/tiledassembly.py beside the polygon and arc tiles, so the cell interaction matrix G (BR, BZ and ψ at targets per unit MR and MZ) is built through the existing tile plan, padding, device placement and persistent compilation cache rather than a bespoke driver. A cell's columns are signed sums of its edge sheets, so a shared edge between two cells appears once with the net sheet (M1 − M2)×n.

Done when (1) the device tile and the §2 host kernel agree to 1e-12 in float64 over the 1696-cell WEST mesh against its own centroids; (2) that 3392-square Gram builds on one H200 with the compile time and the warm execute time each recorded, and a second run hits the persistent cache; (3) the sheet program appears in the kernel-compile census with its compile wall and memory, measured the same way as the polygon tile. Heavy gates run on betelgeuse under gpu_0003_grpA, one job.

§4 — Iron frame and the material solve

Read the iron from the machine description: each iron_core segment's outline, its material and its B-H table. Mesh it without slivers (quality triangles or merged quads; the prototype's merge rule is the floor), and fit the B-H relation as a monotone law with dB/dH > 0 that holds its end values outside the table. Solve F(M) = M − c(|B|)B = 0 with B = Bext + G M by Newton on the differential permeability, written so that the state, operands and factor are runtime variables of one compiled update (the rule in nova/equilibrium/AGENTS.md).

Done when (1) the uniform-field sphere and the closed shell, run through the production path, match the §1 prototype numbers to within their reported precision; (2) WEST 62751 converges in at most ten Newton iterations with every μr inside the table, and a frozen-permeability Picard run on the same case is recorded as failing to converge (the negative control for choosing Newton); (3) the receipt states the mesh, the iteration record and the converged permeability range.

§5 — Accuracy for thin high-permeability parts

Piecewise-constant magnetisation converges at first order and slowly for a thin high-μ shell: 2.8 % at μr = 1000 with 2 cm cells across a 10 cm shell. The WEST outer limb is 0.188 m thick at μr near 400 and the yokes reach 1600, so the far-field shielding is exactly the quantity that converges slowest. Measure the candidates on the shell and on WEST: graded refinement through thin parts, piecewise-linear magnetisation, and a boundary formulation in the reduced scalar potential that keeps unknowns on the iron surfaces only, using the same sheet kernels. Record the choice in the formulation decision below.

Done when (1) the chosen route reaches 0.5 % on the μr = 1000 shell; (2) the WEST midplane field at 1, 2 and 4 m outside the coil edge changes by less than 1 % under one further refinement; (3) the comparison table of the candidates, with their cost on one H200, is in the plan's evidence record.

§6 — Iron as a field source, and the imas-efit control

Let the solved magnetisation contribute ψ, BR and BZ wherever a CoilSet solves grid or point targets, so the stray-field figure, the forward map and any probe read include the iron. Regenerate the WEST stray-field figure with iron beside the air-core curve. Then run the closed-shell control through imas-efit's iron model, with its boundary geometry set to the shell, and report the result to the imas-efit owners: that test says which of the two WEST answers in §1 is the representation and which is the physics.

Done when (1) docs/figures/west-stray-field/ carries a with-iron figure whose curves come from the production path; (2) the imas-efit shell result is recorded with its shielding factor against the exact value; (3) the iron contribution at the WEST in-vessel probes and flux loops is tabulated beside imas-efit's.

§7 — Iron inside the forward solve

The iron responds to the plasma and the plasma to the iron, so a forward equilibrium with iron updates the magnetisation inside the Grad–Shafranov Newton step rather than once before it. Enter through the public forward-solve seam, which this section waits on.

Done when (1) a WEST forward state with iron converges through the seam on the H200; (2) the shift of the magnetic axis and of the boundary flux against the air-core forward state is reported; (3) the iron Jacobian block is checked against finite differences.

§8 — The six discrete limbs

The axisymmetric smear is right for the toroidal average far from the legs and wrong near them. Magnetise six limbs of their real section in the axisymmetric field and evaluate their field with nova's 3D Biot-Savart route, giving |B| along a radial line behind a limb and between two limbs. The n = 6 departure from the average decays over roughly R/6 ≈ 0.8 m, so the correction matters within about 2 m of the legs.

Done when (1) φ-resolved midplane |B| is reported behind and between limbs from the coil edge to 6 m; (2) its toroidal average agrees with the axisymmetric §6 result beyond 2 m to within the stated tolerance; (3) the limb dimensions used are the ones recorded in the limb-geometry decision.

§9 — Validation against measurement

Nothing yet checks any iron model against WEST data. The strongest available case is the pre-magnetisation of pulse 57410, where the central solenoid carries 29 kA per turn (5.7 MA-turns) with no plasma: the iron response is largest and no plasma current confounds it. Predict the in-vessel BPOL and BTNG probes and the flux loops with and without iron and compare with their measurements; then extend to any stray-field measurement outside the machine that the lead can source.

Done when (1) the 57410 vacuum probe and flux-loop residuals are reported for air core, axisymmetric iron and the limb-corrected iron; (2) the comparison names which model the data prefer and by how much; (3) any external measurement used is cited by its source.

§ Decisions

Which magnetisation formulation goes to production for thin high-permeability parts?

Piecewise-constant cells converge slowly on the μ r = 1000 shell (2.8 % at 2 cm). §5 measures the candidates; decide after its table exists.

What are the real dimensions of the six outer return limbs and of the horizontal arms?

The machine description carries only the smeared section, about 0.96 m² per arm. §8 needs each limb's toroidal width and radial depth, and the arms' constant vertical thickness; their product with six must reproduce 5.8 m².
