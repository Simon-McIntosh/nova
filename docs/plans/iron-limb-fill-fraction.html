Iron limb fill fraction

The WEST machine description stores the six discrete iron arms as one axisymmetric slab that thins as 1/R. That keeps the iron section per radian right and makes the poloidal outline wrong: the real arms are flat and 1.2 m thick. This plan keeps the true outline and thins the iron per circumference through a per-cell toroidal fill fraction instead, with a cross-field factor for the limb's own poles, and proves the choice against a 3D solve of six discrete limbs.

Why this plan exists

The stored WEST iron_core outline tapers each horizontal arm as t(R) = 0.9169/R and gives the return limb a radial depth of 0.1875 m. Both follow one rule: thickness times radius is constant, so the section per radian equals that of six arms of 0.960 m². The limb-geometry decision of iron-core-magnetisation records the real section as 0.800 m wide by 1.200 m, constant along each arm and limb. The stored outline therefore has the right reluctance and the wrong shape: the flare on the top and bottom faces does not exist, and the real limb is six times deeper than the stored one.

Three axisymmetric stand-ins were prototyped on nova's production mesh, interaction and B-H fit (scripts/prototypes/iron_limb_fill/, commit 62a05040b):

Here f(R) is the toroidal fraction of the circumference occupied by iron: 1 where the six arms are contiguous at the hub and about 0.764/R outboard. With N the demagnetising factor of the limb section for a field across the limb in the poloidal plane, the iron sees Biron = Bmean + μ0 (1−f)(1−N) Miron per component, with N = 0 along the limb axis. Setting f = 1 recovers the present residual exactly. The prototype used N = 0.4, the elliptic-section estimate for a 0.8 by 1.2 m bar.

The reference is a 3D solve of a WEST-like circuit with six discrete limbs: a round column, a hexagonal hub, six arms and six limbs of 0.8 by 1.2 m, linear iron, uniformly magnetised bricks, driven by the central solenoid, three symmetric coil pairs and a plasma ring. Its toroidal mean is what an axisymmetric model has to reproduce. At the finest meshes (0.05 m cells against 0.1 m bricks):

All three stand-ins carry the same core flux to within 2 % of each other; they differ in the shape of the field. Only the cross-field rule reproduces the in-vessel iron field, where its error is a tenth of the others'. Behind the limbs the smear, a closed shell of full iron, shields too much, and the plain fill fraction, which treats a 0.8 m bar as fine laminations, shields too little. The plain fill fraction's 0.31 to 0.34 in-vessel reading lies outside the 0.10 bound §2 sets, so answering the cross-field decision with no cross-field factor would first require that bound to be re-derived. The numbers are in /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-limb-fill/comparison.json.

Two limits bound what the prototype proves. The reference is not converged: its column flux rose from 12.7 to 13.5 Wb between 0.133 and 0.1 m bricks at μr = 200 and still sits 11 % under the axisymmetric values, so the 3 to 5 % in-vessel figure is a reading on an unfinished reference. And N = 0.4 is an estimate, not a fit. On the real WEST 62751 case the choice matters: against the stored outline the cross-field rule moves the in-vessel iron field by 0.65 mT rms (0.89 mT at most, on a 21 mT contribution), halves the field above the arms and changes the column flux from −0.44 to −0.18 Wb.

imas-efit reads the same tapered outlines into its boundary-current iron model and so inherits the flare. The equivalent there is the flat outline with a position-dependent effective permeability, which its internal-current terms already admit. That work belongs to imas-efit and is recorded as a followup, not a section of this plan.

§1 — A converged six-limb reference

Every tolerance below is judged against the toroidal mean of a 3D six-limb solve, so that solve has to be trustworthy first. The control circuit, its drive and its probes are those of the prototype case (/work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-limb-fill/case.json): sixteen in-vessel points, five midplane points behind the limbs and seven points above the arms and beside the limb corner.

Done when (1) at μr = 50 and 200 the reference's column flux, its in-vessel toroidal-mean iron field and its toroidal-mean field at every outside probe each change by less than 2 % between its two finest resolutions; (2) the same 3D method applied to a fully axisymmetric version of the circuit (the limbs replaced by the complete annulus) agrees with nova's axisymmetric solve of that annulus within 2 % on the same three quantities, and a version with one limb removed is shown to break the sixfold symmetry of the resolved field, the control that the method sees discrete limbs at all; (3) the receipt under /work/projects/imas_gpu/sophelio/evidence/iron-limb-fill/reference/ holds the per-resolution values and the toroidally resolved field at each outside probe, cited from docs/evidence/archive/iron-limb-fill-fraction-landed.html#reference. The measurement is one job on rigel.

§2 — Fill fraction and cross-field factor in the material solve

The production axisymmetric iron solve carries a toroidal fill fraction and a cross-field factor for each cell, and reports the toroidal-mean magnetisation together with the permeability of the iron itself.

Done when (1) with the fill fraction omitted the solution, residual history and field of every existing test in tests/test_magnetisation.py are unchanged to 1e-12 relative, and a test with f = 1 passed explicitly returns the same state; (2) on the control circuit at μr = 50 and 200 the in-vessel iron field is within 10 % of the §1 reference in rms (twice the prototype's worst reading) and at most one third of the section smear's error on that reference, with the cross-field factor chosen by the cross-field decision below, while the solid outline with f = 1 exceeds 100 % on the same measure, the control that the fill fraction and not the outline carries the result; the midplane and above-arm ratios of the table in §0 are reported for the same runs at two cell sizes; (3) the nonlinear WEST 62751 solve on the true outline converges to 1e-8 in at most ten Newton corrections at 0.08 and 0.04 m. Heavy gates run as one job on the H200 (betelgeuse under its reservation), falling back to titan only when a real submission stays pending, and the device is recorded beside every reported value.

§3 — The true outline as machine description

The flat outline, the fill fraction and the cross-field factor are properties of the machine, so they are read once beside the iron segments and not rebuilt in each script. One read of the machine description serves every consumer.

Done when (1) the WEST read returns limb and arm regions on the true outline with a fill fraction per region, and the integral of fill fraction times radius across each arm and limb section reproduces the stored outline's section per radian to 1e-3 relative; (2) the limb dimensions it uses are the ones recorded in the limb-geometry decision of iron-core-magnetisation, and a stored outline that does not obey thickness times radius constant to 1 % is refused by name rather than converted; (3) the script readers of the WEST iron call this read, with no second construction of the flat outline in the tree.

§ Decisions

Where does the cross-field factor N come from?

The prototype's N = 0.4 is the elliptic-section estimate. Decide after §1: a geometric value transfers to another machine without a 3D solve; a fitted one is tied to this circuit.

Are the 2 mm gaps between the stored limb top (Z = 2.4003 m) and arm underside (Z = 2.4024 m) real air gaps?

At a relative permeability near 1000 a 2 mm gap has the reluctance of about 2 m of iron, so the answer moves the column flux. The prototype kept the gaps in every model.
