power_convertor is building an integrated, switched time-domain model of every ITER coil power supply (iter-switched-terminal-transients). Its converters drive the coils, and what the coils present to the converters depends on everything they couple to: each other, the vessel and the other passive conductors, and the plasma. That coupled plant belongs in nova, which owns the conductor geometry, the Green's-function kernels and the L/R circuit solvers (nova/circuit/passive.py, propagate.py, linkage.py, screening.py).

This plan builds the ITER plant, evolves the vessel currents through a scenario, and exports the coupled system for the converter model. Two choices come from the lead (14 October 2026). The plasma is not solved: its position and current distribution are prescribed from DINA's separatrix and flux functions, with the net current adjusted to DINA's. And the passive structure carries a full current model, so its currents evolve with the coils and the plasma.

The power_convertor work so far built a first ITER passive set through nova (power_convertor/scripts/nova_passive_export.py: 458 rings, slowest decay 1.3 s). It found the coil self-inductances (PF1 0.703 H, CS 0.760 H, PF2 0.475, PF3 1.855, PF4 1.560, PF5 1.555, PF6 2.390 H), and it found the ring model's validity ends at the skin depth: 1.8 Hz in the cryostat and 29 to 35 Hz in the vessel. The converters put their lines at 600 Hz and above, so that limit is the first thing this plan removes.

§1 — The ITER conductor set, sectioned and valid across the converter band

The conductors come from the machine description, ITER_MACHINE_DESCRIPTION/3/111001: run 204 for the Stage 1 + Stage 2 coil set (the six CS modules, PF1 to PF6 and the VS3 in-vessel coils), and the pf_passive loops for the passive structure. A switching edge does not divide evenly along a winding, so each coil is split into winding sections: double pancakes for the PF coils and the pancake structure of each CS module. Each section is placed from the element geometry, its turns and the published winding layout.

The machine-description reading already has an owner, which this section extends rather than rebuilds. nova/imas/pulsedesign.py binds ITER(Machine) to the ITER machine description, and nova/imas/machine.py owns the conductor classes (PoloidalFieldActive, PoloidalFieldPassive, Wall, the coil and shell data) and the section seam (MachineSection, CrossSection). A winding section is a series sub-coil carrying part of a coil's turns. If MachineSection can carry one, it is extended; if it cannot, the record states why before any new section type is added. This conductor set supersedes, for the converter work, the 458-ring set power_convertor holds in PassiveStructure (data/iter/passive_structure/matrices.json). That set stays only as the recorded input of the frozen frequency-domain prediction, which cites it.

The passive structure is subdivided wherever a single ring cannot carry the skin effect at the highest frequency the converter model needs. The double-walled vessel, the triangular supports and the cryostat are subdivided through their thickness, and the subdivision is refined until it converges across the converter band, with the upper limit stated. The linkage uses circuit/linkage.py's two-section integration, and the reduction uses PassiveCircuit.

Done when four checks pass:

  • Each coil's self-inductance and mutuals match power_convertor's values above to 0.5 %, and DINA's em_coupling matrices in the DINA-IMAS cases (105013/1, 105014/1) within a stated tolerance.
  • Refining the through-thickness subdivision changes each coil's terminal impedance, with the passive structure coupled, by less than 1 % from 0.1 Hz to the stated upper limit.
  • The resistances are cited or marked nominal.
  • Negative control: removing the passive structure gives the bare coil impedance at every frequency.

Figures go in docs/figures/iter-passive-plasma-circuit/: the sectioned coils and the subdivided passive set in the poloidal plane, and each coil's impedance with and without the passive structure.

§2 — The plasma, prescribed from DINA

The plasma is not solved. Its current distribution at each DINA equilibrium slice is built in four steps:

  1. Check that DINA's separatrix outline is a closed loop. Where it is not (early ramp-up and limited phases), use the last closed flux surface instead and flag the slice.
  2. Evaluate jφ(R, Z) = R p′(ψ) + FF′(ψ)/(μ0R) inside that boundary on nova's plasma grid, from DINA's 1-D flux functions and its 2-D ψ map.
  3. Scale the result to DINA's plasma current, recording the scale factor for each slice as a measure of DINA's own consistency.
  4. From that distribution, compute the flux the plasma links at every coil section and passive element, against time.

The DINA-IMAS cases hold what this needs: separatrix outline, 1-D profiles and a 65 × 129 ψ map, DD 3.38.1, read with imas-python in that version. power_convertor's scenario inventory (§1 of its plan) names any further cases. The reading reuses nova/imas/equilibrium.py and nova/imas/extrapolate.py. The current density in step 2 already has an owner: nova/imas/profiles.py evaluates it from the flux functions (Profile.fluxfunctions, Profile.j_tor_rbs). This section extends that owner with the closed-boundary mask and the scaling to DINA's current, rather than deriving jφ again.

Done when three checks pass:

  • The prescribed current centroid follows DINA's within one grid cell.
  • The distribution of scale factors is reported.
  • The flux the prescribed plasma links at the coils matches DINA's own plasma flux, its ψ map less the coil and passive contributions, within a stated tolerance.

Negative control: moving the distribution 1 cm vertically changes the linked flux by the predicted ∂ψ/∂Z.

§3 — The vessel currents, evolved through a scenario

The passive structure is driven by DINA's coil currents and the prescribed plasma, and integrated with nova's exact zero-order-hold eigenmode propagation (circuit/propagate.py). Its currents are compared group by group with DINA's own pf_passive currents in the DINA-IMAS cases. They are read through the existing reader, PF_Passive in nova/imas/pf_passive.py, and mapped onto §1's conductors through PoloidalFieldPassive, so the grouping has one owner.

The coil voltages then follow from the whole coupled flux: R I + dΨ/dt, with Ψ carrying the coils, the passive currents and the plasma. They are compared with DINA's coil voltages. power_convertor found that bare coil L·dI/dt misses DINA's PF2 to PF5 voltages by about 1 kV rms (iter-power-supply-model §8), and this check measures how much of that miss the passive and plasma coupling accounts for.

Done when the passive currents agree with DINA's by group within a stated tolerance, and each coil's voltage residual is reported as rms and peak. Negative control: removing the plasma drive brings back the bare-coil residual.

§4 — The coupled plant, exported to the converter model

The export is one versioned file per machine description and DINA case. It is written by extending the serialisers nova/circuit/passive.py already ships (save_circuit_system, save_eigenbasis and their loaders) to the coupled plant with its plasma drive, rather than adding a parallel loader. The location resolves through FilePath in nova/database/filepath.py, not a literal path into another repository, and the record states the resolved location power_convertor reads. The arrays are stored so power_convertor can read them without importing nova. The export retires nothing in nova. In power_convertor it replaces the ring model for the switched simulation, which reads only this export. The file carries:

  • the section-level coil inductance matrix and resistances;
  • the reduced passive system: the eigenmodes kept, their decay times and their coupling to every coil section;
  • the plasma's flux drive at every coil section and passive mode, against time, at a stated cadence;
  • provenance: the machine-description run, the DINA case and the nova revision;
  • the reduction error against frequency.

The contract is written down in the record, so power_convertor's switched-circuit solver can take the plant as a state-space multiport.

Done when two checks pass:

  • A round-trip test of the extended serialiser passes in nova.
  • The reduced plant's terminal impedance for every coil matches the full model's to 1 % from 0.1 Hz to §1's upper limit.

This section is the evidence power_convertor's gate g-istt-1 waits on.

§ Decisions

Is the plasma solved or prescribed?

The converter model needs the plasma's flux at the conductors, not a new equilibrium. Prescribing it from DINA keeps the plant consistent with the scenario the coil currents come from, and the scale factor to DINA's current measures that consistency directly.

Where does the passive current model live?

nova owns the conductor geometry, the kernels and the L/R solvers, so the vessel currents are evolved here and exported to power_convertor, rather than rebuilt there.
