Why this plan exists

Lead directive, 2026-10-20: the compile barrier is critical and recurrent, and it gets a plan of its own rather than living as rulings and side notes inside converged-forward-solve. Every program that evaluates a Biot coupling kernel and its derivatives traces the same expression: the harmonic recurrences in nova/biot/elliptic.py and nova/biot/rangefunction.py, and the packed polygon moments in nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py. XLA compiles that expression cold, and the cost does not depend on the mesh. Nothing in today's code bounds it, so each new kernel-backed program pays it again, in the solve, in test fixtures and in a test suite's memory.

Locked rulings this plan inherits, from converged-forward-solve, 2026-10-06. The primal arithmetic (flux, field, booking, residual, null positions) stays bit-identical to the base kernels. Tangents may take a fixed-capacity scan form held to 1e-9 relative against jax.jvp of the base, and exact hand-written tangents stay where they already compile within three times the primal. Relaxing the identity tolerance or the compile clause is forbidden without the lead. The 15 elliptic helpers the point jet reaches already carry private tangents inside three times their primal, so the remaining cost lies in terms that work did not reach; §1 finds them before anything is rewritten.

§1 — Compile census of every kernel-backed program

Measure, before changing anything, where the compiled graph comes from. The programs are the point jet, harmonic_moments and harmonic_pole_moments (nova/biot/elliptic.py:240, :410) and harmonic_multiply (nova/biot/rangefunction.py:134) each alone, packed_analytic_moments (nova/biot/polygonanalytic.py:2641), the jets build's point batch, the kernel-backed topology read, and the certificate map. Each runs in a fresh process with the compilation cache disabled before JAX initialises, and each row asserts its own cache miss from the cache-guard rows, because a warm cache once passed for a cold compile.

The census extends the instruments that already exist rather than writing a third: equations are attributed to kernel call sites by attribute_graph in scripts/prototypes/order2/attribution.py (its expanded_equations, body_share and kernel_call_partition_equations) over the shared lowered-jaxpr descent in tests/jaxpr_scans.py; compile wall and executable bytes are measured as scripts/prototypes/read_route/measure.py does (lower and compile at :202, runtime_executable().serialize() at :223), and generated-code bytes as scripts/prototypes/fast_read/measure_fused.py:51 does. Its rows join the compile ledger in docs/research/compile-cost-evidence.html, so there is one ledger.

Done when docs/research/compile-cost-evidence.html carries a census section reporting, for every program above: (1) unique and expanded jaxpr equations, StableHLO bytes and executable bytes at two sizes (132 and 550 cells, or 4 and 64 points for a kernel alone), so each graph is classed as constant or growing; (2) the cold compile wall and peak host memory sampled every 10 s, on the H200 as one job and on CPU as one sirius job sized from the measured 381 GB; (3) the source functions ranked by the expanded equations each contributes, with owner file and line, as the target list for §2 and §3, and each function's share of the point jet's expanded equations, which is the denominator §2 done-when (2) measures its fall against. attribute_graph today ranks call sites, constructs and primitives and carries bare function names; it is extended to emit this per-source ranking with file and line from the equations' tracebacks, keeping its existing outputs for its current callers.

§2 — Bounded tangents for the ranked kernel terms

Give the terms §1 ranks highest, beyond the 15 elliptic helpers already done, a tangent whose graph is bounded, in one of the two forms the 2026-10-06 rulings accept: a fixed-capacity scan over the term order, or an exact hand-written tangent that compiles within three times its primal. The primal arithmetic does not change.

Done when: (1) the primal of every changed kernel is bit-identical to the base, and its tangent rows agree with jax.jvp of the base to 1e-9 relative, on the 10,000-point polygon and filament jet identity sets used by cfs-kernel-compile-cost; (2) the point jet's expanded equations fall by at least the share §1 attributes to the changed terms, and its cold compile on the H200, fresh process and cache disabled, falls from 83.6 s to 10 s or less; (3) the kernel tests pass at head with no added failure, with both suite arms run as one sirius job.

§3 — A bounded jets build

The jets build compiles jax.jit(jax.vmap(_source_jets, in_axes=(0, None))) once and then runs it batch by batch, so a single compile carries the whole cost: 7,044 s and 381 GB at 2,132 nodes. That cost does not grow with the node count (§0: about 300 MB/s of growth, the same at 282 cells); it is the size of the one point program, which unrolls the derivative graph of packed_analytic_moments and the elliptic terms. Bound that program, using the §2 tangents and the packed-moment terms §1 ranks. The expression is defined twice today: the production row program _row_program at nova/equilibrium/fixed_points.py:389 (its _source_jets at :204), evaluated only by fixed_points.maintain_rows (:411, the call at :476), whose callers today are tests/test_fixed_points.py and prototype measure and solver scripts; nova/equilibrium/support_read.py receives the row window maintain_rows keeps but does not build it, and no read driver in nova/ maintains that window yet, and a hand-written copy in the prototype build at scripts/prototypes/read_route/jets.py:229 and :321. Bound the owner in nova/equilibrium/fixed_points.py and redirect the prototype build to it, deleting the copy, so one bounded program serves both; bounding the copy alone does not meet this section.

Done when: (1) at 282 and 2,132 nodes the point batch compiles once, in at most 600 s and 64 GB peak host memory on CPU and at most 60 s on the H200, fresh process and cache disabled; (2) the built jet rows match the base build at every node at both sizes: values bit-identical, and derivative entries within 1e-9 relative as the tangent-representation ruling allows for a scan-form tangent (or bit-identical where an exact tangent is kept); (3) the 500-cell coarse-tie census test runs against an empty operand store under --mem=64G and passes on its own assertion.

§4 — The topology read meets its compile clause

With the kernel terms bounded, the kernel-backed topology read should meet the compile clause converged-forward-solve already holds it to. This section removes the expected failures that record the barrier, so the suite asserts the bound directly.

Done when: (1) on the H200, fresh process and cache disabled, the kernel-backed topology read compiles in at most 60 s, with an executable of at most 50 MB and at most 64 GB of host memory, at 132, 300 and 550 cells; (2) the strict xfail on test_kernel_compile_growth is removed and the test passes there; (3) the module-level strict marker kernel_compile_expected_failure (tests/equilibrium/test_topology_read.py:39, raising KernelCompileBarrier) is removed from every test that carries it, and the seven kernel_compile_budget fixture ids then pass on their own assertions, in one H200 job that runs the whole file. Leaving the marker in place while the compile meets the bound turns those ids into strict XPASS failures, so the marker removal is part of this clause.

§ Decisions

Which tangent forms may bound a kernel term's graph?

Inherited from converged-forward-solve's kernel-loop-representation and tangent-identity-tolerance rulings of 2026-10-06.

A fixed-capacity scan tangent of harmonic_moments compiled in 1.43 s and agreed with jax.jvp of the base to 1.04e-10, where the exact tangent took 54.1 s. The Hessian only steers Newton and the implicit null tangent, so 1e-9 sits far below every solve tolerance. Relaxing either bound is forbidden without the lead.
