§1 — From projections to honest maps

The localization record produced the first misfit maps, but they are source-model projections: a single filament explains 43–60% of the surviving power and its best location disagrees between drive groups. The honest map is source-free: in the vacuum region the misfit field satisfies the homogeneous Grad–Shafranov equation, so the misfit flux admits an exact expansion in toroidal harmonics (half-integer Legendre functions about the machine's focal circle). Fitting interior and exterior expansions to the measured misfit — probe projections plus calibrated loop fluxes — yields contour maps of the misfit flux valid wherever no unmodelled current flows, and the places where the two expansions cannot be reconciled are precisely where the unmodelled current is.

Three products, per drive group (P4 lower first — its pattern reproduces at split-half cosine +0.92): the misfit flux contour map with confidence bounds, the enclosed-current localization (expansion-mismatch regions plus multi-filament and extended-path fits scored against the harmonic representation), and the non-axisymmetric residue — the fraction of the misfit no axisymmetric expansion reaches, which bounds what a 3D busbar run must explain and feeds mast-nonaxisymmetric-physics.

§3 — A second instrument has landed on the same conductor

An unrelated data path now implicates P4 lower, and the verdict in §2.6 has to account for it. Decomposing a stored EFIT flux map into plasma, external and residual terms — work owned by efit-flux-decomposition — leaves a residual whose Grad–Shafranov operator is an implied missing-current density. On shot 21978 slice 46 that residual current peaks at 1.660757 MA/m² at R = 1.53834 m, Z = −1.15313 m, inside p4_lower, carrying 10.947 kA signed and 181.809 kA absolute. The same run vindicated most of the vacuum model: the recorded conductor currents cancel 96.5548% of the native-grid exterior signal, taking 10.080 MA down to 347 kA.

That is a different instrument answering the same question. This plan localises from 77 probes and calibrated loops; that one localises from a two-dimensional flux map produced by an outside code. They share no sensor, no calibration chain and no response model, so agreement between them is not a consistency check that either could have passed alone. It also sits well with what §2's own evidence already concluded — coil-geometry-inversion returned a bounded negative, so the survivor is current- or sensor-side rather than geometry-side, and a residual current localised inside the conductor is exactly a current-side signature.

Two things this does not yet establish, and neither is a formality. The decomposition's own operator round trip failed its bound — 5.152549e-03 of the stored flux span against a required 1e-6 — so that run is explicitly diagnostic rather than validated, and a localisation drawn through a defective coupling path could move when the path is fixed. And the bilinear grid interpolation carries 4.162463% of the flux span against a residual RMS of 3.797384%, so on the interpolated grid the residual is comparable to its own interpolation error. The 96.5548% cancellation was measured on the native grid and is the trustworthy figure; the localised peak was not.

Note also what this does not do: it does not beat this plan's resolving power. §2.1 measured harmonic filament recovery at 5.4 mm with the placement iterated and 31 mm fixed-focus, with two filaments separable at 100–200 mm. The flux-map residual's value here is independence, not precision — the 1.5–2.3 m figure that closed the feeder hunt was the bound on localising a ring's radius, which is a different problem from placing a filament.

Done-when: the flux-map localisation is either confirmed as a physical result — repeated across slices and shots, with the coupling round trip inside its bound and the interpolation confound controlled or eliminated by working on the native grid — or recorded as an artefact of the defective operator path, with which of the two it is stated plainly. §2.6's verdict then cites both instruments where they agree and names the disagreement where they do not, rather than resting on the magnetics alone.
