Millisecond converged solve

The north star's second half (forward-accuracy-northstar): a single converged solve at order 1 ms per member — converged meaning it satisfies the accuracy certificate, not merely terminates. Every input number is banked: the map application costs 0.784 ms/member at width 1024; the 25.0 ms per-trip quantum is mask reconciliation 12.61 + Newton re-linearization 12.34 with zero launch and preconditioner cost; the landed strict settled exit projects 1.163 ms/slice (1.50×) on the observable bank mix; the open residual-progress-exit-window decision projects 0.915 ms/slice with an explicitly tolerated error; the persistent compilation cache restores a fresh process in 63.7 s with exact-key hits on pure-data changes. Mask-only exit is measured inadmissible and a replicated single-state batch cannot exhibit exit incidence — throughput claims are made on real bank member mixes only.

§ Open questions

The prototype holds one dense Jacobian per trip and refreshes it only when a whole grade ladder is refused, which makes the inner iteration a chord method: on the MAST rows it still contracts to 1e-13 in twelve steps, on the small synthetic CPU machine it contracts linearly at about a quarter per step and needs a 24-step budget where production needs 12. Refreshing the Jacobian every step costs 0.4 to 0.9 ms (warm, H200) and buys quadratic contraction. Options: keep the chord (cheapest step, more steps), refresh every step (fewer steps, plus up to 0.9 ms each), or refresh when the contraction factor of the last step exceeds a threshold (adaptive). Recommendation: adaptive refresh, since the step budget is the quantity the millisecond target constrains and the Jacobian is not the binding cost.

Lead decision 2026-09-05: adaptive refresh. Hold one dense Jacobian per trip by default and rebuild it when the contraction factor of the last accepted step exceeds a threshold, or when a grade ladder is refused, so the step budget stays near the fresh-Jacobian count while the Jacobian cost (0.4 to 0.9 ms warm) is paid only where the chord is contracting slowly.
