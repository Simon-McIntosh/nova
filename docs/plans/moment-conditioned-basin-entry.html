§2 — Predict the moments before any solve, with declared supports

Given the prescribed flux functions p′(ψ) and FF′(ψ), a boundary hypothesis, and the net current, the toroidal current density jφ = R·p′ + FF′/(μ₀R) determines every integral moment of the plasma current up to the flux-map unknown. The deliverable is a measured answer to the owner's question — how confident can we be of these metrics? — on the frozen six MAST references plus the DIII-D score-blind frames: predict M₀ (net current), the current centroid (first radial and vertical moments), and the second-moment / internal-inductance-class content from the flux functions with the reference's own boundary, then with a perturbed boundary ladder, and report per-moment prediction error and its sensitivity to the boundary hypothesis. The existing machinery is inventoried first (f-dbs-moment-survey, survey in flight): the spine's centroid seeding, the biot moment stacks, the differencing-gate extraction that already projects labeled plasma current onto filaments.

Binding design rule — every moment declares its support. The measured hazard from the parity session: the target-current check integrates all domains (933,034.875 A on the audited frame) while IntegralObservation.plasma_current integrates the topology-qualified confined core (416,958.25 A) — a ratio of 0.4469. A moment predicted on one support and compared against, or constrained onto, another is wrong by a factor near 0.4 before any physics enters. Every predicted moment, every constraint, and every receipt in this plan states its defining current integral; a quantity silent on support fails review.
