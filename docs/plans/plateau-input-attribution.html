§1 — Existence is settled, twice, and that changes the question

An earlier framing of this work asked whether a diverted-class root exists below the measured plateau. That question is already answered and asking it again would waste the plan. It is answered from two independent directions.

From the physics. These frames are EFIT reconstructions of real DIII-D discharges — the corpus ships efit_psirz, efit_q95, efit_r_axis and efit_z_axis, and the solve's own control surface is derived from the released EFIT grid. A physical diverted plasma existed at each of these time slices. So the continuum free-boundary problem, with the machine's true wall, true conductor currents and true flux functions, has a diverted solution. There is no question of the physics admitting no answer.

From the solver — and this pillar has since weakened, 2026-08-25. Frame 22086/43 in this same corpus family was banked at a terminal relative residual of 2.911868346631881e-16 with cumulative growth 3.0. It no longer reproduces. On the current-pinned label-seeded route that frame is bounded non-convergent at 5.049e-3, and §3 found nothing separating it from the five plateauing frames on any measured substitution. The banked artifact did not embed its execution source revision and 32 production Python paths differ between that tree and the measured one, so the change is declined attribution rather than a named regression. The existence argument therefore rests on the physics alone, not on two independent legs, and the solver leg should not be cited as current evidence until something reproduces it.

So the plateau is not evidence about existence. It is evidence about inputs. If a root exists physically and the operator can reach machine precision elsewhere, then a solve that stalls at 0.087–0.155 while holding topology is solving a different problem from the one the physical plasma satisfied. The productive question is which of the differences between those two problems is responsible — and the differences are not hypothetical. Four of them are documented in the driver and its receipts.

§2 — Evaluate the residual as a field, at the labelled state

What the solve currently reports is not a field residual. nova/equilibrium/fixed_point.py::_relative_residual is max|g − x| / max(|g|) — the sup norm of F(ψ) − ψ over the whole flux state, divided by the global peak of the mapped state, reduced to one scalar per iteration. So the field residual is computed and then discarded. Three consequences follow and all three matter here: every trace of where the residual lives is thrown away; a sup norm is set by a single worst cell, so “one bad cell at the X point” and “broadly wrong everywhere” are indistinguishable; and normalising by the global peak makes a residual concentrated where ψ is small invisible.

Nobody has looked at that field, and it is the cheapest measurement on this lane. One map evaluation, no solve: apply the forward map once to the labelled state and keep F(ψlabel) − ψlabel as a map rather than a number. Its spatial structure separates the candidates that prose cannot:

  • concentrated at the boundary or the X point → boundary representation, or the wall
  • proportional to a single coil's Green's function → a current wiring error
  • smooth in ψ across the core → a profile or convention scale error
  • oscillating on the stencil scale → discretisation

The prior evidence on this question is stale and must be re-run, not cited. docs/figures/efit-forward-parity/reference-seeded-forward-slice.json reports a label-seeded solve on MAST 21985/51 converging in 5 Newton iterations to fixed_point_defect = 0.0 against a 1e-8 criterion — and landing on the vacuum branch, plasma current below 1% of efm/plasma_current_c, flux map 0.6479 of the reference span away. That receipt was authored at 57a1bfb9 on 2026-08-21. The current-normalisation amplitude that pins the declared plasma current arrived at cf812416 on 2026-08-22, one day later. With a(x, I) = I / Σ unscaled_cell_current(x) renormalising every current moment, a collapse to a current-free root is unreachable by construction. So we do not currently know what a label-seeded solve does on the shipped map. §6 — A smooth transition between diverted and limited

The admissibility predicate is Boolean: a candidate is in the requested topology class or it is refused. Measured consequence — it refuses roughly 85 of every 89 Newton updates, and the two mechanisms built to step around that refusal were both refuted. A hard classifier is the wrong object here for a reason that is physical rather than numerical: diverted and limited are not two disjoint species but the two sides of one continuous configuration, separated by whether the critical flux surface touches a material surface, and a plasma passes smoothly between them as the X point moves toward and away from the wall.

A discontinuous predicate over a continuous transition creates its own difficulty. It has no derivative for a continuation method to follow, so a step that would land marginally on the wrong side is refused entirely rather than reduced; and near the transition an arbitrarily small state change flips the verdict, which is precisely where a free-boundary solve spends its time. Making the transition smooth — a signed margin between the critical surface and the material surface, or a continuous penalty on that margin, in place of a Boolean — gives the step something to descend and makes admission a matter of degree.

Done when a continuous topology margin exists whose sign reproduces the existing Boolean classification on every case the current predicate classifies, its zero set is demonstrated to lie where the classifier flips, and the five score-blind frames are re-measured with the margin in place quoting admitted-advance count out of 89, achieved Newton-step-equivalents and terminal residual against the banked values — with the requested class still held at the terminal state, since a smooth margin that reaches a lower residual by ending in the wrong class closes nothing.

§ Decisions

How is the pseudo-wall arm run — by expanding and contracting the existing rectangle over a stated range, or by sourcing DIII-D's real wall geometry and using it as the topology surface? The rectangle sweep is available immediately and bounds the sensitivity; the real wall answers the physical question but introduces a geometry the competition corpus deliberately does not ship, so its provenance must be stated and it may cross a corpus boundary the challenge intends to hold.

Deduced from the plan rather than left open. Both halves are already mandated: §1 records the wall as an unwired default rather than a physics unknown, so wiring Nova's own machine-description limiter ring is work and not a corpus crossing — the refusal it replaces scopes the absence to the competition rows, not to Nova; and §5's done-when already requires the real ring to become the DIII-D forward path's default topology surface with the pseudo-wall retained as an explicitly selected fallback. The sweep is not thereby redundant: the only existing sensitivity evidence is a two-point expansion probe on one frame, against a predicate that refuses roughly 85 of every 89 Newton updates, so the rectangle sweep bounds the sensitivity that probe cannot. Sweep to bound it, then substitute the real ring, one input per arm.

If freeing the three unknown-tier currents lowers the residual, is the result a solve or a fit? Three free conductor currents are three fitted parameters, and a residual improvement bought with them is not evidence that the forward map reproduces the reference. Decide in advance what the arm is allowed to claim.
