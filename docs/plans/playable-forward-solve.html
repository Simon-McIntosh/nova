§1 — The goal, in the lead's terms

A playable Grad-Shafranov forward solve in Nova: a person moves the plasma about with keyboard controls and watches the flux map update in a Bokeh view at human response time, ignoring true dynamics for now. The controls act on the separatrix directly, through low-degree-of-freedom constraints that move its turning points and its position rather than through a rigid profile target. The forward solve carries the coils and the first wall, so what the person steers is a physical equilibrium under the machine's own actuators. Achieving this enables the next step: training a MAST video decoder that maps forward actions to a synthetic plasma recording, the first step towards a fusion world model backed by physics. The decoder and the world model belong to imas-ambix; this plan delivers the backbone they consume.

§2 — Controls: the pulse-design constraint set on the forward solve

The specification of a flux value and a field gradient at a few points both bounds the separatrix in its R-Z box and sets its shape within it. The inverse design in nova/equilibrium/inverse.py already constrains the boundary flux at the four bounding-box control points (eight with squareness, two strike points when diverted), zero radial field at the outer and inner points (a vertical field line, hence a radial turning point on the low-field and high-field sides), zero vertical field at the upper and lower points, and the null condition at the X-point, with the points generated by ControlPoints in nova/geometry/plasmapoints.py from geometric axis, minor radius, elongation, triangularities and squareness. The transfer of this set onto the forward solve's constraint interface, with compensating circuit directions read from the response matrix, is constraint-augmented-newton-krylov §4 (its followup on the bounding-box rows). This plan consumes those rows as the control vocabulary: bulk R and Z through the box, elongation and triangularity through the turning points, gaps through the wall-gap row, X-point location through the null row. A keyboard action is a change of one of those parameters; the solver re-solves from the previous equilibrium as a warm start.

§3 — Performance: a keyframe budget on the human scale

Human response time gives a budget of order 100 ms per keyframe. The reduced-state plain-Newton route (millisecond-converged-solve §2) measures an accepting Newton step at 2.15 ms and a warm trip at 46 ms on the four MAST bank rows, with 95 percent of a trip being one topology read; a keyframe that warm-starts from the previous equilibrium needs one to three trips, so the budget is reachable once the topology read at the trip boundary is cut and the constrained solve runs on the reduced route. Required here: the constraint rows augmenting the reduced Newton solve rather than only the Newton–Krylov ladder, a warm-start contract (a moved control point re-solved from the previous state converges in fewer trips than from the seed), and a measured keyframe wall on the H200 with the flux rendered, so the interactive budget is a receipt rather than a projection.

§4 — Interface: keyboard controls and a Bokeh view

The Bokeh application under apps/pulsedesign/ already renders a plasma, its control points, X-points and the wall from the inverse design with sliders for minor radius, gap, elongation and the triangularities. The playable solve re-uses that view over the forward solve: a server-side loop holds the current equilibrium, maps key presses to control-point moves (bulk R and Z, elongation, upper and lower triangularity, X-point, gaps), re-solves, and pushes the flux map, the separatrix, the control points, the compensating currents and the keyframe wall to the page. Deliverables: the key map, the solve loop with its warm start, the flux-map render at the keyframe rate, and a receipt of keyframe wall and trips per action on one MAST equilibrium.

§5 — The decoder seam

What the decoder consumes is the sequence of forward states with their actions: the flux map on the lattice, the separatrix and turning points, the coil and compensating currents, and the action that produced each frame. Those cross the boundary to imas-ambix as data, not as code (the coupled-repository rule: ambix owns the corpus reading and the decoder). This plan records the frame schema it emits and the receipt that a recorded steering session round-trips through it; the decoder training itself is ambix work and is not scheduled here.

Where the numbers stand and how the constrained reduced route is posed. Measured on the reduced-state plain-Newton route (millisecond-converged-solve, anchor millisecond-step): accepting Newton step 2.15 ms median on 22086/43, warm trip 46 to 58 ms with 95 percent of the trip being one topology read, a converged solve in three trips. A keyframe re-solved from the previous equilibrium in one to three trips therefore sits at 50 to 150 ms before any further trip-boundary work, so the order-100 ms budget is reachable on this route and the constraint rows are what remains. The constrained reduced route appends the compensating unknowns to the amplitude vector and uses the augmented residual of assemble_augmented_system: the augmented state is [u, c] with u the plasma-cell amplitudes the frozen partition defines and c the normalised compensating unknowns, one per registered row; the reconstruction is external + sum_k flux_delta_k(c_k) + P scatter(u) behind the frozen shadow; the residual is [u - gather(moments(reconstruct)), g_k(reconstruct, c)] with each row scaled as the Newton-Krylov augmentation scales it; the dense Jacobian is one jacfwd over the augmented state per trip; the merit adds the scaled constraint residuals to the production flux merit so a step is accepted on the augmented system. Rows enter through the ConstraintFunctional protocol, so the current-centroid row on main and the bounding-box rows in flight are the same kind of input. The warm-start contract: a moved target re-solved from the previous [u, c] and shadow converges in fewer trips than the same target solved from the cold seed, and the unmoved target re-solves in one trip with zero accepted steps.

What the consumer reads. imas-ambix's physics-carried-playable-plasma (S19, M4) is blocked on nova's forward-solve-api §5, whose receipt must carry, at no extra compile, psi and psi_N on the hex mesh, the O-point, primary and secondary X-points, the LCFS, the strike points and the per-cell domain labels the plasma-cell authority resolves (core, private flux, wall shadow; every other cell outside the LCFS simply open, no scrape-off-layer class). The frame record this section defines carries those fields per frame together with the action that produced the frame (the control parameter moved, its delta, the commanded control-point set), the coil and compensating currents per circuit, the keyframe wall and trip count, and the solve request identity (carrier identity, Nova version, resolved policy), so a recorded steering session is a sequence of forward-solve-api §5 receipts with actions attached and the decoder never re-derives topology from psi.
