Adjudicate whether the private-flux shadow over-rejects the wall extremum

The absent class margin is now attributed, which turns the question into whether the attributed behaviour is CORRECT. The private-flux shadow discards a finite wall extremum on 4 of 12 banked MAST terminals, sending u_wall minus u_x to positive infinity, and the operands it discards are ordinary values: 0.3310275619269073 and 0.3310454781725451 on 21978/35, 0.30190002257031606 and 0.3000846561326388 on 22086/43. The rule exists for a real reason, since a wall extremum lying in the private-flux region is not a confinement boundary and must not set the margin. So decide, per terminal, whether these four rejections are that case or an over-rejection. This matters beyond bookkeeping: dual-branch-selection's selection observable cannot be the exact margin if the margin is undefined on a third of the cohort, and the fork-arming measurement already has to fall back to always-fork on exactly those frames, which floors any armed policy at 2 of 6 however tight its threshold. Show the geometry rather than asserting the verdict - plot the wall extremum, the separatrix and the private-flux region for at least one rejected and one admitted terminal, since a claim about which side of a boundary a point falls on is under-communicated without a figure. If the rejections are correct, then the margin is legitimately undefined there and the selection design must carry a defined fallback rather than treating absence as an error. If they are over-rejections, the shadow test needs the repair and four absent margins become finite.
