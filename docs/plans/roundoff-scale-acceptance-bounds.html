§1 — The second fault, now that the first is closed

The determinism lane separated two faults deliberately and closed one. Fixed persistent executable reuse now makes three fresh production processes return bitwise-identical terminal flux and bitwise-identical values for all 69 registered observables, at a steady-state cost of 0.087 per cent. And acceptance still fails at 67 of 69 bounds. That was predicted rather than discovered: making a verdict reproducible cannot make a criterion passable.

The two bounds that fail score quantities whose continuum value is zero. Every one of the 11 case verdicts that moved under the cache-off arm belonged to conservation.divergence_b or conservation.divergence_j. In the continuum both are identically zero, so neither has a nonzero exact value for a relative bound to be relative to.

But their computed value is NOT roundoff, and this plan was drafted saying it was. Corrected 2026-08-25 after review. The distinction is the whole plan and getting it wrong would have produced a floor orders of magnitude too tight. tests/test_equilibrium_stencil_mesh.py carries the repository's contract explicitly: the continuum divergence identities cancel under commuting central differences, while the ring-mesh least-squares derivatives do not commute and therefore land at a second-order truncation floor. Truncation does not shrink with working precision; it shrinks with the mesh. The receipt values separate the two scales unmistakably: the process-to-process changes are around 1e-16 — one and two units in the last place, 1.3877787807814457e-16 and 2.7755575615628914e-16 — while the divergence_j observable itself is around 1e-10, six orders larger. So the value is truncation-dominated and only its variation is roundoff.

Three quantities must be kept apart, and conflating any two of them produces a wrong floor. The exact continuum identity, which is zero. The non-commuting stencil's truncation floor, which is a real numerical property of the discretisation and scales with h. And the last-bit process-to-process variation, which is roundoff and scales with ε. The acceptance driver already distinguishes them in part: its failure verdict calls divergence_j a fitted field-function gradient computation difference and leaves divergence_b unadjudicated, so neither is currently claimed as pure rounding.

What is wrong today is narrower and sharper than a missing floor: an absolute bound already exists and is already satisfied, and a relative bound overrides it. On case 21978/35 at width 1 all three invocation values sit below the registered absolute_bound of 9.84e-16 — and the case passes [true, false, false] anyway, because a relative_bound of 0.8426 is applied as well and one ULP of movement crosses it. So the repair may not require deriving a new floor at all. It may require the registration to stop applying a relative criterion where the reference has no nonzero continuum value and an absolute criterion is already present and met. That is a much smaller change than this plan was drafted assuming, and it should be tested before any floor is derived.

The consequence is visible in the aggregate figures too: maximum bound ratios of 2.967 and 5.341 against absolute differences at 1e-16. A criterion must be passable by something. Determinism made that crossing repeatable; it did not make it a measurement.

§2 — Classify every bound before repairing any

The temptation is to loosen the two failing bounds, and it must be refused: a criterion widened until the current value passes is derived from an achieved value of the quantity it scores, which is the one thing the standing rule prohibits. The distinction that makes a repair legitimate is whether the scored quantity has a nonzero exact value.

  • Nonzero exact value. A relative bound is meaningful, because there is something to be relative to. Volume, major radius, plasma current and the geometric moments are in this class.
  • Zero in the continuum. Only an absolute bound is meaningful, and it must be derived rather than observed — but from the right term. Where the discretisation does not preserve the identity, the floor is set by truncation and scales with the mesh: an n·ε·scale roundoff floor would be orders of magnitude too tight and would fail a correct computation. Where the discretisation does preserve it, a roundoff floor is the right form. Either way a floor chosen because the current value sits below it is derived from an achieved value and is prohibited.

The classification is not a judgement call and should not be recorded as one: for each registered observable it is a fact about the quantity's definition, checkable by reading how it is formed. Recording it once, per bound, is most of the work — and it is the part that stops this recurring on the next observable someone registers.

Take the cheap test first. Before deriving any floor, check the hypothesis that §1 raises: that the failing cases already satisfy their registered absolute bound and fail only because a relative bound is applied alongside it. If that holds across the failing cases, the repair is a registration rule — no relative criterion where the reference has no nonzero continuum value and an absolute one is present — and no new floor needs deriving at all. Report that outcome plainly if it holds, because it is a far smaller and more durable change than a set of hand-derived floors.

Done when every one of the 69 registered bounds carries a recorded classification of whether its scored quantity has a nonzero continuum value, with the reason stated per bound rather than in aggregate; every bound classified zero-in-the-continuum additionally records whether its discretisation preserves the identity, so a truncation floor is separated from a roundoff floor rather than assumed; each such bound then carries an absolute floor whose derivation is shown — from the discretisation order and mesh where truncation dominates, from operation count and working precision where it does not — and explicitly not from any achieved residual; the measured separation of scales is quoted per bound, with the process-to-process variation beside the observable's own magnitude, since the two differ by six orders on divergence_j and a single figure would hide that; the acceptance route is re-scored under the corrected criteria on the frozen six-case cohort at both registered widths, reporting the new pass count against the banked 67 of 69 and naming every bound whose verdict changed and why; and a test asserts that a bound on a quantity with no nonzero continuum value cannot be registered with a relative criterion. A re-score that still fails is a legitimate outcome provided the surviving failures are on quantities with nonzero continuum values, where a relative bound means something and a failure is a real disagreement.

§3 — What this does and does not unblock

The throughput product is a store of boundaries, moments and conservation diagnostics whose value rests on a consumer trusting a published label. Reproducibility is now established and priced. What remains is whether the acceptance check that certifies a label is measuring anything: two of its bounds currently are not, and until they are, a passing acceptance run and a failing one differ by the last bit of a quantity that should be zero.

This plan does not touch convergence, and must not be read as doing so. The plateau lane established that none of four documented input substitutions moves the residual, and the operator lane established that the forward map admits an analytic fixed point at machine precision while the solver reaches a different one. Those are separate and larger questions. Repairing a criterion changes what the acceptance route can certify; it changes nothing about which equilibrium the solver finds.
