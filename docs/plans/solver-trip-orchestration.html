Solver trip orchestration

The H200 stage profile (receipt docs/figures/single-grid-solver-cutover/h200-solve-profile.json, landed 2026-09-01) settled where forward-solve time goes: one complete fixed-point map application costs 0.784 ms/member at width 1024 (carrier 0.767, response 0.767, standalone topology 1.030, zero host callbacks), while 95.9% of solve time sits in per-trip orchestration — active-set reconciliation, Newton setup and the first Krylov action — repeated across 16 outer trips, with one warmup sweep at 3.2% and extra GMRES actions at 0.9%. Scan latency is negligible (0.12 ms at 16 trips). The lead's ~1 ms/slice target is therefore an orchestration-restructuring problem, not a map-acceleration problem: the ideal collapse of the per-trip quantum floors at 1.75 ms/slice under the 16-trip structure, and only trip-count reduction cuts below that. Lead guidance 2026-09-01: the 1.75 ms floor is acceptable, and the cut-through below it is attractive — investigate both. Baseline provenance stands reconciled on the cutover plan: the committed ladder's width-1024 row records 373.8 ms/member for the full settled active-set solve; 42.77 ms/slice was a separate earlier single-slice measurement.

§1 — Compile cost and cadence

The profile recorded an 800.6 s compile that amortizes below 1 ms/slice only past ~800k slices. Two questions the lane designs need answered as numbers. Cadence: the compile is paid once per (program shape, device, jaxlib) per process — nova's suite and bank lanes are deliberately fresh-process, and each batch-width bucket is its own program — so establish exactly which lanes repay it and whether the JAX persistent compilation cache (jax_compilation_cache_dir) is enabled anywhere in nova's launch recipes; if not, measure the warm-restart compile time with the cache on, on the reserved H200, and state the cache-key stability across our own commits (an XLA/jaxlib bump invalidates; a pure-data change must not). Decomposition: where do the 800 s go — trace-time versus XLA passes (measure with JAX_LOG_COMPILES and the XLA compilation-time breakdown), whether the dual-branch stacked program doubles it, and whether donating buffers or splitting the settled-loop program into per-trip executables moves it materially. Done-when: a receipt states compile seconds cold and cache-warm, the pass breakdown, the per-lane payment cadence (bank regeneration, small lanes, tests), and a recommendation with numbers.

§2 — The per-trip orchestration quantum

Decompose the reconciliation + Newton-setup + first-Krylov block that carries 95.9% of solve time: how much is mask reconciliation (gather/scatter and comparison over the active set), how much is Newton operator setup (re-linearization, preconditioner assembly), how much is the first GMRES action's synchronization, and how much is fixed per-trip launch overhead that fusing the trip body into one program would remove. The S16 factorial arms (run r-20260901T053948341346-sse-h200-step-decomposition) carry the trip census — all 1024 members execute 16 active-set outer trips, 16 attempted and accepted Newton promotions, GMRES=4 — and are the unremeasured baseline this section builds on. Done-when: a receipt attributes the per-trip quantum to named sub-stages with measured times at width 1024, and states the fusion/restructuring candidates with their individual ceilings against the 1.75 ms floor.

§3 — Trip-count cut-through

The 1.75 ms floor stands only while every member pays 16 outer trips. The cut-through question: how many trips does the active set actually need before it settles? Mine the committed bank telemetry (per-trip mask-difference records, the retained per-iteration histories) for the settlement histogram across the MAST twelve-arm and DIII-D five-frame banks — if most members settle in far fewer than 16 trips, a settled-early exit (preserving the own-mask acceptance and best-iterate retention semantics exactly, proven by the existing paired bit-identity tests where the mask does not move) turns the mean trip count into the effective divisor. Fixed-shape constraints apply: an early exit must be a masked no-op continuation, not a dynamic shape. Done-when: the settlement histogram is banked per machine, the projected mean-trip ms/slice is stated against the 1 ms target, and the semantic-preservation test surface for a settled-exit policy is named. Any policy change to solver semantics is a lead decision — this section produces the numbers and the test plan, not the cutover.

§4 — The settled-exit implementation

Added 2026-09-01 under the locked settled-exit-policy decision. Implement the settled-early trip exit as a masked no-op continuation in the fixed-point trip loop: once a member's active-set mask difference has been zero for the qualifying suffix and its acceptance criteria hold, subsequent trips execute as masked no-ops for that member — fixed shapes preserved, no dynamic trip count, telemetry padding distinguishable from executed work exactly as today. The referee surface is untouchable: the seven preservation tests named in settlement-histogram.json (own-mask acceptance inertness and refusal, best-iterate retention, settled-mask retention, constant-mask smooth-solve bit identity, continuation on/off and eager/JIT bit identity) run unmodified and stay green. Acceptance beyond the referees: a paired before/after comparison on the committed banks — bit-identical results wherever the mask history settles identically, and the measured width-1024 ms/slice against the projected 0.22–0.47 band. Done-when: tests green (referees unmodified), the paired bank comparison banked, and the H200 throughput receipt states the achieved ms/slice beside the projection.

§5 — Why the exit never fires at production width

Added 2026-09-01 from the §4 receipt's follow-on. The implementation is proven safe (referees green both lanes, 1024/1024 members bit-identical against the full-trip control, the five committed DIII-D qualified no-op suffixes replayed exactly) yet the width-1024 production measurement executed all sixteen trips on every member — speedup 0.997×, achieved 1.755 ms/slice against the 0.22–0.47 projection. The conjunction tells us why mechanically: members keep accepting Newton promotions after the mask settles, so the strict no-op qualification never holds mid-solve on that workload, while some committed bank histories do carry genuinely qualified suffixes. Open questions this section owns: whether the width-1024 measurement workload (a replicated-member batch) is representative of bank regeneration's member diversity; what fraction of committed-bank members ever reach a qualified no-op suffix before trip 16 (the settlement census counted mask settlement, which this receipt proves is not the same thing); and whether a residual-progress-aware relaxation of the exit (stop when the retained best iterate stops improving beyond tolerance, not only on strict no-ops) could fire earlier without touching the referee semantics — that last is a policy question that returns to the lead with numbers before any implementation. Done-when: a receipt states the qualified-suffix fraction across both committed banks, the workload-representativeness verdict for the width-1024 measurement, and the projected speedup under each candidate exit criterion, dispatching no implementation.

§ Decisions

Adopt a settled-early trip exit (masked no-op continuation) once §3's histogram and test surface are banked?

Lead decision 2026-09-01 on the full census: adopt the settled-early trip exit as a masked no-op continuation, gated on the seven named preservation tests (own-mask acceptance, best-iterate retention, paired bit-identity where the mask does not move) staying green plus a paired before/after bank comparison. Basis: MAST settles in mean 2.0 trips and DIII-D in 4.2 of 16, projecting 0.22-0.47 ms/slice - through the 1 ms target on both machines - and every post-settlement trip is provably a no-op today, so the exit removes work that changes nothing. The fixed-shorter-cap alternative was rejected as leaving 2-4x on the table and risking the 4-trip tail; deferral was rejected because the histogram shape is robust to the open drift question, whose arms are fenced.

residual-progress-exit-window

The suffix census (docs/figures/solver-trip-orchestration/suffix-census.json) gives the trade: the landed strict exit is semantically free and projects 1.163 ms/slice (1.504x) on the observable bank mix (DIII-D 5/5 qualify at trips 3-6, observable MAST arms keep improving, declared-bank lower bound 29.4%); a causal residual-progress window projects 0.915 ms/slice observed (through the 1 ms target) but tolerates an explicit state/residual error and requires paired semantic validation. Mask-only exit is inadmissible. The width-1024 replicated-state measurement cannot exhibit exit incidence and does not bear on this choice.
