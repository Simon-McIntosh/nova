§1 — The archive is a calibration instrument

The programme so far consumed designed vacuum shots — a few hundred out of a 17,111-shot archive. But every pulse carries vacuum intervals: a pre-pulse window before any poloidal coil is energised (pure instrument: offset and drift with zero field), a pre-breakdown window where coils are driven but no plasma exists (exact field computable from recorded currents), and a post-pulse window after termination (exact field again, with the caveat that vessel and case currents decay through it on L/R times the case measurement bounded at 15–146 ms). Edlington (2001) records that MAST itself calibrated this way — the P2-plate compensation coefficients and the plasma-Rogowski cross-calibration both came from vacuum intervals, taken "at the end of a long flat-top pulse to allow induced currents to decay". The instrument chain makes drift a physical certainty, not a hypothesis: MAST pickup pairs are summed and passed through analogue integrators before digitisation, and an integrator's zero walks. The noise cohort measured the walk — median |drift| 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ T/s — and every fit already subtracts a per-shot baseline; what does not yet exist is a systematic per-pulse, per-channel extraction across the whole archive, stored where consumers can use it.
