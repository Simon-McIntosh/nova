§1 — The archive is a calibration instrument

The programme so far consumed designed vacuum shots — a few hundred out of a 17,111-shot archive. But every pulse carries vacuum intervals: a pre-pulse window before any poloidal coil is energised (pure instrument: offset and drift with zero field), a pre-breakdown window where coils are driven but no plasma exists (exact field computable from recorded currents), and a post-pulse window after termination (exact field again, with the caveat that vessel and case currents decay through it on L/R times the case measurement bounded at 15–146 ms). Edlington (2001) records that MAST itself calibrated this way — the P2-plate compensation coefficients and the plasma-Rogowski cross-calibration both came from vacuum intervals, taken "at the end of a long flat-top pulse to allow induced currents to decay". The instrument chain makes drift a physical certainty, not a hypothesis: MAST pickup pairs are summed and passed through analogue integrators before digitisation, and an integrator's zero walks. The noise cohort measured the walk — median |drift| 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁴ T/s — and every fit already subtracts a per-shot baseline; what does not yet exist is a systematic per-pulse, per-channel extraction across the whole archive, stored where consumers can use it.

§2 — Implementation

  1. Window discovery. A machine-agnostic routine that classifies each pulse's timeline into instrument-quiet (no drives), vacuum-driven (drives, no plasma), and plasma intervals, from recorded currents and a plasma-presence signal (Ip channel or its absence). Adapters supply channel maps; the classifier is shared. Post-pulse windows carry a passive-decay guard: exclude the first N × (longest measured case/vessel L/R time) or fit the decay explicitly.
  2. Instrument terms per (pulse, channel). From the instrument-quiet windows: offset, drift rate, drift curvature (integrator second-order walk), and — where both pre- and post-pulse windows exist — the closure test: does the integrator return to its extrapolated baseline after the pulse? A non-closing channel is flagged; the closure defect measures accumulated integrator error, exactly the quantity that corrupts flux loops and long-pulse reconstructions. All terms emitted as diagnostic-correction-schema records (offset and drift_rate kinds).
  3. Opportunistic gain checks. In vacuum-driven windows, the exact solution per channel follows from the described Green's columns and the recorded currents — a per-pulse mini gain fit. Pooled over the archive, these detect scale steps and pair-state changes at the pulse where they happen (the block table's 37 switch points and obv03's mid-life step become measured transitions with pulse-number precision, not bracketed guesses).
  4. Archive sweep and products. A SLURM-friendly sweep over the MAST store (chunked under the one-hour debug wall) producing: the per-pulse signature store, per-channel drift/offset time series across the machine's life, transition catalogues (steps, flips, deaths), and summary figures. The correction-schema instance gains its time-resolved layer from measurement rather than curation.
  5. Portability proof. The routines take arrays and adapter callbacks only; the done-when includes running the window classifier and instrument-term extraction unchanged against a second data source (any IMAS-backed pulse file with pf_active + magnetics is sufficient) to demonstrate machine-agnosticism.

Done-when: window classifier and instrument-term extraction landed in nova/calibrate/ with synthetic-truth unit tests (injected drift, offsets, non-closure); MAST archive sweep banked with per-channel drift series and transition catalogue; corrections emitted in schema form; portability demonstrated on a non-MAST source; findings recorded in a research note.
