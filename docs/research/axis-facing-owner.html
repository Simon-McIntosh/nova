Research input

Axis-facing X-point owner

At 2,098 cells a single booked owner cell, cell 47, carries 59 % of the diverted map's flux error: its booked area fraction matches the analytic one to 1.5 × 10−6 while its booked current is off by 3.3 × 10−3 relative. The candidate the plan names — taking the owner conic from the saddle the read already locates — is measured and refuted as a whole-carrier repair: it passes the 2,098-cell guard at 9.41 × 10−5 but costs 9.3 × at 282 and 9.2 × at 550, because a cell-sized least-squares quadratic absorbs the cubic content a second-order expansion about the saddle drops. Six owner representations spanning the natural design space are measured on all three carriers and none passes all three guards, so the axis-facing owner remains the continuous bottleneck. The diverted cold solve does converge at 282 and 550 under the delivered owner.

Method

The carrier set is the exact clipped single-null diverted certificate, asked for 250, 500 and 2,000 cells, which realises 282, 550 and 2,098. Every row is one all_debug allocation of 380 GB that walks the three carrier sizes inside itself and writes a receipt per size; no row is an array task. The GPU rungs were unavailable for the whole session — betelgeuse reported State=MIXED+DRAIN+RESERVED with Reason=Kill task failed [root@2026-10-17T09:40:32], 2 of its 8 H200s free but admitting no jobs, and all 8 titan P100s were held by the codex-embed service in job 1282615 — so both the map ladder and the cold solves ran on CPU with JAX_PLATFORMS=cpu.

Three quantities are reported at every size. The map flux error is max|flux_solved − analytic| / span against the guards 1.0866 × 10−3 at 282, 5 × 10−4 at 550 and 1 × 10−4 at 2,098. The reconstruction floor is the same quantity for the reference moments themselves, so it is the smallest value any owner booking can reach without the carrier changing. The cold solve limit is 1 × 10−12.

At 2,098 the answer is unambiguous. Cell 47 is the only top-ranked cell that is also an owner cell, it is an order of magnitude above every other cell on relative moment residual (3.335 × 10−3 against 3.6 × 10−4 and below), and patching it back to the reference takes the map from 2.493 × 10−4 to 1.474 × 10−4 — 59 % of the error over the floor. Its residual is [0.1488, 4.70 × 10−5, 2.22 × 10−3]: it lives in the cell current, with the radial moment smaller by four orders of magnitude. Its booked area fraction, 0.02851379310, matches the analytic 0.02851231893 to 1.5 × 10−6 — so the conic gets the size of the owned region right and the weighting of that region wrong. Cell 47 owns 2.85 % of its cell: it is a thin sliver of the separatrix sector, exactly the geometry in which a small displacement of the cut boundary preserves area while moving weight between the two sides, and the residual pattern (large current, negligible radial moment) is the signature of a boundary displaced along one principal direction.

Patching the next five cells changes almost nothing: the cumulative error after six patches is 1.30437 × 10−4, barely below cell 47 alone, and 7.4 × the floor. So one cell carries the concentration and the remaining ~1.3 × 10−4 is spread thin across the other 2,097. Fixing cell 47 alone therefore does not reach the 1 × 10−4 guard, which is why the ladder below looks for a representation rather than a patch.

At 282 and 550 the top-ranked cells are not drivers: patching cell 71 at 282 raises the error from 1.080 × 10−3 to 1.143 × 10−3, and patching cell 8 at 550 raises it from 4.096 × 10−4 to 4.173 × 10−4. Ranks are computed on moment residual magnitude, which is a proxy; the operations that lower this carrier's flux error are the ones done at 2,098. The two low-resolution carriers are limited by a broadly distributed term, not by one cell.

The plan's candidate rests on the claim that the fitted Bell stationary point drifts and that anchoring at the located saddle fixes it. Two facts settle the question. First, the fitted quadratic's slope at the located saddle is not small — up to 1.80 in span units per unit length at 282, still 0.60 at 2,098 — so the fitted quadratic is a cell fit and not a saddle expansion; it has deliberately not been anchored. Second, its own stationary point sits 1.87, 1.63 and 1.29 cells away from the located saddle at 282, 550 and 2,098: the offset shrinks with the cell but never vanishes, so the anchor mismatch is a resolution-independent property of the fit, not a rounding error. Both quantities fall with resolution, which is the same signature the map error has, and it is why an exact-Hessian expansion about the saddle is the better model precisely where the cell is small.

The owner set is small — 8, 7 and 7 cells — and carries 10.9 %, 17.9 % and 18.2 % of the moment residual mass. That a 7-cell set can move the whole-carrier map error by a factor of 9 is the reason this node exists at all.

The plan's named candidate is refuted as a whole-carrier repair. Taking the owner conic from the saddle the read already locates, as the normal form does, does exactly what it promises at 2,098 — 2.493 × 10−4 falls to 9.409 × 10−5, under the guard — and it fails by 9.3 × at 282 and 9.2 × at 550, where it is worse than the representation it was meant to replace. The mechanism is visible in the probe table: at 282 the fitted quadratic carries a 1 %-of-span value mismatch and a slope of 1.80 span-units at the saddle, and those terms are the cell's actual flux content. A second-order expansion about the saddle is a good model of a small cell and a poor model of a large one; a least-squares quadratic fitted over the cell is the reverse. No single conic anchored at the saddle can be both.

The one candidate that keeps the fitted field and takes only its cut direction from the exact saddle Hessian passes 282 and 550 and leaves 2,098 at 2.49325729609 × 10−4, barely moved from the delivered value, and its 550 result is bit-identical to the delivered one. So the branch-cut orientation is not the limiting term: what the owner gets wrong at 2,098 is the cut's shape, not its direction.

The four intermediate anchors fail in both regimes at once, which is what rules out the obvious hybrid. Pinning the exact value and zero gradient onto the fitted curvature, keeping the fitted shape with an exact level, and keeping the exact curvature with a fitted level all land between 1.06 × 10−2 and 3.16 × 10−2 at 282 — worse than either extreme. The pairs that pair an exact constraint with a fitted free parameter do not compose: the constraint and the free parameter are describing the same flux, and forcing one to the exact value while leaving the other at the fit drives them apart rather than together.

Removing the owner altogether costs an order of magnitude at 282 and 550 and two orders at 2,098, which is the calibration check on the guards: they are set for the owner and not for the unowned map.

Verdict on the done-when

The 2,098-cell map guard is not met by any conic owner, and the reason is structural rather than a missing candidate. The delivered half-plane owner sits at 2.49303535497 × 10−4, 2.5 × over the guard, with the reconstruction floor at 1.76641360086e-5, so the headroom is large but the excess is nearly resolution-independent: 4.61 × 10−4, 2.275 × 10−4 and 2.316 × 10−4 over the floor at 282, 550 and 2,098. A systematic term that does not fall with resolution is not something a better conic removes; it is a property of integrating a quadratic over a region the conic only approximates.

The plan's named candidate is measured and refuted. The four derived anchors are measured and refuted. The negative control holds: restoring the fitted Bell stationary point as the owner conic anchor brings the 2,098 map error back to 2.49 × 10−4, the value the defect was recorded at.

The repair the evidence points at is the region, not the field. The reference booking that the map is judged against does not integrate a conic over the owner cells at all: it builds exact normal-form fragment rings from the saddle's own cubic and the straight edge intervals the read has already computed, and integrates the cell's fitted quadratic over those rings. Cell 47's numbers are the tell — the conic gets its area right to 1.5 × 10−6 and its current wrong by 3.3 × 10−3, which is a boundary displaced along one principal direction, not a level or a curvature in error. A conic cannot represent a cubic separatrix; the read's own fragment geometry can, and it is already in the reading. The next node should book the owner cells on the read's normal-form rings rather than on the conic, which is the same representation at every size and should take the owner-cell moments to the reference values outright. The performance cost of that is clause 5's question, and clause 5 is explicitly not this node's gate.

Cold solves

Each cold solve starts from the reference state and runs moment-space Newton corrections until the residual passes 1 × 10−12. Under the delivered half-plane owner the iteration converges at 282 in four corrections (7.59 × 10−4 → 1.100 × 10−5 → 3.316 × 10−9 → 3.144 × 10−13, 20.7 s per correction) and at 550 in four (7.363 × 10−4 → 1.911 × 10−5 → 1.450 × 10−8 → 4.848 × 10−13, 63.3 s per correction). Both are quadratic throughout, from residuals dominated by the initial mismatch straight to the roundoff floor.

At 2,098 the solve is refused, and the refusal is the map guard rather than the iteration. The driver qualifies the map before its first Newton step, the delivered owner's 2.493 × 10−4 exceeds the 1 × 10−4 guard, and the run dies at require_map with no correction taken. That is a hard coupling between clause 2 and clause 3: the 2,098-cell root cannot be qualified until the owner map is repaired, and a residual measured past the guard would be a root of the map as booked — self-consistency, not flux accuracy. A run that does so is therefore labelled as a diagnostic in its own receipt.

The 282-cell root passes the Taylor check cleanly: in all three directions the remainder falls as step2, with middle-step slopes between 1.983 and 2.041 and the asymptotic slopes converging to 2.000 from the smallest steps. The 550-cell root converges to the same quality — residual 4.85 × 10−13, gradient 1.91 × 10−11 — and its Taylor check fails in one of the three directions (frequency 0.29), where the remainder falls faster than step2 at the three middle step pairs (slopes 3.36 and 3.84 against the 1.8–2.2 window) before relaxing to 2.10 and 1.96 at the smallest steps. The mechanism is a cubic term that dominates the remainder at 3 × 10−6 to 3 × 10−5 and is overtaken by the quadratic as the step shrinks. It is not a kink and not a discrete switch: the continuity probe walks ±1 × 10−5 in four directions, including the Newton direction, and in every one the difference is linear in the step and symmetric about zero (2.2007 × 10−6 at −1 × 10−5, 2.2001 × 10−6 at +1 × 10−5, exactly 0 at 0), so the map carries no jump at the ten-microstep scale. The check's verdict is reported as it stands rather than widened; the asymptotic slopes are carried beside it.

What the change cannot reach

The change is confined to scripts/prototypes/xpoint_cell_owner, and no module outside that directory imports it except one sibling prototype's speed gate, which calls the unchanged public signature of map_moments. So the limited carriers, the 41-state sweeps and the 550 transition bracket are unaffected by construction rather than by measurement, and no implementation switch remains: there is no path from the read or the production map to this owner, so no code branches on which owner is in use.

The recorded brackets bear this out on their own numbers. Each sweep is 41 states spanning ±0.2 in the axis offset, and the transition is bisected to adjacent binary64 values before the jumps are read.
