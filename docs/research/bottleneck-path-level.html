Bottleneck level for the wall contact

The wall contact's effective level is the more scrape-off-side of its own tangency level and the bottleneck of the best axis path that reaches it. The read already did this at the orientation revision, inline at the wall score line; this node names the rule in one owned function, proves it on a synthetic private-flux carrier, and measures that the read is otherwise unchanged: the diverted analytic map error is base's value to the last printed digit at both 282 and 550 cells, where an earlier attempt on this node had raised it to 0.412. The item is a measurement, not a change.

The rule, and where it already lived

A wall contact has two levels in play. Its tangency level is the largest flux the contact surface attains on its own, sigma * wall_flux. Its bottleneck is the maximin of the edge passage peaks along the best path that reaches the contact's owning cell from the magnetic axis, computed by _wall_path_ceiling. A private-flux contact can carry a tangency level more axis-ward than the X-point while the axis reaches it only through the saddle, so its tangency alone overstates how much of it is on the plasma side. The effective level takes the more scrape-off-side of the two, which is their minimum.

At the orientation revision e4c8b9815 that minimum was already applied, inline, at the head of read's wall block in scripts/prototypes/fast_map_production/kernel.py, and the base arm of this record preserves it at arm/kernel_base.py:287:

wall_scores = jnp.minimum(sigma * wall_flux, ceiling[wall_cell])

So the requirement of this node was already met at base, and the node's contribution is to give the rule a name, a docstring and a test rather than to change behaviour. The minimum is now the owned function _wall_effective_level, called from the same site:

def _wall_effective_level(tangency, bottleneck):
    """Effective level of a wall contact.

    The more scrape-off-side of the contact's own tangency level and the
    bottleneck of the best axis path that reaches it. A private-flux contact
    whose tangency is more axis-ward than the X-point, but which the axis
    reaches only through the X-point, therefore takes the saddle's level rather
    than its own. Continuous in both operands and free of any reachability gate.
    """
    return jnp.minimum(tangency, bottleneck)

It is applied to the wall contact alone. The rule reaches wall_scores and nothing else: it does not touch which cells count as plasma, the path weight that decides that, or the saddle sector bar. The sector bar and the continuous path weight are byte-for-byte the base's.

The private contact's tangency is more axis-ward than the saddle gate by 0.45, and the bottleneck removes exactly that advantage: the contact takes −0.5, the saddle's level, and not its own −0.05. A second test asserts the level is the more scrape-off-side operand exactly, against np.minimum(tangency, bottleneck) at atol=0, rtol=1e-15 and bitwise equal, a minimum being exact in binary64, so the assertion can fail. A third sweeps the tangency through the bottleneck crossing at 0.5 ± 10−6 and bounds the largest adjacent-sample difference of the level by the operand's own largest step — the level never moves faster than the operand that drives it, so there is no step at the crossing. The same two checks then run against a deliberately non-continuous variant carrying a 0.1 step at the tie, and both must reject it, which is what shows the checks can fail rather than passing on anything. All three tests pass.

An implementation switch at kernel.py:302, and neither a crossing nor a fold.

On the two rising edges the second saddle’s level arrives bit-identical on the wall candidate — 7.967410111760857 × 10−3 at 282 and 7.973164696008964 × 10−3 at 550 — so the non-strict >= takes the diverted branch on an exact tie. The tie is not a coincidence and not a knife edge of the arithmetic; it is the composition meeting itself. The wall candidate is _wall_effective_level(tangency, bottleneck) = minimum(tangency, bottleneck), the bottleneck of a private-flux contact is the saddle’s level, and the wall contact the census has just returned carries that level, so min and the saddle agree to the last bit by construction. Across the 49-state fine scan at 282 cells, all 25 scan rows carrying two live saddles show that second saddle tied to the wall candidate, so the island is systematic rather than one unlucky sample. The tie position coincides with the wall contact in both cases.

On the two falling edges the second root leaves the census altogether — two saddles become one, root count three becomes two, across adjacent doubles — and the class falls with it. This is not a fold. The departing root’s |det H| is 2.74 × 10−5 at 282 and 2.22 × 10−5 at 550 on the last state that still carries it, four and two orders of magnitude off roundoff, so its curvature does not degenerate towards zero: the root finder simply stops returning it. It is the same event on both scales, which is why the 282 and 550 islands behave identically.

The step that moves a number belongs to the saddle selection, not to the label. At 282 the 0.17–0.18 bracket moves the booked current by +114.75 while the boundary flux moves by 6 × 10−7. What moves alongside the class is saddle_index = 1 + jnp.argmax(saddle_score_rows) at kernel.py:239: the departing wall-contact root had the higher level and was winning the argmax, and when it goes the selection returns to the primary X-point at −0.01137. That re-derives form, and with it normal, booking, the booked-cell set and the path weight — everything the booked current is assembled from. The sweep’s booked current does not read diverted at all, which is the direct evidence that the label is not the cause. The comparison’s consumers being label-only, this node leaves kernel.py:302 as it stands and records the selection as the thing to fix.

No number changes, so the label may stay. The clause asked for a continuous composition of the two levels if a consumer changed a number. Measured, none does: qualified reads True on both sides of all four bisections, and the boundary position has no numeric consumer. The label is therefore left as it is, and the bisected jumps above are reported as the selection’s, to be closed against kernel.py:239.

The wall level is now pinned at the read, and the control is not its own input. The bottleneck-level test previously asserted the wall level only through its helper, so a swap of the read’s composition would have passed it. It now composes the wall candidate per contact the way the read does — effective level, then the most axis-ward contact wins — and asserts both that the gated contact takes the bottleneck and that the directly reachable control keeps its tangency. Both forbidden compositions move the selected candidate, so the assertion rejects each: the raw tangency hands the wall candidate to the gated cell and a maximum hands it to the control’s bottleneck. The control’s expectation is now a declared constant rather than the value handed in, asserted to differ from the bottleneck it was given. Under mutation, raw tangency, maximum and bottleneck-only each fail all four checks and the restored composition passes all four; the gate log is negative-control.log in the run directory.

The certified map rows are unchanged. All four rows at head — diverted and limited at 282 and 550 — match the stored base rows in every non-timing field: centre_residual, centre_class, centre_support, the window row and owner counts, the values and jets digests and map_executable_bytes are all equal. The only differing fields are wall clocks, which the absence of a behaviour change makes the expected outcome rather than a caveat.

What the earlier attempt got wrong, and what is left

An earlier attempt on this node read the brief as an instruction to replace the 0/1 axis-path membership with a continuous reachability test and to delete the boolean saddle sector bar. That was the wrong scope. The 0.41 and 0.34 divergences it produced are flat in resolution and are the no-connectivity plateau: cells below the X-point that sit inside the boundary level but are disconnected from the axis — the private-flux pocket — add about 0.41 to the analytic map error once connectivity is dropped. The 0/1 path weight performs exactly that exclusion. The membership and the sector bar are therefore restored verbatim, and the diverted error returns to base exactly (clause above).

One real defect was found and is worth recording even though the base does not have it. On the knife-edge test the earlier, superseded head’s wall candidate had beaten the saddle by 2.6 × 10−22, a couple of ulp, where base ties exactly, because the attenuation it used was written as a subtraction, ceiling − leak * over, which computes ceiling − (ceiling − saddle_score) and in binary64 returns the saddle only to within a few ulp, where jnp.minimum(ceiling, saddle_score) is exact. Any future attenuation of this ceiling should clamp with a minimum against the saddle level rather than subtract towards it.

Cold solves are not owed. They were required only if the read changed in behaviour, and it did not: the head reproduces base to the last printed digit on every map row and every sweep row measured, at both 282 and 550 cells, because the restored kernel differs from base only by the renamed function. The fidelity rows and the two brackets are therefore the whole measurement.
