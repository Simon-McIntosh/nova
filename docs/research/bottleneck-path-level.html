Bottleneck level for the wall contact

The fixed-shape map's axis-path membership was made continuous by giving every wall contact the more scrape-off-side of its own tangency level and its axis-path bottleneck, with the boolean saddle sector bar deleted. The unit test passes and the continuity sweeps pass, but the change fails the map-fidelity gate on the diverted carrier: at 282 cells the diverted carrier is misclassified as limited and the analytic image error rises from 1.09 × 10−3 to 0.412. This page records the implementation, the measurement, and the open defect.

The two switches and what replaced them

At the orientation revision the effective level of a wall contact was already the lower of its tangency level and the best axis path's bottleneck, wall_scores = min(sigma * wall_flux, ceiling[wall_cell]). Two switches sat on either side of that minimum and made the result discontinuous in the moments.

  1. The 1e-8 ramp membership in _continuous_path_weight thresholded each edge's passage against the level through flux_weight = clip(0.5 + (passage - level) / 1e-8, 0, 1). Over a Newton step of order 10−5 that ramp is a step, and the recorded step-scale scan measured support exchanging four cells and the residual jumping by 1.68 with no fractional weight anywhere.
  2. The boolean sector bar in _wall_path_ceiling, barred = normal & ~sector, clamped the bottleneck with where(barred, min(ceiling, saddle_score), ceiling). sector came from the boolean normal-form sidedness test, so a cell edge crossing a branch ray changed the clamp on the instant the ray crossed its midpoint.

The head replaces both. The membership is now the reachability comparison connected = ceiling >= level_signed, and the sector bar's job — holding the bottleneck down to the saddle level along a path that leaves the axis-facing sector — is carried by a continuous attenuation of the bottleneck inside _wall_path_ceiling:

sector_weight = clip(0.5 + margin / width, 0, 1)     # margin = signed distance to the nearest branch ray
near_weight   = clip(0.5 + (radius - distance) / width, 0, 1)
leak          = near_weight * (1 - sector_weight)
ceiling      -= leak * max(ceiling - saddle_score, 0)

margin is the continuous signed distance from an edge midpoint to the nearest curved branch ray (_sector_sidedness), built from the same cross product and branch roll as the boolean test it supersedes, so margin > 0 exactly where the boolean sector test was true. The attenuation is continuous in the moments because both weights are, and it reproduces the clamp exactly where leak reaches one. The effective level itself is one owned function:

def _wall_effective_level(tangency, bottleneck):
    return jnp.minimum(tangency, bottleneck)

_continuous_path_weight is deleted, not left as a dead option, and no call site passes a ramp width, a boolean sector mask, or a bar. The declared mutation on this node — restoring the 1e-8 ramp membership — is no longer reachable from the read.

Unit test on a synthetic carrier

test_bottleneck_level.py runs CPU-only on a five-cell hand-built quad mesh, independent of the analytic certificate machinery. A private-flux contact at level −0.05 is reachable from the axis only through a saddle gate at −0.5: its bottleneck is −0.5, so its effective level is −0.5 and the tangency's 0.45 advantage is removed. A directly reachable control cell at tangency −0.3 has bottleneck 0.5 and keeps −0.3. A second test sweeps the tangency through the bottleneck crossing at 0.5 ± 10−6 and asserts the effective level moves with it. Both pass.

Map fidelity against the base read

The base arm is kernel.py at the orientation revision, loaded into sys.modules under the real module name before any downstream module imports it, so program, measure and the receipts see the base read throughout. Both arms ran the same entry point, the same cache and the same certified carriers on P100 devices in one allocation (titan, job 1288024).

Three facts follow. First, the head is a no-op on the limited carrier: every limited error matches base to the last printed digit, and the 41-state counts below are identical, because the carrier has no live saddle and the attenuation is therefore never applied. Second, on the diverted carrier the head reclassifies the boundary: centre_class falls from 1 (diverted) at base to 0 (limited) at head, with the supported-cell count rising from 179 to 184. Third, the head error is flat in resolution (0.412, 0.335, 0.412 across 282, 550 and 2,098 cells) where the base falls towards the guard, so the defect is not a coarse-cell artefact that finer cells would wash out.

Neither saddle_live nor wall_score differs between the arms by construction, so the flip can only enter through ceiling. That hypothesis was then measured and is not supported. arm/ceiling_diff.py loads both kernels into one process, builds the 282-cell diverted carrier through the same entry point the sweeps use, calls each module's own read on identical geometry, field and roots, and records each module's own ceiling. At the centre offset the two ceilings agree to roundoff on 281 of 282 cells (the single difference is 5.7 × 10−22 on cell 5), both arms read boundary_class = 1 with wall_candidate = 0 and saddle_candidate = 0, and boundary_flux and axis_flux match exactly (−1.082303272716963 × 10−9 and 5.709576474255209 × 10−3). The only measured difference in the reading is the selected-cell count, 179 at base against 180 at head.

So on the geometry the sweep builds, the ceiling is not where the arms diverge; the divergence is one extra member of the selected set, and the 380-fold map error is what that one cell does, not what the attenuation does. This does not yet explain the receipt's own centre_class 1 → 0, which is read from the map row's build through the same ceiling — the two builds reach different readings at the same nominal centre and the difference between them is unexplained. Reconciling candidate.py's map-row build with the sweep's build, and then identifying which cell the head adds and why, is the first step of the open work below. The ceiling-array difference is ruled out as the cause; the membership rule change is the live suspect.

The bracket figure required by this node is not produced. The evidence fragment carries the fidelity figure; a figure of the effective wall level through the transition bracket at base and head needs the per-state wall candidate that the sweep receipt does not currently emit, and the fidelity failure above must be settled first.
