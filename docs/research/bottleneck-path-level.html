Bottleneck level for the wall contact

The wall contact's effective level is the more scrape-off-side of its own tangency level and the bottleneck of the best axis path that reaches it. The read already did this at the orientation revision, inline at the wall score line; this node names the rule in one owned function, proves it on a synthetic private-flux carrier, and measures that the read is otherwise unchanged: the diverted analytic map error is base's value to the last printed digit at both 282 and 550 cells, where an earlier attempt on this node had raised it to 0.412. The item is a measurement, not a change.

The rule, and where it already lived

A wall contact has two levels in play. Its tangency level is the largest flux the contact surface attains on its own, sigma * wall_flux. Its bottleneck is the maximin of the edge passage peaks along the best path that reaches the contact's owning cell from the magnetic axis, computed by _wall_path_ceiling. A private-flux contact can carry a tangency level more axis-ward than the X-point while the axis reaches it only through the saddle, so its tangency alone overstates how much of it is on the plasma side. The effective level takes the more scrape-off-side of the two, which is their minimum.

At the orientation revision e4c8b9815 that minimum was already applied, inline, at the head of read's wall block in scripts/prototypes/fast_map_production/kernel.py, and the base arm of this record preserves it at arm/kernel_base.py:287:

wall_scores = jnp.minimum(sigma * wall_flux, ceiling[wall_cell])

So the requirement of this node was already met at base, and the node's contribution is to give the rule a name, a docstring and a test rather than to change behaviour. The minimum is now the owned function _wall_effective_level, called from the same site:

def _wall_effective_level(tangency, bottleneck):
    """Effective level of a wall contact.

    The more scrape-off-side of the contact's own tangency level and the
    bottleneck of the best axis path that reaches it. A private-flux contact
    whose tangency is more axis-ward than the X-point, but which the axis
    reaches only through the X-point, therefore takes the saddle's level rather
    than its own. Continuous in both operands and free of any reachability gate.
    """
    return jnp.minimum(tangency, bottleneck)

It is applied to the wall contact alone. The rule reaches wall_scores and nothing else: it does not touch which cells count as plasma, the path weight that decides that, or the saddle sector bar. The sector bar and the continuous path weight are byte-for-byte the base's.

What the earlier attempt got wrong, and what is left

An earlier attempt on this node read the brief as an instruction to replace the 0/1 axis-path membership with a continuous reachability test and to delete the boolean saddle sector bar. That was the wrong scope. The 0.41 and 0.34 divergences it produced are flat in resolution and are the no-connectivity plateau: cells below the X-point that sit inside the boundary level but are disconnected from the axis — the private-flux pocket — add about 0.41 to the analytic map error once connectivity is dropped. The 0/1 path weight performs exactly that exclusion. The membership and the sector bar are therefore restored verbatim, and the diverted error returns to base exactly (clause above).

One real defect was found and is worth recording even though the base does not have it. On the knife-edge test the head's wall candidate had beaten the saddle by 2.6 × 10−22, a couple of ulp, where base ties exactly, because the attenuation it used was written as a subtraction, ceiling − leak * over, which computes ceiling − (ceiling − saddle_score) and in binary64 returns the saddle only to within a few ulp, where jnp.minimum(ceiling, saddle_score) is exact. Any future attenuation of this ceiling should clamp with a minimum against the saddle level rather than subtract towards it.

Remaining: the 550-cell bracket is measured in the same job and is reported above; the cold solves at 282 and 550 are owed only if the read changed in behaviour, and it did not — the head reproduces base to the last printed digit on every row measured — so the fidelity rows and the bracket are the whole measurement, and there is no forward-solve node for this item to pair a solve with.
