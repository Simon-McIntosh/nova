Stationary census proposal coverage

A cell's own quadratic can place its stationary seed across a shared edge while the complete field still has a hyperbolic saddle. The census now admits proposals in the cell's edge-neighbour ring and ranks them by the complete field's gradient residual. The remaining coarse-window transitions are folds: the axis and wall saddle merge at measured stationary degeneracies.

Proposal, capacity and selection

For each cell, the local fitted quadratic supplies one seed. A proposal is eligible when its seed is inside its own polygon or any polygon named by that cell's TopologyGeometry.neighbour row. This is an actual shared-edge ring, including trimmed wall cells, not a distance cutoff. The existing operand-derived cross-product rounding bound applies to each polygon in the ring. If a neighbouring fit places its seed in the saddle's cell, the corresponding edge-neighbour cell proposes it even when its own polygon misses it. After polishing, roots are deduplicated by position as before.

The fixed seed reserve is the smaller of the cell count and null_capacity × (one cell + maximum edge neighbours), with previous-root slots covered separately. It is derived from the graph degree and declared root capacity; no measured offset or fitted geometric threshold enters the shape. Accepted candidates are sorted by the norm of the complete field's gradient at the seed, with stable ordering for ties, before that reserve is filled. An overflow remains an explicit census result rather than a silent index truncation.

The broad-window departures are stationary folds

The earlier inference of a coverage hole through 0.1735 and 0.123 used the determinant at the last coarse state that held a saddle. A strongly negative determinant at that state does not rule out a fold before the next state. Solving the three simultaneous equations ∂Rψ = 0, ∂Zψ = 0 and det H = 0 in position and offset locates both degeneracies directly on the same moving field.
