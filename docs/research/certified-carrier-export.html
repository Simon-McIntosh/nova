Research record · measured export and round trip

nova's certified carriers round-trip through the imas-efit boundary trace format

One limited and one diverted analytic certified carrier were written as imas-efit BOUNDARY_LEVEL_TRACE 2 records and reproduced through the codec and through nova's own topology read. The limited record re-reads as limited with its boundary at the outboard wall contact, the diverted record as diverted with its boundary at the lower saddle, and a negated flux grid is refused on each record's declared COCOS-17 polarity.

Scope. This is the reverse of the field swap. The swap reads imas-efit records with nova's read; here nova writes its own certified fields into imas-efit's record layout, so imas-efit's reader and escape-level rule can be run on fields nova generated. The deliverable is the record and the stated decision, not a new physics result.

Record producer. scripts/prototypes/certified_carrier_export/export_records.py. Both carriers load from the content-addressed certificate fixture store (certificate-fixtures, reached through scripts/prototypes/support/certificate_fixtures.py) at 550 cells, so the machine and its wall are the objects the certificate booking gate publishes under.

What each record carries

Each record holds nova's analytic total poloidal flux on a fixed 65×65 grid spanning the wall bounding box, plus the read axis, the record polarity and the per-loop wall. The flux is nova's analytic psi in the written convention, whole-weber flux per poloidal plane, which the record states through its polarity field +1: that is the DD version's COCOS-17 convention.

The wall is emitted as per-loop segment pairs with a one-based loop index, so the codec reconstructs the whole ring from the record alone and no reader has to know the machine.

The store request and the machine's cell count are different numbers. The published request for the coarse certificate machine is the negative sentinel FIXTURE_REQUESTS["coarse"] (−500), while the machine that request builds is the 550-cell carrier this export takes its wall from. The signed request belongs to the carrier's identity digest, so −500 and 500 are two identities and only the signed one is the machine the certificate booking gate published.

A limited record admits no saddle, and the codec stores only reals, so its saddle and wall levels repeat the boundary flux. Writing a NaN there is not merely uninformative: the codec's real formatter has no field width for it and the write raises. The repeat is the encoding choice, not a measured level.

Limited record. nova reads the static reactor carrier as a limited topology. The axis sits at (6.200, 0.000) m and the boundary is the outboard limiter contact at (7.8014, 0.0000) m, at level 2.23×10−14 Wb. There is no admitted saddle. The certificate bound is the analytic closed-form boundary at 0.0 Wb; the read places its boundary 2.2×10−14 Wb inside it.

Diverted record. nova reads the single-null carrier as a diverted topology. The axis sits at (1.7871, 0.0468) m and the boundary is the lower saddle at (1.5025, −1.0173) m, at level −1.39×10−9 Wb. The certificate bound is the analytic separatrix at 0.0 Wb; the read places the saddle 1.4×10−9 Wb inside it. The analytic X-point is (1.50253, −1.01728) m, so the read saddle agrees with the certificate to 4.4×10−4 m.

The analytic cases declare their boundary at zero flux (closed_form_boundary_flux_wb = 0.0), which is the bound both records sit inside. The axis reproduces the analytic axis to 1.6×10−7 m (limited) and 1.1×10−7 m (diverted).

The exported states as poloidal contour panels

Each panel draws the record's own flux on stated levels, the per-loop wall, both null sets (the read axis as a solid triangle, every admitted saddle as a filled cross) and the boundary level as its own contour.

Round trip

Each record is re-read through trace_record.read_trace_record and then re-read by nova's own nova.equilibrium.topology.read on a square lattice built from the record's own wall. The re-read reproduces the axis, the polarity and nova's boundary level within the stated tolerances, under a committed test (scripts/prototypes/certified_carrier_export/test_export_records.py):

  • polarity is the declared +1; the grid is 65×65; one wall ring survives the codec;
  • the axis matches the axis stored in the record to 1×10−12 m and the analytic certificate axis to 1×10−5 m, a stated ~60× margin over the measured 1.6×10−7 m (limited) and 1.1×10−7 m (diverted) agreement;
  • the boundary level matches the record's own fed-back level to 1×10−12 Wb and sits within 1×10−7 Wb of the certificate bound, a stated ~70× margin over the measured 2.2×10−14 Wb (limited) and 1.4×10−9 Wb (diverted) agreement;
  • the re-read class equals the class the export declared.

Negative control. Negating the exported flux grid must make the round trip refuse the record on the polarity it declares, and the refusal is named by its type rather than by a disjunction. Both records refuse with NO_QUALIFIED_AXIS and an unqualified axis; neither returns a different boundary class, and neither is merely invalid for an unstated reason. Declaring the opposite polarity on the same negated grid restores the read — valid, the same boundary class, and a boundary level equal and opposite to the unnegated one — so the guard is the disagreement between the grid's sign and the declared COCOS-17 orientation, and a grid of the wrong sign cannot reproduce the decision its own record carries.

The gate drives the same state through the record producer rather than through the test: NOVA_CERTIFIED_EXPORT_MUTATION=negate-psi corrupts the flux on its way to disk, so the round-trip test is the thing that discovers the corruption. That arm exits nonzero and names test_round_trip[limited] and test_round_trip[diverted] as its failures, which is the evidence that the round trip can fail.

Limits of this record

  • The read geometry is the square lattice the record's own wall admits, chosen so the read is reproducible from the record alone. It is not the certificate machine's own mesh, so the boundary level it returns is the read's answer on that lattice, and the agreement with the certificate bound is a statement about both.
  • The limited record admits no saddle. Its stored saddle and wall levels repeat the boundary flux because the codec's real writer cannot carry a NaN; a consumer must read the class, not those levels, to tell the two records apart.
  • The diverted record's boundary level is negative and small, on the order of the numerical zero and not a physical flux offset.
