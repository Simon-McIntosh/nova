Forward-solve compile-cost evidence

Compilation is currently a first-order forward-solve cost. This record gathers the measured compile and lowering walls that are otherwise scattered across the millisecond solve, cut-cell, batched-operator and playable records. The table keeps the instrument's distinctions: a lower-only probe is not an executable compile, an unrecorded byte count is not inferred from HLO, and a cache refusal is not reported as a cache hit.

§1 — The measured ledger

The compile wall is the recorded cold or warm compilation interval when the receipt exposes one. “Lower-only” names a StableHLO probe that did not produce an executable. “Not recorded” is retained where the source receipt did not expose a value; “not serialisable” is the measured outcome when XLA or protobuf refused to materialise the executable. For the memory-ladder rows, the instruction count is StableHLO and the executable-size field is the serialized executable or proto described by the source comment. For the census rows, the count is optimized-HLO instructions and the byte field is the captured executable context reported by the census.

§2 — Mechanisms already measured

Captured mesh literals. The closure-owned whole-cell program carries mesh-sized arrays as literals. At 300 cells, the constant census finds 856 literals above 1 KiB and 372.55 MiB of captured bytes, with 359.06 MiB in interaction matrices; at 1,000 cells it finds 889 literals and 2.94 GiB, with 2.89 GiB in interaction matrices. The host memory ladder sees the corresponding executable context grow from 462 MB at 300 cells to a 3.74 GiB proto at 1,000 and 19.2 GiB at 2,500. The evidence rows are the whole-cell closure rows and the two census rows above; the detailed literal inventory is docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/program-census/report.md.

Repeated traced copies. A known-present source sentinel, rather than an empty search, finds 120 traced copies of ForwardFluxOperator.normalised_current_moments and 36 copies of ForwardFluxOperator._fixed_design_read in both solve rungs. The older 83-copy premise does not reproduce at this revision. The Python Newton and trip loops are host-only and contribute zero optimized-HLO copies; the compiled budgets are already jax.lax.fori_loop bodies. This is the measured reason the follow-on targets one evaluator state machine and the two repeated bodies rather than rewriting host loops that are not in the executable. Evidence: docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/program-census/report.md and docs/plans/millisecond-converged-solve.html#c-run-r-20260915T124717561490-msc-program-constant-census.

Per-host cache identity. The persistent cache is explicitly selected per recipe, and its key includes the serialized computation, JAX/jaxlib and backend versions, compiler flags and options, and accelerator topology. The runtime identity also records the device topology, which is why a compiled program from one host or accelerator environment cannot be assumed to serve every all_debug node. The 300-cell size receipt names the CPU runtime and its cache directory; the compiled-slice receipt names the H200 runtime and records 12 of 12 same-program reuse with zero backend compile after the retained program. Evidence: /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/reports/nova/s19-codex/mesh-arguments/receipts/size-300.json and docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/compiled-slice-cache/receipt.json.

Constraint-row lowering multiplier. The centroid probe quantifies the extra traced work from one centroid constraint: the unconstrained lower is 423,061 StableHLO instructions and 45.246 seconds, while the constrained lower is 1,036,327 instructions and 147.213 seconds. That is 2.45 times the instruction count and 3.25 times the lower wall before an executable or solve is produced. The receipt preserves both arms as lower-only, so no executable-size claim is attached to this comparison. Evidence: /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/reports/nova/s19-codex/centroid-row/compile-unconstrained.json and compile-constrained.json.

Two-gibibyte serialisation limit. The 1,000-cell whole-cell proto (3.74 GiB), the 2,500-cell proto (19.2 GiB), and the exact-clip 1,000-cell serialized form (4.34 GB) cross the 2 GiB serialization boundary. They therefore cannot contribute a normal persistent-cache hit even when the cache is configured. The exact-clip receipt is explicit that its in-process cold compile and warm executions remain valid while no cross-process cache-hit claim is made. Evidence: the memory-ladder comment above and docs/evidence/archive/cut-cell-current-attribution-landed.html#exact-clip-warm-cost-and-workspace.

§3 — What the cost means for the millisecond solve

The measured walls are not one homogeneous benchmark. The 45.246- and 147.213-second centroid figures stop at lowering; the 74-second 300-cell cache retrieval is a warm backend retrieval; the 77.2-second MAST width-twelve row pays one warm batch compile; and the 1,029–1,074-second exact-clip rows are in-process cold compiles with warm fixed-point solves, not cache hits. The 1,579.189-second DIII-D head row is a real cold width-five compile, while its paired archived base is 169.030 seconds from a cache hit. Keeping these states side by side is the useful result: cache policy changes the wall, but it does not erase the graph and serialization mechanisms that set the cold cost.

The lead's directive on 2026-09-16 is therefore explicit: an hour per compile is too much and must be addressed as the codebase stabilises. The present record already shows the repair direction: pass mesh-sized arrays and profile values as traced arguments, eliminate duplicated current-moment and topology bodies through one evaluator state machine, and preserve a cache key whose host and accelerator identity are stated rather than assumed. The codebase should be judged against the measured rows above, including the negative rows, until the same receipts show a material reduction in cold wall, executable size and host working set.

§4 — Canonical program shapes: the census and the bucket design

The first lever of the compile-cost decision is to make identities share compiled programs. That lever needs the list of shape-bearing capacities each identity presents, not another wall-clock measurement, so the census reads those axes directly: the bank rows from the arrays persisted beside the solver output, the certificate rows from the certificate route's own problem builder, and neither compiles anything. Receipts: docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/program-shapes/program-key-and-pruner.json, .../bank-shape-identity.json, .../program-shape-census.json. The certificate rows are read under an in-process compatibility shim: solovev_certificate.py reads _UNIT_NODE from nova.equilibrium.observation, which does not define it, so the census publishes the constant from its owning module, nova.equilibrium.clip_quadrature, under that name for the duration of the certificate call and restores the module afterwards (recorded under framework_compat_shims in the census receipt). The shim is a name publication only; every capacity vector below is the route's own, and the quadrature axis node count the shim supplies is the same value the route intends to read.

What a compiled solve program is keyed on. _compiled_program_key (nova/equilibrium/reduced_newton.py:2055) is the tuple of the operator program identity, the support cell index list coordinates.cells, the argument layouts, the requested class, the row count and the row signature. The operator identity is ForwardFluxOperator._batch_identity() (nova/equilibrium/forward_operator.py:2204), which carries the value digest geometry_identity together with the source-layout identity and the prescribed/sample booleans. The length of coordinates.cells is a host-computed static shape, derived from np.flatnonzero(profile_participation). Bucketing therefore has to reach every one of those axes, and the identity is the one that is value-keyed today.

The bank: twelve members, one shape — and one program today. Across the twelve bank arms (six MAST shot/slice identities — 21978/35, 21983/35, 21985/35, 21986/46, 21989/55, 22086/43 — each at a pure and a mixed arm) every program-reaching array carries an identical shape and dtype: a 33×33 operand mesh (1,089 nodes), a 37-node wall, a two-entry axis and saddle, a scalar binding flux. Comparing the persisted arrays directly across the twelve shows the mesh coordinates and the wall are bit-identical (max absolute difference 0.0 for radius, height and wall); the only array that differs is flux, which is a traced operand rather than a key. The operator program identity is value-keyed, and here the values agree, so the twelve do not split: they compile to one program, which an independent H200 run confirms by reporting same_cached_program true for all twelve members and twelve cache-reuse passes (docs/figures/millisecond-converged-solve/compiled-slice-cache/receipt.json). The lever for the bank is therefore not a new program key but cache persistence and portability: the same program is recompiled once per runtime-version directory (twelve on this host) and once per rung whose mesh capacity differs. The value digest remains a compile trigger when the mesh values do move — across rungs, and across shots whose reconstruction changes the mesh — which is where capacity-keying applies (as in the census's spacing of the certificate buckets).

The certificate rows: four cases, four capacity vectors, one bucket. At the 300-cell rung the four certificate cases realise 342, 341, 341 and 340 cells, with support capacities of 24, 24, 24 and 26 chords, arc capacities of 3,072, 3,072, 3,072 and 3,328, a 121-node wall in every case, state sizes of 1,529, 1,535, 1,525 and 1,547, and sampled-grid capacities of 1,066, 1,073, 1,063 and 1,086. Four capacity vectors today; one bucketed vector under the bucket set below.

The bucket set. A bucket set is a floor list per capacity axis, and a capacity is rounded up to the first floor that admits it. An axis with no declared floors is rounded up to the next power of two, so an axis never silently drops out of the bucketed signature — dropping one would make two identities look alike on the axes that remain and understate the program count. A tighter variant that keeps only the first two floors of each declared list partitions the sixteen identities the same way — two groups, the same two — which is the useful robustness check: the buckets that decide the partition are the small ones, and the 1089-node MAST mesh sits exactly on a floor. The coarse floors are the ones to argue about. The 1000-cell mesh floor carries a padding ratio near 2.9 on three of the four certificate cases; a floor near 400 would cut that to about 1.17 at the cost of a bucket only the 300-cell rung occupies, and a 2,000-cell floor would instead restore the four-way split. The rung is a policy choice, not a physical constant.

Where padding applies in the builders. Four places, and each needs the padded entries to be inert rather than merely present:

  • Mesh cell capacity. Pad the realised cell count to the bucket floor and carry a per-cell activity mask, so surplus rows of the interaction matrices contribute zero; the state vector length follows the padded capacity, so the traced layout stays fixed.
  • Support capacity. The support cell index list has a static length, so pad it with a repeated sentinel index and carry a per-entry weight that is zero on the padding. A padded entry that reached the support would change the residual, not just its shape.
  • Arc capacity. Pad the moment-geometry support capacity with zero-weight arcs, extending the same rule to the poloidal arc/section reduction.
  • Member width. No census axis measures this one. The source layout's flattened member axis (zero-cost arc, self and Ohmic) is not among the capacities read from the persisted bank arrays or the certificate builder, so its constancy across the two suites is assumed here rather than measured, and the bucket key must not rely on it until an axis counts it. Where the axis does hold, it is held constant rather than padded.

Expected program count. With the bucket set above the sixteen identities present two bucketed capacity vectors: {mesh_axis_nodes 1089, state_size 2048, wall_node_capacity 64} for the twelve bank arms, and {mesh_axis_nodes 900, mesh_cell_capacity 1000, wall_node_capacity 128, sample_node_capacity 2048, state_size 2048, support_capacity 256, arc_capacity 4096} for the four certificate cases. They differ because the bank rows are operand rows: they carry no mesh-cell, support, arc or sampled capacity at all, and their mesh is 1,089 axis nodes against the certificate route's 900. The measurable count to beat is the certificate's four programs today, one per realised capacity among 340, 341, 341 and 342 cells, and the bank-and-certificate suite's five distinct capacity vectors; the bank's own twelve arms already share one program, so there the target is retention rather than reduction. The design target is one program per bucket per suite: two programs for the bank-and-certificate suite at the 300-cell rung, and one compile per bucket for each further rung added. What bucketing does not do: sharing a program reduces the number of compiles, not the size or the lowering cost of the one program that remains. The per-parent copy counts in §2 are a property of the traced body inside a single compile, and a bucket set does not touch them; the two levers compose, they do not substitute.

The cache is the other half of the lever. A shared program is worth compiling once only if the compiled executable is still there when the next identity arrives. The pruner is JAX's own jax._src.lru_cache.LRUCache._evict, ordered by a per-entry -atime sidecar and bounded by jax_compilation_cache_max_size, which nova sets to 8 GiB (nova/jax/config.py:27); there is no cron entry, no user timer and no tmpfiles rule touching the cache path, so nothing else expires it. The installed cache holds 3,958 entries totalling 18.662 GiB across twelve runtime-version directories, median entry age 2.2 days, none older than 16 days. The bound is applied per directory: twelve runtime keys carry twelve independent bounds, so the configured 8 GiB is not a cap on the cache as a whole, and a pre-warm target inside one runtime directory keeps its own 8 GiB budget. One directory already stands at 8.707 GiB, above the configured bound, consistent with the eviction test running against a projected size on each put rather than continuously. The twelve bank identities need one entry today, not twelve: the twelve directories the cache holds are runtime-version keys of one shared program, and the arms are shape- and value-identical, so a capacity-keyed identity would save the bank nothing there — for the bank the lever is persistence across those runtime keys. The certificate rows need four entries today, one per realised capacity; they are one per bucket under the design, and one per bucket per rung as further rungs are added.
