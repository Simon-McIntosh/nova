Research and execution evidence

Contour-tree sweep warm-wall profile

On the H200, the checkout's contour-tree build grows from 1.125 to 79.345 s for the limited carrier and from 1.105 to 72.697 s for the diverted carrier between about 550 and 10,000 cells. Four-rung log-log exponents are 1.473 and 1.458. The join and split sweeps carry that growth: their parent-link traversal count grows near exponent 1.53 while the number of visits grows linearly. A rank-balanced component representation with batched arc emission is prescribed; its 10,000-cell wall is an estimate, not a measured repair.

Method and provenance

The primary module is the assigned checkout's nova/equilibrium/contour_tree.py at revision c3241935f3c51f6d68a4ecc68067db939c28bfcb. The job log and every JSON row print its resolved import path while the worktree exists. One H200 batch job, 1291005, ran all eight analytic carrier configurations on cuda:0. It reused the field, wall, realised hex geometry, and centre-to-vertex interpolation of scripts/prototypes/read_warm_wall/measure.py through the same analytic fixture functions. Geometry construction and sampling precede the tree timers. Each stage is separately JIT compiled, run once to warm, then timed on a second call through jax.block_until_ready; compile-call seconds appear separately below and in each JSON row. Separate executables change fusion and have timing noise, so nested walls and stage sums are not exact partitions of the full build.

The instrument counts every _sweep_tree visit and every _neighbour_roots while-loop iteration. A known three-link parent chain returns depth two, so a uniform zero counter would fail. For all eight configurations, the counted sweep arrays replayed through the builder's unchanged merge logic match the native receipt bit for bit on node_vertex, node_psi, node_valid, critical_type, edges, edge_valid, and overflow: main/identity.log reports 9 passed and EXIT=0. Rotating the visit order by one position fails the same 550-cell limited receipt test on sweep output zero, with EXIT=1 in main/negative-control.log. The complete job log, stage and counter JSON rows, and summary JSON live under /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/runs/r-20261020T061532000394-contour-tree-sweep-profile/; the payload ends EXIT=0.

What grows inside each sweep

_sweep_tree visits exactly one carrier vertex per rank in each pass. Its loop at nova/equilibrium/contour_tree.py:364 calls _neighbour_roots at line 99 for every visit. The current while-loop advances one parent link per iteration; it does not double pointers. The parent update at line 338 makes the current vertex the new root and leaves older links chained. The table records join / split values in that order. Wall per visit is the separately timed sweep wall divided by visits, in milliseconds.

Least-squares parent-round exponents are 1.523 join and 1.540 split for limited, 1.519 join and 1.547 split for diverted. Wall per visit rises with exponent 0.465–0.499, while visits rise with exponent one. The 10,000-cell mean is 240 and 236 parent hops per limited visit, and 223 and 210 for diverted. The maximum diverted split visit needs 4,470 hops. The inferred no-append sweep wall per parent hop is 15.92 μs limited and 15.88 μs diverted, nearly constant at this scale; this ratio includes other per-visit work and is a planning estimate, not an isolated kernel benchmark. Thus growing parent depth, multiplied by linearly many visits, carries the 1.5 exponent. Append estimates are near linear, and the other stages are too small and near linear to explain it.

Sparse export and overflow check

An unlanded sparse builder from commit a194ff511 was exported to the run directory as contour_tree_sparse.py and measured only as a labelled comparison. Its full-build 10,000-cell walls are 77.766 s limited and 69.630 s diverted, fitting exponents 1.506 and 1.503 over four sizes. Its adjacency, split insertion and inverse-map savings therefore leave the sweep exponent intact. These rows came from H200 jobs 1290851 and 1291005, not the one-job main series.

At 550 limited cells, both main and sparse stage diagnostics on the H200 report 18 merged nodes and 17 merged edges against capacities of 256 and 255. Neither adjacency, join append, split append, split insertion, capacity check nor invalid edge endpoint sets overflow in the independent diagnostic; both diagnostic native receipts report overflow=false. A separately compiled sparse full-build timing row reports overflow=true with the same 18 nodes and 17 edges, and an earlier sparse identity replay also disagreed only on overflow. No capacity truncation is shown. The compiled sparse inconsistency remains a product follow-on; it is not excluded from the main identity gate, which passed all seven receipt fields at every configuration.

Prescribed repair and acceptance

Keep the lexicographic flux/value visit order, wall-contact rule and event labels, but replace the current-root chain in _sweep_tree (nova/equilibrium/contour_tree.py:243, visit and parent updates at lines 285–378) with a disjoint-set forest using union by rank and path compression. Replace _neighbour_roots at lines 99–105 with bounded root queries against that forest. Store component birth and wall flags at the chosen representative rather than assuming the latest vertex is the representative. Emit arcs into a per-visit event buffer and compact once after each sweep instead of calling _append_edges at lines 108–134 on every vertex. Keep _carrier_adjacency and _insert_split_nodes, whose measured exponents are near one, unless the final gate shows they need adjustment.

At 10,028 vertices a rank-balanced forest has maximum depth at most 14. Applying that cap to the measured 15.88–15.92 μs no-append wall per parent hop predicts 4.46 s for both root-query passes. Retaining current append estimates and the measured adjacency, split insertion and other walls predicts a 10,000-cell tree wall of 8.99 s limited and 9.76 s diverted. That has insufficient margin for the whole read's 10 s target. A batched append budget of 0.5 s total, to be proven rather than assumed, brings the projected tree wall to about 5.79 and 5.82 s respectively; adding roughly one second of the previously measured non-tree read leaves a whole-read estimate around 6.8 s. The actual repair must demonstrate the ≤10 s whole-read wall on the H200; the estimates are not acceptance results.

The repaired builder must keep node_vertex, node_psi, node_valid, critical_type, edges, edge_valid, and overflow bit-identical on all eight configurations. The existing one-position visit-order mutation must keep failing the identity gate. A performance negative control that disables rank balancing should restore growing parent-hop counts and the near-1.5 warm exponent without changing the receipt, showing that bounded forest depth is the cause of the speedup. The H200 gate also reports visits, total and maximum root hops and 10,000-cell whole-read wall; merely reducing append cost without flattening the root-hop curve does not satisfy this repair.
