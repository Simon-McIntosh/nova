Escape-level rule comparison

Nova's boundary read and imas-efit's escape-level rule are set side by side on the 26 sides of imas-efit's 13 paired trace records. Both rules are read from their own recorded output; neither is changed here and no verdict is stated on which rule is right. The escape columns come from imas-efit's escape-level replay (revision f009d75e), the nova columns from the field-swap per-loop sweep. On JT-60SA sides the comparison is rule consistency only, because no reconstruction there is trusted.

Method

The escape level is imas-efit's maximin, over axis-to-vessel paths inside the obstacle-aware vessel domain, of polarity·ψ. Its label is LIMITED (1) when the bottleneck is on the wall and DIVERTED (2) at an interior saddle. The first three columns of each row are the escape label, the refined escape level and the refined bottleneck (R, Z). Nova's columns are its label, its boundary level (the same maximin read by its own topology rule) and the contact it admits — an interior saddle with position, or a wall contact with no recorded position. The gap is nova's boundary minus the escape level, absolute and divided by imas-efit's axis-to-boundary span. The escape axis column names the axis the escape walk started from; a flag marks the one side that uses the tracked maximum rather than the recorded axis.
