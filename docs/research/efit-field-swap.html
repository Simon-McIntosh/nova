Reading imas-efit boundary trace records with nova's boundary read

Measured evidence fragment

What is being measured

Two codes decide where a plasma boundary is, by different instruments. imas-efit writes a BOUNDARY_LEVEL_TRACE 2 record for each boundary-level state it traces: the rectilinear psi grid, the wall it traced against, and its own decision (verdict, axis level and position, refined saddle level, wall contact level, and the feedback psi it finally used). nova's read decides the same thing from a field and a cell geometry, with merge-path saddle admission selecting the bounding saddle of the axis merge path.

The two are cross-checked rather than converged: imas-efit asked that nova's rule not be shaped by theirs, so this work does not change nova's read. It runs nova's read, a byte for byte unmodified kernel.read with read_robustness.census, on imas-efit's fields, and puts the two decisions side by side.

The adapter, and what it substitutes

imas-efit carries its flux as a tensor-product not-a-knot bicubic spline (type(EZspline2) initialised with bcs=(0,0) at src/EFIT/null_detection.f90), and the record stores the rectilinear grid and psi values that spline is built from. The adapter rebuilds that same spline, so in this comparison the spline is the field: nova's Biot-Savart operator is not used and no plasma current is booked. A disagreement found here is therefore about the two codes' decision rules on one field representation, never about two physics models.

Evaluation avoids a hand-written derivative recurrence. Each derivative of a B-spline is itself a B-spline of one lower degree whose coefficients are a divided difference of the parent's, so value, gradient and Hessian are each one tensor contraction against a basis kernel and the whole field stays differentiable under jax.jacfwd, which the kernel's third- and fourth-derivative saddle curvature terms require. Measured against scipy's bisplev on the JT-60SA near-fold record at 64 pseudo-random interior points: the value agrees to 1e-9 relative and the second derivatives to 1e-6 absolute.

The cell geometry, and the defect the first run had

nova's TopologyGeometry.from_cells takes one set of absolute RZ sampling points per cell and prepends each cell centre to it, then scales the whole stencil by the cell pitch before fitting the six-term local quadratic. Handing it near-origin offsets instead makes every cell's quadratic fit be taken over points clustered at the origin: measured on jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 at a 16×16 in-wall lattice (132 cells, pitch 0.2508 m), the design condition number reaches 1.7 × 104, the fitted stationary point lands 3.2 to 26.9 pitch lengths away, no cell contains its own seed, and RootCensus.candidates is 0 — so the census proposes nothing, the merge-path margin is all -inf, the admitted saddle falls back to slot 0, and the reported boundary level collapses onto the axis level.

Placing each cell's sampling points around its own centre instead: design condition number 19, nine cells propose seeds, four distinct saddles are admitted, and nova's read returns a boundary level of 0.31165 with class diverted — which matches imas-efit's own verdict 2 on that record.

This is worth stating as a finding rather than a bug fix, because the failure mode is silent and plausible: an unqualified seed proposal that produces an axis level labelled a boundary level is exactly the number a reader would take for an answer. Every case whose lattice admits no saddle is therefore reported unqualified in the table below, with the census counts that disqualified it, and nova's read's own fallback is never printed in its place.

The case-by-case decision table

Columns come from two sources and are labelled as such. efit columns are read from the record's own stored decision: verdict, axis_psi, axis_r, axis_z, saddle_refined, wall_level, fed_back_psi. nova columns are produced by running nova's read on the record's spline field over the in-wall lattice: the admitted saddle position is the read's boundary, its level is the field evaluated there, the wall contact level is the maximum of sigma psi over the record's wall points, and the label is the read's boundary_class.

The one disagreement, diagnosed

On jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 the two codes agree on the label and differ on the level: imas-efit's feedback boundary psi is 0.67900 against nova's 0.31165, on a boundary-move span of 0.89785 from imas-efit's own axis. The residual is not roundoff and it is not a representation difference: both codes read the same spline. It is a level difference of about 40 per cent of the axis-to-boundary span, and it is the single most important thing in this comparison — it says the two codes admit a different bounding level even while agreeing that the state is diverted.

What this measurement does not yet establish is which saddle each code admitted. The record carries imas-efit's saddle_refined but not its admitted saddle position; nova's admitted saddle position is recorded in exports/disagreement.json. Resolving the level difference needs the two positions compared, which is the next measurement, not a conclusion available now.

Approximations that qualify every number

  • The cell geometry is a square lattice at the record's own resolution inside the record's wall polygon, not nova's hex or Delaunay carrier mesh, so the Bell cell fit and the cell-boundary census differ in discretisation from a production nova run.
  • The record stores its wall as consecutive segments rather than one closed ring; the adapter closes it by appending the final segment's end.
  • The field is imas-efit's spline, so no nova current booking, no Biot-Savart operator, and no self- consistent equilibrium is involved.
  • A case whose lattice admits no saddle is reported unqualified; its missing nova column is a gap, not a zero.
