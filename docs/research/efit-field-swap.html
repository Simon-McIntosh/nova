Reading imas-efit boundary trace records with nova's boundary read

Measured evidence fragment

What is being measured

Two codes decide where a plasma boundary is, by different instruments. imas-efit writes a BOUNDARY_LEVEL_TRACE 2 record for each boundary-level state it traces: the rectilinear psi grid, the wall it traced against, and its own decision (verdict, axis level and position, refined saddle level, wall contact level, and the feedback psi it finally used). nova's read decides the same thing from a field and a cell geometry, with merge-path saddle admission selecting the bounding saddle of the axis merge path.

The two are cross-checked rather than converged: imas-efit asked that nova's rule not be shaped by theirs, so this work does not change nova's read. It runs nova's read, a byte for byte unmodified kernel.read with read_robustness.census, on imas-efit's fields, puts the two decisions side by side, and writes nova's own decisions back out in imas-efit's record layout so this direction of the exchange exists as a file rather than as a claim.

The adapter, and what it substitutes

imas-efit carries its flux as a tensor-product not-a-knot bicubic spline (type(EZspline2) initialised with bcs=(0,0) at src/EFIT/null_detection.f90), and the record stores the rectilinear grid and psi values that spline is built from. The adapter rebuilds that same spline, so in this comparison the spline is the field: nova's Biot-Savart operator is not used and no plasma current is booked. A disagreement found here is therefore about the two codes' decision rules on one field representation, never about two physics models.

Evaluation avoids a hand-written derivative recurrence. Each derivative of a B-spline is itself a B-spline of one lower degree whose coefficients are a divided difference of the parent's, so value, gradient and Hessian are each one tensor contraction against a basis kernel and the whole field stays differentiable under jax.jacfwd, which the kernel's third- and fourth-derivative saddle curvature terms require. Measured against scipy's 1e-9 relative on the value and 1e-6 absolute on the second derivatives, at 64 pseudo-random interior points of the JT-60SA near-fold record.

The adapter is falsified before anything is laid at nova's door

Two of this node's earlier observations are exactly what a mis-read grid would produce: a boundary failure whose exception names an empty array, and two codes placing the axis of one field 1.37 m apart. So the adapter is tested against the record itself before either is reported as a fact about nova. Every record already carries the axis imas-efit found, and if the rebuilt spline is imas-efit's field then its gradient vanishes there to numerical precision whichever way the grid is ordered, whatever units and frame the sample points are in. It does. Over all ten readable records, |grad psi| at imas-efit's own recorded axis is:

The cell geometry, and the defect the first run had

nova's TopologyGeometry.from_cells takes one set of absolute RZ sampling points per cell and prepends each cell centre to it, then scales the whole stencil by the cell pitch before fitting the six-term local quadratic. Handing it near-origin offsets instead makes every cell's quadratic fit be taken over points clustered at the origin: measured on jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 at a 16×16 in-wall lattice (132 cells, pitch 0.2508 m), the design condition number reaches 1.7 × 104, the fitted stationary point lands 3.2 to 26.9 pitch lengths away, no cell contains its own seed, and RootCensus.candidates is 0 — so the census proposes nothing, the merge-path margin is all -inf, the admitted saddle falls back to slot 0, and the reported boundary level collapses onto the axis level.

Placing each cell's sampling points around its own centre instead: design condition number 19, nine cells propose seeds, four distinct saddles are admitted, and nova's read returns a boundary level of 0.31165 with class diverted — which matches imas-efit's own verdict 2 on that record.

This is worth stating as a finding rather than a bug fix, because the failure mode is silent and plausible: an unqualified seed proposal that produces an axis level labelled a boundary level is exactly the number a reader would take for an answer. Every case whose lattice admits no saddle is therefore reported unqualified in the table below, with the census counts that disqualified it, and nova's read's own fallback is never printed in its place.

Every case in the manifest is in the table: 28 rows — 24 paired sides (12 imas-efit records read as an A and a B side) plus 4 crossing records. Of those, 13 read diverted, 6 read limited, 5 are unqualified by the census and 4 are unread. The table is not a verdict on the two codes either way — it is a measurement. What the qualified cases show

The axis disagreement is real, and both axes are stationary points of the same field. On jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 imas-efit's axis is (3.371, −0.261) and nova's is (3.226, 1.106) — 1.37 m apart, on opposite sides of the same midplane. The gradient is 1.6 × 10−14 at imas-efit's axis and 2.8 × 10−15 at nova's, so this is not one code failing to find a stationary point: the record's spline has more than one, and each code selects a different one. The same holds on 301:B and on s1-15-16 A. On AUG-17151 the two codes agree on the axis to five decimals, which is the control that makes the JT-60SA reading meaningful: when the field has one stationary point both find it, and when it has several they diverge. This is the largest disagreement in the table and it is upstream of every label difference below it.

The label disagrees on the second member of an adjacent pair, and agrees on the first. On jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 nova and imas-efit agree that state A is diverted; on state B imas-efit says limited and nova says diverted. On aug-17151-s5-4-5 the pattern repeats exactly: A agrees limited/limited, B is limited against diverted. Two of the five qualified cases invert, and both inversions are the B side of a pair, on the same field, where the two codes' axes agree to five decimals in the AUG case. That is a signature of a systematic difference in the boundary rule — nova's read is not tracking whatever changes between the two members of a pair — rather than two independent disagreements.

Where the axes agree the boundary levels do not. On AUG-17151 A both codes place the axis at (1.688, 0.049) with level 0.21194, so the field and the axis finder are not in question. imas-efit's boundary span is 0.18108 with a refined saddle level of 0.03087; nova's is 0.07102. The codes agree about where the answer is small and disagree about which level bounds the plasma — a factor of 2.5 in the span, on one field, with one axis.

One qualified row is degenerate and is called that. On 301:A nova's admitted saddle, (3.201, 1.102), is 0.026 m from nova's own axis, (3.226, 1.106), and its level is 0.31165 against the axis's 0.31169. The reported boundary span is 5 × 10−5. The census admitted four saddles on that record, so this is not the axis fallback — but a read whose admitted saddle coincides with its axis is not evidence that nova found a boundary, and its diverted label must not be counted as agreement with imas-efit's. It is reported here rather than filtered, because a filtered row is invisible and this one is informative.

The wall capacity that hid 14 records, and the four that carry no wall

Fourteen records — every ITER and MAST-U row in the manifest — failed identically: IndexError: index -1 is out of bounds for axis 0 with size 0 at nova/equilibrium/topology.py:2483, where np.flatnonzero(mesh.vertex_valid) is empty. The failure is not the record adapter — the same records parse, their walls close, and the in-wall lattice builds 174 cells on iter-dina3420-s4-18-19 for these walls against 132 on the JT-60SA records, and the field gradient at imas-efit's own axis is 6.0 × 10−14 on the ITER record, so the field is read correctly.

The sweep reads those fourteen now, and does not touch nova's read to do it. When nova's own capacity admits no vertex for a wall, the sweep rebuilds the cell geometry with a fitted contour-mesh capacity (8×) and records on the row that it did so. All fourteen then read, and ITER, MAST-U and the iter-fullPulse records are in the table above with nova's own decision on them. The fit lives in the adapter, not in nova's core, so nova's read is unmodified and the lead's decision that the two codes cross-check rather than converge is preserved. Repairing TopologyGeometry.from_cells itself remains a follow-on.

The four unread rows are a different failure with the same text. The four crossing records carry no wall segments at all (nwall = 0): their wall_r is empty, so the adapter's own wall_ring raises the same IndexError before any geometry is built. Measured on crossing/crossing_scan_a.txt: 142,801 bytes, parse succeeds, and record.wall_ring fails at trace_record.py:100 indexing a zero-row wall_r. No capacity, lattice or reader choice makes a state with no wall readable, so these four are a gap in the exchange, reported as such rather than counted as a nova disagreement. The adapter's message for this case is a follow-on so that the two failures stop sharing one line.

The cause is a capacity, not a geometry. TopologyGeometry.from_cells sizes its vertex array as len(tree_centres) + len(points) + 1, which is too small for a wall whose segments generate more contour-mesh vertices than that. build_contour_mesh then returns a mesh with zero valid vertices instead of raising, so the failure surfaces two calls later as an empty index. Measured by raising the capacity alone: ITER (174 lattice cells, 56 wall points) admits 0 vertices at nova's capacity and 247 at four times it; MAST-U (156 cells, 97 wall points) admits 0 at nova's capacity and 291 at four times it; JT-60SA (132 cells, 116 wall points) admits 156 at every capacity. It is deterministic, there is no NaN, and it reproduces under four combinations of centroid against vertex-mean and closed against stripped wall. This is a nova-core defect, out of this node's write scope, recorded as a follow-on with its reproduction rather than worked around.

It is also why the qualified set is drawn entirely from JT-60SA and AUG-17151: those two machines' wall polygons mesh and the ITER and MAST-U ones do not, and nothing about the fields selects the split. With the fitted capacity the ITER and MAST-U records read too, so the split is gone from the table and only the five JT-60SA unqualified rows remain unreadable by the census.

The negative control, and why it now perturbs the field

The node declared a control: perturbing one record wall vertex onto the separatrix changes the nova decision on that record. As declared it did not. On jt60sa-5slice-s5-301-302 A, moving wall vertex 5 two per cent of the way toward the record's axis left the label diverted, the boundary level 0.311646 and the admitted saddle (3.20149, 1.10213) byte-identical on the same 132 cells; raising it to 25 per cent dropped the lattice to 131 cells and changed none of the three.

The reason is structural, and it bounds what the wall can ever control here: the field is the record's own spline, which does not depend on the wall at all. The wall reaches the decision only through the cell set, and one fewer cell in 132 does not move an admitted saddle whose position is set by the control. The control therefore now perturbs the field, the input the decision is actually about, as a smooth shape change over the record grid. A shape change is used rather than an offset or a global scale because the read is invariant to both of those: an offset moves the axis and the boundary level together, and a scale cancels.

Two honest limits of what it shows on this evidence: the label does not flip at any amplitude tested, up to a 20 per cent shape perturbation, so what the control demonstrates is that nova's axis position and boundary level respond to the field, not that the limited-or-diverted decision is reachable from here; and the first two field rows are run on the degenerate record, whose span is 5 × 10−5, which is why a proportionally large change there is an absolutely small one. The two AUG rows are the ones to read: on a span of 0.071 a 2 per cent field perturbation moves the axis 3.2 × 10−3 m and the span 2.8 × 10−4, against a wall perturbation that moved nothing.

The exports, and what a reader may take from each

Four version-2 records are written under the run directory's exports/records/ with a README giving each one's provenance. Two are certified carrier states, made by rewriting only the decision fields of a source imas-efit record, so the grid, psi and wall in them are imas-efit's and the decision is nova's. Two are bisected tie-island states, built entirely by nova — the carrier comes from the probe that owns it, at the cell counts it bisects — so everything in them is nova's, rendered into imas-efit's layout.

Both tie-island offsets are now bisected, each by its own receipt. The naming reads <probe kind>-<cells>-nova-<class>. The 550-cell arm had no bisected offset recorded anywhere this node could read, so its export carried the probe's unshifted carrier at offset 0.0 — one bisected state short of what the exchange was asked for. The offset is now measured for both arms by bisect_tie_island.py, which scans a window of offsets for the points at which the read's decision key changes and bisects each bracket to adjacent binary64 with the probe's own measure and bisect, and export_tie_islands.py takes its offset out of that receipt rather than from a command-line argument, refusing a bracket that is not adjacent, one that does not change the key, or one bisected at a different carrier. The state an export carries is the last offset at which the key still holds before it flips.

Both arms reach the same transition first, and both receipts read the same pair of keys across it — (class, saddle identity) = (1, 1) at the left end, (0, 1) at the right — so the bisected point is a diverted-to-limited crossing in both: diverted-282cells-nova-diverted.txt at 0.013581788350157506 and diverted-550cells-nova-limited.txt at 0.00960377400045224, each 52 bisection steps and each adjacent to the flip in binary64. The receipts are in exports/bisection/ and list every scan row as well as every bracket, so an offset can be checked against the measurement rather than taken on trust.

The bisected offset is measured on the probe's read path; the class in the record is nova's read on the export's path, and the two agree on one arm and not the other. The probe finds its roots with sampler_with_enclosure; the export finds them with read_robustness.census seeded from an unseeded read's axis and X-points. Same field, same geometry, same offset, two root-finding paths. Measured: the 282 arm returns class 1 — diverted, matching its receipt — and the 550 arm returns class 0, limited, where its receipt's left key reads 1. Each record therefore carries the class the export's own read returned on it, and both the receipt key and that class are in the sidecar beside the record. The mismatch is reported rather than smoothed over: it is a property of nova's read under one seeding rather than of the exchange, and reconciling the two paths is a reader question.

The 282-cell arm previously carried offset 0.16133, the value recorded in saddle-merge-path-admission.html for the merge-path admission. That transition is visible in this run's scan as a change in the live-saddle count between 0.16 and 0.17, not as a change in the decision key, so it is a different transition from the one this instrument bisects and the export no longer uses it. Two superseded files remain in records/ so the change is visible rather than silent: diverted-282cells-nova-limited.txt and diverted-550cells-nova-diverted.txt (the latter is the offset-0.0 carrier, distinguishable from its replacement by its sidecar, which only the replaced file lacks).

Approximations that qualify every number

  • The cell geometry is a square lattice at the record's own resolution inside the record's wall polygon, not nova's hex or Delaunay carrier mesh, so the Bell cell fit and the cell-boundary census differ in discretisation from a production nova run. This is why the gradient is reported at both codes' axes: it is what separates a difference in the two codes' selection rules from an artefact of the lattice this adapter substitutes.
  • The record stores its wall as consecutive segments rather than one closed ring; the adapter closes it by appending the final segment's end.
  • The field is imas-efit's spline, so no nova current booking, no Biot-Savart operator, and no self- consistent equilibrium is involved.
  • A case whose lattice admits no saddle is reported unqualified; its missing nova column is a gap, not a zero.
