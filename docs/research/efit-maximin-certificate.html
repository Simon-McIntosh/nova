Escape-level maximin certificates

Three rows of the escape-level comparison (docs/research/efit-escape-level-comparison.html, merged at 03a0f1d04) start from the same axis on both codes, so they test the boundary rule rather than the axis. Both codes claim a maximin of polarity·ψ over axis-to-boundary paths. Each row is settled by a certificate evaluated on the record's own spline: which side's claimed level is attained by a real path, and which side's level is blocked by a cut. The maximin reproduces imas-efit's escape level to six significant figures on all three rows, and imas-efit is right on all three.

Method

Each imas-efit BOUNDARY_LEVEL_TRACE 2 record carries a 65×65 psi grid and the wall ring it was traced against. For each row this node rebuilds the record's own not-a-knot bicubic spline with scipy (RectBivariateSpline, kx=ky=3, s=0), the object the axis-adjudication rebuild check uses, and evaluates polarity·ψ on it. Neither nova's read nor the field-swap adapter is evaluated: the field is the record's own scipy spline. The search runs on an 800×800 square sampling of the spline restricted to the wall ring (step 5.5 mm on MAST-U, 2.6 mm on synthetic).

The maximin from the axis to a target is the largest level L for which {polarity·&psi ≥ L} still connects the two points. It is read off a descending-level union-find (Kruskal), exact for the sampled field: the first level at which the axis node and the target node merge is the maximin. The attaining path is a breadth-first path inside that superlevel set, so its minimum equals the maximin by construction. A blocking cut exists when the target is not in the axis's component at the claimed level; it is reported as the below-level nodes bordering the axis component.

(2) mastu-fullPulse-s1-4-5-B: nova's contact is blocked

Nova reads a limited state with its wall contact at (0.33280, 1.30309) and level 0.020531. That point's own polarity·ψ is 0.020531, so the level is the contact's tangency. But the maximin from the axis to that contact is 0.0196524, attained at (0.57376, 1.12305) — the X-point region imas-efit named as its bottleneck (0.57493, 1.12267), 1.3 mm away. imas-efit's prediction holds exactly: any path from the axis to nova's contact must cross that X-point, and the best level it can carry across is 0.01965. At nova's level 0.020531 (−8.8e-4 above the maximin) a blocking cut of 1478 sampled nodes separates the axis from the contact, so nova's contact sits on a lobe the axis never reaches at 0.020531. On this row nova's read admitted a wall contact at a level it cannot attain.

The attaining path (axis to the contact) is exported as mastu-fullPulse-s1-4-5-B-attaining-path.txt; the cut as mastu-fullPulse-s1-4-5-B-blocking-cut.txt.

(3) synthetic_crossing-A and -B: no cut at imas-efit's level

On both synthetic rows the target is imas-efit's wall point (0.85, 0.2) and imas-efit's level is 0.0142147 (A) and 0.0125772 (B). The maximin from the axis to that wall point is 0.0142147 and 0.0125771 respectively: a path attains imas-efit's level on both, its minimum at the wall point itself. So no blocking cut exists at imas-efit's level — the cut detector finds the axis component reaches the wall point — and nova's claimed maximin, 0.011338 (A) and 0.011404 (B), is an underestimate of the true maximin to that wall point. nova's read admitted only the interior saddle (1.477, −0.422) and its own maximin there is below the wall reach.

The attaining paths are exported as synthetic_crossing_a-attaining-path.txt and synthetic_crossing_b-attaining-path.txt. Because the wall point's own level equals the maximin, the cut test at exactly imas-efit's level is marginal; the decider on these rows is the attaining path, which is unambiguous.

Both synthetic records fail the axis-adjudication rebuild check (docs/research/efit-axis-adjudication.html): |grad ψ| = 2.4e-4 at the axis imas-efit recorded. The two codes nevertheless read the same field: the record's psi grid is one object, the spline is of that grid, and the same grid carries a stationary point at nova's axis (1.43334, 0.19999) where |grad ψ| = 2.3e-13. imas-efit's stored axis is displaced from that stationary point by about 1e-5 m; at the field's curvature there that displacement is the whole 2.4e-4 gradient. So the rebuild flag marks an axis metadata offset, not two different fields, and the maximin columns comparing the two rules are read on one field on both sides.
