Options for a sub-millisecond converged forward solve

The target is a warm-started, converged forward equilibrium in less than 1 ms on an H200 at about 2,000–10,000 cells. This record distinguishes measured solve walls from component timings and estimates. An algebraic residual below 1e-12 does not by itself qualify the flux, root selection, constraint rows or map fidelity.

Budget and existing solver

The solve is a fixed point in cell moments, m = book(read(ψext + Gm)). A two-step solve has less than 0.5 ms per step for the read, membership, booking, image, Jacobian update and convergence check combined; a three-step solve has less than 0.333 ms. Cold compilation and carrier preparation are reported separately. A predecessor's warm factorisation can be excluded from an individual slice only when the receipt identifies which preceding slice produced it and includes the cost of refreshing it over the sequence.

The exact-booking prototype already measured 26.0 ms at 2,098 cells and 85.4 ms at 5,158 cells on the H200. It is thus at least 26–85 times the total target before the fixed-point read or linear solve. This is a component lower bound, not an end-to-end solve timing. The live acceptance section remains the authority for the joint accuracy clauses.

Published speed and its scope

LIUQE's real-time implementation reports a cycle shorter than 200 μs for a 28 × 65 grid and 133 measurements. It uses an iterative Poisson solve with a linear parameterisation of current density for equilibrium reconstruction. This is evidence that a smaller fixed-grid reconstruction can meet a sub-millisecond control cycle, not evidence that exact cut-cell booking and a converged forward map can do so.

The P-EFIT GPU report gives roughly 250 μs per iteration on a 65 × 65 grid. A later EAST implementation report describes eight to nine converged iterations in about 2.5 ms. Per-iteration and full-solve claims must remain separate. FreeGSNKE's solver documentation identifies a Newton–Krylov method with finite-difference Jacobian approximations; it does not provide a sub-millisecond H200 measurement or Nova-certificate error.

Certificate measurement

The prototype in scripts/prototypes/solve_options reads device-built machine groups from the prior certified caches and runs all six certificate/grid rows inside one H200 allocation. Per-stage walls are synchronized medians of five warm calls; compilation, machine load and Bell-mesh preparation are separate. Point-booking moment error is a comparison to exact booked moments at the same analytic field, not yet the mapped flux error or a converged-solve error. The logged module path and carrier key identify the code and data actually measured.

The initial device profile is in flight. Its first 2,105-cell limited row measured exact booking at 47.39 ms, hard-centroid booking at 0.0837 ms and one-pitch sigmoid booking at 0.0889 ms. Their relative-sup moment differences from exact are 0.407 and 0.343. The 2,098-cell diverted row measured 26.15, 0.0826 and 0.0929 ms respectively, with moment differences 0.645 and 0.528. These are distinct physical cases; the point methods use one centroid per cell. The initial script also times topology.read, the older full census, at 8.06 and 7.52 seconds. That is deliberately excluded from the locked read budget; a separate Bell-membership and Gm profile is being prepared.

The required next measurements are the remaining four rung/case component rows, full moving-support structured Jacobian and factor, finite-difference tangent controls, mapped flux bias and convergence order, warm and cold solve totals, and production-seed outcome for each option. Until those receipts exist, no alternative is certified to meet 1 ms. A credible recommendation must account for the cheapest method's physical bias as well as its launch latency.
