Execution evidence

ITER plasma prescribed from DINA

The current prescription follows DINA's centre to 4.14 mm and its plasma flux to 2.17% at the mapped CS/PF coils and 0.41% at flux loops; VS3 still needs a section mapping.

Source and method

Read ITER 105014/1 through imas-python with its native Data Dictionary 3.38.1. The equilibrium has 2,027 slices; 867 carry at least 0.1 MA of absolute plasma current, from 1.22 to 66.63 s. Each active slice supplies a 65 by 129 poloidal-flux map, 50 samples of the two flux-function derivatives, a declared plasma current, magnetic axis, current centre, and a separatrix outline. The last pressure-gradient sample is nonfinite in otherwise usable profiles. The profile interpolant uses the finite samples and extrapolates to the edge.

The prescription evaluates the stored pressure and field-function gradients at each grid point. DINA's derivatives are against total flux; multiplying them by 2π puts them in the flux-per-radian coordinate of the toroidal-current formula. A simple closed separatrix enclosing the magnetic axis defines the current mask. An open or invalid outline is replaced by the outermost axis-enclosing closed contour of DINA's flux map, searched inward from 99.9% of the declared axis-to-boundary span. The endpoint gap and boundary source are retained per slice. Cell currents are integrated over the 93.75 mm square grid and multiplied by the ratio of declared to integrated current. The same cell currents feed axisymmetric ring Green functions at point filaments or averaged over finite conductor sections.

Independent linked-flux check

At 24 indices spanning the active interval, DINA's own profiles_2d.j_tor multiplied by grid-cell area supplies an independent current vector. Its C-order flattening is confirmed by the 24 by 8,385 em_coupling.mutual_loops_grid matrix: the alternate flattening gives multi-weber errors, whereas the chosen order tracks the direct ring calculation. DINA's 8,385 by 15 mutual_grid_active matrix gives plasma-only flux at active conductors. The first 11 columns map to CS3U, CS2U, CS1, CS2L, CS3L and PF1–PF6; CS1's column links both of its stored sections, so their predicted fluxes are summed. This supplies a coil reference even where the 2-D map stops at R = 3 m.

Map subtraction and remaining mapping

A separate diagnostic subtracts finite-section coil and passive contributions reconstructed from the scenario's pf_active and pf_passive geometry and currents from its total ψ map. That route gives a median 1.87% loop residual and a 72.65% maximum, while the VS3 positions inside the map give a 5.40% median and 90.71% maximum. These larger residuals are not ascribed to the plasma prescription: the independent grid mutuals above bound that prescription much more tightly. The geometric subtraction and DINA's stored conductor coupling therefore remain distinct instruments, especially in the low-current tail.

VS3 has two elements in this scenario's pf_active IDS, while DINA's active-grid coupling carries four additional columns after the mapped CS/PF columns. Their section-level correspondence is unresolved. The current API can evaluate a sectioned VS3 conductor set when its geometry is supplied; the independent DINA comparison for those four columns needs the conductor-set mapping.

Vertical-shift negative control

At 5.93 s and the magnetics loop at (R, Z) = (5.7547544, 5.0640967) m, moving the prescribed distribution upward by 1 cm changes its linked flux by −0.0183351 Wb. The local vertical-gradient prediction is −0.0183045 Wb, a 0.167% difference. The focused mutation run removed the displacement from the source coordinates: the same test then measured zero change against a predicted 0.0067701 Wb and failed. The mutation was removed after its failing log was retained.
