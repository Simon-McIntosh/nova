Execution evidence

ITER plasma prescribed from DINA

The current prescription covers both the 2 MA and 15 MA DINA cases. Nova computes every conductor contribution from geometry and current, then compares the prescribed plasma flux with a signed, gauge-aligned reconstruction of DINA's recorded flux.

Prescription and boundary selection

Read ITER 105014/1 and 105052/1 through imas-python at their native Data Dictionary 3.38.1. At each slice above 0.1 MA, the stored 50-point flux-function derivatives are evaluated on the 65 by 129 poloidal grid, masked by an axis-enclosing closed boundary, and scaled to the declared plasma current. The original separatrix endpoint gap, selected boundary source, and any reconstructed-contour fraction are retained per slice. A diverted outline is first cut at its stored X-point; the closed DINA boundary outline is next; a closed ψ contour is a last resort.

Geometric plasma-flux reference

Nova evaluates coil and passive contributions with its finite-section and polygon Green kernels, using DINA's pf_active and pf_passive currents. The current is interpolated at each equilibrium time from its own IDS time base. This matters in 105052/1: equilibrium and passive IDSs each have 2,924 samples, while pf_active has 29,234. An index-based read at the 1.218 s equilibrium slice would select 0.122 s coil currents; interpolation removes that measured error. Both cases have homogeneous_time = 1, so the IDS-level time arrays are used.

The native ψ orientation is inferred from the sign of Ip and its axis-to-boundary flux span: 105014/1 needs sign +1, 105052/1 sign −1. Flux has an additive gauge. At each sampled slice, nova fixes that gauge at the magnetic axis by comparing the signed stored ψ with the sum of nova's conductor flux and nova's Green-kernel flux from DINA's independent profiles_2d.j_tor. The axis gauge has median 0.001 Wb in 105014/1 and −0.429 Wb in 105052/1 after the current-time correction. The reconstructed plasma reference at a covered conductor is signed DINA total ψ minus nova's coil and passive flux and this axis gauge.

The 2-D ψ map covers the two VS3 sections of 14 active-coil sections. For the other 12, nova applies its Green kernel directly to DINA's stored j_tor cell currents, independently of the flux-function prescription; the receipt labels those rows separately. On 105014/1, each of 24 recorded magnetics flux-loop readings replaces the map's total ψ at that loop before the same conductor subtraction and gauge correction. As a positive check on the total-flux instrument, map ψ minus the recorded loop reading has median −0.00152 Wb over 576 comparisons; its 0.487 Wb maximum occurs in early ramp-up.

Each flux check requires |predicted − reference| ≤ max(A, f × |reference|). The absolute floor A prevents a small plasma reference in ramp-up or ramp-down from turning a finite conductor-subtraction error into an unbounded percentage. Case 105014/1 uses A = 1 Wb and f = 10% for ψ-map coil and magnetics references. Case 105052/1 uses A = 3 Wb and f = 5% for ψ-map coils. Both use A = 0.1 Wb and f = 1% where the reference is DINA j_tor through nova's kernel. The final column below is the largest residual divided by its applicable bound; every value must be at most 1.

The largest 105014/1 VS3 residual on the 2 MA plateau is 1.081 Wb against 11.966 Wb of reference plasma flux, or 9.03%. At 0.402 MA, a 0.880 Wb residual against 0.455 Wb of plasma flux has a misleading raw percentage; the 1 Wb floor carries that comparison. In 105052/1 the largest map residual is 2.523 Wb at 2.536 MA against 6.251 Wb of plasma flux, covered by the 3 Wb floor. Above 75% of that case's peak current, the maximum is 1.601 Wb, or 3.10% of its reference. The much tighter direct j_tor results show that the larger map-subtraction residual measures the conductor and gauge reconstruction as well as the plasma prescription.

Controls and reproduction

Moving the source 1 cm vertically at 5.93 s in 105014/1 changed a magnetics loop's flux by −0.0183351 Wb against −0.0183045 Wb predicted from its local vertical derivative, a 0.167% difference. In 105052/1 at 14.72 s, the same displacement at a fixed target changed flux by −0.0379121 Wb against −0.0378468 Wb predicted, a 0.173% difference. A retained mutation that ignored the shift failed the focused test. A separate deliberately tightened geometric-flux limit must also make the scenario gate exit nonzero.
