Machine-description and circuit evidence

ITER sectioned conductor set

The run 204 winding geometry resolves into series pancakes, while the vessel and cryostat rings need independent through-thickness currents before their converter-band impedance can be used.

Sources and winding placement

The active source is ITER_MACHINE_DESCRIPTION/3/111001/204/pf_active, read with its written Data Dictionary 3.42.0. It contains six central-solenoid modules, six poloidal-field coils, and two four-turn in-vessel vertical-stabilization coils. Each CS or PF coil is represented by one rectangular winding pack, so the source supplies its total signed turns and bounding geometry but does not name the pancakes. The 295 sections here place the known winding stack evenly through that rectangle: 40 pancakes per CS module, eight double pancakes in PF1 and PF3–PF5, six in PF2, nine in PF6, and each explicit VS turn as one section. Floating-point remainder goes to the final pancake; every section sum equals the source coil's signed turns.

The winding counts come from the ITER PF assembly account and the ITER CS fabrication account. The vertical-stabilization coil specification confirms four turns per upper and lower coil. Even spacing is a placement approximation because the machine-description rectangles omit individual turn centres and insulation gaps. ITER describes 14 turns per CS pancake, while this machine-description run has 554 effective turns per module, six fewer than 40 × 14. The model distributes the IDS effective turns equally over the 40 geometric layers rather than inventing which six layer connections account for the difference; the independent inductance check measures the resulting terminal effect.

MachineSection carries the sub-coil directly: geometry and signed turns are the only new data. A second section type would duplicate its reader, outline and centroid seam without adding a distinct physical object.

Passive input and the defect reproduced

The vessel input is ITER_MACHINE_DESCRIPTION/3/115004/6/pf_passive, and the cryostat and other outer structures are .../124001/3/pf_passive, both read with their written Data Dictionary 3.40.1. They contain 210 and 248 toroidal loops. Of the 458 source elements, 450 are thick lines with explicit oriented cross sections. The thickest source section is 80 mm. Source loop resistance is retained across independent layers: each layer's resistance is the parent resistance divided by its share of the parent thickness, so their parallel conductance sums to the source conductance. A surface-graded mesh grows geometrically towards the middle while bounding the interior width. The IDS gives no resistance temperature; this study treats those supplied values as nominal at 293 K, without a measured temperature correction.

The earlier single-ring set reproduces the recorded skin-depth limit when its stored resistivity and thickness are checked: the 248 known-present cryostat rings reach a minimum of 1.79845 Hz, and the 210 vessel rings reach 29.3104 Hz. These values come from ρ/(πμ₀t²), with the source ring's own ρ and thickness t. The finite positive counts are the positive control for the minimum calculation; this is a geometric validity limit, not a failure to read the data. Where a future loop omits resistance, the builder refuses it rather than silently assigning a material value.

The active IDS records zero series resistance for every superconducting CS and PF coil, 5.7 mΩ for VSU and 7.91 mΩ for VSL. Those are source values, treated here as nominal at 293 K for the resistive VS conductors because the IDS carries no resistance temperature. Superconductor AC losses and converter feeder resistance are outside this axisymmetric L/R set.

Coupling method

Every winding section and passive layer is one circuit row. Nova's two-section linkage integrates a finite-area source and averages the observer section, then symmetrizes the inductance matrix. The coil–coil block uses finite-source evaluation and a six-point observer cap in each direction; the independently integrated original IDS element boxes use a more resolved observer grid. A binary series map sums section currents and all intra-coil mutual fluxes to each terminal. No value from the earlier terminal matrix is inserted into the sectioned matrix.

Twelve workers built the original passive linkage columns on the fleet allocation. The coil block is cached separately and reused across both passive meshes. Source passive resistance is divided by each layer's share of its parent thickness. The terminal spectrum is the Schur complement of the shorted passive L/R block, evaluated through the same passive modal eigensolve used for circuit evolution. The earlier power_convertor export is reported as a comparison, while the acceptance check compares the section sum with the unsplit IDS element geometry and two analytic inductance limits.

The thin-ring check differs from its analytic limit by 0.4471% against a 5% tolerance; a uniformly filled square-section twelve-turn coil differs by 0.0328% against a 1% tolerance. Both checks resolve the observer section. The analytic formulas and their assumptions are documented in the National Bureau of Standards inductance tables.

Removing passive coupling gives the bare terminal impedance within 1.11e-16 at every sampled frequency on both raw meshes. The declared passive-removal mutation makes the screened expectation fail with one failing test and EXIT=1, so the negative control is sensitive to lost screening.

Thickness-refinement result and cost

Surface grading resolves the skin-current region while allowing a wider middle layer. With a 40 mm interior bound and geometric growth by two towards the middle, 12 and 6 mm outer layers yield 1,312 and 2,163 passive circuits from 458 source loops. The same raw finite-source coil block is used on both meshes. Across 101 logarithmic samples from 0.1 Hz to 5 kHz, refinement changes every coil's coupled terminal impedance by less than 1%; the worst is 0.302164% for VSU at 300.08 Hz. The maximum is 0.252634% at 574.35 Hz, the sample nearest 600 Hz, and 0.0928065% at 5 kHz. The comparison script asserts the 1% criterion through its stated 5 kHz limit and exits successfully. Tightening the criterion to 0.1% on the same receipts refuses it with EXIT=1.

The original passive linkage builds took 268.348 s and 815.094 s on twelve fleet workers. The separately cached finite-source winding block took 9.905 s on eight workers for the first rebuilt spectrum and was loaded on the second. With those matrices cached, the raw 101-point spectra took 0.856 s and 3.296 s. Removing the passive structure gives the bare impedance within 1.11e-16 on each mesh. This is sampled convergence through 5 kHz; the 6 mm surface layer is about the nominal stainless-steel skin depth at that endpoint. Source resistance and the VS series resistance are nominal at 293 K because the IDS does not state their temperature.
