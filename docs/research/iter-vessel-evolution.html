Overview

The sectioned passive conductor set is driven through 105014/1 and 105052/1 by DINA's pf_active coil currents and the prescribed plasma, integrated with the exact zero-order-hold eigenmode propagation of nova.circuit.propagate on the 12 mm mesh and a 6 mm interval. Three measurements follow: the evolved currents against DINA's own pf_passive loops, the coil voltages of the whole coupled flux against DINA's, and section 2's VS3 psi-map residual with the evolved passive flux added at that point.

The ITER windings are superconducting, so their winding resistance is zero and each coil voltage is dPsi/dt alone; DINA's stored zero coil resistance is the physical value, not a gap in the data. The passive flux at VS3 is compared against exactly the source term the psi-map check subtracted, restricted to that kernel's passive columns, so a flux-model difference cannot hide inside the coil contribution.

Tolerance is stated before comparison as peak residual ≤ max(10 kA, 25% of that loop's peak DINA current), reported per loop because the loops range over three decades in current. The 6 mm interval reproduces the 12 mm result: the same 90-91 loops, the same worst loop, and the same voltage and VS3 numbers to the reported digits, so the passive current is resolved on the 12 mm mesh.

Only 58% of the sectioned circuits fall inside a DINA loop cross-section, and they carry 58% of the evolved current magnitude. DINA's passive cover totals 3.4 m2 in r[3.24, 9.72] z[-5.66, 5.68] while the sectioned set spans r[0.2, 14.4] z[-13.1, 16.0]. The comparison therefore covers the DINA-represented subset; 42% of the evolved current sits in structures DINA's pf_passive does not model and is unmatched by construction.

Coil voltages from the whole coupled flux

Each voltage is the gradient of the coupled flux Ψ = LccIc + McpIp + Ψplasma at the section level, summed into DINA's coil channels (CS1U and CS1L into CS1, VSU and VSL into VS3).

On 105014/1 the passive coupling removes 64 V of the 857 V rms the bare coils miss, and 72-77 V of it lands on PF2-PF5 (PF2 168 → 93 V, PF5 242 → 178 V). The plasma term adds back 10 V of rms, contributing its own dPsi/dt on the evolved passive currents. Dropping the plasma drive entirely leaves 780 V, so it is the passive coupling, not the plasma term, that carries the reduction. On 105052/1 the same arms read 783, 593 and 591 V, so the PF2-PF5 miss there is roughly twice as large and the coupling removes roughly half of it (PF2 388 → 177 V, PF4 780 → 301 V).

The anti-series sign of the VSU/VSL winding was taken from the machine description and then tested explicitly. The residual does not resolve it: on 105014/1 the two signs differ by 0.11 V rms (789.60 against 789.72), and although 105052/1 separates them further (591.21 against 593.73 V), that pulse's minimum falls on the opposite sign to 105014/1's. The sign is therefore inherited from the machine description's circuit connections, not measured; the disagreement between the two pulses' minima shows the residual is too small a lever to settle it.

The VS3 residual does not close

Section 2 left a psi-map residual at the two VS3 sections of 0.88 and 1.08 Wb on 105014/1, and up to 2.52 Wb on 105052/1. Substituting the evolved passive flux for DINA's stored passive flux there does not close it; it grows. On 105014/1 the evolved passive flux peaks at 8.40 and 10.08 Wb against DINA's 7.37 and 8.46 Wb, a difference of 2.0 and 3.5 Wb, larger than the residual itself. The residual is therefore not a passive-current modelling gap: DINA's own stored passive currents already reproduce the psi-map at VS3 to about 1 Wb. On 105052/1 the substitution moves 2.52 Wb to 8.47 Wb at the first section and 2.35 Wb to 16.32 Wb at the second, where the evolved flux (11.13 Wb) falls 2.3 Wb *below* DINA's (13.42 Wb). Removing the plasma drive leaves the substitution essentially unchanged (the residuals move from 2.37 and 3.66 Wb to 3.22 and 6.19 Wb on 105014/1), because the passive flux at VS3 is coil-driven.

Caveat: the receipt compares the peak of the residual against the peak of the flux difference, and those peaks fall at different times. A common-time-base restatement is owed before the numbers are read as a per-instant closure.

Controls

The positive control is a single passive ring under a coil-current ramp, integrated with the same zero-order-hold scheme and compared with the analytic L/R solution; it recovers the analytic peak to a relative error of 1.4e-13, so the propagation is exact for a piecewise-linear drive and the ring's decay time is set by its resistance. The negative control mutates the ring resistance handling in a scratch copy, where that analytic decay test fails while it passes on the head arm.
