Overview

The sectioned passive conductor set is driven through 105014/1 and 105052/1 by DINA's pf_active coil currents and the prescribed plasma, integrated with the exact zero-order-hold eigenmode propagation of nova.circuit.propagate on the 12 mm mesh and a 6 mm interval. Three measurements follow: the evolved currents against DINA's own pf_passive loops, the coil voltages of the whole coupled flux against DINA's, and the VS3 psi-map residual with the evolved passive flux added at that point.

The ITER windings are superconducting, so their winding resistance is zero and each coil voltage is dPsi/dt alone; DINA's stored zero coil resistance is the physical value, not a gap in the data. The passive flux at VS3 is compared against exactly the source term the psi-map check subtracted, restricted to that kernel's passive columns, so a flux-model difference cannot hide inside the coil contribution.

What this work is for. The aim is to predict ITER, not to match DINA. DINA is a second code on the same machine description, so agreement with it is evidence about the two codes' shared assumptions, and disagreement is evidence that at least one of them is wrong — neither is a statement about the tokamak. The numbers below are read in that spirit throughout.

Two shares, and they are not the same number. 537 of 1312 sectioned circuits, 41% of the circuits, fall inside a DINA loop cross-section. Those circuits carry 58% of the evolved current magnitude, and the remaining 42% of the current sits in circuits DINA's pf_passive does not model and is unmatched by construction.

Tolerance is stated before comparison as peak residual ≤ max(10 kA, 25% of that loop's peak DINA current), reported per loop because the loops range over three decades in current. The 6 mm interval reproduces the 12 mm result: the same 90-91 loops, the same worst loop, and the same voltage and VS3 numbers to the reported digits, so the passive current is resolved on the 12 mm mesh. The coverage itself explains the comparison's limits: DINA's passive cover totals 3.4 m2 in r[3.24, 9.72] z[-5.66, 5.68] while the sectioned set spans r[0.2, 14.4] z[-13.1, 16.0].

Coil voltages from the whole coupled flux

Each voltage is the gradient of the coupled flux Ψ = LccIc + McpIp + Ψplasma at the section level, summed into DINA's coil channels (CS1U and CS1L into CS1, VSU and VSL into VS3).

On 105014/1 the passive coupling removes 64 V of the 857 V rms the bare coils miss, and 72-77 V of it lands on PF2-PF5 (PF2 168 → 93 V, PF5 242 → 178 V). The plasma term adds back 10 V of rms: driven by the evolved passive currents the plasma contributes its own dPsi/dt. Dropping the plasma drive entirely (the negative control) leaves 780 V, so it is the passive coupling that carries the reduction. On 105052/1 the same arms read 783, 593 and 591 V, so the PF2-PF5 miss there is roughly twice as large and the coupling removes roughly half of it (PF2 388 → 177 V, PF4 780 → 301 V).

This agreement is cross-code consistency, not a validation of the ITER hardware. The 93-235 V rms on PF2-PF5 is nova agreeing with DINA on the same machine description: a shared geometry, a shared passive-structure assumption or a shared error in either would pass both codes and leave the residual small. The measured basis for how well the coupling is actually known is not this number. It is the JT-60SA benchmark's 5-8% on the mutual-to-self inductance ratio M/L, which is what the coupling terms are worth. Read the 93-235 V against that band, not as a hardware tolerance.

The CS modules, PF1 and PF6 are a different story. Their residual is 0.95-2.4 kV rms after the full coupling (CS1 2432 V, CS2U 985 V, CS2L 945 V, CS3U 1075 V, CS3L 1078 V, PF1 1045 V, PF6 1168 V) against 93-235 V on PF2-PF5 and 20 V on VS3. That is not a coupling error. DINA records the converter-side voltage, and at initiation those coils are pinned at the converter limit while they discharge through their switching-network resistors, which sit apart from the converters (lead, 15 October 2026). nova's coupled-flux voltage and DINA's converter-side record are then measuring different circuits during that interval, and the residual is the difference between them.

The anti-series sign of the VSU/VSL winding was taken from the machine description and then tested explicitly. The residual does not resolve it: on 105014/1 the two signs differ by 789.60 against 789.72 V rms, and although 105052/1 separates them further (591.21 against 593.73 V), that pulse's minimum falls on the opposite sign to 105014/1's. The sign is therefore inherited from the machine description's circuit connections, not measured.

The VS3 psi-map residual does not close

An earlier check left a psi-map residual at the two VS3 sections of 0.88 and 1.08 Wb on 105014/1, and up to 2.52 Wb on 105052/1. Substituting the evolved passive flux for DINA's stored passive flux there does not close it; it grows. On 105014/1 the evolved passive flux peaks at 8.40 and 10.08 Wb against DINA's 7.37 and 8.46 Wb, a difference of 2.0 and 3.5 Wb, larger than the residual itself. The residual is therefore not a passive-current modelling gap: DINA's own stored passive currents already reproduce the psi-map at VS3 to about 1 Wb. On 105052/1 the substitution moves 2.52 Wb to 8.47 Wb at the first section and 2.35 Wb to 16.32 Wb at the second, where the evolved flux (11.13 Wb) falls 2.3 Wb *below* DINA's (13.42 Wb). Removing the plasma drive leaves the substitution essentially unchanged (the residuals move from 2.37 and 3.66 Wb to 3.22 and 6.19 Wb on 105014/1), because the passive flux at VS3 is coil-driven.

Caveat: the receipt compares the peak of the residual against the peak of the flux difference, and those peaks fall at different times. A common-time-base restatement is owed before the numbers are read as a per-instant closure.

Controls

The positive control is a single passive ring under a coil-current ramp, integrated with the same zero-order-hold scheme and compared with the analytic L/R solution; the propagated current recovers the analytic peak to a relative error of 1.4e-13, so the recurrence is exact for a piecewise-linear drive and the ring's decay time is set by its resistance, not its inductance.

Two negative controls, each logged. The first mutates the ring's resistance handling in a scratch copy, where that analytic decay test fails while it passes on the head arm. The second mutates the propagation itself — scaling the interval 2% inside integrate_eddy_ode, in a scratch copy outside the repository — and shows both halves of the guard: the propagation test reddens (its analytic agreement moves from 1.4e-13 to 6.0e-3), and the comparison of two decay times that a test once computed for itself still passes under the same mutation, which is why it was replaced.
