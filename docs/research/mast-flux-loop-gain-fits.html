The flux loops carry no unknown gain

Every MAST vacuum fit so far has excluded the flux loops, because a factor of 2π between the store's convention and the kernel's could not be ruled out and one unknown row gain corrupts every inverted current. That exclusion cost the programme the rank the probe array lacks — ten identified directions over thirteen coils, with coil and case inseparable. This record removes the exclusion. The convention question is answered by measurement rather than by reading a unit string, and the answer is that there is no factor to place. The stronger result sits behind it: no individual loop carries a gain either, so admitting the loops does not require calibrating them first.

A second finding was necessary before any of it could be measured at the P4 loops. The level-2 catalog's four FL_P4L positions are the FL_P3L block verbatim, so the two P4-lower channels that publish on every shot reached no described sensor. The repair needed no tolerance and no fit: a flux loop is a fixture wound onto a coil, so the coil it sits nearest is the coil its name identifies, and that relation holds for all 44 named level-2 loops and all 46 reconstruction rows with exactly four exceptions.

The copied block, and the relation that catches it

The level-2 catalog names its 44 flux loops in flux_loop_geometry_channel and gives each a position. Entries 20–23, FL_P4L_1 through FL_P4L_4, hold the four FL_P3L coordinates bit for bit. Which of the two blocks is the copy is not a matter of preference, and it needs no threshold to decide: the shared coordinates sit 15–22 mm from the P3-lower winding pack and 260–388 mm from P4 lower, so they are P3 lower's.

In absolute distance the fault shows up as a no-man's-land. Of the 34 coil-mounted loops, 22 sit within 27 mm of their own winding pack, and the eight P2 loops — fixtures further out from a divertor coil rather than beside a pack face — sit between 58 and 110 mm. The four FL_P4L entries sit at 260, 260, 388 and 388 mm. Nothing at all occurs between 110 and 260 mm.

The code does not use that gap, because a threshold placed inside it would still be a number chosen rather than a comparison made, and its margin is only a factor of 2.4. What it uses needs no threshold: a loop is nearest the coil its own name identifies. Every one of the 44 named loops satisfies that and so does every one of the 46 reconstruction rows, the four FL_P4L entries excepted. As a ratio the separation is far wider than the absolute one: the correctly mounted loops stand at most 0.209 of their own-coil distance to the nearest other coil, while the copied block stands at 17.0 — a factor of 81, again with nothing in between. The same test independently identifies the two reconstruction rows sitting 17 mm from P6 upper, which the level-2 catalog describes no loop for at all.

The repair is served from the reconstruction's own loop table and only where the identification is unique: the free rows nearest the named coil must number exactly what the block does, or the block stays as published and the join refuses the channel. A replacement that is not identified is a sensor pose invented rather than recovered, and a refused channel is the better failure.

The convention, measured

A flux loop links the total flux through its own contour in weber, while a reconstruction's flux function is per radian. Those two differ in exactly the digit that says whether the 2π is inside the quantity, which is why the question could not be settled by reading a unit string. It is settled by fitting: one scale per channel per pulse, the pre-excitation offset removed on the pulse itself, the prediction built from the described winding outlines through the same polygon Green's kernel and the same area weighting the probe response uses.

The fitted scale is 0.9622. A store in weber against a kernel per radian would give 6.2832 and the reciprocal case 0.1592. The measurement is three-quarters of an order from either, on all 28 channels, on every pulse. There is no convention factor to place, which is what MEASURED_FLUX_CONVENTION already asserted and what this now confirms end to end.

Per-channel verdicts

A gain is a property of a channel only when it differs from what every other channel does. A factor the whole set carries is a property of whatever predicts them all, and dividing it out one channel at a time would write the forward model's residual into the data as a calibration. Every departure below is therefore measured against each route's own set median, and a promotion requires both routes to separate the same channel by the same amount — the discipline that promoted obr17 at the probes.

Two things the fits falsify

The restored positions, against the copied ones. A gain absorbs any constant error, so no single pulse can refute a position — a wrong one merely fits a different scale. What a wrong position cannot do is fit the same scale to every pulse, because the flux it gets wrong depends on which coil is driven. Over the cohort the restored positions take 0.9642 ± 0.0383 while the copied ones take 1.7805 ± 0.3859 and range from 0.439 to 2.723. On four single-coil pulses driving four different coils the copied positions need 1.55, 0.45, 1.74 and 1.61 while the restored ones need 0.97, 1.00, 0.98 and 0.97. The archive's own forward prediction, computed at its own P4-lower positions, reproduces fl_p4l_1 to 0.4%.

The near-field screen the probes need, the loops do not. A probe within two winding-pack widths of an excited coil is dropped from that pulse, because a uniform current density over an outline misrepresents how the turns are stacked inside it. Flux is far more forgiving than field: 5 386 of the admissible fits sit inside that cut, at standoffs down to 0.13 pack widths, and they return 0.9588 ± 0.0331 against 0.9728 ± 0.1163 for the 1 677 outside it. The near-field fits are the tighter of the two, so no standoff screen is applied to the loops and the reason is measured rather than assumed.

What is left, and what it is not

Every loop reads 0.9622 of the described flux while the archive's own model reads 1.0074 of its own — a 4.5% disagreement between the two forward models, carried by the set rather than by any channel. Two candidate explanations are tested and one is excluded.

  • Induced coil-case current is not it. The described prediction carries the windings and nothing else, while the archive's 101-circuit model carries the cases and the vessel, and an induced current opposes the coil that drove it — so the deficit should grow with the case current the store measured on the same pulse. It does not. Over 304 pulses the six carrying case current below 2% of their coil read 0.951, no higher than the 298 above 10%, which read 0.959.
  • A per-channel calibration is not it either, which is the point of the verdict table: the departure is common, and what is left after removing it is 1.9%.
  • What remains is a term in the described forward model — turn counts, winding cross-section, or a conductor the description does not carry. It is family-shaped at P2, where six loops sit 3.4–4.5% above the set on this route and within 0.6% on the archive's. That is a handoff to the misfit programme rather than a calibration question, and it does not block admitting the loops: it is absorbed by the same forward model that already predicts the probes.

What this hands on

  • To the inversion: 18 loop channels are admissible as published, with no gain applied and no convention factor. Seventeen of them reach a described sensor; fl_p6u_1 is measurable but not describable until the catalog carries a P6-upper loop.
  • To the open decision on where a convention factor is corrected: there is no factor. The read path and the machine-description artifact would each apply 1.0, so the decision has nothing to apply and can be closed by evidence rather than by choosing a site. Nothing in this node implements either option.
  • To the description's owner: the repair is landed and tested but deliberately not published. physical_snapshot serves the loops as the level-2 catalog printed them unless asked for place_loops, which every measurement above asks for and the packaged registry does not. That default is not caution, it is the invariant the repo already holds: this payload's hash is the identity consumers pin by value, so a reader that quietly built a different payload would make the next census read a hardware reconfiguration out of archived sources that cannot change. Regenerating mast_geometry.json moves that identity and propagates to the published artifact tag and to every consumer resolving through it, so it is a republication with its own node, as the last digest change had. Until it runs the production solve-input map still joins 39 channels, and flipping the default is the one-word part of that node.
  • To the correction ladder: the verdicts are banked as schema-shaped records at ~/.cache/nova-mast/flux-loop-calibration/loop_corrections.yaml — one convention record, 28 gain records (18 recorded, 10 withheld) and three exclusions — rather than merged into nova/calibrate/corrections/mast/magnetics.yaml, whose set_version is a coordinated bump.

Caveats

  • The cohort is the 306 sustained plasma-free pulses of the calibration cohort, sampled at every fourth acquisition sample. 7 063 of 7 283 per-pulse fits clear the shape-agreement floor; the 220 that do not are pulses on which a loop saw nothing it could distinguish.
  • The turn multipliers entering the prediction are the archive's published integers where it states them and the vacuum response fit's own multiplier otherwise, with unity for the two channels the archive publishes as ampere turns. The solenoid's multiplier is the fitted one and is the least well determined of the thirteen.
  • Loop positions are read at the described loop where the join serves one and at the reconstruction's own position otherwise. The two differ by up to 9 mm on the served channels, which is second order for a gain and is not separated here.
  • The archive's forward prediction is efm/silop_c on the reconstruction's own coarse clock, a couple of hundred slices per pulse against the acquisition's tens of thousands. It is an independent route, not a more accurate one.
  • Whether fl_p5l_3's described position or the reconstruction's is right is argued from each table's internal consistency, not measured — the channel publishes no data on any sampled shot, so no fit can arbitrate it.
