Harmonic maps of the vacuum misfit: what the array determines, and where it stops

The localization record scored one filament at a time against the surviving P4/P5 misfit. This record replaces that scan with a representation: in the vacuum region the misfit flux solves the homogeneous Grad–Shafranov operator exactly, so it expands in toroidal harmonics, and the expansion is a map everywhere rather than a score at candidate points. Two things had to be built first — constraint sets that mix probe field projections with calibrated flux loops under per-channel noise, and a fit that carries the described coil currents as unknowns instead of projecting them out.

The headline is not the map. It is that two of the three localisers the section set out to use do not work, and both fail for reasons the data itself shows — while the third does work and puts the strongest survivor below the P4 lower winding, with a resolution that independently reproduces a bound the conductor hunt reached by a different route.

The constraint sets

Each drive group carries 91–93 rows: 74–76 pickup probes as signed field projections in tesla per ampere-turn (obr17 excluded as an established instrument fault), and 17 flux loops as linked flux in weber per ampere-turn. The loop couplings are new here and are built by the probe reduction channel for channel — each loop's flux residual against the described windings regressed jointly on every substantially active ampere-turn waveform on the shot, the coefficient on the P4/P5 regressor kept — over the same 41 (shot, group) rows the probe bank holds, so the two blocks align and a resampling draw can take a whole shot.

Only the 18 loop channels the gain adjudication admits as published are used, and no per-channel gain and no convention factor is applied, because that record measured there is none to apply. All 18 are placed — 17 at a described loop position, with fl_p6u_1 read at its own measured position because the level-2 catalogue describes no P6-upper loop at all — and 17 clear the requirement that at least two of a group's shots served the channel, fl_cc10 being the one that does not. Of those 17, six sit on the centre column and eleven on the outer coils, including both P4-lower loops recovered by the position repair, which is why the loop block reaches exactly where the probe array is weakest.

The two classes arrive in different units and are made commensurable by whitening each row with its own noise: the robust scatter of that channel's per-shot couplings about their median, divided by the root of the shot count and floored at the channel's own quiet-time scatter from the sensor-floor record propagated through the drive span. Median signal-to-noise per row is 10.7 (P4 lower), 15.4 (P5 lower), 5.4 and 5.2 on the upper groups. Degree is chosen on held-out channels and lands at 6, 7, 4 and 1.

Two departures the row set forced. The held-out score is a median absolute whitened error, not a root-mean-square: fl_p2l_4 is mounted on the P2 lower coil and alone pins that coil's described column, so the fold that holds it out leaves its own prediction free to run to twenty times the data and a squared score then measures which fold got the leverage row rather than which degree fits. And the design is whitened before the selection rather than weighted inside it, because an unweighted score over a set mixing tesla with weber answers a question about unit choice.

The maps

The reconciled fit reaches 0.930 of the whitened power on P4 lower, 0.771 on P5 lower and 0.766 on P4 upper. What it does not reach matters as much: the whitened residual stays at 7.5–28.8 standard deviations, so a great deal of structure remains that no axisymmetric expansion of this degree represents. Some of that is the non-axisymmetric residue the plan expects to bound; some is the shot-to-shot systematics the pooled standard error does not cover.

The largest described-coil corrections the joint fit takes up are on the P2 pair — +0.222 and −0.192 ampere-turns per ampere-turn of P4 lower drive, and −0.736 and +0.494 on P5 lower. These are the coil columns absorbing what they can of the misfit, not a claim about P2; they are reported because a fit that was not allowed to move them would have pushed the same content into the harmonic block.

Why the map difference is not a localiser

Contouring the disagreement between the two expansions and reading its peak was the section's stated route to the enclosed current, and taken at face value it gives a confident answer: for P4 lower the peak sits at (1.540, −0.784) at 13.9 resampling sigma, with 34% of the supported plane above three sigma — a spot between the P4 and P5 lower windings that would have read as a find. It is an artefact. The two families differ most next to their own singular sets, and the inner family's singular set is the focal circle, so the peak is a property of where the expansion was centred. Moving the circle moves the peak with it. What the sweep establishes is that the peak's position carries no information about the machine; its significance was measured only at the placement used, where it is high.

This is worth recording rather than quietly dropping. The failure mode is not that the measurement was too noisy; it is that a quantity with an obvious physical reading — where two expansions cannot be reconciled — is dominated by basis behaviour, and its confidence band does not warn about it, because every resampling draw reproduces the same artefact.

What does localise, and how well

The working localiser compares the two families fitted about the same circle and scans the circle. It is a comparison of two source hypotheses differing only in which side of the circle the current is on, and unlike the map difference it is a statement about the data's reach rather than about the basis: at a placement far from any current, both families fit equally and the contrast is zero. It is also strictly more general than the earlier filament scan — at degree n the inner family represents any current distribution near the circle, not one ring.

For P4 lower the half-maximum region is sharply bounded above at Z = −0.95 — hard against the P4 lower winding, whose centroid is (1.498, −1.099) — and open below. In radius it is not bounded at all: the region spans the entire 0.60–2.40 m of the scan. So the measurement says the strongest survivor is produced by current below the P4 lower coil and not by anything in the upper half or on the centre column, and says nothing about its radius. Shot resampling puts the peak at R 1.89 ± 0.21, Z −0.88 ± 0.89.

That extent is a resolution limit, and it agrees with a bound reached independently: the conductor hunt concluded the array cannot localise a ring to better than 1.5–2.3 m. Two different estimators, one scanning conductor candidates and one scanning the expansion's focal circle, return the same scale. The P4/P5 misfit is not a localisation problem this array can close.

The upper groups behave differently and consistently with their weaker patterns: P4 upper's contrast peaks at 0.20 in a compact region on its own winding, and P5 upper's at 0.08 with the half-maximum region covering a quarter of the scanned plane — which is to say, not localised.

The right panel is the one that decides the loop question. The ring sites are drawn from the established machine candidate catalogue, and the extended paths tie all of their signed ring constituents to one amplitude. Current is first fitted as source ampere-turn per drive ampere-turn and then multiplied by 23 turns, so a reported value of one is one whole supply current. The near-perfect lower-group fits are therefore not source identifications: P4 lower, for example, asks the case upper-wall and pack upper-face rings for +842 and −628 supply currents. P5 lower asks its winding-interior ring for +1012. No candidate is physically admissible.

The lower bound passed to a three-dimensional account is the power outside the selected harmonic representation: 2.8% (P4 lower), 36.2% (P4 pair), 38.4% (P4 upper), 2.0% (P5 lower), 0.06% (P5 pair) and 42.9% (P5 upper). These are not claims that every residual sample is a toroidal field: pooled-shot systematics remain in the same number. The direct raw-data fit is the stronger physical rejection, leaving 21.5–86.0% even after the four source currents and all described coil currents are free.

A diametric pair averages a mode with its value at φ + π: all odd modes cancel exactly and all even modes survive. For a uniform 20° feeder footprint the table gives the top-hat Fourier coefficient relative to n = 0. Thus a healthy sum can still leak 98.0% of n = 2, 92.1% of n = 4 and 82.7% of n = 6. Without a measured toroidal width for a localised P4 tail, the honest source-independent upper bound is 100% for every even mode. A failed member restores the odd screen: n = 1, 3 and 5 retain 99.5%, 95.5% and 87.8% for that same feeder footprint, with a 100% width-free upper bound.

The known pair-failure channels are obr17, obv03, obr05, obr18 and obv10. They are useful odd-mode screens only after their per-era gains are applied; obr17 remains excluded here for its established half gain. The correction-coil search found R2 < 0.0005, an amplitude-correlation ceiling of 2.24%, so those coils cannot account for a detected odd component. Flux loops contribute n = 0 only. The 36 saddle loops are the available n-resolving set, but no saddle data enter this constraint bank, so a unique three-dimensional attribution is beyond the present array rather than silently assigned to a conductor.

Measured verdict

The source region closes in height, geometry closes negatively, and circuit configuration supplies the first candidate identity that fits the shot cohort. The family-contrast localiser puts the strongest survivor below the P4-lower winding: its half-maximum region is bounded above at Z = −0.95 m and is unresolved in radius over the complete 0.60–2.40 m scan. This is a region, not a permanent-conductor identification.

Geometry is excluded by two independent functionals

The geometry solve validates both of its inference paths before applying them to MAST. Sensor-space recovery has at most 0.27σ bias with 93.3–95.5% coverage, yet the real optimum demands 11.1–2004 mm RMS pack motion against 0.054–2.221 mm best-mode resolutions. The separate flux-map functional has at most 0.21σ recovery bias and 93.3–95.8% coverage; applied to these reconciled maps, P4 lower, P4 upper and P5 lower demand 20.47–37.20 mm corrections, 21.1–38.2 times their best map-mode resolutions, while P5 upper is unresolved. Sensor projections and gridded flux therefore reject the same static axisymmetric geometry explanation.

The residue is a lower bound, not a three-dimensional measurement

The whitened power outside the selected harmonic representation is 2.80% on P4 lower and 2.04% on P5 lower; it rises to 36.17% on P4 pair, 38.39% on P4 upper and 42.94% on P5 upper. P5 pair is the explicit exception at 0.058%. Every value is a lower bound on what a three-dimensional account must carry and still contains pooled-shot systematics. A healthy diametric pair removes odd modes but preserves even ones; pending a measured localised-tail width, the source-independent even-n leakage bound remains 1.0 and is therefore honestly vacuous. Gain-aware failed-pair channels and the 36 saddle loops are required to separate toroidal structure from the pooled-shot term.

The tests pin the algebraic contracts and synthetic recovery, not the real-data headline values in the tables: those depend on the regenerated constraint bank at ~/.cache/nova-mast/flux-loop-inclusion/constraint_sets.npz. Those measured inputs are recorded here so changes to the bank remain visible rather than being mistaken for unit-test regressions.
