The conductor hunt, and the two limits that end it

The symmetry test excluded a described-geometry error and left one lead standing: current taking a path the description does not carry. The localization maps put that path beside the P4 coils at feeder-scale currents, and the published record named three pieces of hardware that could be it — a link board serving P3, P4 and P5, a toroidally localised P4 coil tail, and the joggles that distribute the winding's missing turn. This record tests them, together with every other conductor position the machine description can state, and finds that the lead does not close.

Two things make the test sharper than the filament scan it follows. The first is a restriction that costs nothing and buys everything: only the toroidal part of a conductor's route has any axisymmetric signature at all, so a feeder is not an arbitrary curve to be fitted but a set of rings at stated places carrying a stated current. The second is a measurement that did not exist a day ago. The flux loops were admitted as published, so the same coupling method the probes use can be run on them — and a loop bolted to a winding pack sees a conductor at that pack, which the probe array cannot.

What an axisymmetric array can and cannot be told about a feeder

A feeder bar runs radially out from a coil, turns, and leaves through a vessel port. Averaged over the toroidal angle, its radial and vertical legs become a radial current sheet, whose magnetic field is purely toroidal. No poloidal pickup coil reads a toroidal field and no flux loop links one. What survives the average is only the part of the route that runs toroidally, and a toroidal run subtending an angle carries that fraction of a turn.

Every feeder therefore reduces, for this array, to a small number of rings carrying a fraction of the supply current at the radius and height of each toroidal run. That is not a modelling convenience; it is the reason a full three-dimensional route inversion belongs to the non-axisymmetric programme and cannot be attempted here. It also fixes the amplitude scale once and for all: a conductor in series with a winding carries the supply current exactly once, so the toroidal fraction of its route cannot exceed one turn of it. One supply current is the ceiling, and on P4 and P5 one supply current is 23 ampere turns.

The flux loops, run the same way as the probes

The probe couplings were computed by regressing each channel's vacuum residual jointly on every substantially active ampere-turn waveform and keeping the coefficient on the P4/P5 regressor. That method is re-run here on the eighteen loop channels the gain fits admitted as published, over the same shots, with the same conditioning guard. It returns 41 (shot, group) rows — the same 13, 12, 7, 7 and 2 rows per group the probes have, because the guard accepts the same shots.

What the loops measure is a residual of 1–9% of the described column, negative on almost every channel, which is the common deficit the gain fits already reported and attributed to the forward model rather than to the instrument. That deficit is proportional to the driven column, so it is exactly what a bound has to be blind to: the driven family's column is projected out of both the measurement and the candidate, and only that column, because removing all thirteen from eighteen channels would leave fewer dimensions than a fit needs.

The bound is then read directly. For each candidate, the amplitude at which its predicted loop signal would equal everything the loops leave unexplained is the most current that could have been there without the loops seeing it. Where the probes want more than that, the candidate is excluded by measurement.

This is where the near-pack degeneracy breaks. A ring inside a winding pack is almost exactly what the coil's own column already is, so the probe array cannot see it at all: the fit needs −369 supply currents at the P4-lower winding interior precisely because what is left after the described span is removed is nearly nothing. The loops, twenty millimetres away, permit only 52 there — and 1.07 at the pack's outboard face, where the probes want 9.35.

The result: two limits, and what clears them

P4 lower is the case that matters and it comes out empty. It is the only target whose surviving pattern reproduces between disjoint shot halves (cosine +0.92 over 13 rows); the other groups pool 7 rows or 2. Of its 21 sites, eleven explain 10% or more, and those eleven need 3.6 to 369 supply currents while running 2.7 to 8.7 times louder at the loops than everything the loops leave unexplained. The two sites the loops are relaxed about, the pack's lower face and the case's lower wall, explain 0.5% and 0.1%. There is no conductor position in the description that both reproduces the stable survivor and could carry the current doing so.

Nothing supports it as the source, and the P4-lower null is the stronger statement.

Why the array cannot do better: the resolution limit

The unconstrained ring scan is the ceiling on this whole candidate class, and it also measures how little a peak means. Scanning 181 radii by 257 heights, the best single ring explains 60.6% of the P4-lower survivor at R = 0.19, z = −0.15, on the centre column; 46.6% of P4 upper at (1.575, +0.694) and 43.3% of P5 lower at (1.450, −1.200), reproducing the two peaks the localization record reported beside the P4 coils.

But the region explaining at least half of that peak spans 1.53 m in radius and 1.16 m in height for P4 lower, and for P5 lower it fills the scan radially at 2.25 m while spanning 2.25 m in height. A single ring is a smooth, slowly varying field pattern, and the described column space absorbs everything that distinguishes one such pattern from another over most of the plane. "Beside the P4 coils" is a peak, not a location, and no amount of further axisymmetric filament fitting will sharpen it. This is the resolution limit that ends the hunt in this direction, and it is why the remaining leverage is the flux loops' different geometric functional and the saddle loops' toroidal resolution rather than more probe fits.

The differential signature, measured rather than sampled

The localization record reported that the P5 member-current difference explains 16% and 40% of obr10's partialled residual at a consistent +279 and +293 nT/At, and handed that on as a constraint any candidate circuit must reproduce. It reported the five strongest channels only. Fitting the whole array changes the reading.

  • It is not an artefact of a nearly-collinear regressor. The member difference spans 0.25–0.62% of the pair sum, which is the first thing to suspect, but 89–100% of it survives partialling out the sum and every other active drive. The coefficients are honestly determined.
  • On P5 it reproduces in shape and not in scale, and the failure to reproduce in scale is itself decisive. The two shots agree at cosine +0.952 raw and +0.89 after the described span is removed, and the per-channel ratio between them is a tight 1.92 (interquartile 1.68 to 2.09) — one pattern, scaled. But their difference spans are 958 and 954 ampere turns, so the coefficient per ampere turn of difference is not a property of the machine. A conductor fed by one member of a series pair would give the same coefficient on both shots by construction. Whatever produces this pattern is not responding linearly to the member difference; the difference regressor is reading something that merely travels with it.
  • On P4 it does not reproduce at all. The three P4 shots give pairwise cosines of −0.78, −0.95 and +0.77 — the pooled P4 difference pattern means nothing, even though obr10 itself reads a consistent +102, +110, +113 nT/At across them.
  • obr10 is not its carrier. Pooled over P5 the coupling reaches −3 000 nT/At on centre-column channels against obr10's +286. What singled obr10 out in the earlier reading was the share of its own partialled residual, which is a statement about how quiet obr10 is, not about how loud the signature is there.
  • It is its own pattern, not the level survivor leaking. Cosines against the level survivors are −0.29, −0.19, +0.40 and +0.02.
  • No placeable conductor produces it. The best site reaches 45% at 147 supply currents; the best below ten supply currents explains 3.7%. Two orders of magnitude is not a modelling gap.

The differential signature therefore stands as a real, reproducible spatial pattern and moves out of the conductor question entirely — twice over, once because no placeable geometry reaches it below two orders of magnitude in current, and once because its amplitude per unit difference is not a constant, which no series conductor can arrange. Its shape — largest on the centre column, sign-flipping between the inboard and outboard bands — is what the toroidal-harmonic and non-axisymmetric programmes are built to read, and the factor of 1.92 between two shots with equal difference spans is the sharpest single clue in it.

What the instruments already exclude, without any fit

Edlington, Martin & Pinfold describe the magnetics hardware, and two of their facts settle candidate classes outright.

  • The coil-case path is watched. Every PF case Rogowski sits in a machined groove in a frame around the coil case, so what it links is the total toroidal current threading that frame; the paper states that induced case currents are obtained by subtracting the feeder bar current from it. That is the pack-total identity the solve-input map now carries as mechanism — the identity is the instrument's definition, not a coincidence. It also means a galvanic current diverted through a coil case would be measured, and the measurement says it is 0.01–0.35% of the coil at flat-top and rate-like rather than level-like. The case path is excluded by the instrument that was built to watch it.
  • The P4/P5 feed path is the one nobody watched. Halo-current detectors sit around each of the six support legs of the P2 and P3 coils, and the paper says the bellows feeds for those coils are monitored too. P4 and P5 have no such monitor. The one galvanic route this record cannot bound with a dedicated instrument is precisely the one at the coils the misfit points to — which is a reason the question stayed open for the machine's life, and a reason the answer will not come from the archive's own current channels.

Verdict

No conductor geometry that the machine description can place reaches the surviving misfit. On P4 lower, the only target with a reproducible pattern, nothing clears both limits: what explains needs current no conductor carries, and what could carry the current explains nothing. Eight of 105 candidates clear both limits elsewhere, the best being a conductor at the P5-lower pack outboard face at 0.98 supply currents explaining 14.4% [1.9, 15.2] — a partial account of one group, not a source.

The reason is measured rather than argued: the array cannot localise a ring to better than one and a half to two and a quarter metres, so a candidate hunt conducted with axisymmetric filaments against this probe set has reached its resolution. The two instruments that are not limited this way are the flux loops, which are used here for the first time and which do the work of excluding every near-pack candidate, and the 36 saddle loops, which are mounted around the very ports the feeder bars penetrate and have not been touched.

Caveats

  • The P4-upper, P5-upper and P5-pair groups pool 7, 7 and 2 shot rows. Their bootstrap intervals are correspondingly wide — several reach from under 1% to over 50% — and the two-row pair group's interval is not a confidence statement at all, since resampling two rows mostly returns those two rows. P4 lower (13 rows) is the only group to build on alone.
  • The loop bound compares a candidate against everything the loops leave unexplained after the driven column is removed. That is a bound on a candidate acting alone; two candidates whose loop signals cancel are not excluded by it.
  • The pair group is scored against the sites belonging to P5 lower and against the structures every drive can reach, because a pair drive has no single member to hang a coil-local site on. A conductor fed by the pair from the upper member's side is therefore represented only by its mirror.
  • Candidate rings are placed at outline bounds and centroids. A conductor a few centimetres from where its outline puts it changes the loop prediction more than the probe prediction, because the loops are the near-field instrument — the same proximity that gives them their leverage.
  • Only the axisymmetric content of any route is tested. A conductor whose toroidal runs cancel in the n = 0 average is invisible to everything here and is not excluded by anything in this record.
  • The unconstrained scan removes the described-span component of every candidate column, so a conductor the coil set can nearly reproduce scores near zero wherever it sits. The maps and the ceiling bound what is visible in the survivor.
  • The member-difference result rests on two usable P5 shots and three P4 shots. The P5 shape agreement is a single pairwise cosine.

Reproduction

Inputs: the banked probe couplings and localization at ~/.cache/nova-mast/misfit-localization/, the loop verdicts at ~/.cache/nova-mast/flux-loop-calibration/loop_verdicts.json, the level-1 store at /work/projects/imas_gpu/mast/level1/shots, and the geometry registry at shot 21978 with the flux loops placed. Hardware facts: Edlington, Martin & Pinfold, Rev. Sci. Instrum. 72, 421 (2001), retrieved to an untracked local path; Kirk et al., arXiv:1312.6507.

Both candidate kernels are checked against the forward model rather than trusted (kernel_check.py). A single ring at a winding's centroid must reproduce that winding's own polygon-kernel column per ampere turn wherever the finite cross-section stops mattering, and it does: over all four members the ratio is 0.9995 to 1.0000 at every one of the eighteen loops, and 0.9995 to 1.0000 at the 54 to 68 probes standing more than 0.8 m off, with interquartile widths under 0.0013. The amplitudes quoted throughout are therefore turns of the supply current in the candidate's toroidal run, on the scale the drive map uses.

What this hands on

  • To the non-axisymmetric programme: the feeder route question in full, with a geometric handle it did not have — the three saddle-loop bands are mounted around the diagnostic ports the feeder bars pass through, at twelve sectors of 20° each, so the sensors that resolve toroidal structure surround the penetrations in question. The member-difference pattern goes with it: real, machine-wide, centre-column dominated, and unreachable by any axisymmetric conductor under 107 supply currents.
  • To the harmonics programme: the resolution limit is now quantified, so the value of loop and saddle constraints can be stated against a number rather than an expectation.
  • To the localization record: two corrections. Its P4-member asymmetry row still carries the retracted 17.3 mm reading; and its obr10 differential row names obr10 as the carrier of a signature that is machine-wide and largest elsewhere.
  • To the flux-loop programme: the loop couplings are banked per drive group and are a second use for the admitted channels beyond the inversion — they are the near-field instrument for any conductor question at a winding pack.
  • To whoever owns the loop-position republication: every loop number in this record is computed with the P4-lower block restored, because the two channels that publish on every shot are P4 lower's and the published coordinates put them beside P3 lower. The packaged registry still serves the copied positions by default, so a consumer reproducing this bound from the published payload would compute it at the wrong place for the two loops nearest the coils in question. That is a second consumer for the repair, alongside the join count.
