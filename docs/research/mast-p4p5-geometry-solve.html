No credible P4/P5 geometry correction is resolvable at this array

Bounded negative result. The exact-Jacobian sensor-space solve converges reproducibly, but its optimum is not a physical coil correction. It demands RMS pack motions of 11.1–2004 mm where the published best-mode resolutions are 0.054–2.221 mm and Kirk's measured case offsets are 0.6–13.1 mm. Removing the probes inside two pack widths then leaves only −35.7% to +0.9% reduction of the independently banked symmetry survivor: three members collapse to essentially zero or worsen, and P5 lower reverses strongly. The array therefore supports no registry geometry change. The surviving signal remains evidence for current or sensor structure that a static axisymmetric pack displacement cannot carry.

The solve that was run

Each member drives the described 23-cell winding lattice. The parameters are rigid R/Z translation plus dilation, stretch, shear and tilt; per-turn and per-vertex freedoms stay frozen. The residual is whitened by that drive group's measured probe floors, all admitted probes remain in the optimisation, and the thirteen described coil-response columns are fitted as nuisance current freedoms. The solver works in the deformation Gram metric, uses exact geometry Jacobians, and applies damped Gauss–Newton steps only to singular directions whose local one-noise displacement is at most 10 mm. Kirk magnitudes were projected into the admitted modes as ±R and ±Z seeds; all four seeds return the same solution to within 0.00022 µm RMS.

The measured-floor recovery ladder validates the inference machinery

For every member and each rigid/affine class, 128 random perturbations were drawn at each of 0.5, 1, 3 and 10 mm RMS inside the admitted mode space. Exact local-model sensor data were generated and independent normal noise was added at every channel's measured floor. Across 4,096 recoveries, maximum mean bias is 0.27 of the predicted one-noise resolution, empirical/predicted resolution ratios span 0.946–1.099, and nominal 95% coverage spans 93.3–95.5%. The flux-map functional passes the same recovery test

The second data functional accepts a two-dimensional plasma-subtracted flux target, one uncertainty per cell and an optional support mask. Predicted maps and their exact geometry Jacobians keep their grid shape at the public boundary; supported cells are flattened only before entering the existing damped Gauss–Newton solver. The metric decomposition, resolvable-mode truncation and nuisance projection are therefore the same implementation as the sensor solve, not a parallel optimiser. For these maps the described drive current stays fixed and only the arbitrary constant flux gauge is projected as a nuisance.

The map ladder uses the reconciled archive's shot-resampling uncertainty and support on a 26×26 exact-node sampling of its 205×205 published grid. Taking every eighth node avoids counting strongly correlated adjacent pixels as independent; it does not interpolate or alter the cell values. The same four amplitudes and 128 draws per rung give another 4,096 recoveries. Maximum mean bias is 0.21σ, empirical/predicted resolution ratios span 0.947–1.068, and normal 95% coverage spans 93.3–95.8%. On the common terms requested, that improves on the sensor ladder's 0.27σ maximum bias and matches its 93.3–95.5% coverage range.

It is not a large correction with high confidence; it is the optimiser proving that no member of the admitted geometry family represents the data.

Bound and disposition

  • Array bound: after current marginalisation and the near-field robustness requirement, the best resolvable affine directions span 54 µm (P5 lower) to 2.221 mm (P4 upper). No coherent P4/P5 correction appears above those floors and inside the 0.6–13.1 mm surveyed case-offset envelope.
  • Geometry disposition: keep the described 23-cell lattices and the established chamfer sides; do not issue a machine-description correction from this solve.
  • Residual disposition: the all-probe extrema are the already-banked near-field trap. The remaining stable survivor stays with unmodelled current paths or sensor-side structure, not axisymmetric rigid or affine pack geometry.
