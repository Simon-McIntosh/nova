Where the P4 numbers came from

Three questions were open. Which source each of the two circulating P4 asymmetry readings came from; whether the asymmetry the model actually carries is a machine feature or a description error; and why the described and published P4 flux loops barely overlap. All three are now answered from the sources on this host, and none of the answers needed a fit.

The two readings are one source and two arithmetics. Both are the registry's own P4 outlines. The area centroid of those outlines puts the upper member 4.6 mm further from the midplane; the mean of the outline's coordinate ring puts the lower member 17.2 mm further out. The ring mean is not a centroid — a vertex average of a chamfered hexagon misses the area centroid by 20 mm on its own — and the closed ring repeats its start vertex, which the two members do not share: P4 lower's ring starts on a far-side chamfer corner, P4 upper's on the midplane-side corner. That single repeated vertex is what flips the sign of the pair difference and inflates it. The plan's §1 figures should be retired; §2.1's are the outlines' real centroids.

The placement asymmetry is not adjudicable as an error, and the footprint difference is. P5's two members carry byte-identical winding lattices offset 8.7 mm in |z|; P4's main block is offset 3.2 mm. Those magnitudes sit inside the as-built case offsets Kirk measured (2.9 / 6.6 / 0.6 / 13.1 mm), they are five-decimal survey-like numbers, and the symmetry scan already measured that the data prefers them to a mirrored geometry. The +5.1% P4 footprint difference is a different animal: it reduces to one parameter — a 27 mm main-row turn pitch on P4 upper where P4 lower, P5 lower and P5 upper all carry 26 mm — with conductor size, turn count, row pitch and chamfer side identical on all four and both packs centred on the same radius. That is a per-member digitisation of one drawing, not a manufactured difference, and correcting it will not move the misfit.

The P4-lower loop block is a copy. The level-2 catalog names its 44 loops, and its four FL_P4L entries hold the four FL_P3L positions bit for bit, on every shot sampled across the campaign. The reconstruction's own loop table carries physically sensible P4-lower positions in the same slots. So the two P4-lower channels that publish data on every shot land 0.37–0.44 m from anything the description has, and the position join refuses them — correctly, but for a reason nobody had named.

Reading one: the ring-closure artifact

The registry authors each active component as a single polygon — the convex hull of the level-2 winding cells — stored as WKB. A polygon's exterior coordinate list is a closed ring: the first vertex is repeated as the last, so a seven-entry list describes a hexagon. Averaging that list therefore weights one corner twice, and for a seven-entry ring the displacement is exactly one seventh of the way from the vertex mean toward the repeated corner. P4 lower's ring starts at (1.423, −1.17451), the chamfered corner furthest from the midplane; P4 upper's starts at (1.4205, +1.02669), the corner nearest it. The two biases therefore point in opposite directions:

Two separate faults compound here and it is worth keeping them apart. A vertex mean is not a centroid for any polygon that is not vertex-regular: on the lower member it already sits 20 mm below the area centroid, because the chamfer contributes two vertices to a corner that carries little area. Repeating the start vertex then adds 8 mm on the lower member and subtracts 14 mm on the upper, and it is that second, orientation-dependent step that reverses which member looks displaced. The registry's own numbers were never wrong; the arithmetic applied to them was.

Reading two: what the model actually carries, and where it came from

The level-2 catalog publishes each active component as a list of rectangular winding cells (<member>_r, _z, _width, _height), and the registry hulls are built from exactly those. The level-1 reconstruction's own 938-element coil table is not an independent second source: its circuits 8–11 reproduce the four members' cell bounds and element means digit for digit. So one table stands behind every reading above.

Every member is three full rows of six turns plus a five-turn cross-over row on the max-|z| side, the cross-over row itself pitched at 25 mm on all four. The cross-over row's apparent radial skew — 1 mm per column on three members, 2 mm on P4 upper — is not an independent difference: it is the main-row pitch minus the cross-over pitch, so it doubles because the pitch changed. The whole P4 footprint asymmetry is one number.

Three arguments make that number a description artifact rather than a machine feature. It would require the inter-turn radial gap of one member to be 3 mm where its three siblings are 2 mm, while the conductor, the axial pitch and the turn count stayed identical and the pack stayed centred on the same radius — that is not how a winding differs from its twin. Kirk (arXiv:1312.6507) states the as-built survey fixed the coil case position and explicitly not the copper inside it, so no metrology backs a copper shape difference. And in the same reconstruction table the P4 and P5 coil-case circuits sit at exactly ±1.100000 and ±0.500000 — round nominal values — while the copper carries five-decimal ones; the case model is nominal and the winding model is not, which is the opposite of what a metrology-driven asymmetry would look like.

The placement asymmetry does not fall to the same argument. P5's members are the same lattice moved 8.68 mm; P4's main block is moved 3.18 mm with the cross-over split adding the rest. Those are inside the envelope of Kirk's Table-1 as-built case offsets (2.9 mm at P4 upper, 6.6 mm at P4 lower, 0.6 mm at P5 upper, 13.1 mm at P5 lower — an up-down difference of up to 9.5 mm at P4 and 13.7 mm at P5), and the symmetry scan measured that the P4 upper survivor prefers the described asymmetry to a mirrored geometry by −0.64 of its power. Verdict: no correction is warranted at the placement, the footprint difference is a model artifact worth recording but not worth chasing — the same scan measured footprint equalisation at +0.1% of the survivor, and only at a gain 126× the variant's own amplitude.

The P4 flux loops: a duplicated block, not a mystery

The level-2 catalog names each of its 44 described loops in flux_loop_geometry_channel. Entries 20–23, named FL_P4L_1 through FL_P4L_4, hold the same four coordinates as entries 12–15, FL_P3L_1 through FL_P3L_4 — bit-identical, on every level-2 shot sampled from 11766 to 28770. The reconstruction's loop table carries P4-lower positions in those slots that are near-mirrors of the described P4-upper set (0.4–5.4 mm in r, 3.6–13.6 mm in |z|), so the correct positions exist on this host; the geometry catalog simply does not have them.

Read down the last two columns: of the eight described P4 loops exactly one — fl_p4u_4 — both has a correct position and carries data. Three more have correct positions and were never published; two have wrong positions and are published on every shot; two have wrong positions and no data. That is the disjointness the plan noticed, with its mechanism named.

Three consequences follow in the published description, all measured against it rather than inferred. Four positions are each carried by two described loops — (1.0354, −1.1036), (1.0354, −1.0831), (1.1630, −1.1036), (1.1630, −1.0826) — so eight described loops occupy four points. Seven reconstruction channels reach no described loop at the registry's 15 mm tolerance: all four fl_p4l_*, plus fl_p5l_3 and both fl_p6u_*, the latter because the level-2 catalog carries no P6-upper loops at all. And five described loops are never reached by any channel. The join serves 39 of 46. Nothing here needs the tolerance loosened: a 15 mm threshold and a 0.44 m error are not in the same conversation.

The pack-total channels

Six coil sets publish a third current channel beside their conductor-current and ampere-turn ones. It is their sum with the case: pXY_current equals pXY_coil_current plus pXY_case_current, sample for sample, to the last bit a 32-bit float carries.

Normalisation matters and the two conventions must not be confused. Scaled by the summed terms the worst case over all 4578 pairs is 1.094×10⁻⁷, just inside a float's own resolution of 1.19×10⁻⁷, with a median of 9.7×10⁻⁹. Scaled by the total the worst case is 2.39×10⁻⁵, but that number is an artifact of one shot where the case opposes the coil and the total collapses to 0.6 kA while its two terms stand at 286 kA each: dividing by what survives a cancellation measures the cancellation. The plan's earlier 6.5×10⁻⁶ over 336 pairs is the total-normalised convention on a smaller sample, and it is consistent with this one. The same identity holds at set level for the two-pack P2 sets — p2Xl_current equals the two packs' coil channels plus the shared case channel, worst 8.8×10⁻⁶ over 1526 pairs.

The six channels are explained but they are not servable, and the measurement is what refuses them. A source-map row converts one channel to one dictionary field by a fixed factor. The pack total's two terms sit in different containers — pf_active/coil for the copper, pf_passive/loop for the enclosure — and the case current is induced, not driven, so the ratio between them is not a constant to fold into a factor: it swings by 0.21 of the coil current at P4 and by 1.21 at P3 within a single shot. The channel is therefore a restatement of two quantities the map already reaches, and nova/imas/mast_solve_inputs.py now says exactly that: the refusal names both terms, states the mechanism, and derives which sets get a row from the drive map rather than from a written list, so a set gains or loses one with its measured case. What has changed is that no excitation channel in the map is refused as unexplained any more.

What this hands on

  • To the flux-loop calibration plan: the four described FL_P4L positions are wrong and the replacement exists in the reconstruction's own loop table at (1.5984, −1.0444), (1.5984, −1.0694), (1.4005, −1.1594), (1.4005, −1.1440). Correcting them turns two channels published on every shot from unusable into usable, and it removes four duplicate described positions. The correction belongs to the level-2 read in the registry recovery, not to a tolerance: the join is refusing a 0.44 m error, which is the right behaviour. Note also that fl_p5l_3 is a disagreement in the other direction — the description's (1.5458, −0.5322) is mirror-consistent with P5 upper and the reconstruction's (1.7463, −0.5322) is not — and that no P6-upper loop is described at all.
  • To the owning provenance plan: §1's asymmetry figures are an arithmetic artifact and should be replaced by the area centroids; the geometry lead stays closed. The footprint difference is real in the description and unreal in the machine, and it is worth 0.1% of the survivor, so no geometry correction is on the critical path. The remaining explanation is unchanged: current on a path that is not the described winding.
  • To any consumer of the registry outlines: take the polygon's centroid, never the mean of its coordinate ring. The ring is closed, and on these chamfered hulls the two answers differ by up to 28 mm and can differ in sign between two members of a pair.
  • Left alone deliberately: the P2 set-level channels stay blocked on their unsourced pack interconnection. The set-level identity measured above does weaken the wording of that refusal — the archive does publish the two packs' currents separately — so it is a candidate correction, but the unmet condition it names is still unmet and changing it belongs with whoever owns the P2 interconnection question. The channel-drive record's "three kinds of current channel" also predates the pack total and now undercounts by one.

Caveats

  • The as-built comparison rests on Kirk's Table 1 as quoted in the owning plan; the paper itself is not held locally, so the envelope argument is only as good as that quotation. The argument it supports is a negative one — the placement asymmetry cannot be called an error — which does not turn on the exact millimetres.
  • Whether the 26 mm or the 27 mm turn pitch is the true one is not settled here. Three members against one is a majority, not a drawing, and no winding drawing was available on this host to arbitrate.
  • The loop comparison uses the reconstruction setup at one shot and the archive channel census at a stride of 40 over the store (383 readable shots of 428 sampled). The duplicated-block check ran on 29 level-2 shots spanning 11766–28770.
  • The pack-total scan sampled every twentieth store (856 shots, 93 unreadable) and covers whole shots rather than the sustained windows the response fits use; nothing about the identity depends on the window.
