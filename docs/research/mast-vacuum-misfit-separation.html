The vacuum misfit is two faults, not one

The closure record left a single unexplained quantity: six channels carrying 56.6% of the vacuum misfit, each 91–253× its own noise floor, on both machine descriptions. This investigation separates that quantity. It is two faults with different physics, different locations, and no measurable coupling between them — one is an instrument error and one is a missing conductor. Neither is a modelling approximation, and neither will move by tuning the solver.

Every number below comes from the banked arrays under ~/.cache/nova-mast/misfit-hunt/; nothing was re-derived for this document, and each figure caption names the file it was drawn from. Read-only investigation across 406 shots for the regression arms and 351 inverted shots for the current solve.

The machine as the probe array sees it

Thirteen active coil families, twenty-four case plates, and seventy-eight probes with their sensitive axes. Thirty of the seventy-eight probes sit in a narrow outboard band spanning P5 upper to P5 lower, and they include every one of the six channels carrying the misfit — the region where both faults live, and where the array's geometric leverage on the P4/P5 currents is concentrated.

Fault one — obr17 is a probe gain of one half

Regressing each channel's vacuum residual on the described per-family field columns (not on the currents) returns a fractional error on each column's weight. For obr17 that error is −0.50, and it is the same −0.50 whichever coil is supplying the field and however large the field is. That is the definition of an instrument gain error, and it is the one thing a geometry correction cannot be.

Four independent routes reach the same number:

  1. The multivariate field-column fit. A single rigid factor accounts for 99.88% of obr17's residual variance on sustained single-coil shots (97.78% on symmetric pairs, 94.06% on pulsed, 98.37% at the gate shots), holding across the 765× amplitude range.
  2. The independently fitted channel scale. 0.5011, fitted by the sensor adjudication before this investigation existed, and never read by the residual gate.
  3. The free current inversion. Solving all thirteen coil currents without reading any drive map halves obr17's residual (7.2 → 3.1 mT) — the partial absorption a gain error permits — and what remains is still linear in the driving coil at R² 0.965.
  4. Source independence. obr17 carries the largest single share of the pooled misfit on both descriptions (22.6% artifact, 25.0% incumbent) at 206× and 210× its own floor. A defect present in two independently authored descriptions is not in either description.

Fault two — a field near P4/P5 that the description cannot produce

The outboard verticals behave in the opposite way. Their residual is not a rescaling of the described field; a substantial part of it lies outside the span of every described coil column, and it does not go away when the currents are set free.

What survives the free inversion stays linear in the P4/P5 drives at a median R² of 0.85 (0.02–0.97 across shots) — so it is a real magnetostatic field proportional to those currents, not noise and not a numerical artefact. It is level-following, not rate-following, which excludes every inductive mechanism. And it is independent of the error-field circuits: the two error-field levels explain obv06's residual at R² 0.00039 and 0.00047, and obv14's at 0.00039 and 0.00048.

The parity argument, corrected

An earlier reading took an antisymmetric residual at mirrored radial probes as evidence for an antisymmetric — that is, mis-wired anti-series — current. That inference is backwards. A pair of up-down symmetric axisymmetric currents makes the poloidal flux function even in Z, so BR = −∂ZAφ is odd and BZ is even. Radial pairs must therefore read antisymmetric and vertical pairs must read symmetric for the same symmetric source. Both hold in the data, so the source current is up-down symmetric and the anti-series hypothesis is refuted rather than supported.

The vacuum flux the description actually produces

Eleven shots each drive one identifiable coil family in sustained fashion, giving a clean read of that family's field at the probe array. These are the field patterns the residual is measured against.

The two pulsed shots are the rate-rich cases, and they are the direct visual counterpart of the level-versus-rate result.

The one substantive finding here is the P4 pair asymmetry. Both members are published at 23 ampere-turns per ampere and both are rank-converged with spreads of 1.6% and 2.6% — yet they invert 26.5% apart (11.2% apart on the arm that applies no calibration scales). The published circuit relations all have counterexamples

Every up-down and inner-outer relation in the published circuit description — each asserting that two members carry the same current — is contradicted by archive shots in which one member is driven and its partner is at rest.

What removing obr17 does to the arithmetic

obr17 is a shared defect, so removing it improves both arms — and it improves the incumbent slightly more, because the incumbent handles that channel slightly worse. The consequence is counter-intuitive and worth stating plainly: correcting the largest single piece of the absolute misfit makes the cutover comparison worse, not better.

A single rigid factor removes 88.3% of obr17's residual amplitude on the artifact arm and 88.5% on the incumbent, leaving 11.7% and 11.5% respectively. obr17's own share is 22.6% and 25.0% of the pooled misfit at 206× and 210× its floor. These are relative changes on a mean-square pooling and are not the gate metric, which is a median of medians and cannot be rebuilt from per-channel shares — the absolute numbers here are not comparable to a stamp. Evidence: obr17_arithmetic.json, computed on the banked channel-gap decomposition with no solve re-run.

This turns the obr17 finding into a decision rather than a fix. Scoring obr17 through its fitted gain is defensible as instrument calibration and is the physically correct thing to do; it also costs 0.010 on the very comparison the cutover is judged by. That trade belongs to the owner of the gate, not to this investigation.

Machine-description artifact inventory, and the publication hazard

Four revisions of the MAST machine description sit on disk. They are four distinct semantic identities and four distinct manifest digests — and they all compute the same registry tag.

All four hold the same six IDS (magnetics, pf_active, pf_passive, tf, wall, master) with byte-identical payload sizes but differing SHA-256, because HDF5 write-time metadata differs even when logical content matches. A second physical family exists at dd-4.1.1-physical-76cf833561e602a7.

The hazard. The tag is derived as dd-{dd_version}-physical-{physical_digest}, and the physical digest is keyed on logical payload rather than on semantic content. Pushing these four revisions to one registry repository would therefore have each silently overwrite the last, and the tag a consumer pulled would not identify what they received. The caches are also not an OCI layout — files live under sha256/<manifest-digest>/ with no oci-layout marker, no index.json, and no blob store — and no push code exists; the reference helper formats a tag string without contacting a registry. Nothing has been overwritten, because nothing has been pushed.

What a publishable tag needs. It must carry the semantic identity, which is precisely the field that distinguishes these four and which the current tag omits. The in-repo precedent is the benchmark push, which is commit-keyed (<benchmark-name>-<commit-sha>) — the opposite convention, and one that would have distinguished all four revisions correctly. A tag carrying either the semantic identity or the authoring commit resolves this; the physical digest alone does not. Evidence: ids_inventory.json.

Named next targets

  1. P4 member geometry or current distribution. The two P4 windings are published identical and invert 26.5% apart with 1.6–2.6% spread and full rank convergence. Either their geometry differs from the description or the current is distributed differently within the pack. This is the single most specific lead, it is local to exactly the right region, and it is testable against the as-built record rather than against the magnetics.
  2. A feeder, busbar, or structural conductor local to P4/P5, absent from both descriptions. The surviving residual is up-down symmetric, level-following, linear in the P4/P5 drives, orthogonal to every described column, and immune to a free current solve. A conductor carrying current proportional to the P4/P5 drives and not present in either description satisfies all five constraints at once; nothing else found so far satisfies more than three.
  3. The obr17 gate-calibration decision. Whether to score obr17 through its fitted 0.5011 gain. Physically correct, arithmetically costly to the cutover comparison, and entirely the gate owner's call. Recommendation: apply it, and re-baseline the cutover budget against the corrected pooling — carrying a known instrument error to protect a comparison is the worse of the two positions.

Caveats that bound every number above

  • The gate metric is a median of medians and is not reconstructible from per-channel mean-square shares. Only relative changes on a mean-square pooling are quoted; no absolute number here is a stamp value.
  • Single-regressor R² ranks by correlation with the residual and is biased toward the highest-amplitude supply on a shot. The multivariate field-column fit is the unbiased reading and is the one every conclusion rests on.
  • Case-current channels are near-proxies for the coil currents that induce them, so a case channel ranking first in a single-regressor table does not separate case from coil.
  • The six gate shots contribute plasma-free windows only, where fields are about ten times smaller than in the scored slices.
  • The inversion runs on plasma-free sustained shots only. No plasma shot is inverted, because a plasma column would absorb whatever the coil columns could not and the orthogonality measurement would be meaningless.
  • Flux loops are excluded from the inversion: their absolute gain is not established — the adjudication fits and divides out a per-channel scale precisely because a factor of 2π between store and kernel conventions cannot be ruled out — and an unknown row gain would corrupt every inverted current.
