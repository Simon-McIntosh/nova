The layout is derivable — and the data refuses it anyway

Turns on a regular grid spanning the pack, with the position vacated where the chamfer cuts it, return exactly 23 turns — the published count — on all four coils with nothing fitted; the vacated corner agrees with efm's cross-over to ~1 mm. The instruments were proven sharp by planted-recovery tests (fill to 0.01, 2 mm displacement to 1 mm, gains to 2%), so the null is about the archive, not the code. Four independent refusals:

  1. Held-out near-probe prediction worsens on three of four coils (−0.37% to −0.45%) against all-positive training gains — classic overfitting; fails promotion outright.
  2. Collateral degradation on 29–46 untargeted channels per coil, largest ON the near probes.
  3. The fitted fill fraction pins to its lower bound on three coils — no interior optimum.
  4. The preferred grid shape disagrees across identically-wound coils (4×6 vs 8×3) — four coils wound the same way cannot have two layer counts.

The decisive finding — the near-probe misfit is campaign-dependent

A rigid-displacement scan (a strictly larger family than any interior layout) runs to the ±5 mm boundary for 5–18% improvement, and its preferred direction reverses between train and held-out exactly on the two coils whose shot split straddles the 192xx/25xxx campaign boundary — and on neither coil whose split does not. A static geometry error cannot flip sign between shot groups. There is a campaign-dependent term in the near-probe readings that no static description can carry: independent, field-shape corroboration of the P5-lower amplitude shift, extended to P4 lower. The next lever is campaign-resolved sensor calibration, not geometry.
