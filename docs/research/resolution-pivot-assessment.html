The resolution pivot: support, not cell count, owns the certificate-map error

This record answers one question and records the strategy it settles. Every number below is read from a run receipt; the three figures are drawn by scripts/prototypes/pivot_figures.py, which refuses to draw if a cited receipt is missing or a plotted value disagrees with the value stated in the run's report.

The question

The forward solve carries its accuracy on the topology read. The open question was where the read's accuracy and cost actually come from, and therefore what to spend next: more cells, a different support for the current booking, a different derivative route through the polygon kernel, or a different mathematical form for the separatrix. Posed directly, the lead asked whether the certificate map's fidelity failure is a resolution failure or a support failure, and whether refinement alone can be trusted to converge it.

The verdict and the decision

The verdict is that the accuracy failure came from the support, not from the resolution; that the saddle normal form is needed at any resolution; that the compile cost is flat in cell count and comes from the kernel point jet; and that refinement converges once the support is right. The decision is refine-with-right-support: carry the accuracy on cell count from about 2,000 cells upward, keep the topology read with its saddle normal form, and stop the kernel-tangent and custom-rule route.

Figure 1 — the certificate map against the realised cells

The map's own fidelity, over the same three rungs, for the two support arms in the diverted and limited cases. Each exact-arm point is decomposed into the term that books the current onto the grid (dashed) and the term that closes the prescribed exterior (dash-dotted), so the arm's remaining residual is visible rather than inferred.

Two things in this figure carry the verdict. The legacy support is erratic: its failure does not shrink monotonically as cells are added, so no amount of refinement can be trusted to converge it. The exact support's dominant remaining term is the booking image, and that term falls in pitch with fitted order 1.5079302 — the half-order behaviour the saddle form is meant to replace, on a support where the geometry is no longer the dominant error.

The exterior-closure term is not a support error and is not read as one. It is a fixture artefact: the prescribed exterior subtracts an analytic-density image integrated on production spline-clipped support seeded by analytic topology, so replacing the support leaves a closure mismatch even at the analytic state (0.00622043464, 0.0176941313, 0.0111257411 at 550, 2098 and 5158 cells). Posing that exterior on the exact support is its own node.

Figure 2 — the topology read's saddle membership against cells

Saddle membership error for the current read against the quadratic-only arm, in the diverted case, with each rung's acceptance bound drawn as a dotted line.

This is the claim that the saddle normal form cannot be traded away for resolution. The quadratic-only arm is not a slightly worse read that more cells would repair: at 2,000 cells it does not admit the X-point at all, so the refinement moves it further from the answer rather than toward it. The current read's error, by contrast, is already three orders of magnitude below its bound at 550 cells.

Figure 3 — where the read's expanded equations live

Expanded equations per packed-kernel call site, native against batched, at 132 cells, with the two cold compile walls labelled. One bar is one call site; the sites are grouped by read stage.

The per-site counts are near-invariant in cell count: the two _sampled_flux sites enclose 47,610 equations each and the six Hessian sites 104,155 each, and the totals move by about one percent between the 132-cell and 550-cell rungs. So the compile cost is a property of the kernel's point jet, not of the mesh, and it does not shrink as the solve is refined. The batching that halves the site count halves the compile wall, to 477.7 s, and still stands about eight times over the 60 s clause — which is why the compile route is a blocker in its own right rather than a resolution choice.

What the three figures decide together

Refinement is the right lever, but only on the right support. The exact support turns a non-converging, order-0.54 legacy failure into a booking residual that falls in pitch with order 1.5079302, and the topology read already sits three orders below its bound at every tested rung and needs its saddle normal form to stay there. Refining further is affordable in graph size, because the expanded-equation count is flat in cell count, but it is not yet affordable in cold compile, because the cost sits in the kernel point jet and halving the call sites only halves it.

What this record rests on, and what it does not claim

  • Every number here is a read of a persisted row receipt or attribution file from a completed H200 job; the three figures are regenerated from those files by one committed script.
  • The figures carry a negative control: the script exits non-zero when a cited receipt is missing or when a plotted value disagrees with the value the run's report states, so a figure cannot silently drift from its receipt.
  • No forward solve was run for this record, so no residual, convergence or force-balance claim is made. The claims are about the read, the certificate map and the compiled cost of both.
  • The bound lines in figure 2 are the run's frozen acceptance formula evaluated at each rung's logged pitch, not a re-derived tolerance.
