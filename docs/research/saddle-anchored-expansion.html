Research input

Saddle-anchored X-point expansion

The X-point cell is booked from one conic anchored at the physical saddle, whose value, gradient and Hessian are the exact Biot–Savart jets evaluated at the saddle the read locates. The map runs: every state that reaches a timing returns a finite residual at every width. It fails the speed gate at 282 realised cells with a ratio of 30.30 against a 1.25 ceiling, and the failure now decomposes into two measured factors. A first build captured the carrier in the compiled program and published a 152.9 MB executable; the rebuild takes the carrier, operands and states as runtime arguments and the executable is 1.69 MB, with no dense HLO constant larger than one element at any width. The cost is therefore not constant capture. It is the slice rule: the gate arm provisions 12.1× the baseline's members and spends 2.5× the per-member cost, and those two factors multiply to the measured ratio.

Method and provenance

The saddle-anchored map is scripts/prototypes/saddle_expansion/expansion.py, measured by scripts/prototypes/saddle_expansion/speed_gate.py. It imports the certified carrier from scripts/prototypes/fine_grid_convergence/context.py, the exact source jets from scripts/prototypes/read_route/jets.py and the axis-facing baseline from scripts/prototypes/xpoint_cell_owner/owner_map.py, editing none of them. Both arms are lowered and compiled in the same process, on the same carrier, and timed in the same allocation; the axis-facing rows are the control the gate is defined against and are measured in that same job, as the brief requires.

The read-only carrier cache is /home/ITER/mcintos/.config/reckon/crew/runs/r-20261014T174345284407-lean-map/cache, the one the accepted fast-map run uses. The cache named in the node brief holds a 5,000-cell machine for two other analytic cases and no diverted carrier at all, so a request against it raised FileNotFoundError during input loading and supplied no evidence. The diverted carrier is the analytic closed-form member under its own offset wall, so its realised cell count runs ahead of the requested count: 250 requested realises 282, 500 realises 550 and 2,000 realises 2,098. The gate's own condition is the 2,098-cell row.

Binary64 is configured before any JAX array is constructed, the runner asserts jax_enable_x64 and a GPU backend, and the H200 log prints the imported module path, worktree, working directory, backend and device. Both builds are SLURM jobs on 98dci4-gpu-0003 (NVIDIA H200 NVL) under betelgeuse and reservation gpu_0003_grpA, one job with the size rungs as rows, never an array. Per-state wall is the median of five timed evaluations after the first call, so compilation is not charged to it.

The structural change, and what it bought

The first build lowered each arm through a closure over the carrier, the operands and the batched state, so XLA hoisted every one of them into the compiled program. The published executable carried 152.9 MB of generated code on this carrier, and at 2,098 cells the hoisted state alone exceeded the device: the runner raised RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED while trying to allocate 263.80 GiB.

The rebuild gives every carrier, operand and state array to the lowered function as a runtime argument, and closes over only the density profile and the topology policy, which carry callables and scalars rather than arrays. The measured consequence is in the executable column below: 152.9 MB becomes 1.68 MB at every width, and the largest dense HLO constant in the lowered module is one element at 282, 550 and 2,098 cells alike. A check in the style of the equilibrium solve tests reads that number off the lowered text, so a reintroduced closure reports itself as a constant that scales with the carrier.

The change does not, by itself, move the wall. The excess was never the constants; it is the integration rule that consumes the conic, which is the subject of the next two sections.

Speed gate

The gate is the brief's condition: the saddle map must cost at most 1.25× the axis-facing map per state at width 128, compile within 60 s and stay under 50 MB of generated code. A batch of 128 states is built from the carrier reference moments plus an independent seed-offset perturbation, so both arms see identical inputs.

What dominates the cost

The rebuild's ratio decomposes exactly into two measured factors, because the arms are provisioned differently and their per-member costs differ.

  • Members provisioned. The axis-facing arm integrates a fixed 16 members by construction. The saddle arm integrates the candidate list the disk sizes: at 282 cells, 194 of the machine's 282 cells lie within the disk radius plus its margin. That is 12.1× the control's provision, and it is the whole of the first factor.
  • Cost per member. The axis-facing arm spends 0.2230 ms across 16 members, 13.9 µs per member. The saddle arm spends 6.7590 ms across 194 members, 34.8 µs per member. That is 2.5×, and it is the whole of the second factor.

12.1 × 2.5 = 30.3, which is the measured ratio to the last digit. The decomposition is not a hypothesis about which term dominates; both factors are read directly off the same two rows, and neither alone accounts for the gap.

The per-member excess is the slice rule, and its shape is visible in the lowering. Every candidate bound becomes a slice — the two disk caps, each polygon vertex, two roots per polygon edge and both discriminant tangent roots, on the order of twenty intervals per member — and each interval carries an 8-node Gauss–Legendre outer rule with an inner rule inside it, about 168 slice evaluations per member. The axis-facing arm pays a 128-node rule but over the same twenty intervals and only 16 members, so its member count is what saves it, not its rule.

A second, decisive measurement comes from the rebuild's own correctness guard. The gate arm is required to reproduce the first build's integration to 1e-12; it does not. The maximum absolute difference in moment_residual over four states is 0.6593670573 against a tolerance of 1e-12, and the runner exits nonzero on that. The two configurations differ only in outer degree (8 against 128) and in the candidate list, and the candidate list cannot explain the gap: the list covers every cell within 1.40 m of the saddle while the disk radius is 0.40 m, so every cell the disk can make live is in both configurations, and the two compute the same live set. The difference is therefore the outer rule, and it is not a small truncation error. The disk cap contributes a half-width sqrt(radius² − u²) whose slope is unbounded as the slice approaches the cap, so the integrand is only Hölder-continuous at the cap endpoint and an 8-node Gauss–Legendre rule converges to it very slowly. That is exactly the term the brief's second clause asks to replace with a closed-form slice-discriminant integral, and the measurement says the replacement is required for accuracy and not only for speed.

A sizing defect found and corrected

The candidate list was grown by a literal one metre of margin while the disk radius is given in pitches. On this carrier the pitch is 0.1005 m, so the 0.40 m radius was already smaller than the margin and the admitted set was most of the machine: 194 of 282 cells. Because the margin was an absolute length rather than a scale of the disk, the admitted share grew as the grid refined, and at 550 and 2,098 cells the width-128 saddle lowering exhausted the device.

The margin is now one median pitch — the saddle motion the list must cover between neighbouring states — which is independent of resolution, so the candidate count is a static shape per carrier in the sense the brief asks for and does not grow along the resolution ladder. The rows reported above were measured before that correction, so the 12.1× member factor is an upper bound on the sizing cost and the corrected measurement is outstanding.

What would fit

The expansion itself is not the expensive part: evaluating one conic and its density is a handful of flops. The cost is in the integration rule that consumes it.

  • Replace the outer quadrature by the closed-form slice integral where the owned level set is a conic. The integrand in the slice coordinate is a quadratic over a region bounded by the conic's own roots, whose moments are available in closed form; the discriminant integral already exists as scripts/prototypes/xpoint_cell_owner/conic_integral.py, which this node imports and wraps rather than re-implements. This is the term the agreement measurement above isolates, and it attacks the 2.5× per-member factor.
  • Size the candidate margin in pitches. This attacks the 12.1× member factor directly, and it is the correction already landed.
  • Chain the two. If the per-member factor falls to the control's and the member factor falls to the disk's true cut count, the product is what the gate measures, and the gate has not yet been shown to be out of reach.

These are a re-parameterisation of the same mathematics, not a retreat from the exact anchored expansion: the conic, its exact Biot–Savart jets and the shared membership/booking integral are unchanged.

Negative control

The declared negative control is that replacing the Newton-located saddle with the fitted Bell stationary point reproduces the connected-lobe map error. It was not run. Its natural host is the map-error clause, which is measured against the analytic sample and cannot be read off a speed row; the map was not exercised against the analytic flux at all.
