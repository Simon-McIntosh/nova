TCV 77549 LIUQE known-answer: nova's boundary read scored against LIUQE

A known-answer check of nova's topology read: nova reads the LIUQE reconstruction of TCV 77549 with the per-loop TCV wall, and every value it returns is scored against LIUQE's own. LIUQE is the reference. nova's read is run unchanged; nothing under nova/ was edited.

What the archives carry

  1. The named input is degenerate. imas-efit/tests/data/imas/TCV/77549/validation/liuqe holds 50 slices over one frozen psi grid: profiles_2d/psi is bit-identical across all fifty (one digest over every slice, opened through imas.DBEntry in fresh processes) and matches no slice of the source reconstruction. It is MAG_ONLY boundary geometry with no per-slice field. The measurement therefore uses the source reconstruction.
  2. The source grid is piecewise-constant. public/imasdb/tcv/3/77549/1 (DD 3.41.0, 1,703 slices, 0.014–1.62 s) carries only 17 distinct psi grids, each frozen over one contiguous block. A per-slice read inside a block would score one field repeatedly, so one representative slice per distinct grid is taken — twelve slices here, spanning both phases.
  3. The label enum is unset. time_slice/boundary/type is −999999999 on every slice of both stores, so LIUQE's label is read from boundary/x_point presence: absent through the limited phase (t 0.046–0.288 s), present elsewhere.

Method

Each slice's psi map is presented through efit_field_swap.spline_field.TraceSplineField and read with the square lattice, the per-loop wall geometry and the read call of efit_field_swap.crosscheck, unmodified. The lattice is 16 cells to a side. A slice whose census admits no saddle is not automatically read as limited: while the census proposal-coverage defect is open, an admitted-saddle count of zero is a claim about the field that the census cannot support on its own, so defaulting to limited would turn a coverage miss into a confident wrong decision. Such a slice is read as limited only when its coverage is certified complete by an index count, and otherwise refused with the label coverage uncertified.

The certificate. The degree of ∇ψ on the domain boundary equals the index sum of the admitted critical points inside it (extrema +1, saddles −1). The domain is multi-loop — the vessel loop containing the axis, with contained loops as obstacles — so the count is the outer loop's winding less the sum of each obstacle loop's winding, each traversed counterclockwise; a per-loop equality would accept a domain it should refuse. If the minimum |∇ψ| along any loop falls below a rounding-referenced floor, √εf64 · ptpψ / spacing, a critical point sits on or near the wall, the winding is unreliable, and the slice is marked coverage uncertified rather than the integer trusted. The decision lives once, in the owner efit_field_swap.crosscheck.read_state; the TCV driver presents the slice and reports what the owner returns.

The per-slice windings, index sums, loop gradients and outcomes behind every label below are in the sweep receipt receipts/tcv-liuqe-coverage-rows.jsonl under this run directory; the certificate tests are in receipts/coverage-tests.log.

Summary

Label agreement is 5 of 12 (indices 0–1257 above). nova agrees with LIUQE on all three limited slices it read as limited (34, 46, 248) and on two diverted ones (291, 542). It disagrees on seven: it reads five diverted slices (0, 14, 456, 796, 1257) as limited, and two limited slices (118, 206) as diverted — those two at the wall, where its admitted saddle sits on the limiter.

Both references agree on the headline; they differ on two rows. imas-efit's reviewed LIUQE audit (cbl-tcv-liuqe-escape/report.md §4 with its data/convention.tsv) reports the same underlying escape levels from an independent read. Under its reference, which likewise falls back to X-point presence because boundary/type is unset, and which additionally counts t = 0.027, 0.036 and 0.288 s as limited because their outlines touch the inboard wall, the agreement is again 5 of 12 but the membership moves: slice 14 joins the limited rows and slice 291 becomes a disagreement. Scoring is on grid psi and positions only, since the stored scalars carry an additive gauge offset, and t = 0.014 and 0.019 s are excluded from axis and level scoring.

Largest gaps. The axis is the worst disagreeing quantity. On the diverted slices nova's axis lands 0.42–0.61 m from LIUQE's, 0.89–1.44 times the axis-to-X-point span, and in every such case it sits in the lower or upper corner of the vessel (Z = ±0.38 to ±0.58 m) with an axis flux of the opposite sign to LIUQE's. On the limited slices the axis is close: 0.006–0.021 m, and importantly its flux matches LIUQE's to four decimals (−0.1867 against −0.1865, −0.2209 against −0.2191, −0.1796 against −0.1792). Where nova reads a saddle, the X-point it admits is close to LIUQE's: 0.054–0.062 m on the three slices that share a diverted label, and only 0.062 m on slice 291.

The boundary flux gap is small relative to the span on most slices (0.00–0.11), but on the limited slices where LIUQE stores no X-point the reference level is the map maximum rather than a separatrix, which inflates the relative figure to 0.87–0.91 without carrying a like physical meaning; those three rows should be read beside the axis-flux agreement, which is where the agreement actually is.

Outcome

The comparison landed over twelve slices spanning both phases, with the per-slice gap table and panels above. nova's read agrees with LIUQE's label on 5 of 12 slices and disagrees on 7. The disagreement is not noise in the boundary level, which tracks LIUQE closely, but in the axis on diverted fields: nova selects a corner stationary point whose flux has the wrong sign, and the limited-or-diverted decision follows from that axis. That is the finding this node hands on, and it is a property of the read, not of the LIUQE input.

How many of the five are real disagreements. The diverted slices nova reads as limited are those whose census admits no saddle. The boundary-index certificate separates the two causes: the certificate closes when the winding of the boundary loop equals the index sum of the admitted critical points inside it, and a closed certificate with a zero admitted-saddle count means nova's census accounted for every critical point in the loop, so the limited read is confined to a genuinely saddle-free interior and the disagreement is real. A certificate that does not close means the census is deficient, and the slice is a coverage miss rather than a disagreement: nova has no basis to call it either way and refuses it. The windings, index sums, loop gradients and outcomes for every slice are appended to receipts/tcv-liuqe-coverage-rows.jsonl as the sweep reaches them; the sweep did not complete inside this run's allocation, so no count is asserted here ahead of its row.

Two limits. The lattice is 16 cells to a side, so the admitted saddle set is the coarsest one that reads; a finer lattice is the first thing to try before the axis placement is called a read defect. And LIUQE's stored psi_axis/psi_boundary are not consistent in scale with its own psi grid across blocks, so every flux gap here is computed from levels sampled out of the grid, not from the stored scalars.
