Vertical position of the converged forward solve

An elongated plasma can move vertically along a weak equilibrium direction. The free 550-cell diverted lobe solve reproduced a line-search stall at 1.54987034415 × 10−3. A current-centroid height row held by one radial-field amplitude reached 2.09179996955 × 10−13 on the same booked map.

Public solve and source

ForwardSolveRequest.vertical_pin and ForwardProfile.solve(vertical_pin=...) accept a VerticalPositionPin. It builds a ConstraintPair from the existing CurrentCentroidConstraint and a ConstraintBinding. The target is the current-centroid height on ALL_DOMAIN booked support. If the caller declares a vertical-field circuit direction, the single unknown is the current along that direction; the focused test solves this branch and checks its flux response. When no set is declared, the unknown is a uniform radial-field amplitude. One owner in constraint.py supplies the axisymmetric −2π R Z BR flux value, gradient and Hessian to both the public pin and the lobe runner.

AugmentedConstraintSystem owns the pin row and compensating unknown. On the converged Newton route, an omitted pin becomes an inactive uniform-field pair: the runtime row mask fixes its unknown at zero while preserving the pinned state's shape. An active row holds the centroid. The public ForwardProfile.solve passes the complete pair through Newton-Krylov's map_arguments, so the target, field image and scale enter its compiled loop as runtime operands. A public test calls the solve with no pin and with two target heights and field scales; all three requests have one compilation-cache identity, with one miss and two hits. Replacing the runtime target with 0.8 m changes the terminal height to 0.8 m and makes the original 0.7 m request unqualified, demonstrating an effect beyond observing the passed argument. A separate lowered test compiles the augmented Newton-Krylov routines once and calls that executable for free and pinned pairs. Profile fitting does not enter either solve.

Elongated analytic carrier

The analytic flux map books two equal-current cells with current, radial first moment and vertical first moment. At each terminal state, the independent test read divides the sum of booked vertical moments by the sum of booked currents; it does not call the solve's centroid observation. The cell centroids are 0.8 and 1.2 m radially and 0.24 and 0.96 m vertically, giving measured extents of 0.4 and 0.72 m. Their ratio is 1.8, above the asserted 1.6 floor. A constant vertical forcing of 0.1/κ² leaves the free map without a root; the inactive unknown stays at zero. The radial-field unknown can balance that forcing while the active centroid row sets the target.
