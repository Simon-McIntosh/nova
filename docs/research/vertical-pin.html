Vertical position of the converged forward solve

An elongated plasma can move vertically along a weak equilibrium direction. The free 550-cell diverted lobe solve reproduced a line-search stall at 1.54987034415 × 10−3. A current-centroid height row held by one radial-field amplitude reached 2.09179996955 × 10−13 on the same booked map.

Public solve and source

ForwardSolveRequest.vertical_pin and ForwardProfile.solve(vertical_pin=...) accept a VerticalPositionPin. It builds a ConstraintPair from the existing CurrentCentroidConstraint and a ConstraintBinding. The target is the current-centroid height on ALL_DOMAIN booked support. If the caller declares a vertical-field circuit direction, the single unknown is the current along that direction. When no set is declared, the unknown is a uniform radial-field amplitude; its axisymmetric flux image is −2π R Z BR at every grid, wall and direct-sample target. Target, scale, basis and seed are operands of the augmented Newton update.

The chord-Newton map in nova.equilibrium.solve uses the same ConstraintBinding owner and an active runtime flag. VerticalPositionPin(active=False) retains the public augmented state layout but fixes its compensating unknown at zero; an active row holds the centroid. The typed public request routes its pair through the existing augmented Newton-Krylov path. The prototype lobe correction keeps state, field images and factor as runtime arguments and compiles once for the free and pinned arms. The focused compiled test passes the binding itself as a runtime pytree input. Profile fitting does not enter either route.

Elongated analytic carrier

The focused test books two equal-current cells with current, radial first moment and vertical first moment. Their vertical separation is 2 × 0.2 × 1.8 = 0.72 m against a 0.4 m radial width, so the carrier elongation is 1.8. Its restoring row weakens as 1/κ², and the observation is CurrentCentroidConstraint.booked_height, the sum of booked vertical first moments divided by booked current. No state coordinate stands in for height.
